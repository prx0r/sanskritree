## Part 1: Foundational Claims

### 1. Camatkāra's First Technical Use = Utpaladeva [T]
Confirmed by Gnoli, cited by Rastogi. Utpaladeva is the first to deploy camatkāra as a technical philosophical term within Kashmir Śaivism.

### 2. Vimarśa = Camatkāra [T — Direct Textual]
**ĪPK 1.5.11 Vṛtti:** Utpaladeva renders vimarśa AS camatkāra. The Vṛtti explicitly equates reflexive awareness with aesthetic relish.

**ĪPK 1.5.11 Vivṛti:** This camatkr̥ti is further understood as viśeṣaprīti (unique/special delight) or viśeṣapratīti (unique experience).

Abhinavagupta's commentary on this verse makes explicit: "vimarśa by virtue of its being camatkāra could alone constitute sentience." The distinction between animate and inanimate IS the presence of camatkāra.

### 3. Svātantrya = Camatkāra [T — Direct Textual]
**ĪPK 2.4.20 Vivṛti:** Utpaladeva develops svātantrya (freedom) in terms of camatkāra. The Vivṛti establishes that what distinguishes the sentient from the insentient is not merely freedom (svātantrya) but freedom AS camatkāra (aesthetic relish). Svātantryābhāvāt (absence of freedom) = camatkārābhāvāt (absence of aesthetic relish).

### 4. Two Formulations of Camatkāra [T — Reconstructed from Utpaladeva's texts by Rastogi]

**Kriyā-formulation (action/process):** camat = savouring (bhuñjānatārūpa), kāra = action (kriyā). Camatkāra is the ACTIVITY of savouring. This is Utpaladeva's own formulation. Pertains to the parāpara (ultimate-non-ultimate) realm.

**Icchā-formulation (will/existential):** camatkāra = icchā (will). "Everywhere savouring by itself is will" (sarvatra camatkāra eva icchā). This is traced to Somānanda's influence. Pertains to the parā (absolute) realm.

Both formulations converge on camatkāra as repose (viśrānti) in the subject.

### 5. Two Directions of Camatkāra [R — Rastogi's reconstruction]

**Existential camatkāra:** The general aesthetic undercurrent interlaces all thought. Joyful valuation of everything as oneself. The system's perception of all categories as "soaked in rasa" (sarasa) vs. non-Trika perception as "dry" (śuṣka).

**Experiential camatkāra:** Pertains to aesthetics proper. Bhakti and art experience. Further subdivided into subjective (the savoring act) and objective (the art object/medium).

## Part 2: Camatkāra as the Principle of Sentience

### 6. What Camatkāra Is [T]
The Vṛtti on ĪPK 1.5.11 renders vimarśa as camatkāra because camatkāra is what distinguishes the sentient from the insentient. Animation = capacity to enjoy oneself by self-objectification. Insentient things utterly lack this.

### 7. Camatkāra as Unimpeded Awareness [T]
Abhinavagupta's two reductions of camatkāra (from Utpaladeva's texts):
1. Camatkāra is unimpeded mental awareness represented by dense uniformity of the spectator's perception
2. It is the mental activity of a savouring subject fully immersed in vibration of wonderful enjoyment/relish
3. Physical changes (trembling, horripilation, etc.) are also called camatkāra

### 8. The Heart (Hṛdaya) Connection [T]
"Entering into heart" (hr̥dayaṁgamatva) = aesthetic savouring = camatkāra. Utpaladeva rationalizes the former as the latter. What enters the heart IS camatkāra.

## Part 3: Recognition (Pratyabhijñā) Has Four Senses

### 9. Utpaladeva's Four Senses of Pratyabhijñā [T — Rastogi's reconstruction from Utpaladeva's texts]

1. **Knowing as unflinching certitude** — no aesthetic bearing
2. **Infinitization (aparimitīkaraṇa)** — consciousness-expansion (saṁvid-vikāsa). Connected to universalization (sādhāraṇīkaraṇa) in rasa theory
3. **Revealing (prakāśana)** — trans-epistemic communication of art's import. Suggestively expressive (vyaṅgya), re-creatively recognitive
4. **Ongoing recognitive synthesis (anusaṁdhāna)** — "aesthetic chewing" (carvaṇā). The processive character of art experience

## Part 4: Beauty and the Lord

### 10. The Lord as "Exceedingly Beautiful" [T]
Utpaladeva describes the Lord as "atisundara" (exceedingly beautiful) and "lalita" (sole charming principle). Rastogi: "This is perhaps the first time in the history of Indian philosophy that reality is reckoned in terms of beauty by an otherwise rigorously gnostic system."

### 11. Bhakti as Rasa / Mahārasa [T]
Rasa experience = pūrṇāhaṃtā-camatkāra (rapturous relishing of perfect I-hood). Bhakti is a rasa by itself, metasentiment (mahārasa) constituting the matrix of all rasas.

### 12. Two Stages of Bhakti = Two Stages of Aesthetic Experience [T]
1. **Sphuraṇāvasthā (emerging state):** unflinching faith = śānta-bhakti (tranquil devotion)
2. **Prarūḍhāvasthā (perfected state):** total self-surrender and absolute identity = dāsya-bhakti (servitude devotion)

## Part 5: Aesthetics as Pure Transaction

### 13. Aesthetics Under "Vimala Vyavahāra" [T]
Utpaladeva places aesthetics under "pure worldly transaction" (vimala vyavahāra). Human activity is twofold: sullied (malina = ignorance-blinded) and pure (śuddha = those who know reality).

Abhinavagupta extends: aesthetic transaction = "a particular human transaction of the pure category."

### 14. Freedom from Impurity = Freedom of Self-Awareness [T]
Utpaladeva: impurity (mala) = either loss of freedom of self-awareness OR loss of awareness of one's innate freedom.

Nirmalatā (stain-free state) = activity ensuring complete enjoyment of one's essential being consisting in freedom and self-awareness.

## Part 6: Music

### 15. Utpaladeva as Music Authority [T]
Abhinavagupta cites Utpala seven times in the Abhinavabhāratī's Geyādhikāra (Book on Music, NŚ chapters 28-36). Utpala was likely a commentator on parts of the Nāṭyaśāstra's music section.

### 16. Utpala's Musicological Positions [T]
- Held that svaras have two kinds of forms: prākr̥tā (natural) and vaikr̥tā (deformed)
- Madhyama is the most important note in gāndharva and sāman
- Disagreed with Dattila and Viśākhilācārya on which notes are indispensable
- His view: the rule that certain notes shouldn't be dropped was a generalization of musical practice, not a rule governing structures
- Argued that ascending svaras generate excessive charm
- Had specific views on tāla structures and the doubling principle

### 17. Music as Nirmala Vyavahāra [R]
Music falls under spotless, immaculate transaction (nirmala vyavahāra) as a cultural activity. It is an outflow of Parā Vāc (Supreme Speech) in its alternative conceptualization as nāda (resonance).

## Part 8: What This Means for Our Thesis

### The ρᵢ/νᵢ Distinction Is Textually Confirmed

The two formulations of camatkāra (existential/kriyā and experiential/icchā) map onto:
- **ρᵢ (basal self-affection):** existential camatkāra — joyful valuation of everything as oneself. This IS the intrinsic character of consciousness.
- **νᵢ(t) (local valence):** experiential camatkāra — the specific savoring of art, beauty, emotion. This varies with context.

### Vimarśa IS Aesthetic Relish

The Vṛtti on ĪPK 1.5.11 makes this explicit. Reflexive awareness IS NOT merely cognitive — it IS aesthetic. This is the single strongest textual support for the entire rasa thesis.

### Freedom IS Aesthetic Relish

ĪPK 2.4.20 develops svātantrya in terms of camatkāra. The freedom of consciousness IS the capacity for aesthetic self-relish. The distinction between sentient and insentient IS the presence of this capacity.

### Recognition Has Four Senses

The fourth sense — anusaṁdhāna (ongoing recognitive synthesis) — directly connects pratyabhijñā to the temporal integration function we identified in the memory analysis. Recognition is NOT a single event — it is an ongoing process of connective synthesis.
