## Core Idea: 19th-century chromatic music (Schubert, Chopin, Wagner) systematically explores efficient voice leading in chromatic space, using augmented sixth chords, enharmonic equivalence, and the major-third system.

## Rasa-Relevant Findings

### Finding 1: Chromaticism as Controlled Expansion of the Aperture
Chromatic music uses more notes per macroharmony but maintains centricity and efficient voice leading. It's a controlled expansion of the available vocabulary without losing structural coherence. This is the auditory analogue of a "nondual but structured" state — more possibilities within a unified field.

### Finding 2: The Major-Third System (Schubert, Wagner)
Schubert and Wagner discovered that mediant relationships (C major to E major, etc.) could create large-scale harmonic motion while maintaining stepwise voice leading. This is the "major-third system" — modulations by major third that are connected by single-semitone voice leading.

This is a *higher-order organizational principle* that wasn't explicitly recognized until Tymoczko's geometric analysis. The composers discovered the geometry through intuition, not mathematics. For rasa: the same geometric structures that constraint auditory experience can be *intuited* without formal knowledge — which is what we'd expect if the structure IS the felt quality.

### Finding 3: The Tristan Chord as a Geometric Necessity
The famous "Tristan chord" (F-B-D#-G#) is not a harmonic aberration but a predictable point in the voice-leading lattice — it's the chord that allows smooth transition between half-diminished and dominant seventh chords. Its "strange" sound comes from its position in the lattice, not from any violation of harmonic rules.

For the project: experiences that seem mysterious or transcendent may be predictable geometric configurations — not violations of the structure of the field but explorations of previously unmapped regions. The Tristan chord is "mysterious" only before you see the geometry.

### Finding 4: Generalized Augmented Sixths
Augmented sixth chords (Italian, French, German) can be generalized to any scale. They function as "pivot chords" that connect different keys through efficient voice leading. The augmented sixth is the chord that exists at the intersection of two key regions.

This is a model for how transitions between different aperture states work: there are "pivot chords" — states that belong to both organizations simultaneously. The Vijñānabhairava's junctions are pivot states. Recognition might be the sustained capacity to occupy the pivot region.
