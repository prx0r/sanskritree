## Core Idea: A scale is a ruler for measuring musical distance. Scales provide alternative metrics over the same pitch space.

## Rasa-Relevant Findings

### Finding 1: Multiple Metrics Over the Same Space
The same pitch interval (e.g., C to E) can be measured as 2 diatonic steps, 3 pentatonic steps, or 4 chromatic steps. The "distance" between two points depends on which ruler you choose. For rasa: experience is not just a raw metric space — it's a space WITH A CHOSEN METRIC. The metric changes what counts as "near" and "far." Recognition may be the capacity to shift between metrics.

### Finding 2: Scales as Generalized Keys
A "generalized key" = <scale, macroharmony, tonic>. Composers have hundreds of possible combinations. The space of possible keys is much larger than major/minor. This is the *aperture constraint set* Lᵢ: what's available at a given moment constrains what can be felt. Creating a "tonal space" with a clear tonic is like establishing a stable aperture configuration.

### Finding 3: Modulation as Voice Leading Between Scales
Scale-to-scale modulation uses the same efficient voice-leading principle as chord-to-chord progression. This is hierarchical self-similarity — two temporal scales, same geometric principle. For T_rec: recognition may be a modulation at the highest organizational level, analogous to a key change that doesn't disrupt the melody.

### Finding 4: Evenness as the Key Scale Property
The most useful scales (diatonic, pentatonic, octatonic, whole-tone) are those with high evenness — near-equal division of the octave. Evenness determines how well a scale can support both harmony and counterpoint. This is overdetermination again: the same scales keep appearing because they optimize multiple constraints simultaneously.
