## Core Idea: There's a continuous tradition from medieval counterpoint to jazz, unified by the combination of harmonic consistency + conjunct melodic motion.

## Rasa-Relevant Findings

### Finding 1: The Extended Common Practice as a Cross-Temporal Invariant
Tymoczko argues that the same basic contrapuntal techniques appear from 11th-century organum through Renaissance, Baroque, Classical, Romantic, jazz, and minimalism. What links them is not style but underlying geometric constraints — there are only a few ways to combine conjunct motion with harmonic consistency.

For the project: this is evidence for cross-cultural, cross-temporal invariants in the structure of felt experience. The rasa hypothesis predicts that these invariants are not culturally arbitrary but reflect the geometry of the auditory field. The extended common practice shows that when composers independently explore the space of harmonic possibilities, they converge on the same structures.

### Finding 2: "3+1" Voice Leading as a Universal Schema
Renaissance composers discovered that triadic harmony works best when three voices move by step while the fourth (bass) leaps by fourth or fifth. This "3+1" schema appears in Palestrina, Bach, jazz, and rock.

The schema is not arbitrary — it's geometrically forced. Three voices can move by step because triads are nearly even; the bass leaps because root motion by fifth connects the most consonant harmonic relation. The 3+1 pattern is the *optimal solution* to the constraint satisfaction problem of writing consonant, stepwise counterpoint. It's an attractor in the space of possibilities.

### Finding 3: Parallel Perfect Intervals as a Developmental Constraint
Medieval music banned parallel perfect intervals (octaves, fifths) because they cause voices to lose their independent identity. The ban is a *constraint on the self-organization of the auditory field* — to maintain multiple independent streams, you need to avoid perfect synchronization.

For the partition problem: the reason phenomenal domains don't fuse may be analogous — parallel perfect intervals (complete alignment of two streams) would cause them to lose their independent identity. The auditory system's ban on parallel fifths may be the same principle that prevents phenomenal field fusion. The constraint is not cultural but *structural* — it follows from the need to maintain distinct streams.

### Finding 4: Chromaticism as Systematic Geometric Exploration
19th-century chromaticism (Chopin, Wagner) is not a departure from tonality but a systematic exploration of the geometry of chromatic voice-leading space. The same principles that govern diatonic harmony (efficient voice leading between consonant chords) extend naturally into chromatic space.

This refutes the claim that tonality "broke down" into atonality. The same geometry operates throughout. The shift is not a breakdown but an expansion of the vocabulary. For recognition: enlightenment is not a breakdown of the self but an expansion of its vocabulary — operating in a larger space with the same principles.
