## Rasa-Relevant Findings

### Finding 1: "Avoid Notes" as Aperture Constraints
Jazz theory encodes "avoid notes" — notes that obscure harmonic function if played at the wrong time. Scale degree 4 in a tonic chord, the leading tone in a ii chord, the tonic in a V chord. These are not arbitrary prohibitions — they follow from the voice-leading skeleton: playing certain notes prematurely blurs the functional identity of the chord.

For the aperture: the kañcukas may be "avoid notes" of consciousness — the principles you can't violate without losing the coherence of the phenomenal field. The "avoid note" is not externally prohibited; it's structurally disallowed by the geometry of the field. Recognition = learning which notes are avoid and why, and developing the freedom to play them consciously.

### Finding 2: Tritone Substitution as Topological Invariance
Jazz's tritone substitution (replacing V7 with bII7) works because both chords share the same tritone interval (3 and 7) — they are inversionally related around the tritone axis. The essential voice-leading structure (3→7, 7→3) is preserved under the substitution.

This is a *symmetry of the voice-leading lattice* — two chords that are different in root position are the same in terms of their voice-leading function. For rasa: this is a case where different phenomenal configurations share the same underlying geometric structure. Two experiences that feel different (major vs minor, pleasure vs pain) may share the same geometric skeleton, differing only in orientation.

### Finding 3: Jazz as the Synthesis of Two Traditions
Tymoczko's central historical claim: jazz synthesizes chromatic voice leading (19th-century) with scale-based composition (20th-century). It is the culmination of the extended common practice — the point where the two strands of modern tonality reunite.

For the project: the most productive frameworks may be those that synthesize previously opposed traditions. The rasa analysis + Tymoczko + QRI + Vijñānabhairava is our "jazz synthesis" — combining Sanskrit aesthetics, geometric formalism, field topology, and experimental phenomenology.

### Finding 4: Voice-Leading Metrics (Appendix A)
Tymoczko shows there is no single correct metric for voice-leading size — but there is a "zone of agreement" among reasonable metrics, defined by the submajorization partial order. This zone corresponds to the region where all metrics agree.

For the project: the absence of a single correct metric is important. The phenomenal field may not have a unique metric — different metrics capture different aspects of organization. Recognition is not finding the One True Metric but navigating between metrics flexibly.

### Finding 5: The Interscalar Interval Matrix (Appendix D)
A formalism for representing the relationships between any two scales as a matrix of intervals. This allows precise measurement of "scale distance" — which scales are close, which are far.

For Πᵢ (participant map): the interscalar matrix is a formalization of how different apertures relate. Two apertures with structurally similar constraint sets are "close" in scale space. The "shared world" may be the region of scale space where multiple apertures' interscalar matrices have high overlap.
