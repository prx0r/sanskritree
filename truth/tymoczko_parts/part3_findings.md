## Rasa-Relevant Findings

### Finding 1: The Möbius Strip as the Shape of Two-Note Experience
The space of all possible two-note chords (unordered pitch-class pairs) IS a Möbius strip. This is not an analogy or a teaching tool — it is the literal geometry of the space. The twist in the strip means that some progressions that look like they go in one direction actually connect to their mirror images.

For rasa: if auditory experience has a specific geometric topology (Möbius strip for dyads), this is a concrete constraint on 𝒢ᵢ^auditory. The felt quality of a two-note interval IS its position on the strip. Consonance/dissonance gradients correspond to movement along specific paths. The twist means that some intervals are "nearby" in ways that aren't intuitively obvious — the tritone (6 semitones) is at the center of the strip where the twist occurs, making it the most ambiguous interval.

### Finding 2: The Ant Parable as a Model of Equivalence Relations
The ant parable is *exactly* Wolfram's observer theory in action. The ant's path appears to bounce off boundaries, but that's an artifact of the quotient operation (ignoring octave and order). The "boundaries" are not real — they're imposed by the choice of coordinate system on the quotient space.

This is a metaphor for the kañcukas: the constraints we experience as "boundaries" of the phenomenal field may be artifacts of the equivalence relations we impose, not intrinsic features of reality. Recognition = seeing the twist, seeing that the boundaries are arbitrarily placed.

### Finding 3: The Decomposition into Parallel + Contrary Motion
Any two-voice voice leading decomposes into pure contrary motion (voices move equal distance in opposite directions = sum of pitches invariant) plus pure parallel motion (transposition). The contrary component lives in a vertical slice of the Möbius strip; the parallel component moves it horizontally.

This is a *dimensional decomposition of the phenomenal field* into an "internal" dimension (relative motion, preserving the sum/center) and an "external" dimension (absolute position). In CTPI terms: the self-world distinction might be a similar decomposition of the field into relative and absolute components. Recognition may be the capacity to shift weight between these components — to experience the field as pure contrary motion (internal dynamics without absolute frame) or pure parallel motion (smooth transposition of everything together).

### Finding 4: Continuous Voice Leading as Paths in a Metric Space
Voice leadings are line segments in the space, with length measuring voice-leading size. The geometry of the space *determines* the possible voice leadings — some paths are short (efficient), others are forced to be long (inefficient). The structure of the space constrains what's musically possible.

This directly supports the claim that 𝒢ᵢ (field geometry) determines νᵢ (felt quality) for the auditory case. If the shortest path between two points in chord space produces the smoothest progression, and smoothness predicts positive valence, then the metric of chord space IS a predictor of felt quality. The test: can we predict valence ratings from voice-leading distance alone, controlling for cultural familiarity?

### Finding 5: The Wallpaper Pattern = Quotient of Higher-Dimensional Space
The 2D pitch space is wallpaper-like (periodic), and quotienting it by octave+order operations produces the Möbius strip. This is a general principle: chord spaces for n-note chords are quotients of Euclidean space by a discrete group of symmetries.

This suggests a general architecture for the phenomenal field: Dᵢ = quotient of a higher-dimensional "raw" space by a set of symmetry operations (the Πᵢ map). Different observers may impose different symmetries — hence different quotient structures = different phenomenal fields. The "shared world" is the underlying space before quotienting; the "private field" is the quotient structure.
