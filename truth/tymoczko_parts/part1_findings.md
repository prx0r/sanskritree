## What's Here
Tymoczko sets up his entire project: identify the five features that jointly constitute tonality, argue they are overdetermined (converge on the same structures), and use them to retell Western music history as an "extended common practice" from medieval counterpoint to jazz.

**The five features:**
1. Conjunct melodic motion — melodies move by small distances
2. Acoustic consonance — consonant harmonies preferred, used at stable points
3. Harmonic consistency — chords in a passage are structurally similar
4. Limited macroharmony — 5-8 notes used over moderate time spans
5. Centricity — one note heard as more prominent, a goal of motion

Tymoczko's central claim: the five features are "overdetermined" — there are only a few ways to combine them, and those ways converge on the same familiar structures (consonant triads, diatonic scales, etc.). The overdetermination *IS* what makes these structures feel special.

**Key quote:** "The materials of tonal music are, in Richard Cohn's apt description, 'overdetermined,' in the sense that they are special or distinctive for multiple different reasons." (p. 4)

### Finding 5: The Randomness Experiment as Aperture Probe
Tymoczko's key experiment: generate random note sequences, then add the five features one by one. Each addition dramatically increases perceived tonalness/orderedness/pleasantness. The sequence goes from "oddly appealing" (pure random) to "rudimentary counterpoint" (conjunct motion added) to "distinctive harmonic identity" (harmonic consistency added) to "recognizably tonal" (macroharmony added).

This is a *controlled perturbation of the auditory aperture* — exactly the Vijñānabhairava method applied to music. We could replicate this experiment with EEG while adding features incrementally, measuring which neural variables track each feature's contribution.

### Finding 7: Two-Level Voice Leading = Hierarchical Self-Similarity
Tymoczko shows that voice leading operates at two levels: chord-to-chord and scale-to-scale (modulation). The same efficient voice-leading principle governs both. This is "hierarchical self-similarity" — the same structure at different time scales.

This maps to our T_rec (recognition) model: recognition may be a similar hierarchical transformation — not at the level of content but at the level of the *meta-structure* organizing content. ### Finding 8: Composer's vs Listener's Grammar = Σᵢ vs Dᵢ
