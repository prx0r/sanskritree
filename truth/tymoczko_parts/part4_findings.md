## Rasa-Relevant Findings

### Finding 1: The Triangular Prism as the Shape of Triadic Experience
Three-note chord space is a triangular prism. Augmented triads (equal octave division = 4:4:4) lie on the central vertical axis. Major and minor triads form two interlocking lattices offset from center. Uneven chords (clusters) are near the boundary.

This gives a concrete geometry for triadic phenomenal space. The "distance from center" = evenness = acoustic consonance. The topology is different for different chord types: triads live in a twisted triangular torus, seventh chords in a twisted 4D hypercube. Each chord type has its own phenomenal geometry.

### Finding 2: The Central Axis as Maximum Symmetry
The central line of the prism contains the augmented triad — the most even division of the octave (4:4:4). This is the point of maximum symmetry, where voice-leading possibilities are richest (the augmented triad can resolve to any major or minor triad by moving one note by semitone).

For rasa: the axis of maximum symmetry = point of maximum potential. The augmented triad is "unsatisfying" alone precisely because it's too symmetrical — it has too many possible resolutions. This inverse relationship between symmetry and satisfaction is fascinating for STV: maximum symmetry does NOT mean maximum pleasure. There's a "Goldilocks zone" where moderate symmetry + determinate resolution feels best.

### Finding 3: The 3D Lattice as Voice-Leading Graph
The 40-chord lattice (Fig 1.3.15 from Ch. 1) is the single-semitone voice-leading graph for all triads. It has a complex topology: a twisted triangular torus. The twist means that the top face glues to the bottom with a 120° rotation.

This is a *finite graph embedded in a non-Euclidean space*. The structure constrains which chord progressions are possible with efficient voice leading. The graph IS the "rules" of chromatic harmony — not as arbitrary conventions but as geometric necessities.

### Finding 4: Voice-Leading Lattices as Algebraic Structures
Voice-leading lattices (Appendix C) are discrete structures where chords are points and edges represent single-semitone voice leadings. These lattices exist for any chord size and are related to the continuous chord spaces.

For the rasa-inexternalism link: the lattice is the discrete skeleton of the continuous chord space. This dual structure (continuous + discrete) mirrors the dual nature of phenomenal fields: continuous geometry at the macro level, discrete structure at the micro level. Recognition might involve the capacity to shift between these two descriptions.

### Finding 5: Two Musical Geometries
Tymoczko distinguishes two geometries: (1) chord-type space = quotient of ordered pitch space by OPTIC, (2) voice-leading space = distance between chords measured by minimal motion. The first classifies chords by their intrinsic structure; the second by how they connect.

This is the distinction between 𝒢ᵢ (instantaneous field geometry) and τᵢ (temporal dynamics). The two geometries are related but not identical — a chord's position in chord-type space constrains its possible voice-leading relations. The mapping from 𝒢ᵢ to τᵢ is not arbitrary — it's determined by the topology of the space.

### Finding 6: Weitzmann's Region
The Weitzmann region is the zone near the center of the prism containing augmented triads and their near-relatives. This region was discovered by 19th-century theorist Weitzmann and independently rediscovered by Tymoczko's geometric analysis. It explains how augmented triads connect to major and minor triads through single-semitone motion.

This is a case of *constraint convergence producing the same structure independently* — overdetermination. The geometry predicts a specific region of harmonic possibility that was discovered empirically by composers. For rasa: overdetermination = multiple independent paths converging on the same structure = that structure feels "right" or "inevitable."
