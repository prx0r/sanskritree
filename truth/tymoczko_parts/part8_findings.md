## Core Idea: Classical functional harmony (I-IV-V-I) is governed by efficient voice leading in the lattice of diatonic triads and seventh chords. The ii-V-I progression is the central schema.

## Rasa-Relevant Findings

### Finding 1: Functional Harmony as Minimal-Energy Path
The most common harmonic progressions (I-IV-V-I, ii-V-I) are the SHORTEST PATHS in voice-leading space between the relevant chords. They are not arbitrary conventions — they are geodesics in the geometry of chord space.

For rasa: the feeling of "inevitability" in a perfect cadence is the experience of following a geodesic. The satisfaction of resolution is the feeling of reaching a minimum-energy state. Valence = proximity to attractor in a constrained geometry.

### Finding 2: The Two Lattices (Diatonic and Chromatic)
Tymoczko describes two complementary lattices: the diatonic lattice organizes chord relationships by scale-step intervals; the chromatic lattice organizes them by semitone voice leading. Functional harmony uses both simultaneously — diatonic for large structure, chromatic for local detail.

This dual-lattice architecture is a model of how the aperture maintains two organizational levels simultaneously: a "macro" level (scale, key, self-model) and a "micro" level (individual chords, momentary contents). Recognition might be the capacity to navigate both levels without confusing them.

### Finding 3: Schenker's Mistake
Schenker attempted to reduce all tonal music to a single recursive Ursatz (I-V-I). Tymoczko argues this is too restrictive — there are multiple coherent ways to organize tonality, and the Ursatz is one among many patterns, not the master template.

For the project: the Rejection of the One True Structure is crucial. There is no single Ur-pattern that all good music (or all good consciousness) follows. Pluralism about organizational principles is more accurate than monism. This supports the critique of universal Śiva: there may be no single Ur-subject underlying all experience, but multiple viable organizational patterns.

### Finding 4: Modulation and Key Distance
Modulation between keys can be modeled as voice leading between scales (the same principle as voice leading between chords). "Key distance" = the minimal voice leading connecting two scales.

This gives a formal definition of distance between "worlds" or "aperture configurations." Two phenomenal fields are "close" if their structural parameters differ by small voice-leading steps. The test: do modulations to nearby keys feel more natural than modulations to distant keys, even controlling for cultural familiarity?
