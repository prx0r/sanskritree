# Part 6 Findings — Chapter 5 (Macroharmony and Centricity)
Lines 6699-8737

## Core Idea: Macroharmony (the total collection of notes used over moderate spans) and centricity (one note heard as most stable) are independent dimensions.

## Rasa-Relevant Findings

### Finding 1: Macroharmony Size as Aperture Width
Macroharmony = the number of distinct pitch classes used. Tonal music uses 5-8 (Miller's 7±2 limit). Chromatic music uses all 12. Macroharmony is a measure of *phenomenal bandwidth* — how many distinctions the field maintains simultaneously.

For rasa: the contraction state of the aperture determines macroharmony width. The Vijñānabhairava's void practices reduce macroharmony toward 0 (silence/space). Recognition may involve controlled macroharmony expansion/contraction.

### Finding 2: Centricity as Self-Model Stability
Centricity = one note serving as goal of motion, appearing more frequently. This is the *stability of the attractor landscape* of the field. A strong tonic means the field's dynamics are organized around one point. Weak centricity means the field is more "flat" — no privileged position.

For SMᵢ (self-model): the tonic is like the self-model of the auditory field. Centricity strength = self-model stability. Music with strong centricity has a clear "I" position; music without it is like egoless awareness. The shift from strong to weak centricity (atonal music) is a T_rec-like transformation at the level of musical self-organization.

### Finding 3: The Chromatic vs Scalar Tradition as a False Dichotomy
Tymoczko argues that 20th-century music theory created a false opposition between "scalar" (Debussy, modal) and "chromatic" (Schoenberg, atonal) traditions, based on a mistaken belief that macroharmony and centricity are not independent. Once you see they're independent, you have a much richer space of possibilities.

This is a *philosophical error* that maps onto the hard problem: theorists assumed that if you abandon the tonic (self), you must abandon the scale (world-structure) too. But the two are independent. You can have selfless structured experience (strong macroharmony, weak centricity) or centered chaos (weak macroharmony, strong centricity). The Vijñānabhairava explores both.

### Finding 4: Pitch-Class Circulation as Measure of Dynamical Change
The rate at which pitch classes enter and leave the macroharmony determines the sense of harmonic motion. Slow circulation = stable; fast circulation = unstable. This is a measure of *temporal entropy* — how fast the field's content changes.

For τᵢ: circulation rate is the rate of change of the field's "vocabulary." Fast circulation = high novelty; slow = high familiarity. The optimal rate may be neither too fast (chaos) nor too slow (boredom) — a Goldilocks principle for temporal dynamics.
