# Deepdive 2 — What the Heck Is the Self or Boundary?

## Central result

There is no single scientifically established “self boundary.”

At least seven boundaries must be distinguished:

[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\partial_i^{\mathrm{skin}} &: \text{anatomical boundary};\
\partial_i^{\mathrm{phys}} &: \text{physiological regulation};\
\partial_i^{\mathrm{causal}} &: \text{causal closure};\
\partial_i^{\mathrm{Markov}} &: \text{statistical conditional independence};\
\partial_i^{\mathrm{action}} &: \text{space of controllable outcomes};\
\partial_i^{\mathrm{self-model}} &: \text{represented self};\
\partial_i^{\mathrm{phen}} &: \text{one private phenomenal field}.
\end{aligned}
}
]

These boundaries may overlap, but none is automatically identical with another.

The aperture problem is:

[
\boxed{
\text{Which physical boundary—if any—uniquely selects}
}
]

[
\boxed{
\partial_i^{\mathrm{phen}}?
}
]

The strongest current candidate is not skin, Markov blanket, information integration, or goal scale alone. It is a **maximal temporally persistent closure of memory, value, counterfactual modelling, and reciprocal control**.

Even that does not logically entail phenomenal unity.

# Part I — Is the self a thing?

## 1. Three levels of self

The Śaiva material and contemporary science both require distinctions among:

### Empirical ego

[
Ego_i
=====

\text{traits, roles, biography, social identity}.
]

### Minimal embodied subject

[
S_i^{\min}
==========

\text{first-person location, ownership, agency, presence}.
]

### Absolute I-consciousness

[
I_\infty
========

\text{the Pratyabhijñā claim of universal self-manifest}
]

[
\text{consciousness}.
]

Disruption of the empirical ego does not imply disappearance of minimal experience.

Disruption of ownership or agency does not necessarily imply absence of manifestness.

This means:

[
\boxed{
Ego_i
\neq
S_i^{\min}
\neq
M_i.
}
]

## 2. The self as process

The best scientific account treats the self not as a fixed inner object but as a dynamically maintained model and control process involving:

* bodily regulation;
* first-person perspective;
* sensory attenuation;
* action prediction;
* autobiographical memory;
* social positioning.

Active-inference accounts of self-representation model multiple hierarchical levels, from bodily regulation through narrative identity. ([OUP Academic][10])

The self is therefore not merely inferred once. It is continually enacted:

[
S_i(t+1)
========

F
\left(
S_i(t),
B_i,
A_i,
M_i,
W_i
\right).
]

# Part II — Depersonalization and selflessness

## 3. Depersonalization is not simple ego loss

Depersonalization commonly involves:

* emotional numbing;
* detachment from body;
* reduced reality or presence;
* altered agency;
* hyper-reflective monitoring.

An active-inference account proposes that aberrant precision control and failures of somatosensory attenuation may disrupt transparent pre-reflective self-modelling. The individual may retain explicit knowledge that the body and thoughts are theirs while losing the ordinary effortless sense of control and reality. ([ScienceDirect][11])

This gives:

[
\operatorname{ExplicitIdentity}_i
\approx
\text{preserved},
]

while:

[
\operatorname{Presence}_i,
\operatorname{Agency}_i,
\nu_i
\downarrow.
]

The important lesson is:

[
\boxed{
\text{self knowledge can survive while self-presence changes}.
}
]

## 4. Pain asymbolia

Pain asymbolia illustrates another dissociation. A person can register nociceptive content without the ordinary aversive significance or “mineness” of pain.

The active-inference interpretation argues that mineness is produced through interoceptive self-modelling, with the anterior insula playing a proposed integrative role. ([Frontiers][12])

Thus:

[
\text{pain content}
\neq
\text{pain ownership}
\neq
\text{pain aversion}.
]

This is directly relevant to the rasa thesis:

[
\mathcal G_i
\neq
\nu_i
\neq
\operatorname{Mine}_i.
]

## 5. Meditative selflessness versus depersonalization

The proposed distinction:

[
\text{meditative selflessness}
==============================

\operatorname{Ownership}\downarrow,
\quad
\rho_i\uparrow
]

versus:

[
\text{depersonalization}
========================

\operatorname{Ownership}\downarrow,
\quad
\rho_i\downarrow
]

is promising but remains partly reconstructive.

Science can more safely distinguish:

[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\text{self-model attenuation};\
\text{bodily presence};\
\text{affective vitality};\
\text{agency};\
\text{valence};\
\text{world engagement}.
\end{aligned}
}
]

Some contemplative states may reduce narrative or ownership structures while preserving positive affect, clarity, and engagement.

Depersonalization often combines altered self-processing with distress, emotional attenuation, and reduced felt reality.

The difference is therefore not **only** affect, but affect is likely a central discriminating variable.

# Part III — The anatomical and bodily boundaries

## 6. Skin

The skin separates much of the organism’s internal physiology from the environment.

But the phenomenal field includes distal objects:

* a tree;
* music;
* distant space;
* remembered events.

So:

[
\partial_i^{\mathrm{skin}}
\neq
\partial_i^{\mathrm{phen}}.
]

Nor are all internal bodily processes consciously included.

Therefore phenomenal boundary cannot mean “everything inside the skin.”

## 7. Peripersonal space

Peripersonal space is the action- and defence-relevant region immediately surrounding the body. It is multisensory, plastic, and influenced by tool use, threat, and bodily ownership.

Bodily self-consciousness includes self-identification, self-location, and first-person perspective, which interact with representations of peripersonal space. ([Frontiers][13])

A tool can extend action-relevant space:

[
\partial_i^{\mathrm{PPS}}
\uparrow.
]

This does not mean the tool becomes a separate conscious field or literally enters all dimensions of the self.

Peripersonal space is therefore a **represented action boundary**, not the phenomenal exclusion boundary.

# Part IV — Markov blankets

## 8. What a Markov blanket is

A Markov blanket is a statistical boundary such that internal and external variables are conditionally independent given blanket variables.

In active-inference treatments, sensory and active states mediate interaction between internal and external states.

Markov-blanket frameworks can describe nested self-organization and partitions at multiple scales. ([PubMed Central (PMC)][14])

But:

[
\boxed{
\partial_i^{\mathrm{Markov}}
\neq
\partial_i^{\mathrm{phen}}.
}
]

## 9. Why not?

Markov blankets can be defined around:

* a cell;
* a neuron;
* a brain region;
* an organ;
* an organism;
* a social collective.

Neural Markov-blanket analyses explicitly permit partitions at multiple scales, from neurons to whole networks. ([ScienceDirect][15])

If every blanket identified one subject, one organism would contain vast numbers of overlapping subjects with no selection rule.

Therefore a blanket is:

[
\boxed{
\text{a candidate statistical partition, not a consciousness marker}.
}
]

It tells us how variables are separated conditionally.

It does not tell us:

* which level is intrinsically privileged;
* which states are co-present;
* whether the bounded process feels;
* whether nested blankets correspond to nested subjects.

# Part V — Levin’s computational boundary

## 10. The cognitive light cone

Levin defines a self through the spatial and temporal extent of the goals it can measure, model, and attempt to affect. This “computational surface” or cognitive light cone is a functional boundary rather than a fixed anatomical shell. ([PubMed][16])

Formally:

[
\mathcal L_i
============

\left{
e:
O_i
\text{ can measure, model, value, or influence }e
\right}.
]

This is much closer to the aperture’s world boundary:

[
\partial_i^{\mathrm{world}}.
]

It captures what belongs to the agent’s field of concern.

## 11. Nested selves

Levin explicitly allows multiple overlapping selves:

[
A_{\mathrm{cell}}
\subset
A_{\mathrm{tissue}}
\subset
A_{\mathrm{organism}}
\subset
A_{\mathrm{group}}.
]

Gap-junctional communication can allow cells to share information and pursue tissue-level anatomical targets inaccessible to each component alone. ([PubMed][16])

This is powerful for explaining:

* multiscale agency;
* functional individuality;
* expansion and contraction of goal boundaries.

But it intensifies the phenomenal problem:

[
\boxed{
\text{If cognitive individuality exists at several scales,}
}
]

[
\boxed{
\text{which scale—if any—is the subject?}
}
]

Levin’s framework deliberately does not impose one unique primary level.

That is scientifically productive but insufficient for phenomenal individuation.

# Part VI — Why does the boundary not scale indefinitely?

## 12. Integration alone is insufficient

A tissue integrates cells.

An organism integrates organs.

A group integrates people.

A society integrates groups.

If integration alone implied consciousness, subjecthood would scale upward without a principled stopping point.

Therefore:

[
\operatorname{Integration}
\not\Rightarrow
\operatorname{PhenomenalUnity}.
]

## 13. Information synergy alone is insufficient

A group may contain synergistic information unavailable to any member.

That establishes:

[
I_{\mathrm{group}}

>

\sum I_{\mathrm{individual}}
]

for certain tasks.

It does not establish one group experience.

Collective affect and interpersonal synchronization can be measured at group level, but group-level annotation and signal convergence remain descriptions of collective dynamics, not proof of a collective phenomenal field. ([arXiv][17])

## 14. Synchrony does not fuse subjects

Behavioural synchronization can increase:

* self–other overlap;
* interpersonal neural synchrony;
* prosociality;
* belonging.

Evidence indicates that self–other overlap and interpersonal neural synchronization can mediate effects of behavioural synchrony on prosocial behaviour. ([PubMed Central (PMC)][18])

But:

[
D_i+D_j
\not\rightarrow
D_{ij}
]

has not been shown.

Synchrony changes relations among fields. It does not demonstrate their numerical merger.

This supports the corrected *pratibimba* model:

[
R_{j\rightarrow i}\subset D_i
]

while:

[
D_j\not\subset D_i.
]

# Part VII — Dehabheda and anusandhāna

## 15. What the Śaiva texts contribute

The canonical aperture specification now separates:

* apoha: content determination;
* anusandhāna: connective synthesis;
* deha-bheda: embodied limit;
* pratibimba: intersubjective influence and expansion. 

The strongest candidate rule is:

[
\boxed{
D_i
===

\operatorname{MaximalDomain}
\left(
\operatorname{Anusandhāna}_i
\right).
}
]

A field consists of the cognitions that can be connected as phases of one stream.

Body difference limits cross-stream synthesis:

[
x\in B_i,\quad y\in B_j
\Rightarrow
\neg C_i(x,y).

## 16. Why this is stronger than skin

The body here is not a shell.

It is the organized process determining:

* shared memory;
* regulatory consequences;
* common action;
* causal history;
* source attribution;
* reciprocal availability.

Thus:

[
B_i
===

\left\langle
S_i,A_i,V_i,\Gamma_i,C_i
\right\rangle.
]

The body acts as the carrier of one mnemonic and regulatory trajectory.

That provides a more principled boundary than skin.

## 17. But the nested-body problem remains

Cells inside the body also possess:

* memory;
* regulation;
* boundaries;
* goals;
* communication.

Why are they not independent phenomenal fields?

Three possibilities remain:

### They are conscious subfields

A nested or combinationist view.

### They are merely functional agents

Agency and cognition do not entail consciousness.

### Their intrinsic activity is absorbed into a larger field

The organism-level closure supersedes local phenomenal individuality.

The third option requires a genuine **exclusion principle**:

[
\boxed{
\text{when lower-level processes enter a higher maximal closure,}
}
]

[
\boxed{
\text{they cease to constitute independent phenomenal tokens}.
}
]

No current scientific theory has established this.

# Part VIII — Wolframian observer quotienting

## 18. A world boundary

A computationally bounded observer cannot discriminate all underlying differences.

It imposes an equivalence relation:

[
x\sim_i y
]

when the distinction is inaccessible or irrelevant to it.

Its effective world is:

[
W_i
===

\Omega/{\sim_i}.
]

This is an excellent formal counterpart to:

[
E_i^{\mathrm{world}}.
]

The observer’s world is constituted by distinctions it can make.

But quotienting does not identify the subject boundary:

[
\boxed{
\text{what the observer distinguishes}
\neq
\text{why the observer is one field}.
}
]

A quotient explains the structure of (W_i), not the existence or exclusivity of (D_i).

# Part IX — A new boundary criterion

The best scientific candidate should combine all the strongest mechanisms while rejecting boundaries that are merely descriptive.

Define a relation:

[

1. (x) and (y) can reciprocally affect one another;
2. their effects alter a shared system-level value function;
3. each can enter common memory and source attribution;
4. perturbing either changes the system’s counterfactual policies;
5. both belong to one persistent developmental trajectory;
6. no larger containing system possesses stronger closure across all five dimensions.

Then:

[
\boxed{
O_i^\ast
========

\operatorname{MaximalClosedSet}
\left(
\mathscr C_i
\right).
}

Its boundary is:

[
\partial_i^\ast

\operatorname{Boundary}

O_i^\ast
\right).

This is stronger than:

* skin;
* Markov blanket;
* causal connectivity;

It requires closure across:

\boxed{

\text{source}

\text{development}.

# Part X — Self, affect, and boundary meet

## 20. A boundary is also a value boundary

A system’s effective self includes what it regulates as part of its own continued organization.

Thus:

[
\partial_i
]

is partly determined by:

[
V_i.
]

Cells coupled into a tissue come to participate in tissue-level goals.

Tools can become action-relevant extensions.

Loved people and social groups can enter one’s field of concern without entering one’s phenomenal field.

This gives three boundaries:

[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\partial_i^{\mathrm{control}} &: \text{what I can regulate};\
\partial_i^{\mathrm{care}} &: \text{what matters to me};\
\partial_i^{\mathrm{experience}} &: \text{what is co-present as me}.
\end{aligned}
}
]

These can expand differently.

A person can care about distant people:

[
\partial_i^{\mathrm{care}}\uparrow
]

without directly experiencing their consciousness:

[
\partial_i^{\mathrm{experience}}
\text{ unchanged}.
]

This is a scientific way to preserve the Śaiva distinction between expansion of contraction and literal fusion.

## 21. Why depersonalization matters

Depersonalization suggests that phenomenal boundary, ownership, affect, and explicit self-knowledge can dissociate.

A person can retain:

[
\partial_i^{\mathrm{phen}}
]

in the sense that their experiences remain private and unified, while losing:

* emotional familiarity;
* bodily presence;
* effortless ownership;
* agency.

Thus:

[
\boxed{
\text{phenomenal exclusion may survive weakening of the}
}
]

[
\boxed{
\text{phenomenal self-model}.
}
]

That means the self-model cannot be the entire explanation of subject individuation.

It explains how the field represents itself, not necessarily why the field is numerically one.
