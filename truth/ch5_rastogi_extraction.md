## Source

Navjivan Rastogi, "Utpala's Insights into Aesthetics and His Impact on Abhinavagupta's Aesthetic Speculation," in *Utpaladeva, Philosopher of Recognition*, pp. [unknown]. 4679 lines extracted.

## Key Findings

### 1. Camatkāra's First Technical Use Is Utpaladeva

Rastogi confirms Torella's claim from Ch.1: "Gnoli also referred to the aesthetical significance of the notion of camatkāra crediting its first technical use to Utpala."

This means the claim we've been making — that Utpaladeva introduced camatkāra into philosophical-religious speculation — is textually confirmed by the scholarship. Utpaladeva, not Abhinavagupta, is the originator of the concept.

### 2. Vimarśa Is Thematized as Camatkāra

Abhinavagupta's conceptualization of vimarśa is expanded in terms of camatkāra in his commentary on Utpaladeva's Vivṛti, specifically on three ĪPK verses: 1.5.11, 1.5.13, and 2.3.8.

This is crucial: vimarśa (reflexive awareness) is NOT separate from camatkāra (aesthetic relish). The connection is explicit in Abhinavagupta's commentary on Utpaladeva's own text. This supports our ρᵢ (basal self-affection) claim: reflexive awareness IS intrinsically affective.

### 3. Two Directions of Camatkāra

Utpaladeva's conceptualization of camatkāra takes two directions:

**Existential camatkāra:** "joyful valuation of everything as oneself." This is the METAPHYSICAL dimension — the recognition that all experience is one's own consciousness, and this recognition IS joy.

**Experiential camatkāra:** the AESTHETIC dimension — the savoring of art, beauty, and emotion. This touches the medium of art, artefact, and aesthetic experience.

This two-direction model maps onto our ρᵢ (basal self-affection — existential) and νᵢ(t) (local valence — experiential) distinction. The Tantrāloka connects both dimensions.

### 4. Bhakti as Rasa / Mahārasa

Utpaladeva's hymns (Śivastotrāvalī) treat devotion (bhakti) itself as a rasa — and not just any rasa, but mahārasa (the great sentiment), the matrix of all rasas.

Rastogi quotes: "rasa experience is nothing but rapturous relishing of perfect I-hood (pūrṇāhaṃtā-camatkāra)."

This connects devotional experience directly to the recognition of universal selfhood. Bhakti IS the aesthetic relish of one's own complete nature, experienced through the form of devotion to Śiva.

### 5. Śānta Rasa

Rastogi discusses the status of śānta rasa (the sentiment of peace/quietude). In Utpaladeva's system, śānta may or may not appear as a separate rasa. When it does, it is "knowledge of truth in the nature of self" (ātmasvarūpaṃ tattvajñānaṃ). In the devotional context, even śānta becomes a form of bhakti.

### 6. The Aestheticizing of Pratyabhijñā

Rastogi describes Abhinavagupta's innovation as the "aestheticizing" of Utpaladeva's Pratyabhijñā philosophy. Utpaladeva provides the philosophical framework; Abhinavagupta extends it into a full aesthetic system. But the key insight — that recognition IS an aesthetic event — originates with Utpaladeva.

### 7. Representation (Ullekha) and Creativity

The chapter discusses Utpaladeva's concept of representation (ullekha) in relation to artistic creativity. The creative act is not copying but a form of re-presentation — the same structure as pratyabhijñā (re-cognition).

### 8. The Vijñānabhairava Connection

Rastogi cites the Vijñānabhairava as a precursor to Utpaladeva's aesthetics, describing it as involving "the embodied self as the stage, cognition-senses as the savouring." This connects our Tier 1 analysis of the Vijñānabhairava as experimental phenomenology to Utpaladeva's theoretical aesthetics.

## What This Confirms for Our Thesis

1. **Camatkāra = Utpaladeva's innovation [T].** The first technical use is by Utpaladeva, not Abhinavagupta. This corrects the common scholarly narrative.

2. **Vimarśa = Camatkāra [T].** Reflexive awareness IS aesthetic relish. Abhinavagupta's commentary on Utpaladeva's text makes this explicit. This is the strongest textual support for our ρᵢ claim.

3. **Two levels of camatkāra [R].** The existential/experiential distinction maps onto our ρᵢ/νᵢ(t) distinction. This is Rastogi's reconstruction from Utpaladeva's corpus, not a single verse.

4. **Bhakti = recognition of pūrṇāhaṃtā-camatkāra [T].** Devotion is the aesthetic relish of complete I-hood. This connects the devotional hymns (Śivastotrāvalī) directly to the philosophical argument.

5. **Utpaladeva as source [T].** Abhinavagupta's aesthetic system is an elaboration of Utpaladeva's insights, not an independent creation. Our citations should prioritize Utpaladeva.
