### Priority 1: High Impact, Good Source Material

#### 11. What the Heck Is Reality?

**Core confusion:** Naive realism — the assumption that perception reveals the world as it is.

**Key questions:**
- Is there an external world? What does physics tell us?
- Hoffman's interface theory: perception is a user interface, not a window
- The MUI argument: evolution favors fitness, not truth
- Kant's phenomenal/noumenal distinction — what survives?
- Quantum mechanics: does observation create reality? (Copenhagen, QBism, Everett)
- The Śaiva position: reality IS consciousness (prakāśa); the physical world is condensed consciousness (TĀ 5:4146)
- Ochema's position: one process, two descriptions — both are real

**Sources:** Hoffman (2008), Kant, QBism (Fuchs), TĀ Vol 5, Bohr, Wheeler's "it from bit"

**Difficulty:** Moderate — touches deep metaphysics but grounded in perception science.

#### 13. What the Heck Is Water?

**Core confusion:** Water is a passive solvent vs. water is an active information medium.

**Key questions:**
- What is water's normal phase? (Liquid ≠ disordered — it has structure)
- Pollack's Exclusion Zone (EZ): 200-500 μm ordered layer, -120 to -200 mV
- Del Giudice's QED coherent domains: ~100 nm, thermally robust (ΔE ≈ 4.6 kT)
- Water as a liquid crystal: what does this mean?
- Does water have memory? What the evidence actually says
- The body is ~70% water — what does this imply?
- Ra water charging: physical mechanism via EZ modulation
- What is NOT supported: homeopathy, unlimited water memory, coherent body-wide matrix

**Sources:** Pollack (2013), Del Giudice et al., Ho (2008), factual-corrections.md

**Difficulty:** High — contested science, need careful evidence grading.

## The Rigor Standard (from the best existing docs)

Every whattheheck document should have:

```
1. Central result box (the one-sentence answer)
2. What X IS NOT (clear error correction)
3. Level 1: What science actually establishes (with evidence grades)
4. Level 2: What the best current theories say (competing accounts)
5. Level 3: What remains unknown or contested
6. The Śaiva / Pratyabhijñā cross-reference (if relevant)
7. The Ochema / MEPIT placement (how this concept fits the framework)
8. Sources with specific citations
```

**Tone:** Explainer-level for the informed non-specialist. Avoid jargon where possible.
Define terms when they must be used. Be honest about uncertainty. No overclaiming.
