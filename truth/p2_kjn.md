## 2/2 — kālāgnirudrasaṃjñā tu nakhāgre nityasaṃsthitam
**P1:** "The [fire] named Kālāgni-Rudra abides constantly at the tip of the nails."
**Varieties:** a) ours (the dissolution-fire at the nail-tips) · b) "the Fire-of-Time-Rudra stands ever at the nails' tips" · c) a metaphorical reading (the nails = the boundary of the body = the edge of the cosmos).
**Most likely:** (a) — the body-locus language of the paṭala (2/5's dehamadhye) supports the literal-bodily reading; the "edge-of-the-cosmos" (c) is the commentary's extension, not the text's.
**Commentary:** the dissolution-fire located at the body's periphery is the microcosm-map in reverse: the tattva-descent (creation) ends at the nails; the fire's blazing upward (2/3) is the withdrawal's return-path. The KJN's cosmography is entirely somatic.

## 3/9 — sarvavyāpī nirañjanaḥ
**P1:** "all-pervading, the Stainless."
**Most likely:** ours — the Stainless in its fullest epithet-form (the apophatic terminus of the cakra-arithmetic, cf. our glossary).
**Commentary:** the lotus-arithmetic (4 → 10⁷ petals) culminating in the *formless* nirañjana is the same ladder as the Kubjikā's sākāra→nirākāra (9/13–16): the arithmetic is the method, the Stainless is the goal.

## 4/15 — the bīja-catalog
**P1:** the run-together list of the siddhi-bījas.
**Varieties:** the list's assignments (hūṃ = māraṇa; klīṃ = vaśīkaraṇa; hrīṃ = ākarṣaṇa...) — our pass-1 presentation followed the print.
**Most likely:** ours, pending a print-check of the run-together text; the standard bīja-assignments of the mantra-śāstra agree with our readings.
**Commentary:** the catalog is the practice-side of paṭala 3's kula-lakṣaṇa: the same wheel, now as the six acts. The kīlaka-term of the Unmattabhairava and the Kubjikā belongs to the same complex.

## 5/26 — tridaṇḍakam
**P1:** "the three staffs" [X].
**Varieties:** a) "the three staffs" (an anatomical trident?) · b) "the three-fold" (tri-daṇḍa as a measure) · c) "the tridaṇḍa [carrier]" (the ascetic's three-staff emblem, cf. KJN 12/5's "tridaṇḍañcaiva").
**Most likely:** (c) — the tridaṇḍa-ascetic appears again at 12/5 (the caryā-forms: "the tridaṇḍa, O Lord of gods") — the term is the emblem, and 5/26's "in the middle of the back, the tridaṇḍa" maps it onto the spine (the three channels as the staffs!).
**Commentary:** if the tridaṇḍa = the three channels of the back, the verse is a somatic map: the eleven cakras + the thousand-petaled, with the spine as the trident — the body as the ascetic's emblem.

## 6/4 — nirāmayanirañjanam
**P1:** "free of illness, the Stainless."
**Most likely:** ours — the jīva as the Stainless is the paṭala's thesis (the body-bound jīva = Śiva "by the mere abandonment of the body," 6/7).
**Commentary:** the identity-claim here is the strongest in the batch: not "the yogī attains the Stainless" but "the jīva IS the Stainless." The commentary should note the paradox against the Kubjikā's nirañjanapada (attained) — the KJN's jīva is the attained goal from the start.

## 11/11 — the pañcāmṛta
**P1:** "the feces, the stream, the nectar, the semen, mixed with blood-and-marrow."
**Varieties:** a) ours · b) a more decorous rendering (the "five purifiers" as the kula's five substances).
**Most likely:** (a) — the text is explicit (viṣṭhā = feces); the decorum-shift (b) would falsify the Kaula register. The rendering stands; the commentary must contextualize, not censor.
**Commentary:** the pañcāmṛta is the "wild" layer in its most concentrated form (STUB-A's evidence): the substance-ritual that the Trika's domestication would reinterpret as internal. The commentary's task: show the TĀ's parallel treatment (TĀ 29, the kula-section — Dyczkowski vol. 11) when reached.

## 16/27–16/37 — the fish-myth
**P1:** the full myth rendered (the Moon-island, the theft by the mouse-Skanda, the fish, the Kaivarta-avatar).
**Varieties:** a) ours · b) the Tucci-version (the Kaibarta-community origin, the Tibetan sources) — the historical echo of the myth.
**Most likely:** (a) for the text; (b) is the commentary's confirmation: the myth encodes the lineage's fisherman-community self-location (confirmed by Wikipedia/Tucci — see PASS2_ROUND2 §7).
**Commentary:** the myth's three layers: (1) the historical (the Kaibarta-origin, the Kāmarūpa-geography); (2) the soteriological (the knowledge swallowed and recovered = the descent and re-ascent); (3) the polemical (the Kaivarta as the leader of the vipras — the fisher-brāhmaṇa inversion, the lineage's answer to the domestication tension).

## 17/33 — sa jīvaḥ pudgalo haṃsaḥ sa śivo vyāpakaḥ paraḥ
**P1:** "He is the jīva, the pudgala, the haṃsa; he is the Śiva, the pervasive, the supreme."
**Most likely:** ours — the identity-series (the jīva as the haṃsa-Siva) confirmed by the haṃsa/nirañjana pairing of the Kaulāvalīnirṇaya (our earlier greps) and the KJN's own 20/2.
**Commentary:** the verse answers the paṭala-6 question in full: the jīva's marks (17/29–32 — not cut, not burnt, not wetted) are the Śiva-marks of the Gītā's acyuta — the body's Lord.

## 20/2 — kapālaṃ bhedamayāti yānti jīvo nirañjanam
**P1:** "the skull goes to breaking; the jīva goes to the Stainless."
**Most likely:** ours — the kapāla-bheda (the skull-breaking at death) as the jīva's release into nirañjana; the verse's eschatology matches 13/8's piṇḍapāta (the skull breaks at the body's fall).
**Commentary:** two verses, one eschatology: at death the skull breaks and the jīva — always already the Stainless — returns to it. The commentary should pair 13/8 and 20/2.

## 25/2 — sārātsārataraṃ param
**P1:** "the more-essential-than-the-essential, the supreme."
**Most likely:** ours — the text's final superlative, the same term as the Akulavīra 99 and the Kubjikā 1.3/1.7: the three lands end with the same word.
**Commentary:** the closing of the KJN ("the unrevealed has been revealed") + the superlative = the batch's own ring: the secret was the context all along.
