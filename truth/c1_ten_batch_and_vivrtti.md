# 10-Text Batch + Spanda Vivṛti — C1 (plain-English interpretations)

# 1. Jñānasaṅkalinītantra — v. 7
"The Vedas, the śāstras, the Purāṇas are like common courtesans; but the Śāmbhavī-vidyā is hidden like a kula-bride." The public scriptures are available to anyone; the vidyā is a bride — hidden, given once, to one. What can be picked up on a street cannot be the deepest teaching.

# 2. Kulasāra — the soma-myth
The gods lost their power — without the surā, without the strength — and the question is how purity is possible at all. The orthoprax world is enfeebled; the kula-mata is the restoration, and the restoration comes through what orthopraxy excludes — the female principle, the forbidden substances, the kula's own order. The world's weakness is why the kula exists.

# 3. Azeṣakulavallarī — the liturgy
The "all-kula vine": the complete liturgy of the Mārtaṇḍa-Bhairava — the fire, sun, and moon discs with their kalās; the nine powers; the planets with their bījas; the door-wardens. The universe summoned into the ritual space, layer by layer. The liturgy is the ontology in action: the kalās and powers the philosophy names are the ones the rite invokes.

# 4. Kākacaṇḍeśvarīmata — the goddesses
The Skeleton-Queen, the Night-of-Time, the Crow-Fierce — the Krama's fierce family. And the worship-scene: the yoginī-hosts around Bhairava — some praise him, some dance, some laugh — "by these, devoted through knowledge, Bhairava is worshipped." The devotion through knowledge is the worship.

# 5. Kulacūḍāmaṇi — v. 1
"Countless is the goddess Tripurā; countless also is Kālikā." Not one goddess, not a fixed pantheon — an uncountable family. The text then lists its canon — the sixty-four tantras — the countable scaffolding around the uncountable center.

# 6. Nareśvaraparīkṣā — the examination
Every school's god is examined and found limited: the Sāṅkhya's prakṛti-god is unconscious, the Vedāntin's brahman is non-agent, the Pāñcarātra's Nārāyaṇa is effect-bound. "Limited power" is the verdict on all of them. Then the Śaiva's own Lord: the one not limited because he is the freedom itself. The tradition's own critical method — every rival's claim tested, only the freedom survives the test.

# 7. Kaulāvalīnirṇaya — the canon
The maṅgala names the lineage's sources — the Yāmalas, the Kulacūḍāmaṇi, the Kulasāra, the Kulārṇava. The canon is self-referential: the texts it cites are the texts we translate. The Kaulāvalī ("the Kaula-line") stands in the row of the kula's books, each citing the others. The lineage's self-image is a library.

# 8. Nityākaulatantra — the fragment
The goddess-list before the great consolidation: the Heart-Line, the Moistening, the Delight, the Shaking, the Love-sick, the Sky-Kalā, the Draining, the Swift — and the twelfth, the Madana. The pool from which the sixteen Nityās of the later Śrīvidyā emerged — the goddesses before they were counted. The prehistory of the Śrīcakra, in eleven names.

# 9. Spanda Vivṛti — v. 1, what spanda is
Spanda — the pulse. Not movement from place to place: the self-experiencing flash of consciousness, "its body the unobstructed light," uncaught by space or time — "the own nature of Śambhu, rich with wonder." The definition is at once metaphysical and aesthetic: the pulse is what reality IS, and it is tasted as wonder. The disciple of Utpaladeva opens his commentary with a verse that is both a definition and a poem. The pulse is the light's self-enjoyment.
