The credibility apparatus is therefore internal: three readings are produced and kept separate (literal / contextual / doctrinal), every contested word is worked in a word-lab with alternative meanings brainstormed, and the synthesis records where the readings agree, where they diverge, and what only a specialist with manuscripts can settle.

## Verse 1.1

> devadeva mahādeva parameśapurātana |
> nandīśa sarvabhūteśa nirañjana parātpara || 1/1 ||

### Sandhi and compounds
- parameśa-purātana: karmadhāraya, "ancient supreme Lord" [T]; alternatively bahuvrīhi "ancient within the supreme Lord" [X]
- sarvabhūteśa: sarvabhūta + īśa, "lord of all beings"
- parātpara: para + atpara, "beyond the beyond"

### Word lab — alternative meanings
- **nirañjana** — the load-bearing term of the whole opening.
  (a) *niḥ + añjana*: "without collyrium (añjana = black ointment)" → unstained, unblackened → free of the darkness of ignorance/māyā. [T, standard]
  (b) "without marking/branding" → attributeless, undifferentiated. [R]
  (c) Buddhist usage: the tathāgata's pristine state; also Advaita epithet. Conceptual reference point, not textual. [R]
  (d) "needing no añjana" — añjana is also the magic eye-ointment that reveals hidden treasures (añjanavidyā); the state that sees without an instrument. [R, speculative]
  (e) As goddess name: Nirañjanā, an epithet of the supreme śakti in Kālīkula texts. [X]
- **nandīśa** — (a) "lord of Nandin" (Śiva's bull/gatekeeper); (b) "lord of bliss" (nandī); (c) a specific Kaula form of Bhairava. The Muktabodha library's own epigraph (*Kathāsaritsāgara* 1.1.46 — Nandi as Śiva's gatekeeper) supports (a) by general usage, but in Kaula invocations (b) is attested. [X]
- **parameśapurātana** — "the ancient supreme Lord"; *purātana* = ancient (cf. purāṇa), here the beginningless Lord who nevertheless precedes every cycle. [T]

### Reading 1 — Literal
God of gods, great god, ancient supreme Lord, lord of Nandin, lord of all beings, O unstained one, O beyond-the-beyond — [X: vocative chain, "nirañjana" left open].

### Reading 2 — Contextual (Kaula)
O God of gods, great Lord, the ancient One beyond the supreme, Nandīśa, lord of all beings — O you who are without stain (free of the darkness of māyā), higher than the highest.

### Reading 3 — Doctrinal (initiatory)
I bow in the vocative of attainment: O Lord of gods and great Lord, ancient source of the supreme Lord, gatekeeper-lord, sovereign of all creatures — O stainless (nirañjana) one, transcendent even of the transcendent: you who practice even now [X: sets up 1.2].

### Verse note
The epithet chain is not ornamental in this genre — each vocative is a *claim about the state being asked for*. "Nirañjana" names the goal; "parātpara" frames its transcendence; "japyate" in 1.2 then reveals why the invocation is structured as address: the Lord's own practice is the subject of the first question.

## Verse 1.2

> kiṃ tvayā japyate nātha kimarthaṃ vāpi tasya ca |
> kenopāyena deveśa nirañjanapadaṃ labhet || 1/2 ||

### Word lab
- **japyate** — passive: "what is chanted *by you*?" The trope: the supreme god himself performs japa; his mantra is the hidden power. Ambiguity: "what do you recite?" vs. "what is recited for you / in your worship?" [X]
- **nirañjanapada** — *pada*: (a) station/state; (b) word/mantra-syllable; (c) foot/support. Reading (b) would make the question "by what means does one attain the *syllable* nirañjana [i.e., the mantra]?" [R]
- **upāya** — means/method. Generic here, but a technical term in the later Trika (upāyas of initiation). No back-projection; note the parallel. [R]

### Reading 1 — Literal
What is chanted by you, O lord? And what indeed is the purpose of that? By what means, O lord of gods, does one attain the station of the unstained?

### Reading 2 — Contextual
What mantra do you yourself recite, O Lord — and for what end? And through what method does a practitioner reach the unstained state?

### Reading 3 — Doctrinal (initiatory)
Pārvatī does not ask what to recite; she asks what the Lord *himself* recites — because the god's own japa is the signature of the lineage's vidyā. Her three questions map the path: mantra (what is chanted), purpose (why), and method (how) — the standard initiatory triad answered in the sequel by the ten vidyās (1.15–1.16) and the yonimudrā (1.12).

### Verse note
The verse establishes the *transmission frame*: knowledge is not taught, it is revealed in response to questioning. The passive "japyate" is the hinge.

## Verse 1.3

> sārātsārataraṃ brūhi yadi me vidyate dayā |
> samayācāravidhānañca sādhanañca prakāśaya || 1/3 ||

### Word lab
- **sārātsāratara** — ablative + comparative: "more essential than the essential." *sāra*: (a) pith/essence; (b) sap/strength; (c) cream (kṣīrasāra). The construction is a deliberate superlative escalation (cf. Bhagavadgītā's "sarvaśāstra-sāra" style). [T]
- **samayācāra** — Kaula technical term. (a) the observances/conventions binding initiates (samaya = the vow or agreement of the transmission); (b) the kula's secret ritual conduct; (c) "behavior in accord with the samaya." In Kubjikā-tradition texts this is a chapter-genre (samayācāra-prakaraṇa). [T]
- **vidhāna** — injunction, ritual rule.
- **sādhana** — practice, the means of attainment. In Kaula: the worship/discipline of the vidyā. [T]
- **yadi me vidyate dayā** — "if there is compassion in you toward me" — the standard petitionary formula.

### Reading 1 — Literal
Tell me that which is more essential than the essential — if compassion toward me exists [in you] — and reveal the injunctions of samayācāra and the sādhana.

### Reading 2 — Contextual
Speak the essence of the essence, if you have any compassion for me; and unfold the rule of the samaya observance and the practice that follows from it.

### Reading 3 — Doctrinal
Pārvatī asks for the *hierarchized* teaching: the supreme essence first (sārātsāratara), then the discipline that guards it (samayācāra), then the practice that realizes it (sādhana). Knowledge → vow → practice is the Kaula curriculum in nuce; the Lord's answer will invert the order — practice and vow are revealed *because* the essence cannot be approached otherwise.

## Verse 1.4

> caitanyaṃ sarvabhūtānāṃ śabdabrahmeti yat punaḥ |
> tatsarvaṃ śrotumicchāmi sādhakānāṃ hitāya ca || 1/4 ||

### Word lab
- **caitanya** — consciousness, the animating principle of all beings. [T]
- **śabdabrahman** — "the Word-Brahman," the sonic absolute. Reference points: Mīmāṃsā's śabdapramāṇa tradition, Bhartṛhari's śabdatattva, and the Śaiva mātṛkā/mālīnī doctrine (the alphabet as the goddess's body). In Kālīkula/Krama the sonic absolute is the goddess as nāda. The verse pairs caitanya and śabdabrahman — consciousness and its mantra-body — as one teaching. [T/R]
- **sādhakānāṃ hitāya** — "for the good of sādhakas" — frames the text as an initiatory manual for practitioners, not speculative śāstra. [T]

### Reading 1 — Literal
And that which is the consciousness of all beings, [called] the Word-Brahman — all of that I wish to hear, for the benefit of practitioners.

### Reading 2 — Contextual
I wish to hear that entire teaching: the consciousness pervading all beings, and the Word-Brahman in which it is embodied — and I ask it for the sake of practitioners.

### Reading 3 — Doctrinal
The pair caitanya + śabdabrahman is the *definition of the vidyā* in this tradition: the goddess is the consciousness of all beings (caitanya) and simultaneously the mantra-body (śabdabrahman) through which she is reached. Hearing alone (śravaṇa) — cf. 1.20 "asyāḥ śravaṇamātreṇa jīvanmuktaś ca sādhakaḥ" — is the declared path. The request is thus self-describing: she asks to hear the teaching that hearing itself completes. [R]

## Verse 1.5

> yadi me prāṇanāthaḥ syāt nigūḍhārthaṃ prakāśaya |
> śapathaṃ me mahādeva vṛhadyoniṃ prakāśaya || 1/5 ||

### Word lab
- **prāṇanātha** — "lord of my life," vocative of endearment. [T]
- **nigūḍhārtha** — "the hidden meaning," the secret import. [T]
- **śapathaṃ me** — the crux of the passage. (a) "I swear to you" — Pārvatī takes the oath of secrecy before receiving the secret (standard mantraśāstra practice: transmission under vow); (b) "the oath that concerns me" — the vow that protects the line of transmission; (c) formulaic: in initiatory texts "śapathaṃ me ... prakāśaya" can mean "reveal to me that which is given only under oath." Subject ambiguous: who swears? [X]
- **vṛhadyoni** (= bṛhadyoni) — "the Great Yoni." (a) prakṛti, the universal womb of creation; (b) the goddess herself — in Kaula, yoni = the śakti, the womb-source of all mantras and worlds; (c) a specific vidyā named Bṛhadyoni [X]; (d) the yoni-state, the origin-point from which the path rises. Note the text's own term five verses later: yonimudrā (1.12) — "whoever does not know the yoni-mudrā..." The Great Yoni is the same complex referred to at the highest level. [T/R]

### Reading 1 — Literal
If you are truly the lord of my life, reveal the hidden meaning. [Receive] my oath, O great god; reveal the Great Yoni.

### Reading 2 — Contextual
If you are indeed the lord of my life, disclose the hidden import. I take the vow of secrecy — now reveal the Great Yoni.

### Reading 3 — Doctrinal
The transmission frame completes itself: the pupil's oath (śapatha) is the condition of the revelation; the secret (nigūḍhārtha) is identified with the Great Yoni — the goddess as womb-source of the ten vidyās and of all worlds. In the Kaula reading, "reveal the yoni" is not a metaphor for a doctrine: the yoni is the *object* of the practice (yonimudrā, 1.12) and the vidyā's own body. The revelation and the reality revealed are one. [R]

### Verse note
The verse ends Pārvatī's questioning. The Lord's answer follows under oath (1.8: satyaṃ satyaṃ...). The symmetry: she swears, he swears — the transmission is sealed by double oath.

## Verse 1.6

> vedānāṃ sāragamyañca taṃ sāraṃ me prakāśaya || 1/6 ||

### Word lab
- **vedānāṃ sāra** — "the essence of the Vedas" — the claim of the Kaula teaching to be the Veda's secret heart. [T]
- **sāragamya** — sandhi: sāra + gamya. (a) "essence-attainable": the essence to be reached through the Vedas; (b) "reachable as the essence"; (c) "attained by the essence" [X]. The reading matters for whether the Vedas are means or obstacle.

### Reading 1 — Literal
And that essence of the Vedas which is to be reached [through them] — that essence reveal to me.

### Reading 2 — Contextual
Reveal to me that essence which the Vedas point to but do not give — the sāra reached beyond their sāra.

### Reading 3 — Doctrinal
The verse is the *supersession claim*: the Kaula teaching is the essence of the essence of the Vedas (cf. 1.3), and the Vedas' function is exhausted in pointing to it. The Lord's answer (1.7) will crown the escalation: "more essential than the essential, beyond the beyond, the highest of the high."

## Verse 1.7

> sārātsārataraṃ devi parādapi paraṃ param || 1/7 ||

### Word lab
- **sārātsāratara** — repetition of the 1.3 epithet, now as the Lord's self-designation of the teaching. [T]
- **parādapi** — parāt + api: "even beyond the beyond" (not "beyond the beyond" but "higher than that"). [T]
- **paraṃ param** — "the highest of the high" (paraṃ = acc. masc. of the thing named, param = "high"). Note: the verse as printed has no verb — it names the teaching without predicating anything of it. [X: possibly a truncated exchange; the response continues at 1.8 with the oath]

### Reading 1 — Literal
More essential than the essential, O Devī — even beyond the beyond, the highest of the high.

### Reading 2 — Contextual
The teaching I will give, O Devī, is the essence of the essence — higher than the transcendent, the summit of the summits.

### Reading 3 — Doctrinal
The Lord names the vidyā without describing it — a deliberate apophatic move: the highest teaching has no predicate. Description would be delimitation. The next verse (1.8) supplies the only predicate allowed: the triple oath of truth (satyaṃ satyaṃ punaḥ satyaṃ), which guards rather than describes.

## Synthesis

### The shared core (all three readings agree)
Pārvatī petitions Śiva for a secret teaching, framed as: (1) the god's own japa, (2) the purpose of that japa, (3) the means to the unstained state (nirañjanapada), (4) the essence of the essence, (5) the samayācāra discipline, (6) the sādhana, (7) the consciousness-Śabdabrahman identity, (8) the Great Yoni. The text is an initiatory exchange: transmission under double oath (1.5, 1.8), for the benefit of sādhakas (1.4), claiming to supersede the Vedas (1.6–1.7).

### Real divergences (what a specialist must adjudicate)
| Crux | Reading 1 | Reading 2 | Reading 3 | Can be settled by |
|------|-----------|-----------|-----------|-------------------|
| nirañjana | left open ("unstained") | "free of māyā's darkness" | "the stainless state = the goddess's own" | Collation + usage in Kubjikā-text corpus (Kubjikāmata, Nityāṣoḍaśikārṇava) |
| śapathaṃ me | "[receive] my oath" | "I take the vow" | "the oath-bound transmission" | Parallels in mantraśāstra transmission formulas |
| vṛhadyoni | "the Great Yoni" (open) | "the goddess as womb" | "the vidyā herself, object of yonimudrā" | Cross-reference to 1.12 yonimudrā and the vidyā-list 1.15–16 |
| sāragamya | "essence to be reached through the Vedas" | "what Vedas point to, don't give" | "supersession claim" | Grammar: compound resolution |
| nandīśa | "lord of Nandin" | "lord of bliss" | Kaula form of Bhairava | Kaula invocation parallels |

### What cannot be settled from this text
1. **Edition.** Chatterjee's 19th-century Bengali-characters edition is the sole basis. 1.7's verblessness suggests possible truncation or a different manuscript tradition. Collation needed.
2. **The vidyā identity.** Whether the promised teaching is one of the ten Mahāvidyās of 1.15–16 (kālī, tārā, mahādevī, bhuvaneśī, ṣoḍaśī, vagalā, mātaṅgī, kamalā) and which one is the "nirañjana pada" is only inferable, not provable, from the opening.
3. **Doctrinal drift.** The Kālī-list at 1.15–16 and the "jīvanmukti by hearing alone" at 1.20 suggest a later, consolidated Kālīkula phase — dating and layering require scholarship outside this text.

### Credibility statement
This document is a **provisional three-reading record**, not an edition. Its claims are: (1) the Sanskrit is quoted verbatim from the Muktabodha e-text with provenance recorded; (2) the three readings are kept separate and every divergence is located at a specific word; (3) the word-labs enumerate the plausible readings and mark which require collation or corpus comparison. Where the tradition's own texts (Kubjikāmata, Nityāṣoḍaśikārṇava, yonimudrā material) can adjudicate, that is named but not performed here.
