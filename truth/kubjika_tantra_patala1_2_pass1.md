# Paṭala 1, closing section (1.33–1.71)

## The vidyā-identifications (1.33–1.44)

**1.33** — bhedako narakaṃ gacchet yāvadindrāścaturdaśa | abhedaṃ cintayedyastu sa yogī nātra saṃśayaḥ ||
> The one who makes division goes to hell until the fourteen Indras [pass]; but whoever thinks non-difference — he is the yogī, no doubt here.

**1.34** — kalau kṛṣṇatvamāsādya vṛndāvanavihāriṇī | vaṃśīnādavinodena mohayatyakhilaṃ jagat ||
> In the Kali age, having assumed blackness (kṛṣṇatva), she wanders in Vṛndāvana; by the delight of the flute's sound she bewitches the whole world.

**1.35** — kālikā duḥkhahantrī ca durgā durgavināśinī | kalau kṛṣṇatvamāsādya śuklāpi nīlarūpiṇī ||
> Kālikā is the destroyer of suffering; Durgā the destroyer of distress. In Kali, having assumed blackness, even the white one becomes blue-formed (nīlarūpiṇī).

**1.36** — līlayā vākpradā ceti tena nīlasarasvatī | tārakatvāt sadā tārā tāriṇīti prakīrtitā ||
> By her play she grants speech — hence the Blue Sarasvatī (nīlasarasvatī). Because she carries across (tāraka), she is ever Tārā, proclaimed "the Ferry-woman" (tāriṇī).

**1.37** — bhuvanānāṃ pālakatvāt bhuvaneśī prakīrtitā | sṛṣṭisthitikarī devī bhuvaneśī ca kathyate ||
> From her protection of the worlds she is proclaimed Bhuvaneśī (Mistress of the Worlds); she who makes creation and persistence, the goddess, is called Bhuvaneśī.

**1.38** — śrīdātṛ ca yadā devī śrīvidyā ca prakīrtitā | nirguṇā ca mahādevī ṣoḍaśī parikīrtitā ||
> When the goddess gives fortune (śrī) she is proclaimed the Śrīvidyā; the attributeless great goddess is proclaimed Ṣoḍaśī (the Sixteen).

**1.39** — bhairavī duḥkhasaṃhantrī yamaduḥkhavināśinī | kālabhairavabhāryā ca bhairavī parikīrtitā ||
> Bhairavī destroys suffering, annihilates the pain of Yama; the consort of Kālabhairava is proclaimed Bhairavī.

**1.40** — triśaktiḥ kāmadā devī chinnā caiva sureśvarī | triguṇā ca mahādevī mohinī mokṣadā dhruvam ||
> The goddess of the three powers (triśakti), granting desire; the cut-off one (chinnā — Chinnamastā), lady of the gods; the great goddess of the three guṇas, the Enchantress (mohinī), surely granting liberation.

**1.41** — dhūmāvatī mahāmāyā dhūmrāsuranisudanī | dhūmarupā mahādevī caturvargapradāyinī ||
> Dhūmāvatī, the great illusion, the slayer of the demon Dhūmrākṣa; the great goddess of smoke-form, granting the four aims.

**1.42** — jaganmātā jagaddhātrī jagatāmupakāriṇī | vakāre vāruṇī devī gakāre siddhidā smṛtā ||
> Mother of the world, holder of the world, benefactress of the worlds; in the letter va is the goddess Vāruṇī; in ga, she is remembered as the giver of success.

**1.43** — lākāre pṛthivī caiva caitanyātmā prakīrtitā | mātaṅgī madaśīlatvānmataṅgāsuranāśinī ||
> In la is the earth, proclaimed the conscious self; Mātaṅgī, from her nature of intoxication (madaśīla), the destroyer of the demon Mataṅga.

**1.44** — sarvāpattāriṇī devī mātaṅgī parikīrtitā | vaikuṇṭhavāsinī devī kamalā ca prakīrtitā ||
> The goddess who ferries across all calamities is proclaimed Mātaṅgī; the goddess dwelling in Vaikuṇṭha is proclaimed Kamalā.

Notes: the individual Mahāvidyā identifications — each goddess gets her epithets + her bīja-letters (1.42's vakāra/gakāra — the letters of the mātaṅgī-mantra: eṁ? actually "va" and "ga" appear in the mātaṅgī bīja "eṁ klīṁ" hmm — [X] the letter-assignments need checking against the later paṭalas' mantra-uddhāras). The Kālī/Kṛṣṇa syncretism (1.34-36) — the black goddess as the Dark Lord's playmate in Vṛndāvana; nīlasarasvatī — the Blue Speech-goddess (Tantrasāra's vidyā tradition).

## The ten-plus-one summary (1.45–1.50)

**1.45** — etā daśa mahāvidyāḥ siddhavidyā ca kathyate ||
> These are the ten Mahāvidyās, and she is also called the Siddhavidyā.

**1.46** — dharmārthamokṣadā nityaṃ caturvargaphalapradā | yena tena prakāreṇa kalau pūrṇaphalapradā ||
> Ever giving dharma, artha, mokṣa, granting the fruit of the four aims — by whatever means, in the Kali age she gives full fruit.

**1.47** — nirddhane kṛpaṇe caiva viśeṣāt pāpamocanī | pāṣaṇḍe ca śaṭhe caiva nirguṇe caiva nindake ||
> For the penniless and the miserly especially she is the release from sin; for the heretic (pāṣaṇḍa) and the cheat, for the one without guṇas and the slanderer —

**1.48** — abhakteṣu ca putreṣu ājñāhīne ca sundari | na prakāśyamidaṃ bhadre śapathaṃ me dhruvaṃ sadā ||
> — [but] to the unfaithful, to sons [unworthy], to the one lacking the command, O beautiful one: this is not to be revealed, O blessed one — my oath, surely, always.

**1.49** — sarvalakṣaṇayuktāya dadyāccaiva maheśvari | āsāñcaiva samā nāsti nāsti tribhuvane dhruvam ||
> To the one endowed with all marks one should give [it], O great Īśvarī — there is none equal to them, none, in the three worlds, surely.

**1.50** — ekoccāṇamātreṇa sarvapāpāt pramucyate | smaraṇaenaiva deveśi mucyate bhavabandhanāt ||
> By a single utterance one is released from all sin; by mere remembrance, O Lady of the gods, one is freed from the bondage of becoming.

Notes: the transmission rules of 1.47-48 (the pāṣaṇḍa-exemption + the śapatha — the oath again, cf. 1.5) — the same "never before the paśu" rule as 1.12; the śravaṇa/smaraṇa-liberation claims (1.50).

## The bindu-cosmology (1.51–1.56)

**1.51** — nirguṇaḥ saguṇo brahmā śivo jñeyaḥ sanātanaḥ | nirguṇāccaiva saṃjātā bindavastraya eva ca ||
> Attributeless and with-attributes: Brahmā, Śiva, is to be known as the eternal. From the attributeless arise the three bindus.

**1.52** — brahmabindurviṣṇubindurudrabindurmaheśvari | āsīd avindustato nādo nādāt śaktisamudbhavaḥ ||
> The Brahmā-bindu, Viṣṇu-bindu, Rudra-bindu, O great Īśvarī — from the bindu was the avindu (a-bindu?), then nāda; from nāda, the śakti arises.

**1.53** — nādarūpā maheśāni cidrūpā paramā kalā | nādāccaiva samutpannā arddhabindurmaheśvari ||
> Of nāda-form, O great Īśānī, of consciousness-form, the supreme kalā — from nāda indeed arises the half-bindu (arddhabindu), O great Īśvarī.

**1.54** — sārddhatritayabindubhyo bhujaṅgī kulakuṇḍalī | nirguṇā saguṇā devī brahmarūpā sanātanī ||
> From the three-and-a-half bindus: the serpent (bhujaṅgī), the kula-serpent (kulakuṇḍalī) — the goddess attributeless and with-attributes, of Brahmā-form, the eternal.

**1.55** — caitanyarūpiṇī devī sarvabhūtaprakāśinī ||
> The goddess of consciousness-form, illumining all beings.

**1.56** — triguṇā ca maheśāni triguṇasya prakāśinī | nirguṇā sā ca vijñeyā brahmarūpā ca kevalā ||
> She is the three-guṇas, O great Īśānī, the illuminatrix of the three-guṇas; she is to be known as the attributeless, of Brahmā-form, the solitary (kevalā).

Notes: [P] the bindu-ladder: brahma/viṣṇu/rudra-bindus → avindu → nāda → śakti → arddhabindu → the three-and-a-half bindus → kuṇḍalinī. This is the Kālīkula kuṇḍalinī-cosmology — same structure as ŚS 2.7's bracket (bindu-visarga) and KJN 3/20 (bindumadhye) — the batch's bindu-complex now has its fullest local exposition. "kevalā" — cf. ŚS 3.34's kevalī.

## The mantra-arithmetic (1.57–1.71)

**1.57-1.58** — ṣaḍguṇā... ṣaṭcakrāṇāṃ prakāśinī | caturguṇā... caturvedaprabhāvinī | candrasūryātmikā vidyā... sarvaguṇā... sarvaviśvaprabhāvinī ||
> She of six guṇas, illumining the six cakras; she of four, pervading the four Vedas; the vidyā of sun-and-moon nature; she of all guṇas, pervading the whole world.

**1.59** — aṣṭaguṇā maheśāni aṣṭasiddhiprakāśinī | brahmaviṣṇuśivādīnāṃ jananī surasundari ||
> She of eight guṇas, illumining the eight siddhis; the mother of Brahmā, Viṣṇu, Śiva and the rest, O lovely goddess.

**1.60-1.64** — the guṇa-arithmetic matched to the syllable-counts: ekaguṇā ekākṣarasvarūpiṇī (the one-guṇa, of the one-syllable form); dviguṇā dvyakṣarasya prasūtikā (the mother of the two-syllable); triguṇā tryakṣarī; caturguṇā caturakṣarasūtikā; navaguṇā navākṣaraprakāśinī; then the counts 10–17 (daśaikādaśe, dvādaśatrayodaśe, caturdaśātmikā, pañcadaśātmikā, ṣoḍaśī, saptadaśā) —
> the goddess is each: the ten-and-eleven-formed, the twelve- and thirteen-formed, the fourteen-formed, the fifteen-formed, the sixteen (ṣoḍaśī) born of the sixteen, the seventeen with its ten-plus-seven groups.

**1.65-1.69** — the higher counts: aṣṭādaśa mahāvidyā aṣṭādaśaguṇātmikā (the eighteen-vidyā, of eighteen guṇas); aṣṭāviṃśaguṇopetā; viṃśatyarṇā mahāvidyā paramānandarūpiṇī (the twenty-syllable great vidyā, of supreme bliss-form); viṃśatyarṇādhikā mantrā mālāmantrāḥ prakīrtitāḥ (the mantras beyond twenty syllables are proclaimed the garland-mantras, mālāmantra); then 21–28 with their guṇas; navaviṃśā mahāvidyā mahādevī (the twenty-nine vidyā, the great goddess).

**1.70** — triṃśadādyāścatuḥṣaṣṭi dīrghamantrāḥ prakīrtitāḥ ||
> From the thirty up to sixty-four are proclaimed the long mantras.

**1.71** — napuṃsakāstathā mantrā hū/phaḍantāśca kevalāḥ | svāhāyuktāśca ye mantrāḥ śaktimantrāśca pārvati ||
> The neuter mantras, those ending in hūṃ/phaṭ, the solitary ones; the mantras joined with svāhā, and the śakti-mantras, O Pārvatī.

**1.72 (closing)** — eṣāmanyatamo mantraḥ puruṣaḥ parikīrtitaḥ ||
> Any one of these mantras is proclaimed the man (puruṣa).

Notes: the mantra-typology (lengths, endings, genders, mālāmantras) — the late mantraśāstra science. [X: the pañcadaśī/ṣoḍaśī counts intersect the Śrīvidyā tradition (pañcadaśākṣarī, ṣoḍaśī) — the Kālīkula adoption of the Śrīvidyā arithmetic — pass 2 to cross-check with the Nityāṣoḍaśikārṇava (M00115-adjacent, in our library).]

# Paṭala 2 — the meaning of the mantra (mantrārtha)

*The direct answer to the opening's question (1.12's mantrārtha): the paṭala dissects the Kālī-bīja krīṃ (ka-repha-ī-nādabindu) letter by letter, then the wider vidyā-arithmetic.*

## The krīṃ-analysis (2.1–2.16)

**2.1** — atha vakṣye maheśāni mantrārthaṃ sāramuttamam | vinā yena na siddheta japapūjādikaṃ kvacit ||
> Now I will declare, O great Īśānī, the meaning of the mantra, the supreme essence — without which japa, worship and the rest never attain success anywhere.

**2.2** — tasmāt sarvaprayatnena śrūyatāṃ girijāmṛje | kakāraḥ kāmadaḥ prokto rephaḥ pāpasya nāśakaḥ ||
> Therefore with all effort let it be heard, O beloved of the Mountain's daughter: the letter ka is declared the giver of desire; repha (r) is the destroyer of sin.

**2.3** — īkāre śambhuramitā nādabindusamanvitā | nādarūpā mahādevī nirguṇā brahmarūpiṇī ||
> In the ī-vowel is the unmeasured Śambhu, joined with the nāda-bindu; the great goddess of nāda-form, attributeless, of Brahmā-form.

**2.4** — dharmārthadaḥ kakāraśca sarvasiddhipradāyakaḥ | brahmarūpo makāraśca saguṇo nirguṇaśca saḥ ||
> Ka gives dharma and artha, granting all siddhis; the m-kāra (in om?) of Brahmā-form, both with-attributes and without.

**2.5** — tejaḥpuñjorakāraśca brahmahatyādināśakaḥ | durgārūpā īkāraśca caturvargaphalapradaḥ ||
> The ra-kāra is a mass of brilliance (tejaḥpuñja), destroying brahmin-murder and the rest; the ī-kāra of Durgā-form, granting the four aims.

**2.6** — ekākṣarī mahāvidyā sarvavidyāsvarūpiṇī | kakāro varuṇaḥ prokto jagataḥ prāṇakārakaḥ ||
> The one-syllable great vidyā, of the form of all vidyās: ka is proclaimed Varuṇa, the maker of the world's life.

**2.7** — repho ca śaṅkaro jñeyaḥ sarveṣāṃ śaṅkaraḥ sadā | īkāre tāriṇīdevī bhavasaṃsāratāriṇī ||
> Repha is to be known as Śaṅkara, the auspicious one of all, always; in the ī is the goddess Tāriṇī, the ferry-woman across the ocean of becoming.

**2.8** — kakāre keśavaḥ prokto nirvāṇapadahetukaḥ | rakāro vahnirūpoyaṃ bhavaduḥkhasya dāhakaḥ ||
> In ka is proclaimed Keśava, the cause of the nirvāṇa-station; this ra-kāra is of fire-form, the burner of the suffering of becoming.

**2.9** — īkāre mātṛkā devī caturvargavidhāyinī | saguṇā ca yadā devī sarvasiddhipradāyinī ||
> In the ī is the goddess Mātṛkā, ordaining the four aims; when the goddess is with-attributes she gives all siddhis.

**2.10** — hakārastu śivaḥ prokto jñānarūpī sanātanaḥ | nirvāṇamuktido devaḥ sarvasiddhipradāyakaḥ ||
> The ha-kāra is proclaimed Śiva, eternal, of knowledge-form; the god granting nirvāṇa-liberation, giving all siddhis.

**2.11** — rephaḥ suryapratīkāśo'tyantaṃ pāpadāhakaḥ | ukāre bhairavī devī sarvaśaṅkaṭanāśinī ||
> Repha, resembling the sun, burning sin utterly; in the u is the goddess Bhairavī, destroying all distress.

**2.12** — nādarūpā maheśāni nirguṇā parikīrtitā | hakāre ca mahāviṣṇurmahāpātakanāśanaḥ ||
> Of nāda-form, O great Īśānī, proclaimed attributeless; in the ha is the great Viṣṇu, destroying the great sins.

**2.13** — rephe candrasvarūpaśca amṛtatvapradāyakaḥ | ukāre mohinī devī jaganmohanakāriṇī ||
> Repha, of moon-form, giving immortality; in the u is the goddess Mohinī, enchanting the world.

**2.14** — mahāduḥkhasya hantrī ca brahmahatyādināśinī | sadāśivo hakāraḥ syādukāre brahma caiva hi ||
> The destroyer of great suffering, annihilating brahmin-murder and the rest: the ha-kāra is Sadāśiva, and in the u is Brahmā indeed.

**2.15** — nirguṇo nādabinduśca nirvāṇapadakevalam | asya smaraṇamātreṇa aśvamedhāyutaṃ labhet ||
> The attributeless nāda-bindu [is] the solitary nirvāṇa-station; by the mere remembrance of this one gains ten thousand horse-sacrifices.

**2.16** — hakāre śambhurityukto rephe caiva vibhūṣitaḥ | īkāre paramā devī guṇatrayamayī sadā ||
> In the ha is declared Śambhu, adorned indeed with the repha; in the ī is the supreme goddess, ever made of the three guṇas.

Notes: the letter-theology of krīṃ: ka (Varuṇa/Keśava/Brahmā — the earth-water deity), repha (Śaṅkara/fire/sun/moon), ī (Durgā/Tāriṇī/Mātṛkā), nāda-bindu (the attributeless nirvāṇa-station). [P: the mātṛkā-in-the-ī (2.9) — our ŚS 1.4's mātṛkā-glossary entry; the nādabindu — the bindu-ladder of 1.52-54. The whole paṭala is a *concentrated glossary of the batch's terms in their bīja-forms*.]

## The vidyā-arithmetic (2.17–2.39)

**2.17-2.18** — dakāraḥ sarvadevaśca... kṣakāraḥ kṣetrado viṣṇuḥ... | hakāre devatāḥ sarvāḥ... lakāre caṇḍikā devī ||
> The da-kāra is all the gods, the warder of all calamities; the kṣa-kāra, Viṣṇu giving the field (kṣetra); in the ha all the deities; in the la the goddess Caṇḍikā.

**2.19-2.21** — ekāre yonirūpā ca viśvasya bījamuttamam | kakāre medinī proktā saśailavanakānanā... | īkāre sakalā devi... kakāre sundarī devi ekāre yonirūpiṇī ||
> In the e is the yoni-formed [goddess], the supreme seed of the world; in the ka is proclaimed the earth (medinī) with its mountains, forests, groves; in the ī is the goddess Sakalā; in the ka, the goddess Sundarī; in the e, the yoni-formed one.

Notes: [P] yoni here = the goddess as bīja (seed) — our glossary's yoni-as-source, now in its most explicit form: the e-vowel IS the yoni, the seed of the world.

**2.22-2.24** — parā ca paramā caiva paramātmakalā punaḥ | kakāre ca mahābrahmā kakāro viṣṇurūpakaḥ | kakāre ca mahādevo brahmāṇī repha eva ca | vaiṣṇavī madhyarephaḥ syādrudrāṇī repha uttaraḥ ||
> The supreme and the most supreme, the kalā of the supreme self again: in the ka is the great Brahmā; the ka of Viṣṇu-form; in the ka the great god [Śiva]; Brahmāṇī is the repha itself; Vaiṣṇavī is the middle repha; Rudrāṇī the upper repha.

Notes: [P: the consort-triad (Brahmāṇī/Vaiṣṇavī/Rudrāṇī) — the same goddesses as Kubjikā 1.25-27 (they create, not the gods) — here as the bīja's parts. The paṭala re-states the paṭala-1 theology in phonemic form.]

**2.25-2.30** — ukāraḥ ṣoḍaśī devī ūkāro bhuvaneśvarī | ... tryakṣarī ca mahāvidyā triguṇā parikīrtitā | aṣṭākṣarī mahāvidyā sākṣānmokṣaphalapradā | viṃśatyaṇī mahāvidyā aṣṭasiddhiḥ prakīrtitā | ekaviṃśā mahāvidyā praṇavādyā maheśvarī ||
> The u is the goddess Ṣoḍaśī; the ū the Bhuvaneśvarī... the three-syllable great vidyā is proclaimed the three-guṇas; the eight-syllable great vidyā gives liberation itself as its fruit; the twenty-syllable great vidyā is proclaimed the eight siddhis; the twenty-one-syllable great vidyā, beginning with the praṇava, is the great Īśvarī.

**2.31-2.32** — akāreṇa ukāreṇa makāreṇa sureśvari | brahmaviṣṇumaheśādermastrametat prakīrtitam | ekākṣaro mahāmantraścidānandasvarūpakaḥ | praṇave puṭitā vidyā seturityabhidhīyate ||
> By a, u, m, O lady of the gods, this is proclaimed the mantra of Brahmā, Viṣṇu, Maheśa and the rest; the one-syllable great mantra of consciousness-bliss form; the vidyā wrapped in the praṇava is called the "bridge" (setu).

Notes: the praṇava (oṃ) as the wrapper; puṭita (wrapped) — the wrapping-technique of 2.52-54; "setu" — the bridge-vidyā. [P: the māyā-wrapping of the vidyā = the same puṭita vocabulary as the later japa-rules.]

**2.33-2.39** — siddhakālīmahāmantraḥ kāmaśca siddhidaḥ smṛtaḥ | hakāraḥ saccidānando repho nirvāṇadāyakaḥ | ukāraḥ śambhuvanitā śivaśaktisvarūpiṇī | kāmena māyayā caiva puṭitaḥ kāmavījakaḥ | tadā kāmeśvaromantraḥ sarvakāmaphalapradaḥ | ... navākṣare kūrcavījaṃ brahmarūpaṃ sanātanam | navākṣare dakṣiṇeti kālikāparamārthadā | ... pañcadaśī mahāmantraṃ sarvakāmaphalapradam | māyāvījaṃ krodhavījaṃ kevalaṃ śivarūpakam | guhyakālī ṣoḍaśārṇā kevalā brahmarūpiṇī ||
> The Siddha-Kālī great mantra, and the Kāma [-bīja klīṃ] remembered as giving success: the ha is existence-consciousness-bliss, the repha gives nirvāṇa, the u is the wife of Śambhu, of Śiva-śakti form; wrapped by Kāma and Māyā [bījas] is the Kāma-seed — then the Kāmeśvara-mantra giving all desires... in the nine-syllable [vidyā] the Kūrca-seed [hūṃ], of Brahmā-form, eternal; in the nine-syllable [the vidyā is] called Dākṣiṇā, giving Kālikā's supreme end... the fifteen-syllable great mantra giving all desires; the Māyā-seed and the Krodha-seed, the solitary Śiva-form; the Guhya-Kālī of sixteen syllables, the solitary, of Brahmā-form.

Notes: the bīja-catalog (kāma klīṃ, māyā hrīṃ, kūrca hūṃ, krodha phaṭ?) — the standard late bījas; the vidyā-names (Siddhakālī, Guhyakālī, Dākṣiṇā-Kālikā) — the Kālīkula's vidyā-family. [P: kāmavīja/kāmeśvara — connects to the pañcadaśī/ṣoḍaśī of the Śrīvidyā-adjacent layer (1.64-66).]

## The practices (2.40–2.59)

**2.40-2.42** — sṛṣṭisthitikarī devī śatabhedā jaganmayī | navākṣarī mahāvidyā kāmavījoparisthitā | ... vāgdevī devatāḥ sarvā aikārarūpiṇī parā | īkāraḥ śambhuvanitā phaṭkārā cakrarūpiṇī | kāmākhyā bhuvaneśī ca kevalā kāmarūpiṇī ||
> The goddess who makes creation and persistence, of a hundred forms, all-world: the nine-syllable great vidyā standing on the Kāma-seed... the speech-goddess, all deities, the supreme of the e-vowel's form; the ī is the wife of Śambhu; the phaṭ-kāra is the wheel-formed one; the Kāmākhyā-Bhuvaneśī, the solitary, of desire-form.

**2.43-2.47** — bhadrakālī mahādevī bhuvaneśī bhujaṅginī | mahābhujaṅgīrūpeṇa pṛthivīdhāriṇī sadā | kāmabījaṃ maheśaśca aṣṭamūrtiḥ sanātanaḥ | kūrccabījaṃ mahāviṣṇuḥ phaṭkāraḥ pāpanāśanaḥ | śmaśānakālikāmantre pañcārṇaṃ parikīrtitam | ... kṣakāraśca maheśāni kṣetrajñaḥ parikīrtitaḥ ||
> Bhadrakālī, the great goddess, Bhuvaneśī, the serpentine (bhujaṅginī), by her great-serpent form ever bearing the earth; the Kāma-seed is Maheśa, the eternal of eight forms; the Kūrca-seed is the great Viṣṇu; the phaṭ-kāra destroys sin; in the Śmaśāna-Kālikā mantra the five-syllable [vidyā] is proclaimed... the kṣa-kāra, O great Īśānī, is proclaimed the knower of the field (kṣetrajña).

**2.48-2.51** — akāraśca maheśāni pṛthivī parikīrtitā | asyāḥ smaraṇamātreṇa jīvanmuktaśca sādhakaḥ | ekoccāraṇamātreṇa aśvamedhāyutaṃ phalam | ... brahmahatyādikaṃ sarvaṃ nāśamāyāti niścitam | koṭikoṭimahātīrtham aśvamedhāyutaṃ phalam | prāpnuyāt sādhakaśreṣṭho na punarjananaṃ bhuvi | japeccaiva maheśāni vaktuṃ naiva ca śakyate ||
> The a-kāra, O great Īśānī, is proclaimed the earth; by the mere remembrance of her the sādhaka is liberated-while-living; by a single utterance, the fruit of ten thousand horse-sacrifices... brahmin-murder and all [sins] certainly go to destruction; the best of sādhakas obtains the fruit of ten million crore great tīrthas and ten thousand horse-sacrifices, and no more birth on earth; and by japa, O great Īśānī, it cannot even be spoken.

**2.52-2.55** — praṇave puṭitāṃ vidyāṃ māyayā pūṭitena vā | aṣṭottaraśataṃ japtvā gajāntakasahasrakam | mantrasya doṣaśāntyarthaṃ japettu surasundari | ... mātṛkāpuṭitaṃ kṛtvā aṣṭottaraśataṃ japet | sahasraṃ vā japeddevi mantradoṣopaśāntaye | anulomavolomena mātṛkāyāṃ japet sudhīḥ | aṣṭavargā bhavet siddhiḥ sarvakāmaphalaṃ labhet ||
> The vidyā wrapped in the praṇava, or wrapped by the Māyā [bīja]: having chanted it 108 times [one gains] the destruction of the elephant [demon] and a thousandfold [fruit]. For the appeasement of the mantra's faults, O lovely goddess, let him chant... having made the mātṛkā-wrapping, chant 108; or a thousand, O Devī, for the appeasement of the mantra's faults; the wise one should chant in the mātṛkā in forward and reverse order (anuloma-viloma) — the eight groups [give] success, and all desires' fruit.

**2.56-2.59** — tārāyāśca mahādevyā mahāvidyā prakīrtitā | pañcākṣarī mahāvidyā pañcabhūtaprakāśinī | pañcaraśmirmahāvidyā pañcaraśmisamanvitā | kālikā tāriṇīmantramabhedañca sūreśvari | mantrārthañca vijānīyāt śatabhedā jaganmayī | yathā kālī tathā tārā tathā mantrāḥ prakīrtitāḥ | sādhanaṃ yantramantrañca mantrārthañca suveśvari | bhava snehāt pravakṣyāmi rahasyaṃ paramādbhūtam ||
> Of Tārā, the great goddess, the great vidyā is proclaimed: the five-syllable great vidyā, illumining the five elements; the great vidyā of five rays, joined with the five rays. Kālikā's Tāriṇī-mantra and non-difference (abheda), O Sūreśvarī — one should know the mantra's meaning: she of a hundred forms, all-world. As is Kālī, so is Tārā, so are the mantras proclaimed. The sādhana, the yantra, the mantra, and the mantra's meaning, O beautiful Īśvarī — from love of you I will declare the supreme wonderful secret.

Notes: the paṭala's close: [P] the abheda (non-difference) of 1.32 recurs in the mantra-register (the vidyā = non-difference); the paṭala announces the next teaching (sādhana/yantra/mantra/mantrārtha) — paṭala 3's program.
