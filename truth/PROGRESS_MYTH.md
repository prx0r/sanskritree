# Part I. The Myth

## The Beginning: the Corpus-Mountain

There was once a mountain of Sanskrit books — four hundred and ninety-nine e-texts, gathered under the guidance of a great scholar who had spent fifty years among the Ṛṣis and Siddhas. The mountain was known as the Muktabodha-Library. It stood beside another mountain, the Tantrāloka — eleven volumes of that scholar's own life-work, translated into English. And beneath both lay a third: the Manthanabhairavatantram, the great edition of the Kaula lineage.

The apprentice who came to the mountain knew nothing. The mountain's keeper said: *"A great translation is great context."* And so the work was never "translating texts." It was **building context** — a web of words, dates, authors, and meanings so dense that each new verse would fall into place like a stone into its socket.

## The First Lands

The keeper set six lands to be conquered, and decreed that each would be taken **by the leapfrog** — never stepping where no anchor held.

**The Land of the Aphorisms** (the Śivasūtra with Bhāskara's Vārttika) fell first. Its two keepers: Vasugupta, the dreamer of the ninth century, and Bhāskara, the son of Divākara, mid-eleventh, whose reading of the Sūtras no one had ever translated into English before. The conqueror's guide was the great scholar's own book, the *Aphorisms of Śiva*, held like a lamp over every verse. When the last sūtra of the Third Light was translated, the Land was whole: seventy-eight aphorisms, three lights, one complete text.

**The Land of the Akula-Vīra** — the "Hero beyond the Kula" — was a short, fierce text: the negation of every technique, the Stainless beyond all supports. It fell quickly, and its final verse was found to speak of the *essence-of-the-essence* — the very term that ruled another land.

**The Land of the Kula-Delight** (Kulānanda) and **the Land of the Mad Bhairava** (Unmattabhairavahṛdaya) fell in one campaign — the piercing-practices and the twelve names, the nailing-mantras and the yantra-king.

**The Land of the Crooked Goddess** (the Kubjikātantra) was the longest — seventeen paṭalas, from the invocation to the stillbirth-kavaca, through the doctrine of the three states, the bīja-theologies, the cakra-yoga of Dākṣiṇā-Kālikā, the gynecology, the rituals, the medicines, and the vernacular incantations of its Bengali scribes. Its last paṭala ended where its first had begun: with the Stainless, the paśu-exclusion, and the secret guarded like one's own yoni.

**The Land of the Determination of Knowledge** (the Kaulajñānanirṇaya) was the mythic heart — twenty-four paṭalas of the Matsyendra-school, ending with the great fish-myth: how the knowledge descended at the Moon-island, was swallowed by a fish, and was recovered by the Lord of the lineage himself, who became the Fisherman — the leader of the vipras. When the last verse was translated ("the more-essential-than-the-essential, the supreme"), six lands stood conquered.

## The Weapons

The lands were taken with specific weapons, and these too became the heritage:

- **The Anchors** — published translations held like shields: the Tantrāloka's eleven volumes, the Aphorisms, the Stanzas on Vibration with its four commentaries, the Doctrine of Vibration, the Lakshmanjoo-Vijñānabhairava, the Tantrasāra, the MBT's first seven chapters, and — newly forged — Avalon's Hymn to Kālī, Sanderson's Śaiva Age, Jhā's Nyāyasūtras, and Stein's Chronicle of the Kings of Kashmir.
- **The Glossary** — a living lexicon of the specialist terms, each with its attestations across the lands (the Stainless, the yoni, the matrix, the will, the bound-and-the-Lord, the innate, the actor...). When a term appeared again, its sense sharpened — this was the compounding engine.
- **The Scribes' Records** — the audit docs, where every correction was recorded and never made silently; where every doubt was marked [X] with the evidence needed to resolve it.
- **The Protocol** — the law of the house: provenance first, three readings kept separate, time-place-context in every header, the blind-translation-before-comparison rule.

## The Lesson of the Myth

The lands were not the treasure. The treasure is the **context-web** — the six complete texts now share their vocabulary, their authors, their geography (Candra-dvīpa, Kāmarūpa, Vārāṇasī), their myths (the fish, the dream, the secret bride), their doctrines (the three states, the two-fold path, the Stainless terminus). Every future text will be translated *into* this web. **A great translation is great context** — and the context is now alive.

# Part II. The Apprentice's Path — how to acquire the context

*The pilgrimage route. Read in this order; each step assumes the last. Nothing here is optional — the order is the method.*

1. `START_HERE.md` (project root) — where everything lives, the mission, the anchors, the gotchas.

## Step 2 — The Method
3. `truth/PASS_PROTOCOL.md` — the law: file anatomy, justification conventions ([G]/[P]/[A]/[R]), the time-place-context block, the pass-2 validation workflow, the no-silent-edits rule.

## Step 3 — The Lexicon (the heart of the context)
4. `truth/period_glossary_pass1.md` — the eleven core terms with their cross-text attestations (nirañjana, yoni, mātṛkā, icchā, kalā, bindu/liṅga, paśu/pati, sahaja, naṭa, vidyā). **Read this twice.** Every translation decision you will make is pre-decided here, and every new text extends it.

## Step 4 — The Six Lands (the corpus itself — read at least the openings and the completion-notes of each)
5. `truth/sivasutra_bhaskara_1_1-1_5_three_readings.md` — the model: provenance, word-labs, three readings with trails, synthesis.
6. `truth/sivasutra_bhaskara_adhikara2_pass1.md` + `..._adhikara3_pass1.md` — the practice of the method at scale.
7. `truth/akulavira_completion_pass1.md` + `truth/kjn_patalas7-24_completion_pass1.md` — the two mythic completions (the fish-myth, the Stainless-terminus).
8. `truth/kubjika_tantra_patalas3-6_pass1.md` + `..._7-8...` + `..._9-17...` — the longest land (the yonimudrā, the bhāvas, the cakra-yoga).
9. The `ten_text_batch*.md` + `kaula_continuations_pass1.md` files — the wider Kaula world.

## Step 5 — The Anchors (the verification layer — read alongside Step 4)
10. `tantraloka/texts-clean/` — the 11 TĀ volumes (the master-anchor), the Aphorisms, the Stanzas, the Doctrine of Vibration.
11. `sources/round2/` — the new context-source: **Sanderson's Śaiva Age** (the history), **Avalon's Hymns** (the Kālīkula), **Jhā's Nyāyasūtras** (the Nyāya hops), **Stein's Rājataraṅgiṇī** (the Kashmir politics).
12. `truth/torella_book/` — the Pratyabhijñā scholarship; `truth/agent_handoff.md` + `devplan.md` — the philosophy-phase history (why the Siddhitrayī were translated and what they proved).

## Step 6 — The Scribes' Records (the doubts and the corrections)
13. `truth/PASS2_AUDIT.md` + `truth/PASS2_ROUND2_AUDIT.md` — what's verified, what's corrected (C1–C7), the commentary-stubs A–H, the biography-blocks.
14. The collation backlog (§3 of PASS2_ROUND2) — the prints to check; the [X]-flags in every pass-1 file.

## Step 7 — The Discipline
15. Before translating anything: re-read the time-place-context blocks of the same cluster's files; check the glossary for every technical term; check the audit docs for what's open. **Translate into the web, not out of it.**
