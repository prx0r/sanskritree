# Aperture Process Identity Theory — Razor Statement

## The Core Thesis

A conscious subject is one concrete, maximally closed, temporally persistent, embodied process. That process is externally describable as causal and functional organization and intrinsically exists as one private phenomenal field.

```
Dᵢ ≡_int Oᵢ*
```

This is a token identity hypothesis, not a derivation. Like every identity theory, it proposes that two independently characterized sets of properties — extrinsic (causal, functional, biological) and intrinsic (phenomenal) — are two accesses to one concrete token. It cannot be proven from exclusively extrinsic premises because the intrinsic side is absent from those premises. This is the epistemic asymmetry defense: failure of derivation does not entail ontological separation.

## What the Framework Claims

### The functional anatomy of a subject

- Manifestation (prakāśa/jñāna): content appears
- Self-manifestation (vimarśa/camatkāra): appearing is intrinsically self-affective, not jaḍa mirroring
- Retention (smṛti): active temporal reconstruction
- Determination (apohana): content is determinate via exclusion of alternatives
- Connective synthesis (anusandhāna): contents belong to one stream
- Generation (vikalpa): counterfactual possibilities are produced
- Valuation (rāga): states have endogenous significance for persistence
- Agency (kriyā): the process can act on its own behalf
- Boundary (deha-bheda): the process is embodied and thereby individuated
- Trajectory (spanda/kāla): the process has developmental continuity

### Identity hypotheses (extrinsic ⟷ intrinsic)

- Connective synthesis ⟷ phenomenal unity
- Retention + trajectory + generation ⟷ lived temporality
- Determination ⟷ determinate phenomenal content
- Valuation ⟷ valence
- Boundary ⟷ phenomenal privacy
- Agency ⟷ experienced authorship
- Self-manifestation (vimarśa) ⟷ the reflexivity that distinguishes sentient from insentient

These are testable cross-domain identity hypotheses, not definitions. Each can fail if the proposed extrinsic variable systematically dissociates from the intrinsic one.

### The individuation criterion

Subjecthood tracks the maximal domain of reciprocal closure across memory, value, counterfactual generation, determination, integration, agency, and embodiment — not any single measure. The aggregation rule is an open research question.

## What the Framework Does Not Claim

- **No threshold.** Whether maximal closure is an existence condition (required for any phenomenality) or only an individuation condition (determines whose field it is) is left open. Low-closure processes may be under-individuated phenomenal smears rather than non-conscious.
- **No bridge-principle solution.** The seven-dimension conjunction does not solve the mapping problem — it inherits it from every identity theory. The dimensions are grounded in independently established textual and empirical science (ĪPK triad + Levin + memory research + active inference), which constrains *which features are in the set* but not *how they aggregate*.
- **No derivation of manifestness.** Why any closed determination regime has an intrinsic aspect is a postulate. The epistemic asymmetry defense is the reason it might still be true despite being underivable.
- **No universal subject.** Whether all apertures participate in a common manifest ground or one universal consciousness is an explicitly optional metaphysical extension.
- **No claim to outperform IIT on bridge-principle selection.** The aggregation problem is harder for us than for a single-scalar theory, not easier.
- **No claim that ρᵢ is independently variable from Mᵢ.** The pairing (Mᵢ,ρᵢ) notes their interdefinability and the tradition's claim that reflexivity is what distinguishes sentient from insentient.
- **No claim that ρᵢ = camatkāra is derivable.** It is inherited from the Śaiva textual tradition and occupies the substantive horn of the deflationary/substantive fork, which is unresolved.

## The Three Gaps

1. **Individuation gap:** Content exclusion (apohana) does not entail phenomenal exclusion. The boundary criterion is a research question.
2. **Co-presence gap:** Functional integration does not entail phenomenal unity. Connective synthesis is proposed as identical to unity, not as its cause.
3. **Manifest-value gap:** Objective significance does not entail felt valence. Valuation is proposed as identical to valence, not as its cause.

None of these gaps is closed. Each is a site for empirical testing: if the proposed extrinsic variable dissociates from the phenomenal one under perturbation, the identity fails.

## What Makes This Distinct

Active inference unifies functional descriptions (value, prediction, action, self-model). This framework adds:
- **Token identity:** the claim that the unified function IS one private field, intrinsically
- **Privacy and boundary:** an account of phenomenal individuation that active inference lacks
- **The Śaiva functional anatomy:** a millennium-old decomposition of subjectivity with structural parallels to contemporary science

IIT also starts from phenomenal axioms and proposes physical mappings. This framework differs in that its bridge principles are grounded in a specific functional anatomy (ĪPK triad) that was developed independently of the identity claim. This does not solve the bridge-principle problem — it only constrains which features are candidates.

## The Honest Open Problems

| Problem | Status |
|---|---|
| All three gaps | Open — sites for empirical testing |
| Closure aggregation | Open — unfinished |
| Manifestness | Open — postulate defended by epistemic asymmetry |
| Bridge-principle selection | Open and not shown narrower than single-scalar theories |
| Mᵢ/ρᵢ fork | Open — substantive horn (camatkāra) held by textual authority, not derivation |
| Existence/individuation | Closed — concrete fix owed in predictions |

The framework is a defensible research program — not a completed theory. Its core identity thesis is philosophically coherent, its functional anatomy shows structural parallels across the Śaiva tradition and contemporary research programs (active inference, memory science, Levin's bioelectric work) at the level of analogous taxonomic positions — a testable convergence claim, not an established one. Its gaps are explicitly acknowledged and testable, and it makes a genuinely distinct contribution from active inference, IIT, and other frameworks. What it does not have is a derivation of manifestness, an aggregation rule, or a principled solution to bridge-principle selection. Those are the frontiers.
