# Śivasūtra — C1 (plain-English interpretations)

## 1.1 — caitanyam ātmā
You are not a body that happens to be conscious. You are consciousness itself — the awareness in which everything appears. And it is not a distant cosmic Self: it is one's own nature, the most intimate thing there is. The "self" of the old schools is not a substance inside you; it is the self-experiencing awareness you already are.

## 1.2 — jñānaṃ bandhaḥ
What binds is not ignorance as a lack. It is the possessive cognition itself — the chatter of "I" and "this is mine." The world divided into me and mine. First the text says: you are consciousness. Then: the ordinary way of using consciousness is the trap.

## 1.3 — yonivargaḥ kalāśarīram
The machinery of the trap. Four powers — the Mother, the Eldest, the Fierce, the Left — are the sources from which everything flows; and their body is the alphabet, the letters from A to Kṣ. Speech saturates thought, and the letters are the energies that do the saturating. The sources are the goddesses; their body is language itself.

## 1.7 — turyābhogasaṃvit
The Fourth is not a fourth state reached after waking, dreaming, and deep sleep. It is the consciousness present in their distinction. You move through the three states all day; what stays constant is the awareness that witnesses them. That witnessing awareness — savoring all the states, being none of them — is the real subject.

## 1.10 — aviveko māyāsauṣuptam
When discernment goes off, you are not merely ignorant. You are māyā asleep, and you asleep in it. Deep sleep is not nothing; it is the state where distinctions dissolve. The ordinary, undiscriminating mind is already in that sleep, and does not know it.

## 1.13 — icchāśaktir umā kumārī
The supreme power is will — and the goddess is its face. The maiden is the will before it has given birth to anything: the unspent wanting that precedes every particular desire. She is called kumārī because she kills māyā — the will that has not descended into the world's division is itself the destroyer of the trap.

## 3.19 — kavargādiṣu māheśvaryādyāḥ paśumātaraḥ
The letters, grouped in their classes, are the mothers — and they govern whoever is bound. They give birth to the speech-world in which the bound one lives: his thoughts, his desires, his sense of self are made of language, and the language is made of these letters. For the bound, the mothers bind; for the Lord, the same mothers liberate. Same letters, two functions.

## 3.40 — abhilāṣādbahirgatiḥ saṃvāhyasya
The bound one is conveyed — carried like freight — by his own craving. Desire is the engine that drives the body outward into the world of objects. You do not walk outward on your own; your longing carries you.

## 3.44 — nāsikāntarmadhyasaṃyamāt
If you master the breath at the single midpoint within the nose — the heart's center — what need of the whole apparatus of left and right channels, the whole suṣumnā-theology? Nothing. The middle suffices. The direct path needs no machinery.
