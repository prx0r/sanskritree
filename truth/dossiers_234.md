# Dossiers 2, 3, 4 — Findings and Next Steps

## Dossier 2 — Abhinavagupta on Universalization

### Texts available
- **Tantrāloka** — 17,684 lines in `sources/gretil_tantraloka.txt`. This is Abhinavagupta's magnum opus. Contains key passages about universal selfhood (viśvātmatā), anusandhāna, and the nature of Śiva as all-pervading consciousness.
- **Parātriṃśikā with Abhinavagupta's Vivaraṇa** — 18 entries in GRETIL dataset. A shorter work focused on the nature of supreme consciousness.

### Key Tantrāloka passages found

**On universal selfhood:**
- 3.282-283: "viśvātmakatvaṃ" and "sraṣṭā viśvātmaka iti prathayā bhairavātmatā" — the creator is the universal self; this is the nature of Bhairava. Consciousness manifests the universe within itself.
- 5.104: "viśvātmatvena saṃvidam" — consciousness is attained through universal selfhood
- 9.136: "viśvātmatāsthiteḥ" — established in universal selfhood
- 15.276: "viśvaśarīraḥ san viśvātmatvaṃ gataḥ sphuṭam" — having the world as body, one clearly attains universal selfhood

**On connective synthesis (anusandhāna):**
- 3.245: anusandhāna appears in the context of the three levels of speech (vaikharī, madhyamā, paśyantī)
- 16.242: "knowledge belonging to one body cannot perform anusandhāna with other bodies" — this is CRUCIAL: it supports the argument that anusandhāna (connective synthesis) is local to each cognitive stream

**On Śiva's nature:**
- 3.276: "vyāpi svatantraṃ sarvajñaṃ yacchivaṃ parikalpitam" — Śiva is all-pervading, independent, omniscient

### Key finding from Tantrāloka 16.242
> "bhogyajñānaṃ nānyadeheṣv anusandhānam arhati"
> — knowledge that is enjoyed in one body cannot perform connective synthesis with other bodies

This is significant: Abhinavagupta explicitly says anusandhāna (the connective synthesis that Utpaladeva uses to argue for the unified knower in APS 17) does NOT operate across bodies. This supports the reading that "sarvārthasaṃvidām" in APS 17 refers to cognitions within one stream, not across all streams. It's evidence AGAINST the transpersonal universalization reading.

### What's missing
We don't have Abhinavagupta's Vivṛtivimarśinī (his longer commentary on ĪPK). The ĪPK itself is also not directly available (only cited in Sambandhasiddhi). The Tantrāloka passages provide indirect evidence.

## Dossier 4 — Bhartṛhari / Helārāja on Meaning-Alternative

### Texts available
- **Vākyapadīya** — 6,041 lines in `sources/gretil_vakyapadiya.txt`. Complete root text by Bhartṛhari.

### Key passages found

**On śabdatattva as ground (1.1):**
> anādinidhanam brahma śabdatattvaṃ yad akṣaram /
> vivartate 'rthabhāvena prakriyā jagato yataḥ //
"Brahman, the word-principle, without beginning or end, the imperishable — from which the process of the world transforms as objects."

**On sphoṭa (1.115):**
> sphotasyābhinnakālasya dhvanikālānupātinaḥ /
> vyaktaye svasya rūpasya śabdatvena vivartate //
The unitary meaning-whole (sphoṭa), which is beyond temporal sequence, manifests through sounds that follow time — to reveal its own nature.

**On pratibhā (2.148, 2.152):**
> abhyāsāt pratibhāhetuḥ sarvaḥ śabdo 'paraiḥ smṛtaḥ /
"Some hold that every word is a cause of pratibhā (intuitive flash) through repeated practice."

> yathā dravyaviśeṣāṇāṃ paripākair ayatnajāḥ /
> madādiśaktayo dṛṣṭāḥ pratibhās tadvatāṃ tathā //
"Just as special powers like intoxication arise effortlessly from the maturation of particular substances, so pratibhās arise for those who possess them."

> viśiṣṭopahitāṃ ceti pratibhāṃ ṣaḍvidhāṃ viduḥ /
"They know six kinds of pratibhā: innate, from covering, from appearance, from yoga, from special qualification, and from superimposition."

**On relation (2.369):**
> akṛtrimo hi saṃbandho viśeṣaṇaviśeṣyavat /
"Relation is not artificial, like qualifier and qualified."

**On meaning-unity (2.457):**
> pratisvatantraṃ vākyaṃ vā bhedena pratipadyate /
"Each independent sentence is understood separately."

### What's missing
- **Helārāja's commentary (Prakīrṇakaprakāśa) on Book III** — NOT available. This is the key text for the meaning-alternative, as it discusses relation, time, action, whole/parts, universal/particular.
- Iyer's Sanskrit edition with Helārāja's commentary exists as a published book but is not in our digital collection.

### What Bhartṛhari contributes to the universalization question
The Vākyapadīya suggests an alternative model of unity: not a persisting subject, but a unitary meaning (sphoṭa/pratibhā) that is grasped holistically across sequential elements. This could explain synthesis and recognition without requiring a metaphysical owner-substance. But this requires Helārāja's commentary to develop fully.
