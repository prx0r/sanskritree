# Śivasūtra — P3/T2 (the alternative-reading translation)

*Pass 3 of the flow (= T2). Strategy: re-derive each crux-verse from the IAST asking "what else can this mean?" — the alternative taken where live, the fork marked. T1 = the pass-1 files; R1 = `p2_sivasutra.md`. Anchor: Dyczkowski (on disk).*

## 1.1 — caitanyam ātmā
**T1:** "Consciousness is the Self."
**T2:** "Consciousness is one's own" — *fork: ātmā as "the self" (the noun) vs "one's own nature" (the possessive-adjectival sense, the Śaiva svarūpa-reading). The Vārttika's own gloss ("caitanyam ātmano rūpam" — consciousness is the self's FORM) supports the svarūpa-reading: the sūtra states consciousness = one's own nature, not a hypostatized "Self." The bare identity of T1 is the anchor's; T2's "one's own" is the Vārttika's. Both live; the sūtra's bareness allows both.*
**Commentary-seed:** the fork dissolves in the doctrine: one's own nature IS the Self — the possessive is the nondual move.

## 1.2 — jñānaṃ bandhaḥ
**T1:** "Knowledge is bondage."
**T2:** "A cognition is bondage" — *fork: jñānam as the mass-noun "knowledge" vs a single determinate cognition. The Vārttika's gloss ("ahaṃ mamedam iti yad jñānam" — that cognition which is "I" and "mine") makes T2's the commentary's reading: not knowing-as-such, but the possessive cognition.*

## 1.3 — yonivargaḥ kalāśarīram
**T1:** "The group of yonis is the body of the kalās."
**T2:** "The womb-group is the body of the energies" — *fork: yoni = "source" (Bhāskara's gloss, the anchor) vs the literal "womb"; kalā = "the energies" (the anchor's rendering) vs the transliteration. T2 takes the anchor's register; T1 keeps the technical terms. The fork is register, not meaning.*

## 1.7 — jāgratsvapnasuṣuptabhede turyābhogasaṃvit
**T1:** "In the distinction of waking, dream and deep sleep, [there is] the consciousness of the Fourth's experience."
**T2:** "The consciousness that savors the Fourth — [it abides] in the waking-dream-sleep division" — *fork: turyābhoga = "the Fourth's experience" (bhoga = enjoyment — the savoring) vs "the expanse of the Fourth" (the anchor's reading of ābhoga). The Vārttika's bracket ("svasvarūpaikaghanatāvimarśas turyabhūḥ" — the reflection of one's own nature's single mass) supports the savoring-reading: the Fourth is *tasted* in the three states' division.*

## 1.10 — aviveko māyāsauṣuptam
**T1:** "Non-discrimination is māyā's deep sleep."
**T2:** "Lack of discernment: the deep sleep that māyā sleeps" — *fork: māyā-sauṣuptam as the genitive ("māyā's sleep") vs the appositional ("māyā IS the deep sleep"). The Vārttika ("avivekaḥ... saiva māyāvṛtijālapoṣakatvāt prakīrtitā" — non-discrimination is proclaimed māyā because it nourishes the covering-web) supports the apposition: non-discrimination = māyā = the sleep. T2's double-identity is the sharper.*

## 1.11 — tritayabhoktā vīreśaḥ
**T1:** "The enjoyer of the triad is the Hero-Lord."
**T2:** "The Lord of Heroes enjoys the three" — *fork: the compound's direction — the subject-predicate (the enjoyer IS the Hero-Lord) vs the possessive (the Hero-Lord, who enjoys). The Vārttika's "bhoktā svīkaraṇāt proktaḥ" (called the enjoyer from his taking-in) keeps T1's predication; T2's is the looser rendering.*

## 1.13 — icchāśaktir umā kumārī (KSTS4: icchāśaktitamā)
**T1:** "The power of will is Umā, the maiden" (the variant followed).
**T2:** "The maiden is the supremely-willed power" — *fork (the C1-correction): icchāśaktitamā (Bhāskara's own reading — "the most-will-power") vs the umā-variant (Abhinava/Kṣemarāja's). T2 renders the primary reading with the predication inverted; T3 should carry icchāśaktitamā as primary.*

## 1.15 — hṛdaye cittasaṃghaṭṭād dṛśyasvāpadarśanam
**T1:** "In the heart, from the collision of the mind, the vision of the visible-as-sleep."
**T2:** "In the heart, from the mind's meeting, the seeing of the visible's dream" — *fork: saṃghaṭṭa = "collision/friction" (the churning-image) vs "union/meeting" (the contact — gentler); dṛśyasvāpa = "the visible-as-sleep" (the objects sleeping) vs "the visible's dream" (the objects' dream-state — the genitive). The Vārttika's "saṃghaṭṭa iṣyate" (collision is admitted) supports T1's friction; the svāpa as the objects' dissolution supports either.*

## 2.1 — cittaṃ mantraḥ
**T1:** "The mind is Mantra."
**T2:** "Thought is the mantra" — *fork: citta = "mind" (the anchor) vs "thought" (the function-sense); the article (T2's "the mantra" — the specific) vs the principle-capital (T1). The Vārttika's abhedopacāra (the metonymic identity) works with either; "the mind is Mantra" (T1) keeps the principle-register.*

## 2.4 — garbhe cittavikāso viśiṣṭo'vidyāsvapnaḥ
**T1:** "In the womb [of consciousness], the mind's expansion — distinguished [as such] — is the sleep of avidyā."
**T2:** "In the womb, the mind's expansion is the distinct sleep of ignorance" — *fork: viśiṣṭaḥ as "distinguished/particular" (modifying the expansion or the sleep — the KSTS4 reading) vs the Vimarśinī's aviśiṣṭa ("the unparticularized"). The editions fork here; T2 takes viśiṣṭa as modifying svapnaḥ (the sleep is the particular one); the anchor follows the Vimarśinī. The fork is recension-deep.*

## 2.6 — gurur upāyaḥ
**T1:** "The Master is the means."
**T2:** "The teacher is the way" — *fork: guru = "Master" (the capitalized principle) vs "teacher" (the person — but the Vārttika explicitly de-personalizes: the guru IS the gracious power, śaktir upāyaḥ); upāya = "means" vs "way." T1 is the doctrine's reading; T2 is the surface.*

## 3.1 — ātmā cittam
**T1:** "The Self is mind."
**T2:** "One's own is thought" — *fork: the reverse of 1.1 — ātmā as "the Self" vs "one's own"; cittam as "mind" vs "thought." The Vārttika's "cittaṃ sarvatra śāsane" (the mind in every teaching) supports the noun; the 1.1-parallel suggests the possessive. The pair 1.1/3.1 is the ring: consciousness = one's own; one's own = mind.*

## 3.9 — nartaka ātmā
**T1:** "The Self is the dancer."
**T2:** "The self performs the dance" — *fork: nartaka as the noun ("the dancer" — the identity) vs the agent-noun's verbal force ("the one who dances"). The Vārttika's naṭa-imagery (the actor) supports the identity; T2's verbal reading is the looser register.*

## 3.19 — kavargādiṣu māheśvaryādyāḥ paśumātaraḥ
**T1:** "In the ka-varga and the rest, Māheśvarī and the others are the mothers of the bound."
**T2:** "In the ka-classes and the rest stand Māheśvarī and the others — the mothers who bind" — *fork: paśumātaraḥ = "mothers of the bound" (the genitive) vs "the mothers that bind" (the compound's verbal force — they MAKE the bound). The Vārttika's bracket (they cast the bound down — "adhaḥ pātayanti") supports T2's active sense; the glossary's paśu-ruling supports T1's. Both live; the active is the fiercer.*

## 3.33 — sukhāsukhayor bahirmananam
**T1:** "The thinking of pleasure and pain [is] outward."
**T2:** "Outward-thinking is pleasure and pain" — *fork: the predication's direction — the thinking-of-pain as the outward (T1, the Vārttika's "bahirmananaṃ matam" — the outward thinking is held) vs outward-thinking AS the pain (T2, the identity). The Vārttika's "anāvarakaṃ bhavet" (it becomes non-covering) supports T1: the outward thinking, when it does not enter, does not cover.*

## 3.40 — abhilāṣādbahirgatiḥ saṃvāhyasya
**T1:** "From longing, the going-out of the conveyed one."
**T2:** "Desire: the going-out of the drivable" — *fork: saṃvāhya = "the conveyed one" (the karma-self, per the Vārttika's bracket) vs "the drivable/chariotable" (the vehicle-image — the body-instruments as the chariot). The Vārttika's saṃvāhyākhyasya karaṇacakrasya (the instrument-wheel called the conveyed) supports the instrument-chariot reading — T2's is the more vivid.*

## 3.44 — nāsikāntarmadhyasaṃyamāt kim atra savyāpasavyasauṣumneṣu
**T1:** "From the restraint in the middle of the nose — what is there in the left, right, and suṣumnā [channels]?"
**T2:** "By the restraint at the nose's inner midpoint — what need of left and right and suṣumnā?" — *fork: kim atra as the rhetorical question ("what is there in...") vs the dismissal ("what need of..."). The anchor's "(The movement of the vital breath is stilled) by concentrating on the centre within the nose. Of what use (then) are the left and right channels or Susumna?" — the dismissal is the anchor's; T2 follows it.*

## 3.45 — bhūyaḥ syātpratimīlanam
**T1:** "Again there is the re-closing [into the Lord]."
**T2:** "Let there be the closing again" — *fork: syāt as the assertion ("there is") vs the optative ("let there be" — the prayer/aspiration). The Vārttika's bracket (the return "pratyāpadyate" — he returns) supports the assertion; T2's optative is the liturgical register.*
