## What tradition it belongs to

The **Kubjikā (Kaula) tradition** — the goddess Kubjikā ("the crooked one"), the Western *āmnāya* (pāścimāmnāya), whose root text is the *Kubjikāmata-tantra* (ed. Goudriaan & Schoterman 1988, dated by them to the **10th century**). Dyczkowski is the scholar to whom Sanderson handed the Kubjikā material. The goddess's emblem is the triangle: "a basic form of Kubjikā's maṇḍala is indeed a triangle that represents the goddess's womb (bhaga, yoni), from which the universe originates" (Dyczkowski, Tantrāloka vol. 2, n. on Ciñcinīmatasārasamuccaya 5.14cd–15ab).

## Dating the content, independently of the edition

Internal evidence that this is a **late medieval Kālīkula compilation** in Kubjikā lineage dress — later than the 10th-c. Kubjikāmata:

1. **The fixed Daśamahāvidyā list** (1.15–16) in canonical order — a late-medieval consolidation (cf. Mantramahodadhi, 16th c.; Śāktapramoda, 17th c.). Earlier layers in the library show other groupings (kālī tārā mahāvidyā tripurārṇā maheśvarī; iyaṃ nārāyaṇī kālī tārā śūnyanivāsinī in Bṛhannīlatantra, Karpūrādistotra).
2. **"Jīvanmukti by hearing alone"** (1.20) — vidyā-śravaṇa doctrine of the mantraśāstra epoch.
3. **The sampradāya-emphasis** (1.30–31) — lineage-apologetic framing of late Kaula compilations.
4. **yonimudrā** (1.12) as currency — late mantraśāstra standard (Acārasāratantra, Agamakalpalatā, Ānandatantra, etc.).

**Provisional conclusion:** anonymous late-medieval (≈15th–17th c.?) Kālīkula mantraśāstra manual in the Kubjikā lineage, surviving only in a 19th-c. Bengali print. **Not** the old Kubjikāmata.

## How this context informs translation

- The text is a **manual of vidyā-worship**, not a philosophical treatise: the "essence of the Vedas" is *a goddess and her mantras*, not a doctrine.
- Technical terms are read through **Kālīkula mantraśāstra usage** (nirañjana = the uncoloured/unmanifest state; yoni = womb-source, the triangle, the vidyā's body), with the Trika glosses (Tantrāloka) admitted as conceptual parallels — the traditions share lineage memory — but not imported wholesale.
- Bengali transmission means **phonological wobbles** (v/b) are expected; recorded, not silently "corrected."

# Part II. Word-level analysis with usage parallels

## Verse 1.1 — devadeva mahādeva parameśapurātana | nandīśa sarvabhūteśa nirañjana parātpara

| Item | Form | Analysis |
|------|------|----------|
| devadeva | deva+deva | karmadhāraya, voc. sg. m. "god of gods" |
| mahādeva | mahā+deva | voc. sg. "great god" |
| parameśapurātana | parameśa+purātana | karmadhāraya, voc. "the ancient [who is] the supreme Lord" |
| nandīśa | nandi(n)+īśa | tatpuruṣa, voc. — (a) "lord of Nandin"; (b) "lord of bliss" (nandī) |
| sarvabhūteśa | sarvabhūta+īśa | tatpuruṣa, voc. "lord of all beings" |
| nirañjana | niḥ+añjana | bahuvrīhi, voc. "without stain" |
| parātpara | para+atpara | bahuvrīhi, voc. "beyond the beyond" |

**Parallels for nirañjana (load-bearing term):**
- TĀ 3.172ab: "The Stainless (nirañjana) is the Supreme Abode, while its principle is the powerholder who is 'stained' (sāñjana) — made manifest."
- Dyczkowski's gloss (Tantrāloka vol. 2): "'Añjana' means a 'stain' or 'colouring', and by extension denotes manifestation, which seemingly stains or colours consciousness. Thus 'nirañjana' is not only the stainless but also the unmanifest."
- TĀ 3.108cd: "through swift absorption (samāveśa) the yogī becomes stainless (nirañjanaḥ)."
- TĀ 5: "aṣṭavidyeśvarair yukto vītamāyo nirañjanaḥ" — the Lord, free of māyā, stainless.
- Akulavīratantra 97 (Matsyendranāth school): "nirālambapadaṃ śāntaṃ tathātītaṃ nirañjanam" — the stainless as the state beyond even the kula's support.
- Kaulāvalīnirṇaya / Kuladīpikā / Tripurāsārasamuccaya: "keciddhaṃsapadaṃ nirañjanapadaṃ kecinnirālambanam" — the nirañjana-station as a meditation station alongside haṃsa-pada.
- **Kubjikātantra 9.15 (internal):** "nirākāraṃ samāsādya nirañjanapadaṃ labhet" — "attaining the formless, one attains the nirañjana-station." The phrase *nirañjanapada* of 1.2 is this text's own technical formula.

**Parallel for nandīśa:** vocative address to Śiva in ritual texts (Acintyaviśvasādākhyam: "nandīśa śṛṇu tatsarvaṃ samāhitamanāḥ") — a formulaic vocative like our chain.

## Verse 1.2 — kiṃ tvayā japyate nātha kimarthaṃ vāpi tasya ca | kenopāyena deveśa nirañjanapadaṃ labhet

| Item | Form | Analysis |
|------|------|----------|
| kiṃ | kim | interrogative, acc. sg. n. (object) |
| tvayā | tvad | 2sg instr. "by you" |
| japyate | √jap | passive 3sg pres. "is recited" |
| nātha | nātha | voc. "lord" |
| kimarthaṃ | kim+artha | acc. n. adverbial "for what purpose" |
| vāpi | vā+api | "or indeed" |
| tasya | tad | gen. sg. "of that" |
| kenopāyena | upāya | instr. sg. m. "by what means" |
| deveśa | deva+īśa | voc. "lord of gods" |
| nirañjanapadaṃ | nirañjana+pada | acc. sg. n. — tatpuruṣa "station OF the stainless" (karmadhāraya "the stainless station" also possible) |
| labhet | √labh | optative 3sg "might one attain" (potential question) |

**Parallels:** the upāya-question formula "kenopāyena ... labhet" recurs in the library's vidyā texts (cf. the same collocation in Kaulajñānanirṇaya-style dialogues); nirañjanapada as station: internal 9.15 + Kaulāvalīnirṇaya (above). The passive "japyate" — the god's own japa as the vidyā's signature — is the genre trope of the Kālīkula opening (cf. the mantra-theology of the Karpūrādistotra in our library).

## Verse 1.3 — sārātsārataraṃ brūhi yadi me vidyate dayā | samayācāravidhānañca sādhanañca prakāśaya

| Item | Form | Analysis |
|------|------|----------|
| sārātsārataraṃ | sāra + abl. + tara | "more essential than the essential" — acc. sg. n., object of brūhi |
| brūhi | √brū | 2sg imperative "tell" |
| yadi ... dayā | yadi + dayā nom. sg. f. + vidyate 3sg | "if compassion exists [toward me]" — me gen./dat. |
| samayācāravidhānaṃ | samaya+ācāra+vidhāna | acc. sg. n. "the rule of samaya-conduct" |
| sādhanaṃ | sādhana | acc. sg. n. "the practice" |
| prakāśaya | √prakāś caus. | 2sg imperative "reveal, make manifest" |

**Parallels for sārātsāratara (with direct Kālīkula use):**
- Bhuvaneśvarīmahāstotra, Bṛhattantrasāra, Śāktapramoda, Agamakalpalatā: "sārātsārataraṃ puṇyaṃ mahāvidyaughavigraham" — the most-essential-of-essential, the pure, **whose body is the host of the Great Vidyās**. In the very texts of this text's own milieu, "essence of the essence" names the vidyā-complex.
- Kaulajñānanirṇaya frame: same Sādhu... kathayiṣyāmi structure of reply.

**Parallels for samayācāra:** a chapter-genre in Kubjikā-tradition texts (Kubjikāmata's samayācāra sections); also "samaya" as the initiate's vow in Kaula usage — the discipline that guards the transmission. In this late stratum it is best read as "the observance prescribed by the samaya (vow/convention of the lineage)."

## Verse 1.4 — caitanyaṃ sarvabhūtānāṃ śabdabrahmeti yat punaḥ | tatsarvaṃ śrotumicchāmi sādhakānāṃ hitāya ca

| Item | Form | Analysis |
|------|------|----------|
| caitanyaṃ | caitanya | acc. sg. n. "consciousness" |
| sarvabhūtānāṃ | sarvabhūta | gen. pl. m. "of all beings" |
| śabdabrahmeti | śabdabrahman+iti | "the Word-Brahman, thus [called]" |
| yat | yad | rel. pron. acc. "which" |
| punaḥ | — | "moreover" |
| tatsarvaṃ | tat+sarva | acc. sg. n. "all that" |
| śrotumicchāmi | √śru + √iṣ | infinitive "to hear" + 1sg "I wish" |
| sādhakānāṃ hitāya | sādhaka + hita | gen. pl. + dat. sg. "for the benefit of practitioners" |

**Parallels for śabdabrahman:**
- TĀ 15.8–9 (Dyczkowski vol. 2): "And that which is Nāda and Bindu is this Śakti... Yogis who meditate on the true nature of things call it Śabdabrahman."
- TĀ 1 (vol. 1): "'Sphoṭa' is Śabdabrahman (the Absolute Word)."
- TĀ 3 (vol. 3): "Beyond the plane of the Sound Absolute (Śabdabrahman), present at..."
The pairing caitanya+śabdabrahman = the goddess as consciousness and as mantra-body — the vidyā definition.

## Verse 1.5 — yadi me prāṇanāthaḥ syāt nigūḍhārthaṃ prakāśaya | śapathaṃ me mahādeva vṛhadyoniṃ prakāśaya

| Item | Form | Analysis |
|------|------|----------|
| yadi me prāṇanāthaḥ syāt | prāṇanātha nom. sg. + √as opt. 3sg | "if you are the lord of my life" (address as predication) |
| nigūḍhārthaṃ | nigūḍha+artha | acc. sg. m. "the hidden meaning" |
| śapathaṃ me | śapatha acc. sg. + me | crux — see below |
| vṛhadyoniṃ | bṛhat+yoni (v/b) | karmadhāraya, acc. sg. f. "the Great Yoni" |

**The śapatha crux.** Three readings, all grammatically defensible:
(a) "I swear" — formulaic absolute ("[by] my oath"); Pārvatī vows secrecy before transmission (transmission-under-oath is the mantraśāstra standard; cf. the oath formula "śapathaṃ me" in Sarvollāsatantra and Prāṇatoṣiṇī in our library).
(b) Object of prakāśaya: "reveal to me the oath-bound [teaching]" — the content given only under oath.
(c) "The oath that binds me" — the vow's protective function.
Chosen in all three translations: (a) — because the parallel texts use it as an interjection, and the second prakāśaya already governs vṛhadyoniṃ (a double object would need a connective).

**Parallels for vṛhadyoni / yoni:**
- Ciñcinīmatasārasamuccaya 5.14cd–15ab (via Dyczkowski vol. 2): Kubjikā's maṇḍala triangle = her womb (bhaga, yoni), source of the universe.
- Kubjikātantra 1.12 (internal): "mantrārthaṃ mantracaitanyaṃ yonimudrāṃ na vetti yaḥ / na siddhyati..." — the yoni-mudrā as the condition of mantra-success. The Great Yoni of 1.5 and the yonimudrā of 1.12 are one complex.
- Yonimudrā is standard currency across the library (Acārasāratantra, Agamakalpalatā, Ānandatantra, ...).

## Verse 1.6 — vāsanāsiddhirūpañca bhāvasiddhiñca yat punaḥ | vedānāṃ sāragamyañca taṃ sāraṃ me prakāśaya

| Item | Form | Analysis |
|------|------|----------|
| vāsanāsiddhirūpaṃ | vāsanā+siddhi+rūpa | acc. sg. n. "the form [of accomplishment] that is vāsanā-siddhi" |
| bhāvasiddhiṃ | bhāva+siddhi | acc. sg. f. "the accomplishment of the state" |
| vedānāṃ sāragamyaṃ | veda + sāra + gamya | acc. sg. n. "the essence-attainable of the Vedas" |
| taṃ sāraṃ | sāra | acc. sg. m. "that essence" |

The verse's triad: vāsanā-siddhi (perfection of latent disposition), bhāva-siddhi (perfection of the state of being), and the Vedic essence beyond them — the petition's climax. "Gamya" keeps the Vedas as pointer, not as container.

## Verse 1.7 — sādhu sādhu tvayā devi yaduktaṃ tat śṛṇuṣva me | sārātsārataraṃ devi parādapi paraṃ param

| Item | Form | Analysis |
|------|------|----------|
| sādhu sādhu | — | "well done, well done" (reduplication = emphasis) |
| tvayā | tvad | instr. "by you" |
| yaduktaṃ ... tat | yad...tad | correlative: "what you have said — that" |
| śṛṇuṣva | √śru | 2sg imperative middle "hear" |
| me | asmad | gen. "from me" |
| parādapi | parāt+api | abl. + particle "even beyond the beyond" |
| paraṃ param | para acc. + para | "the highest of the high" |

The correlative yad...tat + the switch of speaker (Pārvatī uvāca → sadāśiva uvāca at the paṭala's head) mark the answer's beginning. "śṛṇuṣva" — the hearer is now the goddess; the frame inverts.

## Verse 1.8 — nityānandāt parānantaṃ śabdabrahmasvarūpakam | satyaṃ satyaṃ punaḥ satyaṃ satyaṃ satyaṃ punaḥ punaḥ

| Item | Form | Analysis |
|------|------|----------|
| nityānandāt | nitya+ānanda | abl. sg. m. "from/than eternal bliss" |
| parānantaṃ | para+ananta | acc. sg. n. "the supreme infinite" |
| śabdabrahmasvarūpakam | śabdabrahman+svarūpaka | bahuvrīhi, acc. sg. n. "whose form is the Word-Brahman" |
| satyaṃ ... punaḥ punaḥ | satya | the oath formula: "truth, truth, again truth; truth, truth, again and again" |

**Parallel for the triple oath:** the oath-formula with repetition (satyaṃ satyaṃ) is the transmission-sealing convention (cf. the "satyaṃ satyaṃ" vow formulas in Kaula texts; the double-oath structure matches the śapatha of 1.5 — pupil swears, teacher swears).
