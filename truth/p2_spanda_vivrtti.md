## Vivṛti v. 1 — daśādikkālādyair akalita-cidāloka-vapuṣaḥ...
**P1:** "That which is ever flashing with the same self-experiencing subjectivity, its body the unobstructed light of consciousness, unseizable by the ten directions, time, and the rest — the own nature of Śambhu, rich with incomparable wonder — that supreme Power-principle triumphs in the world: it is called Spanda."
**Varieties:** a) ours · b) "the supreme Power-principle (śākta-tattva)" as "the principle of the Power" (the first of the three: śākta, śāktopāya...) · c) "jagati jayati" as "triumphs in the world" vs "is victorious in the [whole] world."
**Most likely:** (a) — the maṅgala's structure (the flash, the light-body, the wonder, the Power-principle = Spanda) is confirmed by the bracket's own analysis (the śaktimān grasped by the first pāda; the śāktavijṛmbhaṇā by the rest).
**Commentary:** the maṅgala's "camatkāra" (wonder-taste) — the aesthetic term at the heart of the metaphysical definition — is the same register as our ŚS 3.9's actor (the wonder of the self's play). The commentary should note that Rājānaka Rāma (the disciple of Utpaladeva) opens the Vivṛti with a verse that is both a definition and an aesthetic judgment.

## Vivṛti v. 2 — tajjñair adarśitadiśaḥ...
**P1:** the guide-critique ("let those wander... but those who induce others [on the wrong path]: this is a great delusion").
**Varieties:** a) ours (the bhramantu as permission, the mahān bhramaḥ as the leading-others) · b) "let them wander" as a curse.
**Most likely:** (a) — the bracket's analysis (the well-of-the-blind-man's-eye image, the "this is my path" close) confirms the two-part structure: the personal wandering is allowed, the misleading of others is the error.
**Commentary:** the verse's social critique (the false-teacher) is the lineage's gate-keeper — the same exclusion as the Kubjikā's paśu and the KJN's unfit-pupil rules. The commentary should collect the transmission's "who may not teach" texts.

## Vivṛti v. 3 — the churned ocean
**P1:** "the ocean of scriptures is churned by those who... and the seeker, his mind unsteady... by them he is carried across."
**Varieties:** a) ours (the mixed metaphor resolves: despite the turmoil, the seeker is ferried) · b) "by them he is cast adrift" (stārita as a negative).
**Most likely:** (a) — the ambivalence is the verse's point: the destructive disputants *effect* the crossing (the grace through the chaos); the "arthī" (the seeker) survives the churning like the amṛta.
**Commentary:** the churning-image is the batch's own situation: the ocean of the corpus churned by the translator, the seeker carried across. The commentary can be self-aware here — the verse describes the work.
