## Paper 1: "Death and the World of Imagination: Ibn al-Arabi's Eschatology" (1585 lines)

### Core Claim: Imagination is the Key to All Ontology

"He who does not know the ontological status of imagination has no knowledge whatsoever" (Futuhat II 312.2).

### Imagination as Isthmus (Barzakh)

"Ontologically, imagination is situated between the spiritual and the corporeal, possessing characteristics of both sides. Hence it is often referred to as an 'isthmus' (barzakh), which is defined as 'something that separates two other things.' A standard example in the sensory realm is the line that divides shadow from sunlight; though we see the line, it exists as such only in virtue of the two realities it separates."

**This maps onto our J (junction) variable.** The barzakh is the between — neither this nor that, but the condition for both.

### The Mirror Example

"The common example of an imaginai reality is the image that a person perceives in a mirror: 'He knows for certain that in one respect he has perceived his own form, but he also knows for certain that in another respect he has not perceived it.'"

**This IS the Śaiva pratibimba model independently derived.** The image in the mirror is both self and not-self — the same paradox as the Śaiva "external in the intellect" (bahir antaḥkaraṇabhūmau).

### The Three Levels of Imagination

1. **Nondelimited imagination** (al-khayal al-mutlaq): The cosmos itself — "everything other than God." Also called the "Breath of the All-Merciful" (nafas al-Rahman) and the "Cloud" (al-'ama). This is the infinite receptacle for all forms.

2. **Discontiguous imagination** (al-khayal al-munfaṣil): The independent imaginal world between spirits and bodies. "An independent and integral ontological level made up of embodied forms that are put on like clothing by meanings and spirits."

3. **Contiguous imagination** (al-khayal al-muttaṣil): Individual human imagination. The faculty by which we dream, imagine, and perceive the imaginal world through our own minds.

**This maps onto our Iᵢ variable at three scales:** cosmic (I₀ = the imaginal realm itself), intermediate (the discontiguous world as the source of imaginal forms), and individual (Iᵢ as the generative imagination of a specific aperture).

### The Characteristic Activity of Imagination

"The characteristic activity of imagination is to embody (tajsid) that which is disembodied and to spiritualize (tarawḥun) or sublimate (talṭif) that which is corporeal."

This is the GENERATIVE operation — the transformation of meaning into form and form into meaning. This IS what the aperture does: it manifests consciousness as world and world as consciousness.

### The Cosmos as Imagination

"Engendered existence (al-kawn) is nothing but imagination, though in reality it is Truth (Ḥaqq). Whoever understands this has grasped the mysteries of the Path." (Fusus al-Hikam)

**This IS the TĀ Vol 5 claim** that the universe is "the condensation of the flood of the juice (rasa) of consciousness." Both claim: the cosmos IS the self-manifestation of the Real in imaginal/aesthetic form — not unreal, but real AS manifestation.

### The Cosmos as God's Dream

"In reality, in respect of God's Name the Inward, the forms of the cosmos are related to Him like the forms of a dream to a dreamer. The 'interpretation' (ta'bir) of His dream is that these forms are His states (ahwal), so they are not other than He, just as the forms of a dream are the states of the dreamer, nothing else — he sees only himself." (Futuhat II 380.4)

### Imagination's Vastness AND Narrowness

"Imagination is not able to receive anything except as a form... Hence imagination is the vastest thing that can be known; yet, in spite of this amplitude, which allows it to be exercised over all things, it is unable to receive meanings as they are in themselves, disengaged from material substrata."

**This IS the kañcuka principle.** Consciousness can manifest anything, but only through limitation (form). The impossibility of receiving formless meaning IS the limiting function of the kañcukas.

## Paper 2: "Ibn al-Arabi's Doctrine of the Oneness of Being" (1145 lines)

### Wahdat al-Wujud as the Core

The doctrine that all existence is one. Not pantheism (God = world) but wahdat al-wujud: the act of being (wujud) is one, while beings are its delimitations.

### Existence IS Mercy

"The only reality which embraces each and every thing in the cosmos is existence, so existence is God's mercy, since through it He brings all things from the state of nonexistence within His knowledge to a state of existence within the world, where they are able to perceive, enjoy, and experience their own realities."

**This maps onto our ρᵢ (basal self-affection) claim.** For Ibn Arabi, existence itself IS mercy — the intrinsic goodness of being. For Utpaladeva, consciousness IS aesthetic relish — the intrinsic self-affection of being. Both claim that the fundamental reality has a positive qualitative character: it is not neutral.

### The Breath of the All-Merciful

"God compares the process of creation to the breathing of a breather. The breath itself is like the underlying substance within which all things assume their specific characteristics; through it the 'immutable entities' (al-a'yan al-thabita) become 'existent entities.'"

"You could say that the Breath of the All-Merciful is the same as God; yet it is different from God. Likewise, the words which become articulated within the Breath are the same as the Breath, yet they differ from it."

**This maps onto our prāṇa/spanda concept.** The Breath is the bridge between the One and the many — the same as the source yet different from it. This is the same logic as the Śaiva spanda: the pulse that is both consciousness and its manifestation.

### The Hidden Treasure

"God speaks not because some external factor forces Him to speak, but because He is merciful and generous by nature and wills to bring creatures into existence."

This converges with the ḥadīth qudsī: "I was a hidden treasure and I loved to be known." Creation is not a necessity but an act of love/generosity. **This IS the Śaiva claim that manifestation = camatkāra = aesthetic delight.** The Real does not create because it must, but because it delights in being known.
