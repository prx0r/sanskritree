# Akulavīratantra, vv. 1–18 — R2 (the comparison, with the real forks)

*Phase 4. T1 = the pass-1 (syntax-bound). T2 = the alternative-reading translation. This R2 is adversarial to BOTH: it overturns T1 twice (vv. 9, 16), improves it once (v. 2), resolves an [X] without emendation (v. 14), and records the genuine interpretation-space.*

## The genuine forks and their adjudications

### Fork A — v. 9: pāpabandhavit, active vs passive
**T1:** "...all that **destroys sin-bonds** [yet remains bound]."
**T2:** "...all of it **is itself pierced by the bonds of sin**."
**Adjudication: T2 wins.** The compound pāpa-bandha-vit: -vit from √vid ("knowing") gives "knowing the sin-bonds" (odd); the passive from √vyadh ("pierced") gives "pierced by the bonds of sin" — matching the verse's polemic (the schools' attainment is itself bound). The anchor's paraphrase ("enmeshed in useless discussions") supports the passive-negative register. **T1's "destroys" was a guess with no morphological support. Corrected.**
**Commentary:** the corrected reading sharpens the whole polemic: the schools' knowledge does not cut the bonds — it is the bonds. The text's epistemology of exclusion is total.

### Fork B — v. 16: gamāgame, "āgama and its negation" vs "in coming and going"
**T1:** "...so say **āgama and its negation**."
**T2:** "...**in their coming and going**."
**Adjudication: T2 wins.** The cross-text check: the KJN 14/136 — "na iḍā piṅgalā caiva suṣumṇā ca **gamāgame**" — the identical collocation, there unambiguous: "in coming and going" (the breath's movement). The channel-context (iḍā, piṅgalā) confirms: the tranquil is not in the channels' traffic. **T1's clever reading was a misreading — the parallel settles it. Corrected.** (This is the flow working: the alternative-exploration, checked against the corpus, caught a T1 error.)
**Commentary:** the verse now coheres with the channel-list: not in iḍā/piṅgalā, not in their coming-and-going — the breath-technique refused in its own vocabulary.

### Fork C — v. 2: the direct address
**T1:** "...to be guarded with effort by **one who desires** the eternal state."
**T2:** "...**you who desire** the eternal state must guard it with effort."
**Adjudication: T2.** The genre's pedagogical directness (the KJN's "śṛṇu tvaṃ" — "hear, you") supports the address; "yadīcchan" (if-you-desiring) is naturally second-person. Minor but improves the force.
**Commentary:** the text's "you" is the reader — the secrecy-rule is spoken to us directly. The commentary should note the shift from the impersonal to the personal across the corpus's openings.

### Fork D — v. 14: the [X] resolved without emendation
**R1** (earlier): emend svabhāvamatimataṃ → svabhāvam upagataṃ.
**T2:** read the print as svabhāva-mati-mataṃ — "the tranquil [state] **held as the own-nature-thought**" (mataṃ = "held/known as").
**Adjudication: T2's construal is the better repair** — it needs no emendation, and the "held-as" reading fits the verse's epistemic register (the state as the mind's own construal, then the manomaya-paradox). The emendation is demoted to a footnote. **The [X] is upgraded to "resolved-by-construal, print-check pending."**
**Commentary:** "whose mind is made of mind" (manomayam) + "held as the own-nature-thought" — the verse becomes the subjectivity-thesis: the tranquil state is the mind's own substance known as such.

### Fork E — v. 6: gurum, the noun vs the adjective
**T1:** "...the partless **guru** of the Kaulas."
**T2:** "...the partless, **the weighty** [state] of the Kaulas."
**Adjudication: both live; T1 (the noun) primary.** The noun-reading ("the guru") is the tradition's (the archive: "the partless guru") and matches the batch's guru-identity doctrine (the KJN: "himself the pupil, himself the guru"; the Akulavīra's own v. 26). But the adjective ("the weighty/supreme") is grammatically clean, and the two readings converge: the state IS the guru-principle. T3 keeps "the partless guru" with the adjective noted. **OPEN-adjacent — the commentary's job, not the translation's.**
**Commentary:** the convergence is the doctrine: in this tradition the supreme state and the guru are the same referent. The fork dissolves into the theology.

### Fork F — v. 15: paṭhet, "recite" vs "read/find"
**T1:** "...nor in the domain of nāda, is it to be **read [found]**."
**T2:** "**It is not to be recited**... at the divisions of the supports and marks, nor in the domain of nāda."
**Adjudication: T1 (the epistemic) primary, T2 noted.** paṭh = "recite" is the lexical first sense, and the ritual-prohibition register (the text forbidding its recitation in those contexts) is genuinely possible — but the verse's position in the negation-ladder (the state, not the text, is the subject) favors "found." The recitation-reading is the commentary's alternative.
**Commentary:** if the recitation-reading is right, the verse is meta-textual: the Akulavīra's *text* must not be ritually recited at the supports — the anti-technique extending to the text itself.

### Fork G — the maṅgala: aṅga, māyā-mala
**aṅga:** "born from his own body/limbs" (T1) vs "born of his own members [the lineage-circle]" (T2). Adjudication: T1 primary — the sahaja-birth image (the teaching as the teacher's own nature); the retinue-reading secondary.
**māyā-mala:** "māyā's impurity" (T1) vs "māyā and impurity" (the dvandva, T2). Adjudication: the dvandva is grammatical and the sense barely shifts (both are the released things); T1 stands, the dvandva noted.

### Fork H — vv. 3–4, 11: the plural and the compound
**akulavīreṣu:** "in the Akulavīra" (T1, the state) vs "in the Akulavīra-ones" (T2, the class of the realized). Adjudication: T1 primary — the state-locus matches v. 4's "tatraiva"; the adepts-reading is a real alternative, noted as OPEN-adjacent (the dharmas dissolve in the realized — a plausible reading of a class-plural).
**sarvasiddhi:** "and all attainments" (T1) vs "the All-Siddhi" (T2, the compound-name). Adjudication: T1 primary (the enumerative rhythm: "the siddhi of all, and all attainments"); the name-reading noted.
