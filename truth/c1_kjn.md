# Kaulajñānanirṇaya — C1 (plain-English interpretations)

## 2/2 — the fire at the nails
The cosmos maps onto the body, and the withdrawal of the universe begins at the fingertips. The Fire-of-Time — the Rudra who is the end of all things — abides at the nails' tips: the outermost edge of the body is the edge of the cosmos. When it blazes upward, the world withdraws. Creation and destruction are not events out there; they are states of the body.

## 2/8 — the two steps
The Lord's cosmogony is two steps: one step set down in withdrawal destroys the world; one step set down in creation brings it forth. Two steps, not an elaborate cosmology — the whole universe is the stride of the divine. Creation and destruction are one movement, the same foot setting down twice.

## 3/9 — the lotus-arithmetic → the Uncoloured
The meditation-ground grows petal by petal: four, eight, twelve, sixteen, sixty-four, a hundred, a thousand, a crore. And then, above all the arithmetic: the free, immovable, all-pervading lotus — the Uncoloured. The point of the counting is to run out of counting. All the petals are the formed; the Uncoloured is what the formed is for.

## 4 — the powers
The noose-thrust, the piercing, the speaking images, the shattering of pots and stones — a manual of psychic powers, complete with their mantras. Read in the frame of the whole text, they are the demonstration: the proof that the body-map works. The powers are not the goal — the text says so repeatedly; they are the evidence. And the paṭala ends where the doctrine lives: the bound one, placed dissolved in the supreme liṅga, goes to liberation.

## 5 — the death-conqueror
The moon-meditation: the white, cool moon streaming its nectar; the milk-ocean lotus with its thousand crore petals; the self sprinkled by the floods. The claim is literal: no old age, no disease, no death. What the practice actually does is dissolve the body's identification — the self bathed in the amṛta, re-identified with the immortal. The death-conquest is not a magic trick; it is the death of the false self, practiced until the practice is the state.

## 6/4 — the jīva IS the Stainless
Not "the jīva will attain the Stainless" — "the jīva is the Stainless." The life-force in your body is already the supreme, the partless, the eternal. What seems to be the little self, the bound one, is the whole; the body was the only disguise.

## 11/11 — the five purifiers
The five "purifiers" are the substances the orthodox world calls impure: the stool, the flow, the nectar, the seed, mingled with blood and marrow. The claim: they purify because the ritual makes the participants face the undivided nature of reality. The orthodox distinction between pure and impure is exactly the division the Kaula dissolves. The text means what it says; the reinterpretation comes later.

## 16 — the fish-myth
The lineage tells its own origin as a story. The knowledge descended at the Moon-island; a mouse made off with it; a great fish swallowed it; the Lord of the lineage took the form of a fisherman and split the fish's belly. Three layers: the historical — the founders were of the fishing community, and the hero literally claims "I am the Fisherman"; the soteriological — the knowledge is swallowed by the world and must be recovered from within it; the polemical — the fisherman becomes "the leader of the brāhmaṇas," the lowest caste-form carrying the highest teaching.

## 17/33 — the identity-series
"He is the jīva, the pudgala, the haṃsa; he is the Śiva, the pervasive, the supreme." The little self, the individual, the swan — and the absolute — are the same referent. The verse states it as the conclusion of everything before. And the following verses draw the consequences: the self is not cut by weapons, not burnt by fire, not wetted by water. Then the quietest radicalism: "resorting to the human body, that Śiva plays on earth." The absolute is the one playing as you.

## 20/2 — the skull-breaking
At death, the skull breaks — and the life goes to the Uncoloured. Not a judgment, not a rebirth — a return. The skull is the last cage; its breaking is the release into what the life always was. Two verses across the text say the same thing: when the body falls, the skull breaks, and the jīva — already the Stainless — returns to it. Death is the reveal, not the end.

## 25/2 — the final superlative
"More secret than the secret, the more-essential-than-the-essential, the supreme — the unrevealed has been revealed." The secret was never a thing to be hidden; it was the context — the whole web of the lineage's knowledge, now revealed.
