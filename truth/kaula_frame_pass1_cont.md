# Kaula Frame Texts, Continued — KJN Paṭala 4–5 + Akulavīratantra vv. 19–49 — Pass 1

*Cluster T2 continuation. Same 10th–11th c. Kaula dialect; the siddhi-paṭala (KJN 4) is the practice-side of the kula-lakṣaṇa, the mṛtyuñjaya (KJN 5) the death-conquering meditation, the Akulavīra's second half the systematic negation and the ethics of the state.*
*Sources: Bagchi 1934 (M00027) · Calcutta 1934 (M00003) · No English anchors — first translations.*

---

# I. Kaulajñānanirṇaya, Paṭala 4 — the powers (siddhi)

**4/1** — adya me saphalañjanma tapastaptaṃ sureśvara | adya me nirmalandehaṃ tvatprasādena bhairava ||
> Today my birth bears fruit, today my austerity is fulfilled, O Lord of the gods; today my body is pure, by your grace, O Bhairava.

**4/2** — svagṛhe svadeśe vā pāśastobhaṃ yadā bhavet | nigrahānugrahañcaiva krāmaṇaṃ haraṇaṃ tathā ||
> When the binding-thrust (pāśastobha) occurs in one's own house or country — and the restraining and the favoring (nigrahānugraha), the transference (krāmaṇa), the taking-away (haraṇa) —

**4/3** — pratimājalpanañcaiva ghaṭapāṣāṇasphoṭanam | kathaṃ me sarvasaṃkṣepāttvameva śaraṇaṅgatā ||
> — and the speaking of images (pratimājalpana) and the shattering of pots and stones (ghaṭapāṣāṇasphoṭana): how [are these done]? In brief tell me — you alone are my refuge.

**Bhairava speaks:**

**4/4** — sādhu sādhu mahādevi kathayāmi mahātape | kulapiṇḍaṃ samālikhya nāḍī nāḍīmukhaiḥ saha ||
> Well asked, well asked, O great goddess — I will tell you, O great ascetic: having drawn the kula-body (kulapiṇḍa) together with the channels and their mouths (nāḍīmukha) —

**4/5** — vidyāmantramayaṃ piṇḍaṃ bhuktimuktipradaṃ śubham | nāḍī nāḍīmukhairvāpi adhorddhaṃ rephadīpitam ||
> — the auspicious body made of vidyā and mantra, giving enjoyment and liberation; the channels with their mouths, above and below, illumined by the repha (r) —

**4/6** — vāyuviṣṇu(ḥ) sabinduśca taptacāmīkaraprabham | janmamaṇḍalasaṃlīnaṃ dhvāntateja(ḥ)samaprabham ||
> — the wind-Viṣṇu with the bindu, radiant like molten gold; dissolved in the birth-wheel (janmamaṇḍala), of the brilliance of the light-of-darkness (dhvāntatejas) —

**4/7** — sādhakaḥ sādhyadehe tu paśudehe tu hṛdgatam | karastobhādikaṃ bhadre karotyevaṃ carācaraiḥ ||
> — the sādhaka, in the body to be worked (sādhyadeha), in the bound one's body, in the heart: performs the hand-thrust (karastobha) and the rest, O blessed one, upon the moving and unmoving.

**4/8** — bindunāda(sta) tathā śaktirdīptadhyānamathāpi vā | janmamanḍalasaṃlīnaṃ dhvāntateja(ḥ)samaprabham ||
> The bindu, the nāda, the śakti, the blazing meditation — the [whole] dissolved in the birth-wheel, of the light-of-darkness brilliance.

**4/9** — sādhyadehagatañcintya nimeṣārddhaṃ stubhyate paśuḥ | dhyātvā vidyullatākāraṃ śaktirvai vyomapañcake ||
> Contemplating [it] in the body-to-be-worked, within half a blink the bound one is thrust (stubhyate); meditating on the lightning-vine form, the śakti in the fivefold space (vyomapañcaka) —

**4/10** — stubhyate ca jagatsarvaṃ ghaṭikārddhe varānane | śaktivijñānavedhena manākhyena mahānmane ||
> — the whole world is thrust within half a ghaṭikā, O fair-faced one; by the piercing (vedha) of the śakti-knowledge, by the unmana (manākhya), O great-minded one —

**4/11** — baddhvā caitat pāśena parvatānapi lālayet | paradehe parāśaktirnādenākramya vedhayet ||
> — binding this with the noose, one can rock even mountains; in another's body, the supreme power, mounting by the nāda, pierces [it].

**4/12** — calane bālyane caiva baddhvā mudrābandhane | atītānāgatañcaiva pṛṣṭāsau kathayiṣyati ||
> In [the target's] movement and speech, binding with the mudrā-bonds — asked, he will tell the past and the future.

**4/13** — akārādihakārāntaṃ kulapiṇḍasya bhairavi | śaṃkhābhiṣekavijñānaṃ dantakāṣṭhādikaṃ priye ||
> From the a-kāra to the ha-kāra [is] the kula-body, O Bhairavī; the knowledge of the conch-consecration, the tooth-stick and the rest, O beloved —

**4/14** — puṣpāñjalikarastobhamekaikaṃ varṇadīpitam | māraṇoccāṭanañcaiva stambhamohādikaṃ priye ||
> — the hand-thrust of the flower-offering, each one illumined by its syllable: killing (māraṇa), expulsion (uccāṭana), immobilizing (stambha), deluding (moha), and the rest, O beloved —

**4/15** — śāntikampauṣṭikaṃ vāpi ākṛṣṭiñca vaśantathā | pañcāśavarṇasaṃyogaṃ jñātvā sarvaṃ kuru priye ||
> — pacification (śānti), tremor (kampa), strengthening (pauṣṭika), attraction (ākṛṣṭi), and subjugation (vaśya): knowing the union of the fifty syllables, do all, O beloved.

**[The bīja-catalog]** — the siddhi-bījas: hūṃ = killing; yūṃ = expulsion; rūṃ = fever-making; buṃ = study-binding; lūṃ = immobilizing; śūṃ = pacification; ṣūṃ = nailing (kīlana); kṣeṃ/kṣuṃ = the bound one's seizure; klīṃ/kṣṇīṃ = subjugation; kleṃ/kṣṇoṃ = agitation (kṣobhaṇa) and delusion; soṃ = immediate conviction; ho/haḥ = poison-destruction; hro/hraḥ = the drawing of blood and the yoginīs; jūṃ/saḥ = death-conquering (mṛtyuñjaya); sroṃ = the thrusting of the noose (pāśastobha), in Kāmarūpa; bhrū/tra = the ḍākinī-siddhi; jhura = the rākṣasī-siddhi; lūṃ = the lākinī-siddhi; luṃ/ka = the kusumālinī-siddhi; yūṃ = the yoginī-siddhi; hrīṃ = attraction.

**4/16** — ekaikena tu bījena varṇarāśirvibhedayet | kurute vibhidaṃ karma yaddevi manasepsitam ||
> By each single bīja one divides the mass of syllables; one performs the diverse acts that the mind desires, O Devī.

**4/17** — dīkṣānteṣṭikakarmeṣu viparītaṃ mātṛkaṃ (care)t | yojayettāṃ kulādhā(raṃ)tu sarvaṃ jñātvā dṛḍhalakṣavit ||
> In the rites of initiation and the fire-offering, one should perform the reverse mātṛkā; joining it to the kula-support (kulādhāra), knowing all, with firm marks.

**4/18** — tejorūḍhamparaṃ liṅgaṃ taṃ līnaṃ sthāpayet paśuḥ | svātantro muktimāyāti sarvahṛdbhavane tu saḥ ||
> The supreme liṅga, mounted in brilliance — the bound one, placing himself dissolved in it, independent (svātantra), goes to liberation, in the house of all hearts.

**4/19-4/20** — sarvajñaguṇasaṃpūrṇa ityevaṃ bhairavo'bravīt | ... gopitavyamprayatnena duṣṭānāmbhaktivarjitam | na deyaṃ bhaktihīnasya kaulikīṃ siddhimicchatām ||
> Full of the qualities of omniscience — thus Bhairava spoke... it is to be guarded with effort from the wicked, from the devotionless; it is not to be given to the one lacking devotion, even to those who desire the Kaulikī-siddhi.

Notes: the siddhi-paṭala: the kulapiṇḍa (the mantra-body = the 50 syllables), the channels, the bīja-catalog for the six acts (ṣaṭkarmāṇi), the reverse-mātṛkā in dīkṣā — and the closing turn (4/18): the supreme liṅga as the real aim. [P: mātṛkā/kulādhāra/bindu-nāda-śakti/vyomapañcaka — the batch's glossary in practice-register; "nāḍīmukha" — cf. the ŚS 3.5 nāḍīsaṃhāra; the paśu at 4/18 = the aspirant himself, placed in the liṅga.]

# II. Kaulajñānanirṇaya, Paṭala 5 (opening) — the death-conqueror (mṛtyuñjaya)

**5/1** — mṛtyuñjayammahādeva kathayasva prasādataḥ | ajñānatamasācchannā yathā bhrāntirvinaśyati | kathayasva mahādeva bhrāntinirnāśanaṃ param ||
> The death-conqueror, O great god — tell it by your grace: how the delusion (bhrānti), covered by the darkness of ignorance, perishes. Tell, O great god, the supreme destruction of delusion.

**5/2-5/4** — yenaiva jñānamātreṇa dhyānābhyāsena nityaśaḥ | sarvadvandvavinirmukta sa gatābhyāsatatparaḥ | sādhayet sakalārthānāṃ manasā yat samīhitam | uttiṣṭha khaḍgapātālaṃ rocanāñjanapādukam | guṭikākāśagamanaṃ rasañcaiva rasāyanam | antarddhānambhaveddevi tathānyañca rasāyanam ||
> By mere knowledge, by the constant practice of meditation, freed from all dualities, devoted to the practice: he accomplishes by mind whatever he desires — the sword (khaḍga), the underworld (pātāla), the ointments (rocanā, añjana), the boots (pādukā), the pill, the sky-going (kāśagamana), the elixir and the rasa-chemistry (rasāyana); he becomes invisible, O Devī, and the other rasāyanas [too].

**5/5-5/7** — śṛṇu tvaṃ ekacittantu kathyamānaṃ mayā priye | sitañca śītalaṃ divyaṃ sugandhaṃ bhūritejasam | candrāhlādakaraṃ divyaṃ āgacchantaṃ khamadhyataḥ | sravantaṃ bahurandhreṇa acirānmṛtyujidbhavet | na jarāmaraṇantasya vyādhirogo na vidyate ||
> Hear with one-pointed mind what is being told by me, O beloved: the white, the cool, the divine, the fragrant, the greatly radiant — the divine moon, delight-giving, coming from the midst of the sky, streaming through its many apertures — [contemplating it,] soon one becomes the conqueror of death; for him there is no old age, no dying, no disease, no sickness.

**5/8-5/11** — kṣīrodārṇavamadhyasthaṃ śvetaṃ kamalavistaram | sahasrakoṭipatraṃ vā tanmadhye ātmānaṃ budhaḥ | tasmiṃścaiva niviṣṭaṃ tu sitābharaṇabhūṣitam | tādṛśenaiva padmena upariṣṭādyaṭitampriye | kṣīrodavi(śe)ṣaiḥ sarvaissiñcyamānamanusmaret | mucyante sarvarogaistu jarāmaraṇabandhanaiḥ | krīḍate vividhairbhogairicchārūpañca jāyate ||
> The white lotus-expansion in the middle of the milk-ocean — with a thousand crore petals — in its middle the wise one [contemplates] himself, entered into it, adorned with white ornaments; sprinkled from above by a similar lotus, O beloved, by all the excellences of the milk-ocean — he is freed from all diseases, from the bonds of old age and death; he plays with manifold enjoyments and becomes of whatever form he wills.

**5/12-5/14** — athānyaṃ sampravakṣyāmi mṛtyuñjayaṃ viśeṣataḥ | dhyātvā candrāsanasthañca ūrddhaścandreṇa ghaṭṭitam | sīkaraiḥ śītalairdivyaiḥ siñcyamānamanusmaret | jarāmaraṇanirmuktaṃ sarvavyādhivivarjitam | saṃvatsaraprayogeṇa sāmānyaṃ yoginīkulaiḥ | anyañca adbhutandevi śṛṇu tvaṃ kulabhāvini ||
> Now I will tell the death-conqueror specifically: meditating [on one] seated on the moon-throne, struck from above by the moon, sprinkled by the cool divine showers — freed from old age and death, free of all diseases. With a year's application it becomes common [knowledge] among the yoginī-clans. And another wonder, O Devī — hear, O lady of the kula:

Notes: the mṛtyuñjaya meditation (the moon in the sky, the milk-ocean lotus, the self-in-the-lotus); the same structure as our Kālīkula texts' visualization-practice. [X: the "candra" here — the moon as the nectar-source; pass 2 to cross-check with the KJN's own later sādhana-paṭalas.]

# III. Akulavīratantra, vv. 19–49 — the great negation and the ethics

**19-20** — praveśanirgame naiva nāvāhanavisarjanam | na karaṇairnāsanaṃ mudrairnāmāse bhinnatāluke (?) || na nirodho na coddhāro nātītāṃ cālanaṃ na hi | na preryaprerakañcaiva na sthānannaiva cāśrayam ||
> No entry, no exit; no invocation, no dismissal; no seat (āsana) with the instruments, no mudrās, no fixing of the pierced palate. No restraint (nirodha), no raising (uddhāra), no moving of the past; no impeller and no impelled; no place, no support.

**21-22** — na cātmanaiva tad grāhyaṃ grāhyātītapadaṃ bhavet | etat pakṣavinirmuktaṃ hetudṛṣṭāntavarjitam | sabāhyābhyantarantatra ekoccāraṃ carācaram || na dūre na ca vai nikaṭe na bharito na ca riktaka(ḥ) | na unno na so'dhika ebhiḥ pakṣairvivarjitam ||
> Nor is it to be grasped by the self — it is the state beyond the graspable. Free of these alternatives (pakṣa), without argument and example (hetudṛṣṭānta): within and without, the one utterance, moving and unmoving. Not far, not near; not full, not empty; not high, not low, not more — free of these alternatives.

**23** — yaśca viṃśātmako hyeṣa pudgala nāsti yatra vai | yatra lakṣaṃ na vidyeta akulavīra sa ucyate ||
> Where this twenty-formed one (viṃśātmaka — the twenty tattvas?) and the person (pudgala) do not exist; where no mark (lakṣa) is found — that is called the Akulavīra.

**24-25** — yasyaivaṃ saṃsthitaṃ kaścit samarasa saṃsthitaḥ | sa brahmā so hariśceśaḥ sa rudro sa ca īśvaraḥ | sa śivaḥ paramadevaḥ sa somārkāgnikastathā | sa ca sāṃkhyaḥ purāṇāśca arhantabuddha eva ca ||
> Whoever stands established in it — established in the equal-taste (samarasa) — he is Brahmā, he is Hari, he is Īśa, he is Rudra, he is Īśvara; he is Śiva, the supreme god; he is Soma, sun, fire; he is the Sāṅkhya, the Purāṇic [deity], the Arhat and the Buddha.

**26** — svayaṃ devī svayaṃ devaḥ svayaṃ śiṣyaḥ svayaṃ guruḥ | svayaṃ dhyānaṃ svayaṃ dhyātā svayaṃ sarvatra devatā ||
> He himself is the goddess, himself the god; himself the pupil, himself the guru; himself the meditation, himself the meditator; himself the deity everywhere.

**27** — yādṛśena tu bhāvena puruṣo bhāvayet sadā | tādṛśaṃ phalamā(va)pnoti nātra kāryavicāraṇāt ||
> By whatever state (bhāva) a man always contemplates, of that kind he obtains the fruit — no deliberation of works here.

**28-29** — asyaiva hi nāmāni pṛthagbhūtāni yogibhiḥ | anāma tasya gīyante bhrāntijñānavimohitaiḥ || dharmādharmasamākliṣṭāvikalpatamaśchāditāḥ | tena muñcanti saṃsāraṃ narakaṃ yonisaṃkulam ||
> Of it, by yogis deluded in their erroneous knowledge, the many separate names are sung — [but] it is the Nameless (anāma). Tormented by dharma and adharma, covered by the darkness of constructs, they do not thereby leave saṃsāra, the hell crowded with wombs.

**30-33** — akulavīraṃ mahadbhūtaṃ yadā paśyanti sarvagam | sa bāhyābhyantare nityaṃ ekākāraṃ carācaram || nistaraṅgaṃ nirābhāsaṃ padabhedavivarjitam | sarvāvayavanirmuktaṃ nirlayaṃ nirvikārajam || adṛṣṭanirguṇaṃ śāntaṃ tattvātītaṃ nirañjanam | sarvajñaṃ paripūrṇañca svabhāvaścaivamakṣayam || kāryakāraṇanirmuktamacintyamanāmayam | māyātītaṃ nirālambaṃ vyāpakaṃ sarvatomukham ||
> The great being Akulavīra, when they see it as all-going — within and without, eternally one-formed, moving and unmoving; waveless, appearing-less, free of the divisions of states; free of all members, dissolution-less, change-less; unseen, attributeless, tranquil, beyond the principles, the Stainless (nirañjana); all-knowing, complete, of own-nature, imperishable; free of cause and effect, unthinkable, without illness; beyond māyā, supportless, pervasive, facing everywhere.

**34-35** — samatvaṃ ekabhūtañca ūhāpohavivarjitam | akulavīraṃ mahadbhūtam astināstivivarjitam || na mano na ca vai buddhirna cintācetanādikam | na kālaḥ kalanāśaktirna śivo na ca indriyaḥ ||
> The equality, the one-being, free of inference and rejection — the Akulavīra, the great being, free of being and non-being. Not mind, not intellect, not thought and consciousness and the rest; not time, not the power of kalana; not Śiva, not the senses.

**36-38** — na bhūte gṛhyate so hi na sukhaṃ duḥkhameva ca | ... na raso virasaścaiva na kṛto na ca jāyate | na cchāyā na ca tāpastu na śīto na ca uṣṇavān | na (dṛśyate man) statra udayāstamanavarjitam | na sīmā dṛśyate tatra na ca tīrthyaṃ na caiva hi ||
> It is not grasped in the elements; not pleasure, not pain; not taste nor distaste; not made, not born; not shade, not heat; not cold, not warmth; no rising and no setting is seen there; no limit is seen there, no pilgrimage [valid] —

**39-41** — advaitamacalaṃ śāntaṃ saṃgadoṣavivarjitam | nirākulaṃ nirvikalpañca nibaddhañca malakṣaṇam || anāthaṃ sarvanāthañca unmanā madavarjitam | anigūḍhamasandhiñca sthāvaraṃ jaṅgameva ca || jvalajjvalanabhūmyā ca āpoñcaiva tathaiva ca | sarvaṃ samarasaṃ pūrṇaṃ akulavīrantu kevalam ||
> Non-dual, immovable, tranquil, free of the fault of attachment; unagitated, construct-free — and [yet] bound, of the nature of the impurity? [X] The lordless and the lord of all; the unmana, free of madness; the unhidden, the unjointed, the stationary and the moving. By the blazing fire and the earth, and the waters likewise: everything is the equal-taste, full — the Akulavīra alone.

**42-45** — yasyaiṣā saṃsthitā muktiḥ sa mukto bhavabandhanāt | na tasya mātāpitā (vā)bāndhavaṃ na ca devatā || na yajñaṃ nopavāsañca na kriyā varṇabhedakam | tyaktvā vikalpasaṃghātam akulavīralayaṃ gatāḥ || na japo nārcanaṃ snānaṃ na homaṃ naiva sādhanam | agnipraveśanaṃ nāsti hemantabhṛgu nodanam || niyamo'pi na tasyāsti nopavāso vidhīyate | pitṛkāryaṃ na karotāti tīrthayātrāvratāni ca ||
> For him in whom this liberation stands, he is freed from the bonds of becoming: he has no mother, no father, no kin, no deity. No sacrifice, no fasting, no caste-dividing rite — abandoning the mass of constructs, they go to the dissolution in the Akulavīra. No japa, no worship, no bathing, no fire-offering, no sādhana; no entering the fire, no [ritual] fanning of the fire in winter. No restraint (niyama) is his, no fasting is prescribed; he does not do the ancestor-rites, nor pilgrimages and vows.

**46-49** — dharmādharmaphalaṃ nāsti na snānaṃ nodakakriyā | svayaṃ tyaja sarvakāryāṇi lokācārāṇi yāni ca || samayācāravicārañca kṛtakā bandhakāni tu | saṃkalpañca vikalpañca ye cānye kila dharmiṇaḥ || bhave yogī nirācāro paśucāravivarjitaḥ | siddhiśca vividhākārā pātāle ca rasāyanam || pratyakṣañca yā labdhaṃ na gṛhnīyāt kadācana | sarvañca pāśajālañca adhomārgapradāyakaḥ || eteṣu mocanā nāsti akulavīravivarjitāḥ ||
> No fruit of dharma and adharma, no bathing, no water-rites: abandon by yourself all the works, all the worldly customs. The deliberation of the samaya-observance — those made [observances] are bondages: the saṃkalpa and the vikalpa, and the other so-called dharmas. The yogī should be without rites (nirācāra), free of the behavior of the bound (paśucāra). And the siddhis of various forms — the rasāyana in the underworld — [even] what is directly obtained, one should never accept: all that is the net of bonds, giving the downward path. In these there is no liberation — for those devoid of the Akulavīra.

Notes: the Akulavīra's second half completes the apophatic map: the systematic negation (not-this, not-that — through vv. 19-41), then the *ethics of the state* (vv. 42-49): the liberation is ritual-less, caste-less, vow-less — and even the siddhis are refused (they are the net of bonds). The nirañjana (v. 32) and samarasa (v. 24, 41) — the equal-taste terminus. [P: "paśucāra" — the bound one's behavior — our glossary's paśu in its ethical form; the samaya-observance (samayācāra) of the KJN and Kubjikā is here *negated* — the Akulavīra as the beyond-the-kula stance (cf. the title). The two texts of the Matsyendra bundle thus stand in tension: KJN teaches the kula's observances; the Akulavīra dissolves them. Pass 2: keep this tension recorded — it is the lineage's own dialectic.]
