New Fragments of the Īśvarapratyabhijñā-vivr̥ti1

Yohei Kawajiri

Introduction

The Pratyabhijñā school is named after the work of Utpaladeva (c.
925-75) who is a disciple of Somānanda (c. 900-50), though Somānanda is
recognized as the founder of the Pratyabhijñā school. According to
Madhurāja (c. 1000-50) who was from south India and travelled to Kashmir
to become a disciple of Abhinavagupta (c. 975–1025), the following works
are regarded as the authoritative Pratyabhijñā texts:2

• Īśvarapratyabhijñā-kārikā (ĪPK) of Utpaladeva

• Īśvarapratyabhijñā-vr̥tti (ĪPVr̥) of Utpaladeva

• Īśvarapratyabhijñā-vivr̥ti/ṭīkā (ĪPViv) of Utpaladeva

• Īśvarapratyabhijñā-vimarśinī (ĪPV) of Abhinavagupta

• Īśvarapratyabhijñā-vivr̥ti-vimarśinī (ĪPVV) of Abhinavagupta

The ĪPViv is a commentary on the ĪPK and ĪPVr̥, both of which are written
at the same time. The ĪPV and the ĪPVV are commentaries on the ĪPK and
the ĪPViv, respectively. Among these, the ĪPViv is only extant in a
fragmentary form. After Torella published an article on the ĪPViv in
1988, he recently edited a part of the ĪPViv in his articles.3 His
edition is based on a transcript copied by K.C. Pandey himself and the
original of it. The original manuscript written in Śāradā script (Skt.
ms No. 30) was discovered in the National Archives of India, New Delhi.4
It contains the part of the ĪPViv relative to ĪPK1.3.6-1.5.3 and the
introduction to ĪPK 1.5.4-5.

At present, this is a unique manuscript of the ĪPViv, though so
fragmentary. Except this part of the ĪPViv edited by Torella, one can
glean some passages of the ĪPViv not only from the works of
Abhinavagupta and Kṣemarāja but also from the footnotes of the KSTS
edition of the ĪPV.5 According to Torella (2007c: 479, fn. 8), those
footnotes must have been in the margin of a manuscript of the ĪPV, that
given the siglum Gh in the edition of the ĪPV.6

In the course of search for the manuscripts of the Pratyabhijñā works, I
acquired photocopies of a manuscript of the ĪPK along with the ĪPVr̥ (ms
No. 4408) kept in the Akhila Bhāratīya Saṁskrit Parishad, Lucknow.7 This
manuscript is written in Devanāgarī script and contains marginal notes
here and there. I began examining them. Very soon, I realized that some
passages in the margin are from the ĪPViv, by means of collating them
with the already edited text of the ĪPViv, and expected that there would
be new fragments of the ĪPViv in the margin. With reference to this
manuscript, it is noteworthy that most of the passages in the margin are
from the Pratyabhijñā texts. A scribe of this manuscript seems to
confine himself to add his own comments.

In this paper, I will present new fragments of the ĪPViv. This paper is
devoted to the diplomatic edition of the new fragments of the ĪPViv
relative to ĪPK1.1.1-1.5.3.

Concluding Remark

We have seen:

1. in the manuscript of the ĪPK preserved in Lucknow, a number of
excerpts from the ĪPViv are found;

2. new fragments of the ĪPViv are found;

3. they are mainly from the avataraṇikā of the ĪPViv to the ĪPK; and

4. with reference to the part of the ĪPViv edited by Torella, the
fragments of this manuscript give not only the avataraṇikā but also part
of the commentary for the ĪPK and ĪPVr̥.

It is true that a part of the text of the ĪPViv has been transmitted in
north Indian manuscripts, but it is unlikely that the full text of the
ĪPViv has been transmitted there. At least, the scribe of this
manuscript did not have it. For, if he had known of it, he would have
given the part of the commentary for the ĪPK as well as the avataraṇikā
of the ĪPViv with reference to the part of the ĪPViv which are not
edited.

Nevertheless, what is important here is that we can glean the new
fragments of the ĪPViv from this manuscript. We should notice the
marginal notes of this manuscript not only from the aspect of gleaning
them but also from the aspect of the manuscript transmission of the
Pratyabhijñā texts. It is urgent that we should examine the rest of the
marginal notes in this manuscript.
