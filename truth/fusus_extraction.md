# Fusus al-Hikam — Key Extraction for the Aperture Model

## Source: Ibn al-Arabi, *Fusus al-Hikam* (The Bezels of Wisdom), tr. Abrahamov, 8701 lines.

---

## Part 1: Core Metaphysical Framework (from the Introduction)

### 1.1 Wahdat al-Wujud (Unity of Being) [T]

"True existence is one and it is God's Existence. Thus, God's Essence, attributes and names, and the cosmos including all its phenomena are one existence." (Introduction, p. 6)

**Key distinction:** God's Essence (dhat) cannot be known; His names can be known. The names are "neither Himself nor other than Himself" — they are relationships between God and His self-manifestation.

### 1.2 The Fixed Entities (A'yan Thabita) [T]

"The holy overflow is responsible for the production of the fixed entities, possible intelligible forms possessing potential power of becoming existents in the world, reminiscent of the Platonic ideas." (Ch.1, n.34)

Two overflows:
1. **Holy overflow (fayd aqdas):** Real's self-manifestation in the fixed entities (potential)
2. **Sacred overflow (fayd muqaddas):** His self-manifestation in concrete forms (actual)

### 1.3 The Hidden Treasure [T]

"God, the hidden treasure, desired to be known." (Introduction) — the motivation for creation is not need but love — the desire to be known.

### 1.4 Mirror Analogy [T]

"The cosmos was created as an unpolished mirror. Adam is the polishing." (Ch.1)

"The Perfect Human Being is placed in the universe to complete God's self-manifestation, because without him the mirror, that is, the cosmos, is not smooth and so things are not clearly seen in it." (Introduction, p. 4)

## Part 2: The Perfect Human (Al-Insan al-Kamil)

### 2.1 Adam as the First Perfect Human [T]

"The Real wanted to see the essences of His uncountable Most Beautiful Names... in an all-inclusive being containing all of them and qualified by existence. Through this being, His mystery will be revealed to Him." (Ch.1)

"The human being (insan) relates to the Real as the pupil (insan al-ayn) relates to the eye, and through the pupil seeing occurs. Hence, he is called insan, because through him the Real looks at His creation and has mercy on them."

**Key parallel:** This IS the Śaiva pūrṇāhantā (perfect I-hood) — the being in whom the absolute is fully reflected.

### 2.2 Muhammad as the Supreme Perfect Human [T]

"When Ibn al-Arabi speaks of Muhammad, he does not mean the historical prophet, but the spirit of Muhammad (al-haqiqa al-muhammadiyya), which preceded all existents." (Introduction, p. 7)

### 2.3 The Human Being as Both Created and Eternal [T]

"This human being is both created in time and is eternal, coming into being and living forever. He is both the separating and unifying principle. The world owes its existence to him." (Ch.1)

## Part 3: The Heart (Qalb) — Parallel to Vimarśa

### 3.1 The Heart Encompasses the Real [T]

"The heart — I mean the heart of the knower of God — derives from God's Mercy, but is more encompassing than Mercy, because the heart encompasses the Real, while His Mercy does not." (Ch.12)

### 3.2 Taqallub — Change as the Heart's Nature [T]

"The heart (qalb) derives its name from taqallub (change/flucutation), because it changes itself by way of various forms and attributes." (Ch.12)

### 3.3 Intellect Restricts; the Heart Expands [T]

"God did not say 'for whoever has an intellect' (in Qur'an 50:37), because the intellect means a limit and it restricts the matter to a single trait, and the reality denies restriction in the cosmos." (Ch.12)

**Key parallel:** This IS the Śaiva position that the intellect is a kañcuka (limitation). The heart (vimarśa) is the capacity that does not limit but encompasses change and fluctuation.

### 3.4 The Heart's Capacity for Endless Self-Disclosure [T]

"The forms of His self-manifestation have no end." The heart must be as unlimited as the self-disclosures it receives. "When one considers the Real without restriction, he does not deny Him, affirming Him in every form He manifests Himself."

**Key parallel:** This maps onto camatkāra as the ever-renewed wonder of recognition. The heart's capacity to receive endless forms IS vimarśa as camatkāra.
