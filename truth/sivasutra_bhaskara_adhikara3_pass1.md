### Time-place-context assessment
- **PERIOD:** third prakaraṇa of the Vārttika — the "vibhūti" (powers) section, the āṇava-path complement to the śāktopāya of adhikāra 2. The author's colophon states the whole Vārttika = 390 verses; his signature appears: "kāśmīrika-śrīmadācārya-bhaṭṭadivākarātmaja-bhaṭṭaśrībhāskara-viracite" — Bhāskara, son of Divākara, of Kashmir.
- **PLACE/PATH:** Kashmir, KSTS line. The e-text records multiple manuscript witnesses (k, kh, g = ga-pustaka) with readings noted inline — including one textual admission that a half-verse at 3.16 is filled from the "ga" manuscript. - **GENRE/REGISTER:** sūtra-commentary shifting register: much of adhikāra 3 is *practice-theology* (dhāraṇā, prāṇa, mudrā, japa, vrata) embedded in philosophical prose — the mantraśāstra/āgama register surfaces more here than in adhikāras 1–2.

# The vibhūti-path: opening (3.1–3.7)

## 3.1 — ātmā cittam
**The Self is mind.**
Vārttika: "Mind (citta) [here] is declared a synonym (paryāya) [of the Self]. In outwardness, its grasp is conceptualization (saṃkalpa); in inwardness, the self-thinking (svātmamanana) is mantra. Free of distraction in inwardness, shedding the contracted mind-state, it takes up its innate (sahaja) conscious self-nature. Then, by firmness, in the practice-state, free of dependence on the sense-functions, with immediate knowledge-and-action, it can — like Śiva — create a conceived object externally, visible to all, without regard to earth, water, fire and the rest."
Notes: the reversal of 3.1 vs 1.1 (caitanyam ātmā): here the Self IS mind — because citta is consciousness-in-descent (per 2.1's bracket). The saṃkalpa/mantra pair. [P: sahaja — the register's "innate" (glossary).]

## 3.2 — jñānaṃ bandhaḥ
**Knowledge is bondage.**
Vārttika: "Knowledge, from attachment to objects by passion (rāga) and the rest — the mind unshaken of rajas-and-tamas impurity, distracted — unable to obtain its own power by seizing its prior real nature, and lacking the capacity to destroy the covering of light, cannot fashion the common constructs; therefore attachment to objects itself is the root of the obstruction of free creation. The knowledge characterized by passion etc. through contact with objects — that itself is bondage, covering the mind's own nature. But by the practice of non-attachment within and without, when it is to-be-thought, he — the seer of it — is indeed free."
Notes: 3.2 repeats 1.2 verbatim as a sūtra but the Vārttika glosses differently: here the emphasis is the *obstructed-creativity* reading (jñāna as rāga-attachment) and the practice of non-attachment (anāsakty-abhyāsa) — the āṇava-path version of the doctrine. [D: the anchor renders both; pass 2 to compare the two glosses.]

## 3.3 — kalādīnāṃ tattvānām aviveko māyā
**Non-discrimination of the principles from kalā onwards is māyā.**
Vārttika (with the full tattva-catalog in verse, 3-3's kārikās): kalā = mere limited agency-capacity; vidyā = the corresponding limited knowing; rāga = the "this is desired" bondage; kāla = the kalana of time; niyati = the determinism of karma's fruit; puruṣa = the experiencer of pleasure/pain; guṇas (sattva/rajas/tamas) = the mind's modes; ahaṃkāra = the "I"-sense; manas = the director of the senses; buddhīndriyas = the sense-organs; karmendriyas = the organs of action; tanmātras = the mere-appearance-of-sound etc.; pāñcabhautika = the element-body. "Their non-discrimination (aviveka) is māyā, the deluder: by māyā the atoms, made to grasp the limited-subject state within the kañcuka-puryaṣṭaka-bhautika bodies woven of this tattva-web, cover the ever-risen self-luminous seer-nature. For those resting in the practice of discrimination, however, māyā's domain is transcended."
Notes: the tattva-catalog (kalā → earth) = the impure creation; viveka (discrimination) as the māyā-transcending practice. [P: aviveka/viveka pair — 1.10 (aviveko māyāsauṣuptam) now receives its full catalog; the same māyā-kañcuka vocabulary as TĀ 9 (our reference stack).]

## 3.4 — śarīre saṃhāraḥ kalānām
**In the body is the withdrawal of the kalās.**
Vārttika: "The kalās are declared to be the own-capacities of the principles; their aggregate is the body. By the contemplation of the dissolution of the body's parts into their respective causes, in reverse order (prātilomyena), culminating in the conscious self that supports all — there arises the body of supreme awareness (parabodhadeha). Alternatively: by the practice of overriding (ullaṅghana) all functions forcibly — one-pointedness free of constructs — one attains the lordship of the construct-free consciousness. Thus the seed of perfection, sprouting from the withdrawal of the kalā-expanse that is the covering of one's own nature, flashes forth."
Notes: prātilomya (reverse-order dissolution) = the āṇava path's core technique; the two readings (dissolution-contemplation vs function-overriding) are both offered by the author. [P: the tattva-laya doctrine of KJN paṭala 2 (our Kaula file: "śivamadhye gatā śaktiḥ...") — same ladder, same terminus.]

## 3.5 — nāḍīsaṃhāra-bhūtajaya-bhūtakaivalya-bhūtapṛthaktvāni
**The withdrawal of the channels, the mastery of the elements, the isolation of the elements, the separations of the elements.**
Vārttika: "In this material body, by the Lord's freedom, [the yogī has] sovereignty, like a householder over his house. The withdrawal of the channels (nāḍīsaṃhāra) is [attained] by the dissolution of the breaths into the space of consciousness — the mouth of all the channels. The winds depend on the channels; their support is the all-pervading consciousness. Element-mastery (bhūtajaya): contemplating the stability/fluidity/heat/motion/porosity and their opposites in the elements, the awakened Lord masters them. Element-isolation (bhūtakaivalya): their negation — isolation without adjuncts. Element-separations (bhūtapṛthaktva): the Lord, by the touch of his power, makes each element separate, joined or otherwise."
Notes: the four siddhi-verbs of the sūtra; "like a householder over his house" — the gṛhapati image (cf. 3.40's saṃvāhya). [P: the dhāraṇā-technique (holding the elements with their seed-syllables at the cakra-stations) matches the KJN 3/3–3/9 cakra-arithmetic — same practice-family, Trika phrasing.]

## 3.6 — mohāvaraṇāt siddhiḥ
**From the covering of delusion, [even] the siddhis.**
Vārttika: "From authentic reflection, the appearance of siddhis — dwelling on the lower ground — belongs to the province of māyā; it covers the yogi's own nature. But when — for their attainment — there arises in that state the surge of excitement, desire and the rest, [which obscures] the self, then by fixing the mind on the prior or final limit, the supreme perfection characterized by omniscience and the rest arises."
Notes: the doubled reading: siddhis born of māyā's covering (the allure) vs the perfection born of crossing the covering. The kāmakrodhalobhaharṣabhayatrāsa list (desire, anger, greed, joy, fear, terror).

## 3.7 — mohajayād anantābhogāt sahajavidyājayaḥ
**From the conquest of delusion — the infinite enjoyment — the conquest of innate vidyā.**
Vārttika: "From the conquest of the many-branched delusion, the fully-awakened one's uncontrived vidyā arises — the consciousness of the full "I" (pūrṇāhaṃvimarśa). Or: from the conquest of delusion, dwelling in the pure-vidyā station governed by the Rudra-knower named Ananta (the Infinite), he attains the innate light (sahajāloka)." [bracket: "in the pure-vidyā station the sovereignty is enjoyed on all sides by the knower named Infinite" — lyablope, ablative reading.]
Notes: the two readings (pūrṇāhaṃvimarśa vs the Ananta-Rudra station); sahaja-vidyā — the prakaraṇa 2's title-word returns.

# The awakened one's play (3.8–3.13)

## 3.8 — jāgrad dvitīyakaraḥ
**Waking is the second hand [of the knower].**
Vārttika: "'Knowledge is waking' was taught [in 1.8]; by it, surveying its own domain, [the yogī] gathers everything into one — then, free of delusion, he always stands awakened, a lord of yogins, free of division, like a hand within a hand — hence "second": [the bracket glosses] the sūtras are many-faced (viśvatomukha): previously 1.8 was expounded with knowledge as anuvāda; now with knowledge as vidheya — the waking-knowledge as the *gathering* (ādāna) activity — the world, illumined by form, color, mind-stuff, is surveyed as non-different and gathered into a single awareness."
Notes: viśvatomukhatva (many-facedness) of the sūtras — the author's own hermeneutic principle: the same sūtra, different emphases. [P: this is the same interpretive principle as our three-readings method — the tradition itself acknowledges re-reading.]

## 3.9 — nartaka ātmā
**The Self is the dancer.**
Vārttika: "As a mature actor (prauḍhanaṭa), skilled in the rasas through feeling (rasabhāvanā), endowed with voice, gesture, and the satvika-states, rightly follows [the rasa], so the Self, flashing in its own nature, flashes everywhere — through absorption in the rasa of each being, adorned with the play of each activity — it is called the dancer, since it takes up every state. [bracket:] In this play, reeling with the rasa of supreme bliss like a mature actor — knowing the rasa, the bhāva, their abhāsa — entering the vessel of the limited subject (mitamātṛtā), manifesting the imitated rasa and states through bodily gesture without forgetting itself — performing the world-play through the ordered motion of the senses, without desire for gain or aversion to loss — it plays, delighted, its seeing-power unlost, everywhere self-shining."
Notes: THE aesthetic-metaphysical core of the adhikāra: the actor-image (from our glossary) as the full doctrine of the awakened one. [P: naṭa/naṭavat — now four attestations in Bhāskara (1.2, 2.9, 3.9) + our Siddhitrayī. camatkāra/rasa vocabulary — same aesthetic register as the Spanda Vivṛti's maṅgala we translated.]

## 3.10 — raṅgo'ntarātmā
**The stage is the inner Self.**
Vārttika: "The Self whose fortune is outward-opening (bahirunmeṣa) is called the dancer; its inner closing (antarnimeṣa) is proclaimed the inner Self. That itself is called the stage (raṅga) because it is the support of the eightfold subtle body (puryaṣṭaka): the place where the manifold roles are taken up — on which, taking the stations of human, animal, and other bodies, the conscious Lord performs the five acts (pañcakṛtya) of creation and the rest through the functioning of the instruments — there the all-pervading consciousness dances with creation, persistence, withdrawal."
Notes: puryaṣṭaka (the eight-member subtle body: the five sense-capacities + manas + ahaṃkāra + buddhi) — the stage on which the Lord dances. [P: puryaṣṭaka — cf. 1.2's bracket (Mīmāṃsaka's puryaṣṭaka) and 3.41-44.]

## 3.11 — prekṣakāṇīndriyāṇi
**The senses are the spectators.**
Vārttika: "Because the supreme conscious self is independent, the senses move in their own functions like spectators (prekṣakā) of the all-pervading consciousness's dance — and they cannot cover its nature. [bracket:] The independent conscious self alone illumines the instruments; everything shines by its light; without it the world would be blind, deaf, dumb. The instruments, enlivened by it, revealing the undivided self, offer the yogi — like a connoisseur-spectator (sahṛdayaprekṣaka) — the wonder-taste of the nectar of supreme experience, so that the wheel of his senses does not, as with the bound man, rotate him in the world of suffering."
Notes: the aesthetic-theatrical triad complete: dancer (3.9), stage (3.10), spectators (3.11). sahṛdayaprekṣaka — the "connoisseur-spectator" of rasa-theory — the yogi as the ideal audience of the world-play. [P: the rasa-theory register connects to our truth/rasa work and the aesthetic tradition.]

## 3.12 — dhīvaśāt sattvasiddhiḥ
**By the power of the intellect, the attainment of reality.**
Vārttika: "The intellect (buddhi), settling on the objects of sound and the rest as non-different from the conscious nature — by the tradition-taught reasons — and, its lingering impurities destroyed, free of the stain of rajas-tamas activity, seeing the self-luminous light everywhere — produces the yogi's establishment in the shining of reality (sattva)." [variant k: sattvaśuddhi (purification); kh: sattvabhitti (foundation).]
Notes: dhī (intellect) as the instrument of the āṇava path; sattva = reality (with the variant readings noted).

## 3.13 — siddhaḥ svatantrabhāvaḥ
**The state of freedom is accomplished.**
Vārttika: "His freedom is accomplished in the manner taught — by which the whole world from Śiva to the earth is borne, seen, illumined, and held in his own power. This free-state, of the nature of innate knowing-and-doing (sahajajñatvakartṛtva), tames everything: as by the perfected alchemical herb (rāsāyana) iron becomes gold, so by the contemplation of the Śiva-nature everything, contemplated, stands in his power."
Notes: the alchemical image (rāsāyana — gold-transmutation); sahaja again.

# The body and its powers (3.14–3.18)

## 3.14 — yathā tatra tathānyatra
**As there, so elsewhere.**
Vārttika: "As omniscience and the rest flash in the body over which the Self presides, so everywhere — by the power of his own support [the knower's] omniscience and the rest flash unobstructed in the bodies deemed 'other' (parakīya)."
Notes: the sūtra's claim: the awakened one's powers are not body-bound. [The author's added sūtra follows:]

## 3.14bis — visargasvābhāvyād abahiḥsthitestatsthitiḥ
**From the naturalness of emission, of one dwelling outside, his state [follows].**
Vārttika: "From the creation-nature (sṛṣṭisvabhāva) of the conscious form, of the vibration (spanda) born with the body of time through the kalana of activity, there is no birth — from the kalana of that construct-born one — of [the Lord] dwelling outside the [world], his state [is the akāla-pada, the timeless station]. [bracket:] The Lord, whose nature is the will-to-create, bearing the object-to-be-created as arisen within himself, the ground, contemplated as non-different from himself, never forgets himself; even in māyā's domain the object's manifestation, being interior to the supreme light, shines externally by his freedom. Therefore the tattva-known one's conditioning by the conditioned (dharmidharmopahitatā) never overcomes him."
Notes: [X] — the author states this sūtra is absent from the Vimarśinī ("vimarśinīgranthe na vidyate, etadgranthe eva granthakṛtā saṃgṛhya vyākhyātam") — Bhāskara's own addition to the sūtra-text, flagged as such. The akālapada (timeless station) — the terminus of the body-powers cluster.

## 3.15 — bījāvadhānam
**The fixing of the seed.**
Vārttika: "The seed of the entire world is the conscious self, the supreme; the fixing (avadhāna) is the reflection on it with attentive mind (sāvadhānena cetasā). By that the timeless station is reached, from the abandonment of the grasp of delusion and the rest. [bracket:] The removal of the limited-I contemplation, then the unbroken firm practice of the mind's fixing in the power-station (śāktapada) that is the cause of the universe — that is the means of attaining the time-free nature."
Notes: bīja = the conscious self as seed; avadhāna = the fixing-practice. [P: bindu/bīja vocabulary — cf. KJN 3/20's bindu, ŚS 2.7's bindu-visarga; same seed-cosmology.]

## 3.16 — āsanasthaḥ sukhaṃ hrade nimajjati
**Seated in the posture, he sinks happily into the lake.**
Vārttika: "For knowers, practicing with the effort proper to the means of the conscious self's entry — even in the kumbhaka (retention) that cuts both currents, by the recaka-stream that thrusts up the pure fire born of the sun-moon collision (udāna) — leaping past the stations below up to the dvādaśānta (twelve-finger space) — resting there, he sinks without effort into the ocean of the Śiva-consciousness-nectar at the dvādaśānta, its waves the multiform world. From the continuous savoring of that rasa, the attainment of the unmeasured supreme power-station, the unbroken freedom within and without, is accomplished."
Notes: the practice-detail (kumbhaka/recaka/udāna/dvādaśānta) — the haṭha-layer of the āṇava path. The "lake" (hrada) — cf. 1.23's mahāhrada (the great lake) of the first adhikāra. [X: the e-text has a data-entry artifact at this sūtra (a Hindi scribble "vyākhyā kahā kā hai nahīpatā") — the printed KSTS 4 must be checked.]

## 3.17 — svamātrā nirmāṇam āpādayati
**By his own measure he brings creation into being.**
Vārttika: "The Lord's own power (svā śaktiḥ), characterized by knowing-and-doing, is his strength, the cause of the arising of beings; hence she is the mother of the world (jaganmātā), supreme, of the powerful one. By her, his own measure, he brings creation into being as pleases him — quickly, by the force of his freedom. [bracket:] From the cultivation of the two means — fixing (avadhāna) and entry (praveśa) — the resting in that station, he is able to create the object, flashing with unprecedented features, by his own power whose truth is agency and the rest; that creation itself signals the resting in the abode of freedom."
Notes: svamātrā (his own measure — the goddess as the measure of creation); jaganmātā — the mother-goddess as the creative power. [P: the goddess-as-power doctrine — cf. our Kālīkula files (Brahmāṇī creates, not Brahmā — Kubjikā 1.25-28); same theology, Trika phrasing.]

## 3.18 — vidyāvināśe janmavināśaḥ
**In the destruction of vidyā, the destruction of birth.**
Vārttika: "The vidyā that is conditioned (sopādhikā), object-directed — that is the impure vidyā, the cause of rebirth. Its destruction is the contemplation of the arising of innate vidyā (sahajavidyodaya-nibhālana). With it, the supreme freedom of the self is manifested; hence no further birth, resting on one's own power. This is liberation-while-living (jīvanmukti), the siddhi that gives the timeless station. [bracket:] The vṛtti-knowledge, object-shaped, is called impure vidyā; it binds. By its opposite, the pure vidyā's contemplation, one gains the innate awareness; with the gain of one's own power, the karma-bondage — the body-instruments aggregate assisted by the āṇava and māyīya impurities — is dissolved."
Notes: jīvanmukti — [P: the term of our Kālīkula files (Kubjikā 1.15/1.20: jīvanmukto dhruvaṃ naraḥ) — now in the Trika's own voice: liberation-by-vidyā-destruction, not by hearing.]

# The matrix and the phonemes (3.19–3.24)

## 3.19 — kavargādiṣu māheśvaryādyāḥ paśumātaraḥ
**In the ka-varga and the rest, Māheśvarī and the others are the mothers of the bound.**
Vārttika: "The previously declared body of the sound-mass (śabdarāśi), made of the kalās, two-formed as seed and yoni (bījayoniprabheda): the seed is Śiva himself; the yoni is the power called māyā — the eight groups from ka to kṣa. The eight mothers (māheśvarī etc.) — presiding over them — generate for the bound ones attachment to mixed karma-fruit (the ghorā mark); for those whose minds are attached to objects, they cast down lower and lower; ghoratara; but aghorā gives Śiva. [bracket:] The supreme speech-power (parā vākśakti), one with Śiva, manifesting the triad of will and the rest, unfolding the mātṛkā in the form of the classes (varga) and their members, in the bound knowers' constructed-cognition state, effects their identity with the body and the rest through passion, hatred, desire, greed — hence the mothers, named ghorā and the rest, cast the bound down, self-alienated, by attachment to objects; for the Lords (pati), they offer the rasa of the self's unfolding with wonder — never producing the perception of difference."
Notes: [P: the māheśvarī-mothers = the eight mātṛkās of the alphabet-groups (kavarga etc.) — the same eightfold as our ŚS 1.3's kalās and the mātṛkā-doctrine of 2.7; the bīja/yoni pair of the alphabet — the seed (Śiva) and yoni (māyā-power) — yoni again as śakti, per our glossary. The ghorā/aghora pair = 1.4's doctrine.]

## 3.20 — triṣu caturthaṃ tailavad āsecyam
**In the three, the fourth is to be poured like oil.**
Vārttika: "Among the three phoneme-forms taught (the mother-divisions), whatever remains — the fourth — is the supreme Śiva. It is to be contemplated (anusaṃdheya), poured (āsecya) into the three like oil into water. [bracket:] By the mothers named ghorā/aghora and the rest, the gross-subtle phonemic constructs are presided over; the pouring of the rasa of consciousness (cidrasa) at their beginning and end — the contemplation of the consciousness-essence as pervading, spreading further and further like oil — dissolves the perception of difference."
Notes: tailavadāsecya — the oil-pouring image (pervasion); cidrasa — the consciousness-taste; the "fourth in the three" = the Śiva-principle in the phonemes.

## 3.21 — magnaḥ svacitte praviśet
**Drowned [in the world], he should enter his own mind.**
Vārttika: "The yogī, desiring to leave the outward "this"-tendency, withdrawing the object-flavored function, should — by the rule of the burning coal (aṅgāravahninyāya) — enter the phonemes with construct-free awareness. With the perfection of entry into the phonemes, by the Bhairava-mudrā, even the enlivening of mantras is accomplished."
Notes: aṅgāravahninyāya — the coal-fire rule (entering the inner heat); the Bhairava-mudrā.

## 3.22 — madhye'varaprasavaḥ
**In the middle is the inferior birth.**
Vārttika: "Of the uttered phoneme, the three parts — beginning, middle, end: in the beginning and end the Śiva-nature stands (the will-to-rise and the rest); in the middle is the birth — the falling (bhraṃśa) — of the bound one, [who dwells] in the mere phoneme; for the Lord, the flashing power-nature is taught. [bracket:] In the will-to-utter and its cessation, the beginning-and-end limits, the phonemes of the middle-speech (madhyamā) rest in paśyantī — the undivided station — the effortless Śiva-nature; in the middle limit, their manifold forms flash — the falling away from construct-free awareness."
Notes: the three-limbs of the phoneme (initial/medial/final) as the map of bondage and freedom — the phonology-theology of the adhikāra. [P: the speech-levels (paśyantī/madhyamā) of 2.3's bracket — the doctrine recurs in its own turn.]

## 3.23 — prāṇasamācāre samadarśanam
**In the movement of the prāṇa, equal vision.**
Vārttika: "The supreme own-capacity of the form of all-knowledge-and-action — that is the prāṇa named paranāda (the supreme sound), able to enliven all. Its movement (samācāra) is the absorption flashing in phonemes, words, atoms; by it the non-difference-experience — equal vision (samadarśana) — is attained. [bracket:] Absorbed in the independent light called paranāda — the life of all lights — the words and mantras that have spread to vaikharī, by firm practice of the experience of non-difference, gain the supreme power."
Notes: samadarśana — [P: the Bhagavadgītā's term (sama-darśana — equal vision, BG 6.29-32) — here in the Śaiva register: non-difference-experience. paranāda — the supreme sound — the prāṇa as sound. The four-speech complex again (vaikharī here).]

## 3.24 — mātrāsvapratyayasaṃdhāne naṣṭasya punarutthānam
**In the union of the measures with one's own awareness, the re-arising of what was lost.**
Vārttika: "The tattva-kalās from māyā to the earth [bracket: the material causes of the element-body], made one with the consciousness-rasa by the union of the awareness of identity with oneself — then the body of being (bhāvaśarīra) also rises, made of the dense rasa of consciousness-and-bliss; gaining the power of the stations of all-agency, he sits in the supreme lordship of complete knowing-and-doing everywhere. The measures (mātrā) [are] the manifestations, and the awareness (pratyaya) in each domain — the filling of each by its own awareness is the union (saṃdhāna); in it, the re-arising of the lost being and the rest. Thus wherever he makes the union of measure-and-awareness, there agency is his."
Notes: mātrā = the manifested measures (the tattva-kalās); pratyaya = awareness; their saṃdhāna (union) = the resurrection of the world as consciousness. [P: saṃdhāna — cf. 1.6's śakticakrasaṃdhāne; the term's two faces: the powers' self-union and the world's re-union.]

# The vrata and japa (3.25–3.28)

## 3.25 — śivatulyo jāyate
**He becomes equal to Śiva.**
Vārttika: "Because Śiva stands at the head of the whole family of causes, ever the adjunct-free light by nature — he, the ever-joined (nityayukta), equal to him, doing even the [ordinary] moving, is a liberated-while-living sādhaka, by the previously taught knowledge-and-power. [bracket:] The one who has won that station by the surpassing cultivation of the Śiva-nature — despite the body's presence, tasting the joy of liberation-while-living, he is compared to Śiva; the embodied liberation is reached after the exhaustion of the prārabdha — then identity itself."
Notes: śivatulya (equal to Śiva — not identical — the bracket: "tulya" comparison until death); jīvanmukti again; prārabdha (the ripening karma).

## 3.26 — śarīravṛttir vratam
**The body's functioning is the vow.**
Vārttika (the full kārikā-list): the skull (kapāla), the skeleton (kaṅkāla), the staff (daṇḍa), the five mudrās, the ash (bhasma) as the supreme radiance, the three guṇas as the sacred thread, the flag (dhvajikā) as the great path, the senses as ornaments, the wanderings in the world as the haunt, the constant attachment to the burning ground of the heart (hṛcchmaśāna) — "whoever's movement is the festival of the Hero-Lord (vīreśasyotsava) — this is to be known as the great vow (mahāvrata), of the unbarred own-state. [bracket:] The pāśupata-vrata: the external accoutrements — skull, staff, mudrās, ash, thread, flag, ornaments, dwelling, wandering — used as unmanifest matter; but manifesting the marks (vyaktaliṅgitā), one becomes a dharma-hypocrite, smeared by the stain."
Notes: the pāśupata-vrata interiorized: the body itself is the vow; the external Kapālika apparatus rejected in favor of the inner "burning ground of the heart." [P: the vīreśa/Hero-Lord term — cf. 1.11's tritayabhoktā vīreśaḥ; the pāśupata lineage background of the Śaiva tradition.]

## 3.27 — kathā japaḥ
**Talk is japa.**
Vārttika: "Whatever the awakened one, mounted on the agent-ground (kartṛdharā), utters — that is japa for the vow-holder; he is the supreme man from japa. The japa with the support of power — utterance and movement (uccārasaṃcāra) — is called kathā. Thus the fourfold: śākta, haṃsa, paudgala [and the niṣkala]. [bracket:] The niṣkala (partless) as the praṇava-member; the haṃsa of the nature of the nāda-kalā; the jīva-named, breath-formed, carrying the 1,000 + 600 [breaths] in unbroken stream — the fourfold mantras (niṣkala, haṃsa, paudgala, śākta) revolving in the self-deity-reflection, never cut off."
Notes: the fourfold japa (śākta/haṃsa/paudgala/niṣkala); kathā (talk) as japa — the awakened one's speech is mantra (cf. 2.7's "whatever he utters flows as mantra"). [X: the haṃsa-count ("sahasraṃ ṣaṭśatāni" — 1600) — the breath-count of the haṃsa-mantra; variants noted.]

## 3.28 — dānam ātmajñānam
**The gift is self-knowledge.**
Vārttika: "The making-known of the self's nature — the supreme knowledge, the perfect manifestation of what was taught and will be taught — that is the supreme gift (dāna) to those awakened by grace (śaktipāta). From it, the perfect destruction (kṣapaṇa) of the bonds of the bound. The word dīkṣā is taught here as meaning 'giving-destruction' (dāna-kṣapaṇa)."
Notes: the etymology dīkṣā = dāna + kṣapaṇa — initiation as the gift that destroys bonds. [P: dīkṣā-terminology — cf. our Kaula and Kālīkula files' transmission frame; the "gift of knowledge" as the guru's act.]

# The cosmology of the free (3.29–3.33)

## 3.29 — yo'vipastho jñāhetuśca
**He who stands in the womb [of the universe] and is the cause of knowledge.**
Vārttika: "By wombship (yonitva) the supreme Lord stands in the world; of the nature of knowledge-and-action, he is the cause of the making-nothing of the despicable. Hence the yogī, standing in the world-form station, knowledge-led, is the supreme cause of enjoyment and liberation. [bracket with the alternative readings:] the Lord as the cause of the universe... or: the yogī, having mounted the agent-ground, can awaken those to be taught by his knowledge-power; or: in the bound-knowers' stations, among the māheśvarī-mother-groups, the Self flashes as the lord of their wheel (cakreśvara); the mothers, his rays, ever worship him by harvesting the essence of objects — thus the time of the celebration of the power (śāktollāsamaya)."
Notes: yoni again — "wombship" as the Lord's relation to the world (our glossary's yoni = source, now with the Lord as the source); the cakreśvara reading ties to the mothers of 3.19.

## 3.30 — svaśaktipracayo'sya viśvam
**The accumulation of his own powers is the world.**
Vārttika: "The powers of the powerful one, his own consciousness and the rest — their accumulations (pracaya), the ever-fresh flashing vibrations (navanavollāsaspanda) — those are to be known as the world: by the power-nature the Lord flashes as the world, flashing always in himself. [bracket:] As Śiva's world is his own supreme power, so the yogī, equal to Śiva, has the world as the unfolding of his own awareness-power, the vibration of the ever-fresh power of action."
Notes: the world as the *accumulation* of the powers — the vibhūti doctrine's cosmology: creation = the powers' agglomeration. [P: the spanda-vibration vocabulary (navanavollāsa — ever-fresh flashing) — cf. the Spanda Vivṛti's visphurati.]

## 3.31 — sthitilayau
**Persistence and dissolution.**
Vārttika: "By those same power-accumulations, of the form of the function's flashing, the conscious self performs persistence and dissolution by its own will. [bracket:] Persistence: the illumination of the world resting on him, in the time-measures of the station-lords, while outward-facing; dissolution: the closing (nimeṣa) of the higher stations — the resting of the conscious knower — both are his own power-accumulations: what is closed and opened is of the nature of the awareness-power."
Notes: [P: nimeṣa/unmeṣa — the pulse-pair of 1.5-1.6, now as cosmology; the "power-accumulations" as the world's persistence and dissolution.]

## 3.32 — tatpravṛttāv apyanirāsaḥ saṃvettṛbhāvāt
**Even in their activity there is no cancellation, because of knowership.**
Vārttika: "In the activity of creation and the rest, the all-pervading consciousness never abandons its own state — from knowership (saṃvettṛbhāva); non-cancellation (anirāsa) is non-loss, the qualification of knowership; thus the states come and go but the state-holder never. [bracket:] Even in the emergence of creation, the own-nature abides — from the knowership consisting in the reflection of the Fourth's wonder (turyacamatkāra) — never cast out. The five acts (pañcakṛtya): the conscious self, performing them, revealing its own lordship in each act's flashing, ever-risen as the wonderer of the beginning-and-end of things that perish — this shows its witness-nature: the states revolve, the witness does not perish."
Notes: anirāsa — the non-cancellation of the witness; [P: pañcakṛtya (the five acts) — cf. 3.10's bracket; turyacamatkāra — the Fourth's wonder — the aesthetic term in the epistemological claim.]

## 3.33 — sukhāsukhayor bahirmananam
**The thinking of pleasure and pain [is] outward.**
Vārttika: "The bliss-function arising from the touch of the world's dearest objects; the unbliss-function of their failure — the twofold, manifold (ādhyātmika and the rest); both, even when they arise, by the firmness of reflection, are experienced like blue-and-the-other [objects] without the I-and-mine — not covering the self-nature, without the bound one's touch of egohood. The outward-thinking (bahirmanana) of pleasure and pain... the eightfold prakṛti: tamaḥ-moha (self-identity with the body), mahāmoha (the outer — son and the rest — as self), tāmisra (anger at the family's harmers), andhatāmisra (the terror of death)."
Notes: the Sāṅkhya-tainted list of the four darknesses (moha, mahāmoha, tāmisra, andhatāmisra — the 8 prakṛti-forms of delusion from the purāṇic/Sāṅkhya tradition); the "like blue-and-the-other" — the perception of the world without egohood.

# The freedom-claims (3.34–3.38)

## 3.34 — tadvimuktastu kevalī
**But freed from that, he is the solitary (kevalin).**
Vārttika: "Freed from those two — pleasure and pain — and from the deluded state (mūḍhabhāva), the Lord, solitary (kevala), self-abiding, by the pervasion of all: when the thinking of pleasure and the rest no longer enters within, untouched by them and by the delusion they breed, with the lossless glory of the self, resting in the sole pure knower-form — the yogī tastes the fruit of yoga."
Notes: kevalī — [P: the Jain-derived term (kevalin) repurposed in the Śaiva register — "the solitary" = the one who rests alone in consciousness.]

## 3.35 — mohapratisaṃhatas tu karmātmā
**But he who is compacted with delusion is the karma-self.**
Vārttika: "Delusion (moha) — stupidity, non-exertion, ignorance (avidyā), dullness, covering — the synonyms: when, by the avidyā-synonym delusion, he is continuously pervaded, having manifested the I-identity with the body and the rest, he performs the mixed good-evil karmas, denying the whole sky-form self, yearning for the varied karma-fruits, wandering through the many births of men and the rest. [bracket:] the karma-self, the saṃsārin, the paśu."
Notes: [P: paśu — our glossary's bound one; the karma-self triad (karmātmā, saṃsārī, paśu) — the paśu-doctrine of 1.2 and 2.9's brackets culminates.]

## 3.36 — bhedatiraskāre sargāntarakarmatvam
**In the cancellation of difference, agency in another creation.**
Vārttika: "The self, awakened (prabuddha) — everything being known, nothing distinct exists — by the yoga of the unwinding (udveṣṭana) of the karma-bonds, mounted on the agent-ground, not deliberating anything beyond the supreme Śiva-nature, rejects the perception of difference rooted in body-egohood and the rest; with the unshaken state of the return to own-nature, having gained the great power, he can create unprecedented creations (apūrvasargān) decorated with ever-fresh features, without crossing his own will."
Notes: sargāntara (another creation) — the free creation of new worlds; [P: apūrvollekha (ever-fresh feature) — the navanavollāsa of 3.30; the creativity-doctrine connects to our truth/ work on svātantrya and imagination.]

## 3.37 — karaṇaśaktiḥ svato'nubhavāt
**The power of the instruments [is] from the self's experience.**
Vārttika: "The power in the instruments for the production of another creation — that is established from the self's experience, resting on the uncontrived strength (akṛtrimabala). [bracket:] As even worms, in the free constructs of dream, are seen to have the capacity to create unshared objects — gardens, forests, mountains, ponds, cities — so the yogī, by deepest absorption, resting on the uncontrived power, mounted on that ground, creates whatever work he wishes by the instrument-power. The previously stated means of producing the world's variety is here re-stated to show the many kinds of siddhis."
Notes: the worm-dream argument (svānubhava — the self's own experience as evidence); [P: karaṇaśakti + akṛtrima — the natural/uncrafted pair of our glossary.]

## 3.38 — tripadādyanuprāṇanam
**The enlivening by the three-fold starting with the states.**
Vārttika: "The Fourth (turya), having the three states (jāgrat and the rest) as its beginning (tripadādi), is the fullness; by it the enlivening (anuprāṇana) of the beings — the giving of one's own power. [bracket:] By the service of the taught means, the Fourth — the dense consciousness-and-bliss, the Śiva-principle, the enlivener of the three states — when it is attained, in the state of ordinary dealing, the desired objects — songs and the rest — are tasted with the inward reflection, and the knower-principle, unobscured, flashes like lightning."
Notes: tripadādi (that whose beginning is the three states); [P: the Fourth (turya) — cf. 1.7's turyābhoga; the lightning-image (vidyudvat) of the flashing awareness.]

# The closing path (3.39–3.45)

## 3.39 — cittasthitivaccharīrakaraṇabāhyeṣu
**As the mind's state, so in body, instruments, and externals.**
Vārttika: "The mind's own-state — its stillness, its non-loss of nature — [bracket: in outwardness the mind-form contracted; in the return to the dense consciousness-bliss nature by the means; even the constructed body and the rest do not leave the true nature; in the prior state everything flashes like a dream-whim, hardening in outwardness; in reality, within and without, it is the flashing of the self-nature, solidified as this-and-that, dissolving again into the consciousness-rasa] — as that, in the body, in the instrument-aggregate, in the external objects — wherever he makes the construction, there the flashing [occurs]."
Notes: the "as-the-mind's-state-so-everywhere" claim — the realized one's perception of the world as the self-nature's modes (the mirror-image of 3.14's "as there, so elsewhere").

## 3.40 — abhilāṣād bahirgatiḥ saṃvāhyasya
**From longing, the going-out of the conveyed one.**
Vārttika: "The will (icchā) arising from the surging of the self's power toward the filling of activity — that is longing (abhilāṣa); from it, of the conveyed one (saṃvāhya) — the instrument-aggregate — the going-out (bahirgati), the movement with activity, impelled by the self's power. [bracket with the second reading:] The one conveyed (saṃvāhya) among the stations of the powers Khecarī, Gocarī, Dikcarī, Bhūcarī — the karma-self, the paśu, conveyed from one yoni to another; from his bondage to the āṇavamala named longing (the root of the mass of bonds), abandoning the experience of own-nature, he goes out into the object-world — the covering of own-nature, the outward movement of the conveyed one; in its thickening, the delusion-function, resting on the Bhūcarī-power-wheel, conveys the bound ones."
Notes: [P: Khecarī/Gocarī/Dikcarī/Bhūcarī — the four carī-powers of 1.5's Vārttika bracket and the TĀ's expansions — now the full set as the machinery of conveyance; abhilāṣa (longing) as the root-bond — the passion-theology of the paśu.]

## 3.41 — tadārūḍhapramitestatkṣayājjīvasaṃkṣayaḥ
**For him whose knowing is mounted on that, from its destruction, the destruction of the jīva.**
Vārttika: "In the arising of longing, the prior limit is the seer-ground (draṣṭṭadharā); resting there, [the yogī] is awakened, knowing the supreme. When he crosses the seer-ground by the power of his own strength's exertion, he — of risen knowledge — is called the awakened knower. His knowing (pramiti) — the self-awareness-knowledge — when he is mounted on it, even then the destruction of longing occurs. From its destruction, the destruction of the one named jīva — the karma-self. [bracket:] The grace arisen from the accumulation of merit: he, mounted on the seer-ground, tasting the self-nature-joy — the inner-outer world illumined by the rays of his own awareness, from the kañcuka up to the tanmātras — when he abandons the longing for the bonds, at once the conveyed self's eightfold body (puryaṣṭaka) is stilled."
Notes: the three grounds (draṣṭṛ — seer; the jīva/karma-self destruction); [P: puryaṣṭaka — the stage of 3.10 — now the conveyed one's body, stilled; the saṃvāhya-terminology culminates.]

## 3.42 — bhūtakañcukī tadā vimukto bhūyaḥ patisamaḥ paraḥ
**Clad in the element-covering, then released, again equal to the Lord, supreme.**
Vārttika: "The five great elements are bonds — the covering (kañcuka); he is the element-clad (bhūtakañcukī). Unreleased = bound, in the manner previously told. Mounted on the ground of all-knowing and all-doing, he is equal to the Lord, supreme — no doubt, from the full self-experience. [bracket:] With the destruction of the longing-impurity, even while the connection with the material body appears, by the firm mounting on the all-agent-knower ground, experiencing the supreme consciousness-bliss-dense Śiva-nature, he is like Śiva — not Śiva himself, since identity is reached only after the body's fall."
Notes: the patisama (equal to the Lord) — the sūtra's claim and its bracket-limitation (tulya, not identical — cf. 3.25).

## 3.43 — naisargikaḥ prāṇasaṃbandhaḥ
**The connection with the prāṇa is innate.**
Vārttika: "The prāṇa-connection of the element-body — that is innate (naisargika): the prāṇa is prāṇamaya (made of life). [bracket:] For the one risen to that awareness, even with the certain fall of the element-covering, the prāṇa-puryaṣṭaka connection is admitted as innate: consciousness itself, flashing as the world by the contraction-appearance, taking the grasping-ground of enlivening, wandering through the yonis of men, beasts, gods — till the prārabdha is exhausted, it does not leave the prāṇa-and-element-covering, like the wheel's continued turning after the stick is removed."
Notes: the "wheel after the stick" image (daṇḍāpagame cakrabhramavat) — the prārabdha-doctrine; [P: prārabdha — cf. 3.25's bracket; the yoni-wandering (yonīṣu) — the yoni-term in its cosmological face.]

## 3.44 — nāsikāntarmadhyasaṃyamāt kim atra savyāpasavyasauṣumneṣu
**From the restraint in the middle of the nose — what is there in the left, right, and suṣumnā [channels]?**
Vārttika: "The inner-nose is the dvādaśānta; for those whose minds are fixed there, by the restraint (niyama) the mastery is accomplished; hence the self-state shines. The middle here is the heart; by the withdrawal of the prāṇa there, with the mind's function dissolved, the supreme self-arising occurs. With the mastery of the prāṇa accomplished, what is the use of the left, right and suṣumnā paths of the breath? [bracket:] This sūtra prescribes the worship of the prāṇa-self — the unstruck sound-brahman (anāhata-śabdabrahman) — for the practice of the self-perfection of the one equal to Śiva: the prāṇa, the prāṇamaya, the prāṇa for emission-and-filling, the mastery of the prāṇa-channels — the ancillary, as sung by Śiva in the glorious Svacchandatantra."
Notes: the sūtra's rhetorical question (kim atra — "what is there in...") answers: nothing special — the middle suffices; the explicit citation of the **Svacchandatantra** (M00091 in our library) — the author's named source; [P: anāhata-śabdabrahman — the unstruck-sound brahman — cf. 2.7's bracket (anāhatadhvani) and our śabdabrahman glossary entry (TĀ 15.8-9).]

## 3.45 — bhūyaḥ syātpratimīlanam
**Again there is the re-closing [into the Lord].**
Vārttika: "By the vision taught, this whole world is the unfolding of his will; hence the atom, by the power of māyā, separate — by the teaching of the path, with the destruction of turbidity (kāluṣya), re-closes (pratimīlana) into the Lord: entering the non-separate state in the supreme Lord, he becomes the complete dense bliss — Śiva. [bracket:] The essence-summary of the whole Śaiva procedure: the supreme Śiva himself, by the surging of his own will, unfolds the sprout of Śiva-Śakti and the rest, presiding over the pure path; never leaving his dense-conscious nature there; in the lower path, playing the world-drama with the intention of self-forgetfulness, he takes the māyā-knower-state; he himself, with the fiercest grace, plunging into the knower-of-the-field state by the Śaiva-knowledge-path, with the destruction of turbidity, emerges as the complete consciousness-bliss-dense supreme Śiva — returning to his own state. End."
Notes: the Vārttika's closing — the full circle: the Śiva-drama (viśvanāṭyam — the world-play of the actor-dancer of 3.9) and the return (pratimīlana — re-closing, the opposite of unmeṣa). The colophon: "the Vārttika of the son of Divākara, the master Bhāskara, of Kashmir" — 390 verses.
