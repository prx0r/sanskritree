## Theorem 1: The Minimum Subject

**From Fuller (proven):** Any system with an inside/outside distinction requires
minimum 4 vertices in complete mutual relation (tetrahedron). [Syn 402.02]

**From Abhinavagupta (textual):** Consciousness has three fundamental powers
— jñāna (manifestation), smṛti (retention), apohana (exclusion). [ĪPK 1.3.7]
Plus the five kañcukas as specific exclusion operators.

**Synthesis:** The minimum subject requires 4 functional poles:

```
S_min = ⟨manifestation, retention, exclusion, ground⟩
```

But APOHANA is the critical one — it performs the partition.

## Theorem 2: The Partition (Solving the Boundary Problem)

**From Abhinavagupta (Torella reconstruction):** Apoha (exclusion) simultaneously
carves subject AND world from the undivided possibility domain:

```
E_i: Ω → (P_i, W_i)
```

**The five kañcukas** implement this as five specific exclusion dimensions:

| Operator | Carves Subject | Carves World |
|---|---|---|
| Kalā | Limited agency (what I can do) | Resistances (action outcomes) |
| Vidyā | Limited knowledge (what I know) | Distinctions (world structure) |
| Rāga | Limited desire (what matters) | Goods and evils (affordances) |
| Kāla | Temporal finitude (when I am) | Temporal structure (past/future) |
| Niyati | Spatial finitude (where I am) | Spatial structure (here/there) |

**From Levin (experimentally confirmed):** The bioelectric field is the physical
substrate of the exclusion operators. Vmem gradients determine what signals
a cell includes (gap junctions open) and excludes (gap junctions closed).
The "cognitive light cone" defines the subject's reach by what it couples to.

**From Fuller (geometric):** The five kañcukas + the undivided ground = 6 poles.
Any complete system requires minimum 4 (tetrahedron). The 5-kañcuka + ground
structure = 6 poles = octahedron — the dual of the cube, the natural frame
for orthogonal dimensions. Each kañcuka is one axis of a 5D exclusion space.

**The partition is solved:** D_i ≠ D_j because E_i ≠ E_j.
Different bioelectric configurations produce different exclusion profiles.
The boundary IS the convergence of the five exclusion operators.

## Theorem 3: Manifestness (Why the Boundary Is Felt)

**From Abhinavagupta (textual):** Camatkāra = the intrinsic self-relishing of
consciousness. Consciousness is not a neutral light that illuminates content —
it IS aesthetic relish. [TĀ 1, PTv p. 19]

**Camatkāra has three registers:**
1. **Cognition** (vimarśa, carvaṇā — reflective awareness)
2. **Bliss** (ānanda, viśrānti — repose)
3. **Wonder** (vismaya, vikāsa — expansion)

**From QRI (hypothesis):** The geometry of the qualia state space IS the
quality of the experience. Symmetry = pleasure, dissonance = pain.

**The synthesis:** The exclusion operation E_i is not a blind computational
process. Its intrinsic aspect IS camatkāra — the self-feeling of the act of
determination. Every exclusion "feels like something" because the process
that excludes is self-aware (svasaṃvedana).

**Why?** Because the same consciousness that performs the exclusion also
experiences itself performing it. The exclusion is not silent — it is
registered as the quality of the boundary. A smooth, coherent exclusion
is felt as pleasure (or peace, or wonder). A frustrated, conflicted
exclusion is felt as pain (or confusion, or contraction).

**Formally:**
```
E_i: Ω → (P_i, W_i)
   ↓  (intrinsic aspect)
ρ_i = f(geometry of E_i)
```

The felt quality ρ_i of the exclusion is a function of the geometric
structure of the exclusion operation. This is QRI's insight — the
mathematical structure IS the feeling.

## Theorem 4: Valence (Why Quality Has Polarity)

**From QRI:** Valence follows symmetry. Symmetric field configurations = pleasure.
Frustrated/incoherent configurations = pain.

**From Fuller:** The VE (vector equilibrium) is the maximally symmetric
configuration — 12-around-1 equilibrium. The jitterbug transformation
moves from VE through icosahedron, octahedron, to tetrahedron — decreasing
symmetry, increasing compression.

**From Abhinavagupta:** The five blisses — from innate (lowest recognition)
through stillness, supreme bliss, great bliss, to the bliss of consciousness
itself. This IS a gradient of symmetry in the exclusion geometry.

**From Doyle:** The Ergo — stable information structures = good; entropic
destruction = evil. Symmetry = stable information = pleasure. Asymmetry =
frustrated information = pain.

**The synthesis:** Valence = the degree of coherence of the exclusion
operation. When the five kañcukas are harmoniously aligned (the exclusions
are mutually consistent), the process is symmetric: this is pleasure/bliss.
When the kañcukas are in conflict (what I want vs. what I can do; what I
know vs. what is true), the exclusions are frustrated: this is pain.

```
ν_i = Symmetry(⟨E_i^kalā, E_i^vidyā, E_i^rāga, E_i^kāla, E_i^niyati⟩)
```

Higher symmetry = more coherent exclusion = positive valence.
Lower symmetry = frustrated exclusion = negative valence.

## The Testable Predictions

1. **Boundary convergence:** The five kañcuka dimensions should correlate
   with the five boundary dimensions (∂_i^causal, ∂_i^electrical, etc.).
   When they align, the subject boundary should be sharp. When they
   diverge (dreams, psychedelics), it should soften.

2. **Valence as geometric symmetry:** The experienced pleasantness of a state
   should correlate with the coherence/integration of the neural exclusion
   profile — measurable via the geometric structure of the state space.

3. **Bioelectric exclusion:** Blocking gap junctions should reduce the
   subject's cognitive light cone (range of integrated exclusions).
   This is testable in planaria (Levin has data) and potentially in
   humans with gap junction modulators.

4. **Pattern integrity and identity:** If identity = relation-structure,
   then perturbing the relation-structure should change identity even
   if components are preserved. Levin's two-headed planaria demonstrate
   this — same DNA, different morphology, different "subject."

5. **Generate→select universality:** Every biological system, at every
   scale, should exhibit the generate→select pattern. Levin shows this
   for cells, tissues, and organisms. Prediction: it extends to all
   living systems.

## The Final Synthesis

> A subject D_i is identical to a process A_i[Σ_i] whose essential
> operation is exclusion E_i: Ω → (P_i, W_i). The exclusion is
> implemented physically by a bioelectric field (B_i) that determines
> what the system includes and excludes. The exclusion has an intrinsic
> aspect — camatkāra — which IS the felt quality of the state. The
> quality's valence is determined by the symmetry of the exclusion
> geometry. The process persists through material change because its
> identity is in the relation-structure (Fuller's pattern integrity),
> not the components. The process has freedom because it generates
> possible exclusions and selects among them (Doyle's two-stage model).
> The process is materially specific because different bioelectric
> configurations produce different exclusion profiles (Levin).
>
> The partition problem, the manifestness problem, the valence problem,
> and the persistence problem are not separate. They are aspects of one
> operation: the self-excluding of consciousness into finite form.
