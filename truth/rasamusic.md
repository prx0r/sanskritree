# Rasa Music — What Tymoczko's Geometry Actually Establishes for the Rasa Hypothesis

## Preamble

This document applies the same critical standard as the RASAANALYSIS critique. It distinguishes four levels of claim:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
C_1:&\quad \text{supreme consciousness is intrinsically self-relishing};\\
C_2:&\quad \text{every phenomenal state has some primitive self-affection};\\
C_3:&\quad \text{hedonic valence is mathematically determined by symmetry};\\
C_4:&\quad \text{stimulus geometry is identical with felt quality}.
\end{aligned}
}
\]

Tymoczko's geometry of music bears primarily on C₃ and C₄, indirectly on C₂, and not at all on C₁. The question is: what exactly does it establish, and what remains conjecture?

---

## Part 1: What Tymoczko Actually Established

### 1.1 Chords Can Be Represented as Points in Quotient Spaces

Tymoczko's core mathematical result: the set of all n-note chords (unordered pitch-class sets) forms a connected orbifold — a quotient of Euclidean space by a discrete group of symmetries (the OPTIC operations: Octave, Permutation, Transposition, Inversion, Cardinality change).

- Two-note chords: a Möbius strip
- Three-note chords: a twisted triangular torus (the interior of a prism with the top face glued to the bottom with a 120° twist)
- Four-note chords: a twisted 4D hypercube

The structure of these spaces is mathematically determined by the symmetries we choose to ignore. If we consider chords as unordered pitch-class sets (the standard music-theoretic definition), the space IS a specific orbifold. This is not interpretative — it's a theorem.

**Status: Established (mathematical).**

### 1.2 Voice-Leading Distance Defines a Metric on Chord Space

The minimal distance voices must travel to transform one chord into another defines a metric on chord space. Tymoczko shows that any reasonable measure of voice-leading size satisfies the "submajorization partial order" — a principle that prevents voice crossings from reducing measured distance. This gives a well-defined "zone of agreement" for comparing voice-leading sizes.

- Geodesics in chord space correspond to efficient voice leadings.
- The most common harmonic progressions (ii-V-I, I-IV-V-I) follow geodesics.

**Status: Established (mathematical + music-analytic).**

### 1.3 Near-Evenness Correlates with Consonance and Efficient Voice Leading

Chords that divide the octave nearly evenly (triads, seventh chords, perfect fifths) share two properties:
1. They are acoustically consonant (frequency ratios with small integers)
2. They can be linked by efficient voice leading to their transpositions

Uneven chords (chromatic clusters) cannot be linked efficiently but allow conjunct melodic motion within a static harmony.

This is the "overdetermination" Tymoczko emphasizes: the same chords are special for multiple independent reasons.

**Status: Established (music-theoretic + psychoacoustic).** The correlation between near-evenness and acoustic consonance is well-supported. The connection to efficient voice leading is a mathematical consequence.

### 1.4 The Five Features Are Overdetermined

Tymoczko's five features (conjunct melodic motion, acoustic consonance, harmonic consistency, limited macroharmony, centricity) independently constrain musical structure. When combined, they converge on a small set of familiar forms (diatonic scales, consonant triads, standard progressions).

- Combining conjunct motion + harmonic consistency → consonant chords
- Combining harmonic consistency + limited macroharmony → familiar scales
- There are only a few ways to satisfy multiple features simultaneously

**Status: Established (music-theoretic).** This is a genuine discovery about the structure of Western tonal music. Its cross-cultural generalizability is plausible but less certain.

### 1.5 Modulation and Chord Progression Follow the Same Geometry

Scale-to-scale modulation uses the same efficient voice-leading principle as chord-to-chord progression. The same geometric relationships hold at two temporal levels.

**Status: Established (music-analytic).** Supported by analysis of specific compositions from Clementi to Debussy to Reich.

### 1.6 The "Extended Common Practice" Shows Cross-Style Convergence

The same contrapuntal techniques (3+1 voice leading, efficient voice leading between consonant chords, stepwise resolution of tendencies) appear across medieval, Renaissance, Baroque, Classical, Romantic, jazz, and minimalism.

**Status: Established (music-historical).** Tymoczko provides specific analytical examples. The claim is not that all music follows these patterns but that these patterns recur across styles that share the five features.

## Part 2: What Tymoczko Does Not Establish

### 2.1 Geometry Does Not Establish Pleasure = Symmetry

Tymoczko does not claim that orbifold symmetry determines valence. He claims that orbifold geometry constrains what voice leadings are possible. The relationship between geometry and felt quality is mediated by:
- Acoustic consonance (a psychoacoustic phenomenon with multiple competing explanations)
- Learned expectation (cultural conditioning)
- Statistical surprise (violation of predicted patterns)
- Register, timbre, rhythm, dynamics (all outside the geometric model)

A consonant chord progression that follows a geodesic may still sound bad if the timbre is harsh, the rhythm is incoherent, or the listener is unfamiliar with the style.

**Status: Not established.** Tymoczko's geometry describes stimulus structure (𝒮), not phenomenal experience (𝒫). The mapping from 𝒮 to 𝒫 requires psychoacoustic and phenomenological bridges that Tymoczko does not provide.

### 2.2 The Geometry Describes Stimulus Space, Not Phenomenal Space

The distinction between four spaces remains:

\[
\mathcal S:\text{stimulus space (chord progressions, scales)}
\]
\[
\mathcal N:\text{neural-state space (EEG, fMRI)}
\]
\[
\mathcal P:\text{phenomenal space (what experience feels like)}
\]
\[
\mathcal R:\text{report/behaviour space (ratings, descriptions)}
\]

Tymoczko formalizes 𝒮. He notes that perceptual issues exist but explicitly leaves them to psychologists: "I am a musician, not a scientist, and although I will sometimes touch on perceptual issues, I will largely leave empirical psychology to the professionals."

**Status: Not established.** Correlations between 𝒮 and 𝒫 require independent investigation. The geometry of stimulus space is not the geometry of experience.

### 2.3 Acoustic Consonance Is Not the Same as Positive Valence

Tymoczko cites evidence that consonance preference is innate and cross-cultural. But:
- Consonance preference is contested among roughness, harmonicity, and learned exposure accounts
- Even if innate, preference for consonance over dissonance does not establish that consonance = pleasure or that dissonance = pain
- Dissonance is aesthetically useful and often preferred in context (the Tristan chord is dissonant but beloved)
- Musical pleasure depends on many factors (expectation, tension-resolution, familiarity, narrative) that are not reducible to consonant intervals

**Status: Not established.** Acoustic consonance is a real psychoacoustic phenomenon. Its relationship to valence is complex and context-dependent.

### 2.4 Overdetermination Does Not Prove Consciousness = Aesthetic Relish

That multiple constraints converge on the same musical structures is interesting and may explain why those structures feel significant. But it does not establish:
- That this significance is identical with the Tantrāloka's camatkāra
- That the same overdetermination structure holds for non-auditory modalities
- That overdetermination is what consciousness IS rather than a property of some conscious experiences

**Status: Not established.** Overdetermination is a formal property of constraint satisfaction problems. Its relationship to consciousness requires additional premises.

### 2.5 The Extended Common Practice Does Not Prove Universal Invariants

That similar techniques appear across Western styles from 1100 to 2000 does not establish that they are:
- Universal across all human cultures
- Determined by the nature of consciousness rather than by cultural transmission
- Invariant across non-musical modalities

**Status: Not established.** The extended common practice is a claim about Western music. Its extension to cross-cultural universals is plausible but requires independent evidence.

### 2.6 Tymoczko Does Not Address the Partition Problem

Nothing in Tymoczko's geometry explains why one listener's auditory field is exclusive to that listener. The geometry constrains what chords and progressions are possible within a field; it does not explain why fields are private.

**Status: Not addressed.** The partition problem (Λₚ) survives.

## Part 3: What Is Directly Applicable to Rasa

### 3.1 The Overdetermination Principle

Tymoczko's most important finding for the rasa hypothesis: **overdetermination** — when multiple independent constraints converge on the same structure, that structure feels special, significant, or "right."

This directly parallels the Tantrāloka's claim that consciousness IS rasa (aesthetic relish). The "relish" may be the felt quality of constraint convergence — the experience of multiple independent paths leading to the same configuration.

**What this means for rasa:** overdetermination may be the *formal mechanism* underlying the experience of beauty, significance, or aesthetic satisfaction. It is not that geometry = pleasure; rather, the experience of multiple constraints converging on a unique solution IS what we call aesthetic satisfaction.

**Formalizable:** For a stimulus s satisfying constraints C₁...Cₙ, define:

\[
\text{Overdet}(s) = |\{C_i: C_i(s) \text{ is satisfied}\}| - \min_{s'} |\{C_i: C_i(s') \text{ is satisfied}\}|
\]

The hypothesis: felt quality (rasa) is monotonic in Overdet(s), controlling for other factors. This is testable.

### 3.2 The Metric Structure of Stimulus Space

Tymoczko gives us a concrete, formalized metric for auditory stimulus space. This enables precise measurement of:
- Distance between chords (voice-leading size)
- Distance between scales (interscalar intervals)
- Distance between keys (modulation distance)

**What this means for rasa:** if we want to test whether phenomenal similarity follows geometric distance, Tymoczko gives us the geometry to test against. The null hypothesis is that two chord progressions close in voice-leading space feel similar because they use similar notes — not because geometry IS felt quality. To distinguish, we need to hold other factors constant and vary only geometric distance.

**Testable prediction:** Two chord progressions that are equally close in every other respect (register, timbre, rhythm, familiarity) but differ in voice-leading distance should produce different similarity ratings. This distinguishes the geometric model from a model based purely on shared pitch content.

### 3.3 The Independence of Macroharmony and Centricity

Tymoczko shows that macroharmony (available note collection) and centricity (presence of a tonal center) are independent dimensions. This is crucial for the basket of variables model of consciousness.

**What this means for rasa:** The phenomenal field has at least two independent structural parameters that can vary independently. A field can have:
- Wide macroharmony + strong centricity (chromatic music with a clear tonic)
- Wide macroharmony + weak centricity (atonal music)
- Narrow macroharmony + strong centricity (diatonic tonal music)
- Narrow macroharmony + weak centricity (modal music without a clear final)

This independence supports the claim that Mᵢ (manifestness) is not identical with any single content variable. You can change one parameter without necessarily changing the other.

### 3.4 The Two-Level Voice Leading Structure

The same geometric principle (efficient voice leading) operates at two temporal levels: chord-to-chord and scale-to-scale. This is hierarchical self-similarity.

**What this means for rasa:** if T_rec (recognition) involves a transformation at the higher organizational level, the voice-leading model provides a formal analogy: recognition is a "modulation" of the field's macro-structure that preserves the micro-structure. The Clementi example (Fig 1.3.10) shows a key change that is a single-step voice leading between scales — the whole piece shifts organizational frame by moving one note one semitone. Recognition may be similarly minimal: a single-step reorganization of the field's macro-principle that transforms everything while changing almost nothing.

### 3.5 The "Avoid Note" Principle

Jazz theory encodes "avoid notes" — structural positions that, if filled, blur functional identity. The avoid note is not externally prohibited; it's structurally incompatible with maintaining the current organization.

**What this means for rasa:** the kañcukas may be "avoid notes" of consciousness — the principles you can't violate without losing the coherence of the phenomenal field. Recognition may involve learning which notes are avoid and why, and developing the freedom to play them consciously — i.e., to occupy configurations that the normal aperture avoids.

### 3.6 The Constraint Satisfaction Model of Compositional Choice

Tymoczko's framework treats composition as a constraint satisfaction problem: given a set of desired features (conjunct motion, harmonic consistency, limited macroharmony), what configurations are possible? The constraints dramatically limit the options — there are only a few viable paths.

**What this means for rasa:** the same constraint satisfaction architecture may describe the aperture. The phenomenal field is not arbitrary but is the *solution* to a constraint satisfaction problem defined by the kañcukas (or analogous limiting principles). The "space of possible conscious states" is the set of configurations satisfying the constraints. Tymoczko shows that for the auditory case, this space has a specific non-trivial geometry (Möbius strip, twisted torus, etc.). The rasa hypothesis is that this geometry IS the structure of felt experience.

## Part 4: What Is Not Directly Applicable

### 4.1 The "Five Features = Kañcukas" Parallel Is Suggestive but Not Evidence

The mapping of the five features onto the five kañcukas (conjunct motion → niyati, acoustic consonance → vidyā/rāga, etc.) is a structural parallel. It does not establish:
- That the kañcukas are literally the five features
- That the five features are derived from a single underlying principle
- That the kañcuka system is cross-culturally valid in the way the five features may be

**Verdict:** Interesting reconstruction (R), not textual or empirical evidence.

### 4.2 Tymoczko Does Not Support QRI's Symmetry Theory of Valence

Tymoczko's orbifold symmetries are not the same as QRI's proposed symmetry of a phenomenal object. They are:
- Symmetries of chord space (a highly specific mathematical structure)
- Not demonstrated to correspond to symmetries of any neural or phenomenal object
- Not shown to predict valence across diverse stimuli

Tymoczko explicitly distances himself from perceptual claims. His work provides a precise formal language for describing stimulus structure but does not bridge to phenomenology.

**Verdict:** Tymoczko's geometry is a necessary precondition for testing STV in the auditory domain but does not itself support STV.

### 4.3 No Support for Universal Consciousness

Nothing in Tymoczko's geometry suggests that the constraint structure of chord space is ultimately the expression of a single universal consciousness. The geometry is a property of the auditory system interacting with acoustic structure — a local, embodied, evolved phenomenon.

**Verdict:** Compatible with many ontologies (materialist, idealist, dual-aspect). Does not discriminate between them.

## Part 5: The Testable Predictions

### 5.1 Overdetermination → Felt Quality

**Prediction:** Stimuli with higher overdetermination (satisfying more independent constraints simultaneously) will be rated higher on measures of beauty, significance, and aesthetic satisfaction, controlling for familiarity, complexity, and valence.

**Test:** Generate chord progressions that satisfy 1, 2, 3, 4, or 5 of Tymoczko's features, measure ratings. Do more features always produce higher ratings? Or is there an optimum?

### 5.2 Voice-Leading Distance → Phenomenal Similarity

**Prediction:** Two chord progressions with small voice-leading distance will be rated as more similar than progressions with large voice-leading distance, controlling for shared pitch content.

**Test:** Rate similarity of chord pairs with controlled voice-leading distance. If voice-leading distance predicts similarity beyond shared notes, the geometry model gains support.

### 5.3 Macroharmony + Centricity Independence

**Prediction:** Macroharmony width and centricity strength can be manipulated independently to produce four distinct classes of musical experience. Listeners will distinguish all four classes reliably.

**Test:** Generate stimuli in four conditions (wide/strong, wide/weak, narrow/strong, narrow/weak), collect free-description and rating data. Do the four conditions produce distinct phenomenal clusters?

### 5.4 The Two-Level Model of Recognition

**Prediction:** If recognition is a "modulation" (scale-level voice leading), then musical modulations should share neural signatures with recognition events reported in contemplative practice. Both should involve a minimal shift in macro-organizing principle that transforms the whole field without disrupting micro-content.

**Test:** Compare EEG signatures of musical modulation with meditation-based recognition events. Hypothesized common signature: a transient decrease in global field coherence followed by reorganization at a different frequency.

### 5.5 The "Avoid Note" Model of the Kañcukas

**Prediction:** The kañcukas (or analogous constraint principles) function as "avoid notes" of consciousness — structural positions that cannot be occupied without losing field coherence. If this is correct, then violating a kañcuka constraint should produce predictable changes in phenomenal structure (loss of self-boundary, loss of temporal continuity, etc.).

**Test:** Develop an experimental protocol modeled on jazz "avoid note" pedagogy: systematically approach a constraint boundary and observe whether the field reorganizes. The Vijñānabhairava's methods are natural candidates.

### 5.6 The Constraint Satisfaction Model of Aperture States

**Prediction:** The space of possible conscious states is not arbitrary but is the solution space of a constraint satisfaction problem. Different constraint sets produce different "phase spaces" of possible experience. Changes in constraint satisfaction (relaxation, addition, substitution) correspond to changes in phenomenal state.

**Test:** Formalize the kañcukas (or an empirically derived set of constraints) as a mathematical constraint satisfaction problem. Generate the predicted space of solutions. Test whether actual reported experiences (from phenomenology, psychology, contemplative reports) fall within this predicted space.

## Part 6: The Bottom Line

Tymoczko's geometry of music provides:

**Established:**
- A formal metric space for auditory stimuli
- The overdetermination principle (multiple constraints converge on few structures)
- The independence of macroharmony and centricity
- Two-level hierarchical self-similarity of voice leading
- Cross-style recurrence of basic contrapuntal patterns

**Not established:**
- That geometry = felt quality (C₄)
- That symmetry determines valence (C₃)
- That the geometry of stimulus space IS the geometry of phenomenal space
- Any claim about consciousness being unified, universal, or self-relishing (C₁, C₂)

**Directly applicable to rasa:**
- Overdetermination as a candidate mechanism for the experience of aesthetic significance
- A testable formal language for stimulus structure
- The independence of field parameters (macroharmony, centricity)
- The constraint satisfaction model of possible configurations
- The "avoid note" principle as a model of the kañcukas

**What rasa adds to Tymoczko:**
- The claim that overdetermination is not just a formal property but IS experienced as beauty/significance
- The claim that the constraint space is not arbitrary but reflects the nature of consciousness itself
- The claim that the constraints can be relaxed through recognition

**The gap that remains:**
Tymoczko gives us the geometry of 𝒮 (stimulus space). Rasa requires a geometry of 𝒫 (phenomenal space). Bridging this gap requires:
1. Showing that phenomenal similarity tracks geometric distance in 𝒮
2. Showing that the constraints on 𝒮 are also constraints on 𝒫
3. Showing that the overdetermination of 𝒮 is experienced AS the quality of 𝒫

None of these is established. All are testable.
