# The Sanskritree Project — Complete Compendium

## Part 1: The Frontier Question

### 1.1 The Equation

```
Dᵢ = Λₚ(M, Πᵢ, Σᵢ)
```

**The question that never changed:** Does intrinsic biological self-concern constitute phenomenal presence, or merely accompany it?

### 1.2 The Three Summits

| Summit | Explains | Established | Gap |
|--------|----------|-------------|-----|
| **Sanskrit** (M) | Manifestation cannot be reduced to external description | Prakāśa argument: Established(x) → Manifest(x). Transcendental priority of manifestation. | Does not explain why M → private Dᵢ |
| **Greek** (Πᵢ) | Shared ground does not require shared field | Participation model: Mᵢ = πᵢ(M₀). Common source + real local distinction without collapse. | Does not explain what individuates πᵢ |
| **Science** (Σᵢ) | Organization produces bounded agency | Markov blanket, Levin morphospace, causal emergence. | Does not explain why boundaries are felt |

### 1.3 The Identity Hypothesis

> **A phenomenal subject is the intrinsic, valenced presence of one causally closed, self-maintaining trajectory to itself.**

Two testable identity claims:
1. **Intrinsic biological significance = felt valence?**
2. **Objective causal closure = phenomenal co-presence?**

### 1.4 What Was Cut

| Claim | Reason |
|-------|--------|
| D(0,1) → S (metaphysical substrate) | Difference proves contrastive structure, not a substrate |
| Gödel = proof of consciousness | Gödel is an analogy, not a theorem about consciousness |
| Hard problem = measurement problem = incompleteness | Same abstract pattern, not the same technical problem |
| Postmortem karmic fields | Notation without mechanism — no carrier, dynamics, or metric |
| Remote viewing | Unsupported — quarantined |

### 1.5 What Survives

1. Self-exemption error: observer cannot coherently treat itself as unconditioned
2. Recognition = subtractive change in self-positioning (σ-flip: Sv(O) → ¬Sv(O))
3. Three models of source relation live: duplication, identity, participation
4. The frontier is exactly Int(Pᵢ*) = Dᵢ

## Part 2: Translations

### 2.1 Ajadapramātṛsiddhi (APS)

**Source:** Sanskrit Wikisource + GRETIL dataset
**Verses:** 27 kārikās with Vṛtti
**Status:** Fully translated

**Key arguments:**
- Stages A-B (verses 1-15): Reflexivity argument — bare luminosity without self-apprehension would be inert. "This" rests in "I."
- Stage C (verses 16-17, 25): Universalization attempt — "sarvārthasaṃvidām" is ambiguous between "all cognitions within one stream" and "all cognitions of every subject."
- Tantrāloka 16.242: Abhinavagupta limits anusandhāna to one body, blocking the transpersonal reading.

**Verdict:** Strong phenomenological core (reflexive manifestation, relational synthesis). Universalization from "all objects are within manifestation" to "all manifestations are numerically one subject" is an invalid quantifier shift.

### 2.2 Sambandhasiddhi (SS)

**Source:** Sanskrit Wikisource (KSTS vol 34, Muktabodha M00080)
**Verses:** 21 kārikās with Vṛtti
**Status:** Fully translated with full commentary

**Key arguments:**
- Relation = rūpasaṃśleṣa (conjunction of forms) = aikātmya (identity of many)
- The knower cannot be divided within a single cognitive act (mātṛbheda argument, verse 10)
- Verse 8 assumes the Pratyabhijñā ontology (cinmātravapus) rather than arguing it

**Verdict:** Sophisticated theory of relation as consciousness's self-unifying activity. Does not independently bridge the universalization gap.

### 2.3 Īśvarasiddhi (IS)

**Source:** GRETIL dataset (13 entries)
**Verses:** 13 kārikās
**Status:** Fully translated

**Key arguments:**
- Nyāya-style design argument: organized effects require an intelligent cause
- Verse 56 explicitly defers nondual proof to the ĪPK
- Deliberately dualistic framework

**Verdict:** At most a cosmic supervisor, not identity of all subjects with that supervisor.

### 2.4 Siddhitrayī Overall

**Verdict:** None of the three texts contains the missing nondual proof. The project set out to find whether Utpaladeva has a non-circular argument from local reflexivity to universal subject. All three translated. None supplies it.

### 3.2 The Three Mechanisms of Partition

#### Mechanism 1: Dehabhedāt — Body Differences as Partition

> "The consciousness, which is all things, that contracts due to the differences between the bodies." (TĀ 28/373cd-374ab)

**The cascade:**
- Body differences (dehabhedāt) → contraction → private domain
- Mutual reflection (pratibimba) between bodies → expansion
- The body IS the partition boundary, not a container

**All related passages:**
- TĀ 28/373cd-381ab (the core verse)
- TĀ 28/204cd-205ab (teacher-disciple across body difference)
- TĀ 10/9787 (body obscures like a chest)
- TĀ 6/8648-8650 (contraction is one with the body)
- TĀ 3/10534-10538 (body as vessel of consciousness)

#### Mechanism 2: Rāga-Niyati — Craving + Fixation

> "The principle of Attachment is just the craving for all objects; the principle of Restraint is that same craving when it arises with some particular fixed object." (TĀ 3/17-18)

**The vicious circle:**
```
Rāga (generalized reaching) → Niyati (fixed onto specific) → karma → more rāga
```

**All related passages:**
- TĀ 3/17-18ab (core definition)
- TĀ 6/9899-9915 (the five coverings as system)
- TĀ 7/12740-12785 (vicious circle of craving and necessity)
- TĀ 5/21665-21685 (sequence of emanation of kañcukas)

#### Mechanism 3: Prāṇa — The Descent Bridge

> "The vital breath is the first manifestation of consciousness. The intellect and the rest depend upon it, because they are vitalized by it." (TĀ 4, line 1110)

**The cascade:**
```
Pure consciousness → prāṇana (undifferentiated life force) → breath (prāṇa/apāna) → body
```

**All related passages:**
- TĀ 4/1110 (core statement)
- TĀ 10/7655-7705 (initial emergence of life force from consciousness)
- TĀ 10/10738 (vital breath as first product of consciousness)
- TĀ 2/138-141 (Prāṇakuṇḍalikā as middle term of emission)
- TĀ 5/3675 (breath as link to the pulse of consciousness)

### 3.3 The Three Undoings

| Mechanism | Undoing | How |
|-----------|---------|-----|
| Dehabhedāt | Pratibimba (mutual reflection) | Bodies interacting expand contracted consciousness |
| Rāga-Niyati | Śaktipāta (grace) | The uncontracted touches the contracted, breaking the cycle |
| Prāṇa descent | Pratyabhijñā (recognition) | Sees body as consciousness, not separate from it |

### 3.4 Missed Leads

**Lead 1: Space, time and form cannot divide Śiva (TĀ 1/59cd-60ab)**
Consciousness is not divided by space, time, or form. They appear to divide, but the division is conditioned by thought constructs based on bhedābheda. The partition is epistemic — but appears ontological.

**Lead 2: The death-moment contraction (TĀ 10/10443-10458)**
At death, the division between breaths shatters → residual prāṇana. The moment is one of such extreme contraction that the body is seen as an inert object. Contraction is graded, not binary. At maximum contraction, consciousness separates from body.

**Lead 3: Orgasm = emissive power (TĀ 10, line 22838)**
"The initial moment of orgasm as well as emissive power (visargaśakti) of consciousness about to pour out into creation." Pleasure and creation are the same event. Valence (feeling good) IS manifestation.

**Lead 4: Prasiddhi epistemology (TĀ 11, lines 13340-13368)**
A priori certainty grounds all knowledge. Babies know food nourishes without inference. Certainty is not derived — it's the ground. The "feeling of reality" is intrinsic, not produced by evidence.

### 3.5 Identity Test Conclusion

The hypothesis that consciousness = aesthetic relish (camatkāra/rasa) holds up better than any other claim in the Tantrāloka. The identity test found strongest support in Vol 4, 6, 7, and 10. The weakest support was in Vol 5 and 8-9 (thin on theory).

**Key finding from the test:** Ahaṅcamatkāra (Vol 10) — the aesthetic rapture of "I am." Selfhood IS wonder. Not "self has wonder" but "self = wonder." This is the deepest insight of Trika phenomenology.

## Part 6: The Remaining Frontier

### 6.1 What Is Established

1. Manifestation has transcendental priority (prakāśa)
2. Manifestation is reflexive and determinate (vimarśa)
3. Reality is dynamic, not static (spanda)
4. Body differences produce phenomenal exclusivity (dehabhedāt)
5. Attachment and necessity form a paired contraction mechanism (rāga-niyati)
6. Breath is the bridge between consciousness and embodiment (prāṇa)
7. Recognition is subtractive — it changes self-positioning, not content (pratyabhijñā corrected)
8. Certainty is intrinsic, not derived (prasiddhi)

### 6.2 What Is Not Established

1. **That intrinsic biological significance = felt valence.** The Tantrāloka asserts consciousness = rasa (aesthetic relish), but this is a metaphysical identity, not a proven derivation.

2. **That objective causal closure = phenomenal co-presence.** The two covary in the text but the identity is asserted, not derived.

3. **That one universal consciousness underlies all domains.** The participation model (shared ground, distinct fields) is equally compatible with the textual evidence.

4. **That unrestricted freedom follows from intrinsic articulation.** V₁-V₄ do not entail V₅ (svātantrya as omnipotence).

### 6.3 The One Question

> **Does intrinsic biological self-concern constitute phenomenal presence, or merely accompany it?**

Everything else in this compendium maps the terrain around that question. The answer, if it exists, is not in any text we've examined.
