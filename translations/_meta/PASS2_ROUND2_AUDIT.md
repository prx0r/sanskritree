## 1. Dyczkowski's method (studied from his works on disk — the model for our pass-3 commentary)

Sources: *Aphorisms of Śiva* (intro + foreword + sūtra-sections), *Stanzas on Vibration* (the Rājānaka Rāma biography), *Tantrāloka* vol. 1 (the acknowledgments/method statement).

**The method's features (what we adopt):**
1. **The sūtra-list in the front** — his renderings with parenthetical insertions marked (e.g., "The consciousness which is the expanse of the Fourth State (abides constantly in) the various (states) of waking..."). Our version: the pass-1 working translations already exist per-verse; a consolidated sūtra-list per text is a pass-3 deliverable.
2. **Biography-first**: the Rājānaka Rāma biography (the gotra of Kāntha Dhāumyāyana, the Kanouj-migration with Lalitāditya's retinue, the poet-brother Muktākana at Avantivarman's court, the Suramatha lineage) — the model for our author-notes. Our parallel: Bhāskara (mid-11th c., son of Divākara), Matsyendranāth (10th–11th c., the Candra-dvīpa/Kāmarūpa geography), Rājānaka Rāma (disciple of Utpaladeva), Kṣemarāja (early 11th c.).
3. **Per-sūtra: translation → commentary-translation → Exposition** — the comparison of commentaries as the interpretive engine ("continuously comparing Bhaskara's commentary to Ksemaraja's position"). Our version: the three-readings format already separates the layers; the *Exposition* corresponds to our Part-2 doctrinal reading, which becomes the commentary.
4. **Numbered endnotes** with textual variants and parallel-text quotations (the Netratantra, the Svacchandatantra, the Śrītantrasadbhāva quoted in his notes). Our version: the [X]-flags + the cross-text network in the period-glossary.
5. **The myth-of-origin framing** (the dream, the rock, the Siddha's oneiric reception). Our version: each text's frame-myth (the KJN's fish-story, the Śivasūtra's dream-rock, the Kubjikā's paśu-secrecy) as the commentary's entry-point.
6. **The method-statement** (the acknowledgments = intellectual provenance; the dedication to Lakshmanjoo). Our version: the PASS_PROTOCOL + the time-place-context blocks.

## 2. Period-context notes (from the literature, 2026-08-09)

Key framing facts for the commentary layer (Wikipedia/Kashmir Shaivism + Dyczkowski's own intros):

- **"Kashmir Shaivism" is a 19th-century umbrella term** — coined by the Bengali pandit Gangadhara Kaviratna — for the Trika + Krama subschools of the Kaula tradition, developed in Kashmir after 850 CE as a **domestication of 'wild' Kaula traditions**. Lawrence: "Radical practices were toned down, concealed under the guise of propriety, or interpreted as metaphors of internal contemplations."
- **[STUB-A: the domestication lens]** — Our batch spans both sides of this process: the KJN/Akulavīra/Kubjikā are the "wild" layer (the caru, the yoni-practices, the māraṇa-rites, the paśu-exclusions); the Śivasūtra/TĀ-stack is the domesticated layer (the brāhmaṇical frame, the smṛti-quotations, the scholastic register). The commentary should use the domestication narrative as a lens: e.g., Kubjikā paṭala 11's Yājñavalkya-quotations inside a Kālīkula rite show the domestication in real time; the KJN 16's Kaivarta-myth (the Fisherman as the brāhmaṇa-leader) is the lineage's own answer to the domestication tension.
- **The Trika's scriptural authorities** (per the literature): Mālinīvijayottara, Siddhayogeśvarīmata, Anāmaka-tantra — two of which are in our library (Mālinīvijayottara M00160 + Abhinava's Vārttika M00158). The commentary can quote them.
- **The dates**: Somānanda (875–925, Śivadṛṣṭi = the first full scholastic work of the Trika); Utpaladeva (c. 900–950); Abhinavagupta (c. 950–1016); Kṣemarāja (early 11th c.); Jayaratha (1150–1200, TĀ-commentary); the decline after 1200 CE (the Kaula lineages surviving as wandering ascetics into the 18th c.).
- **The Śivasūtra-myth**: the dream-revelation to Vasugupta, the Mahādeva-mountain rock with the sūtras inscribed (the lithic counterpart of the oneiric) — the framing our ŚS-commentary should open with (cf. Muller-Ortega's foreword).

## 4. Commentary stubs (the pass-2/3 bridge — Part 2 seeds)

*Convention: `[STUB: ...]` = an interpretive idea anchored to evidence, to be deepened in pass 3+. Written in the Dyczkowski-Exposition style (translation → comparison → interpretation).*

**STUB-A (the domestication lens)** — see §2 above. Applies to: KJN 11 (the pañcāmṛta), KJN 18 (the abhiṣeka), Kubjikā 8 (the māraṇa-rites), Kubjikā 11 (the Yājñavalkya-frame). The comparative question: which practices survive the domestication in the TĀ (e.g., the pañcāmṛta in TĀ 29's kula-section — checkable in Dyczkowski vol. 11).

**STUB-B (the bhāva-triple as social taxonomy)** — Kubjikā 7's divya/vīra/paśu is not merely soteriological: it maps the Kaula community's self-image against the brāhmaṇical order (the paśu = the orthoprax who cannot see the deity in the kula-forms; the vīra = the initiated; the divya = the perfected). [Evidence: 7/26-31's paśu-disqualifiers; the ŚS Vārttika's paśu/pati pair; the KJN 11's advaita/paśu opposition. Compare Lawrence's domestication thesis.]

**STUB-C (the actor-metaphor as the lineage's signature)** — the naṭa-image spans Bhāskara (ŚS 1.2, 2.9, 3.9), our Siddhitrayī (APS 22–24), and the KJN 16's myth (the Lord's play). [Evidence: the four attestations; the aesthetic register (rasa, camatkāra) in the Vārttika's brackets. The commentary-idea: the actor-metaphor is the tradition's answer to the problem of divine agency without alienation — the Lord performs without being bound.]

**STUB-D (the yoni-doctrine's two faces)** — the batch shows yoni as (a) the śakti-source (ŚS 1.3's catuṣṭaya; Kubjikā 2's bīja-yoni; KJN 9's 84,000 yonis) and (b) the literal practice-object (Kubjikā 9/26-27's kulastrī-yoni mantras; KJN 11's pañcāmṛta). The domestication narrative suggests (a) is the Trika-facing face, (b) the Kaula-facing face — the same term, two registers. [Evidence: the glossary-entry; the Ciñcinī-triangle note (Dyczkowski vol. 2).]

**STUB-E (the nirañjana-terminus)** — the Stainless as the apophatic goal across the batch: the Śivasūtra's anāvṛta (1.1), the Akulavīra's nirañjana (32, 110), the KJN's jīva (6/4) and kapāla-bheda (20/2), the Kubjikā's nirañjanapada (1.2, 9/15), the TĀ 3.172-gloss. [Evidence: the period-glossary entry. The commentary-idea: nirañjana is the batch's shared name for the terminus of the "uncoloured" — the consciousness unstained by manifestation — and its recurrence across the Kaula and Trika lines is the strongest evidence of the corpus's internal unity.]

**STUB-F (the kṛtakā/sahaja distinction as the lineage's self-diagnosis)** — Akulavīra 56 + KJN 11 + Kubjikā 7: the Kaula path is two-fold (the artificial observances vs the spontaneous samarasa); the Akulavīra negates the kṛtaka-limb, not the lineage. [Evidence: the pass-2 C7 finding. The commentary-idea: the kṛtaka/sahaja distinction is the lineage's own theory of its domestication — the "wild" core (sahaja) vs the domesticated shell (kṛtaka).]

**STUB-G (the fish-myth and the lineage's self-location)** — KJN 16's Matsyendra-myth (the Candra-dvīpa, the fish-belly, the Kaivarta-avatar) locates the transmission at the intersection of the Kāmarūpa-geography and the brāhmaṇical order. [Evidence: 16/27–16/49; the colophons ("candradvīpavinirgate"); Kubjikā 7's Kāmarūpa-pīṭha. Compare the MBT-intro's Matsyendra-section (lines 4384+).]

**STUB-H (the puraścaraṇa's math as the ritual-science register)** — Kubjikā 9's japa-counts (8,000; 6,000; 9 lakhs; 108; 1,000) and the paṭala 12's prāṇāyāma-mathematics (16/64/32 breaths) are the Kālīkula's version of the tantric ritual-science that the Trika's TĀ systematizes. [Evidence: the Jñānārṇava-citations (paṭala 12's vyākhyā — the library has the Jñānārṇavatantra); the Gautamīya-citations.]

## 5. The ideas for making this great (2026-08-09)

1. **The commentary-stub layer** — Part 2 of every pass-1 file carries `[STUB: ...]` ideas; each pass deepens them. The stubs written now (with the period-context hot) become the pass-3 commentary's skeleton.
2. **The period-essay per cluster** — a short (1–2 page) essay per period-cluster (T1–T7, N1–N3) on the period's context: the political history (the Kārkoṭa/Utpala dynasties for the Kashmir texts; the Bengal transmission for the Kubjikā), the doctrinal rivalries (Siddhānta vs Kaula; the Buddhist pramāṇa-schools for the Nyāya hops), the key figures. Written from Wikipedia + Dyczkowski's intros + the texts themselves. The first: the Kaula-corpus essay (T2).
3. **The biography-blocks** — Dyczkowski's biography-first model applied: one block per author (Matsyendranāth, Rājānaka Rāma, Bhāskara, Kṣemarāja, Gaṇananātha, Jñānānanda) consolidating what the corpus + anchors tell us.
4. **The anchor-comparison reports** — for each text with a published translation (ŚS: Dyczkowski; Akulavīra: archive.org; KJN: Mukhopadhyaya pending): a per-verse comparison table (ours vs anchor, classified: agree / lexical / doctrinal / error).
5. **The commentary-questions register** — a running list of interpretive questions that the texts raise and the commentary should answer (e.g., "what does the pañcāmṛta mean in TĀ 29?"; "is the kumārī-arithmetic of Kubjikā 7 socially real?"), each with the evidence-locators. Pass 3 answers them one by one.
6. **The manuscript-geography map** — the colophons' sites (Candra-dvīpa, Kāmarūpa, Vārāṇasī, the pīṭha-lists) mapped against the texts' transmission-histories — the "where it was written and where it travelled" layer.
7. **Wikipedia-reading protocol** — for each new text: read the Wikipedia page on the author and the tradition, extract the date-facts and the framing-concepts into the time-place-context block (already done for Kashmir Shaivism, Matsyendranāth; to do: Kṣemarāja, Utpaladeva, Abhinavagupta, Kālīkula/Kubjikā, the Mahāvidyās, Tarkasaṃgraha-line for the Nyāya hops).

## 7. Biography-blocks (Dyczkowski's biography-first model — draft v1)

**Matsyendranāth (the KJN/Akulavīra/Kulānanda author)**
- fl. early 10th c. (born 10th c., died 11th c. per Wikipedia); born at **Chandradwip, Bengal (now Barishal, Bangladesh)** — the Candra-dvīpa of our colophons ("candradvīpavinirgate" — "issued at the Moon-island")!
- Tucci (on the Tibetan Siddha-literature + Tāranātha): of the **Kaibarta (fisherman) community of Kāmarūpa** — the exact claim of our KJN 16 (the "ahaṃ so dhīvaro" — "I am the Fisherman" — and 16/37's "the Fisherman is the leader of the vipras") — the myth encodes the historical self-location
- The legend: born under an inauspicious star, cast into the ocean, **swallowed by a fish**, overheard Śiva-Pārvatī's yoga-teaching at the ocean's bottom — the fish-myth of KJN 16 confirmed as the lineage's self-narrative
- One of the 84 mahāsiddhas; the Nāth-founder; guru of Gorakṣanāth; associated with Kaula Śaivism; revered in Nepal (the Rato Matsyendranāth chariot-festival at Bungmati; the shrine brought from Assam per the Bungmati-inscriptions); mentioned in the Sabaratantra as one of the 24 Kāpālika siddhas
- **[STUB-G → full commentary seed]** — the KJN 16 myth + the colophons + the Tucci-claim + the MBT-intro's Matsyendra-section (lines 4384+) = a complete biography-commentary for the Matsyendra-school texts

**Rājānaka Rāma (the Spanda Vivṛti author)** — from Dyczkowski's Stanzas intro: direct disciple of Utpaladeva, first half of the 10th c.; the gotra of Kāntha Dhāumyāyana; the ancestor Nārāyaṇa migrated from Kanouj (possibly with Lalitāditya's retinue, 8th c.); the poet-brother Muktākana (court of Avantivarman, 855–83); possibly the author of the Sarvatobhadra-Gītā-commentary; a "king of poets"; possibly the Śrīrāma who founded the Suramatha lineage (per Kṣemarāja's Netratantra-commentary).

**Bhāskara (the Śivasūtra-Vārttika author)** — from Dyczkowski's Aphorisms intro: son of Divākarabhaṭṭa, ≈mid-11th c. (contemporary of/soon after Utpaladeva; quoted by Abhinavagupta); his lineage-claim: Vasugupta → Kallaṭa (the non-Kṣemarāja line); his "mysticism of Light."

**Kṣemarāja (the Nareśvaraparīkṣā + Vimarśinī author)** — early 11th c.; disciple of Abhinavagupta; the author of the Śivasūtravimarśinī, Spandanirṇaya, Pratyabhijñāhṛdaya, Svacchandatantroddyota, Netratantra-vṛtti, and the Nareśvaraparīkṣā (in our batch).

**The period-backdrop for the commentary** (from the literature): the Trika's flowering c. 900–1200 (Somānanda 875–925 → Utpaladeva c. 900–950 → Abhinavagupta c. 950–1016 → Kṣemarāja → Jayaratha 1150–1200); the decline after 1200 (the Kaula lineages continuing as wandering ascetics into the 18th c.); the "Kashmir Shaivism"-name itself = a 19th-c. coinage (Gangadhara Kaviratna). For the Bengal-line (the Kubjikā/Kālīkula): the late-medieval consolidation (the Daśamahāvidyā-list, the Bengali prints, the vernacular incantations) — a separate, later transmission-world.
