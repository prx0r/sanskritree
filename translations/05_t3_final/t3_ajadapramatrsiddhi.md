## Verses 1–4 — the opponent's challenge and the reply's seed

**v. 1** — yathā sadasatāṃ naiva viśeṣo 'sti nijātmani | jaḍājaḍānām apy evaṃ nāstyasāv iti niścayaḥ ||
> Just as, in their own selves, the existent and the non-existent have no distinction, so, too, of the inert and the non-inert there is none — this is the conclusion. [the stream-of-consciousness opponent; the isolated-particularity accepted]

**v. 2** — prakhyopākhyā ca sattā yad yad asattā viparyayaḥ | naitat svarūpaṃ spṛśati vastuno jñātṛgāmy adaḥ ||
> Whatever is manifestation (prakhyā) and designation (upākhyā) — that is existence; non-existence is its reverse. That does not touch the thing's own form; it belongs to the knower. [ADJUDICATED: the two-modes (prakāśa/vimarśa) per the Vṛtti; the report's vimarśa-dossier]

**v. 3** — prakhyopākhyāviṣayatāpy arthasyātmā na yujyate | parāpekṣitayā naika-rūpatve sattvalaṅghanāt ||
> Nor does the object's self-hood hold even in being the object of manifestation-and-designation: being dependent on another, not one-formed, it oversteps existence. [the other-dependence is the force; the density-flag carried]

## Verses 9–10 — the anti-inert-light (the report's established A-thesis)

**v. 9** — tulyaiva prāgvadātmani... nirvimarśasya prakāśasyāpi sphaṭikādivad viśvāvabhāsane 'pi asvatantratvāt svātmanyasatā jaḍena ca bhāvena sādṛśyameva syāt... etena śāntabrahmavādanirāsaḥ kaṭākṣitaḥ ||
> Equal indeed, as before, in the self: even the non-reflexive manifestation, like crystal, in the illumination of all — being non-independent, being unreal in its own self — would have likeness to the inert being. By this the calm-Brahmā-view is glanced at [and refuted]. [ADJUDICATED: the crystal-image + the Śānta-Brahma-refutation carried]

**v. 10** — pratiṣṭhāspadatāmiyāt... nirvimarśā saṃvitsvato'labdhasattākā paraṃ vyavasthāpayituṃ nālaṃ gaganakusumamiva saurabhādijanane ||
> [The non-reflexive awareness] would come to be the ground of [another's] support — the non-reflexive awareness, its existence unattained from itself, is unable to establish the other, like a sky-flower [unable] to give birth to fragrance.

## Verses 11–15 — the "I am this" (the report's A→B transition)

**v. 11** — ... bhinnaṃ vastu idantāvimarśena prakāśanārhaṃ satpunarahantātmanā vyavasthāmiyāt ||
> But again, by the "I" as the self: the divided thing, fit to be manifested by the reflexion of the "this," would, being [so manifested], be established by the "I"-nature. [ADJUDICATED: the this-I transition pressed — the Vṛtti's "unestablished from itself without the resting-in-I"]

**v. 15** — ... vimarśaḥ so 'ham ityayam ||
> This reflexion — "I am this" (so 'ham): the awareness whose self is the reflexion of the "I," standing in the place of life — from whose support even the inert thing, by its unity with the reflexive knower, comes to the non-inertness of resting in the "I"-state. [ADJUDICATED: the report's flagged reading — "the fulfillment of the segmented 'this' is the resting-in-own-nature"]

## Verse 16 — the universalization (the report's contested C-thesis)

**v. 16** — dvidhā sa eṣa evātmā mito 'parimitas tathā | prāṇādinā niruddho 'ṇuḥ paramātmā tv akhaṇḍitaḥ ||
> This very self is twofold: limited and unlimited. Restricted by breath and the other limiting conditions, it is the minute individual; as the supreme self, it is undivided. [OPEN — the report: "states, rather than demonstrates, the numerical identity; universalization remains contested." The T3 carries the honest flag: the A-limited = A-unlimited is asserted, not proved.]
