## Patala 2 - the dharmadharma-vicara

**2/1** - ataḥ paraṃ pravakṣyāmi jñānaṃ trailokyadurlabham | kārikātattvasadbhāvaṃ siddhanāthakulodbhavam ||
> After this I will declare the knowledge rare in the three worlds: the kārikā's true reality, born of the Siddha-nātha's lineage.

**2/2** - candrasūryavinirmuktaṃ dvāraṇākṣavivarjitam | pṛthivyāpastaṃ tathā yogī vāyurākāśameva ca ||
> Free of moon and sun, free of the doors and the eyes, the yogi [goes] beyond earth, water, wind, and the ether itself —

**2/3** - pañcabhirāheto deho śrūyate tattvanāyake | sarveṣāṃ vyāpakaḥ śānta vyāptī tasya na vidyate ||
> — the body is heard [to be built] of the five, in the Lord of principles; the tranquil is the pervader of all — no pervasiveness is his own. [OPEN: "pañcabhirāheto" corrupt — the five-built body vs the unbounded pervader; the Bagchi print]

**2/4** - vyāpakatve sthitaḥ sākṣādyathā kṣīreṣu sarpiṣaḥ | kathitaṃ tattvabhirvastu jñānaratnaṃ sudurlabham ||
> Standing in pervasiveness directly — as ghee [is] in milk: the thing taught by the principles is the knowledge-jewel, very hard to obtain.

**2/5** - ātmāparavinirmuktaṃ cittaṃ caiva nirāśrayam | cittāśrayaṃ bhaveddvandvaṃ nirāśrayañ camauṣadam ||
> Free of self and other, the mind-stuff [is] supportless; the support-of-mind is the pair; the supportless is the remedy (auṣadha). [ADJUDICATED per R2: the auṣadha-emendation adopted — the jewel→remedy chain; the print's "mauṣadam" flagged, print pending]

**2/6** - manasastu vidhaṃ proktaṃ rājasantāmasantathā | sāttvikantu tṛtīyañca tribhirdharmeṇa lakṣaṇam ||
> The mind's kind is proclaimed the rajas and the tamas likewise; and the sāttvika third — by the three [the mind] is marked with the dharma.

**2/7** - tāmasaṃ cañcalaṃ kṣudraṃ rājasaṅgatirāgatiḥ | sāttvikaṃ tṛtīyaṃ jñeyaṃ dharmayuktaṃ sadā sthitam ||
> The tamasic is restless, low; the rajasic — coming and going; the sāttvika third is to be known as joined with the dharma, ever standing.

**2/8** - caturthantu manaścaiva kathayāmi viśeṣataḥ | guṇatrayavinirmuktaṃ sadā nirvāṇalakṣaṇam ||
> And the fourth mind I declare specifically: free of the three guṇas, ever the mark of nirvāṇa.

**2/9** - uttamottamacaturthantu yena saṃśuddhacetasā | sarvāgnivinayātītamindriyātītamavyayam ||
> The fourth, the best of the best — by which, with purified mind: beyond the restraint of all the fires, beyond the senses, the imperishable.

**2/10** - anābhyāsaṃ sadā tattvaṃ sarvacintāvivarjitam | cittābhāvavinirmuktaṃ nimittaṃ tattvalaukikam ||
> The principle [is] always without practice, free of all thought; freed even from the mind's non-being — the mark of the world-transcending principle. [ADJUDICATED: the both/neither — neither the mind nor its absence; T2's "even" carried]

**2/11** - abhāvaṃ bhāvanātītaṃ cittācittavivarjitam | cittādyacintitaṃ jñānamavācyajñānanirṇayam ||
> The non-state, beyond cultivation, free of mind and non-mind: the knowledge unthought from the mind's first — the determination of the inexpressible knowledge.

**2/12** - yathaiva sadātyuktāḥ tāvaccittaṃ sucañcalam | cañcalaṃ bhāvamityuktamacalaṃ muktilakṣaṇam ||
> So long as [the states] are ever-spoken, so long the mind is very restless: the restless is called the [world-]being; the unmoving is the mark of liberation.

**2/13** - cittāyuktaṃ yadā cittaṃ tadāsau cañcalaṃ smṛtam | cittātītaṃ yadā cittaṃ niścitamacalaṃ bhavet ||
> When the mind is joined with mind, then it is remembered as restless; when the mind is beyond mind, it becomes certain, unmoving.

**2/14** - sarvacintāvinirmuktamabhāve niścalaṃ bhavet | na yogādhyātacittastu na ca lakṣaṇasādhakam ||
> Free of all thought, in the non-state it becomes unmoving — not the yogic [mind], not the inner-mind, nor the work of the marks.

**2/15** - na nirodho bhavet tatra na kāyaśūnyameva ca | na bahirantamantasthaṃ na cānta ādimadhyagaḥ ||
> There is no suppression there, nor the body's voidness; it is not the outer, not the inner-dwelling, not inner — not [in] the beginning, the middle.

**2/16** - na tejo vāyurākāśaṃ pṛthivyā āpabhāvanā | na buddhirna cāhaṃkāro na mana indriyo na ca ||
> Not fire, wind, ether, earth, nor the water-cultivation; not the intellect, not the ego, not the mind, nor the sense.

**2/17** - na cittācittakastattvaṃ cintātītaṃ sthitaṃ sadā | na cintā yastu vijñeyā jñātavya kālaśāsanaḥ ||
> The principle is not mind-or-non-mind; it stands ever beyond thought. The one to be known is not thought — the Lord of time is to be known.

**2/18** - yo so niścintasarvajñaḥ samādhiparameśvaraḥ | jñātavyaṃ mokṣasadbhāvaṃ guptabhedaprakāśitam ||
> He — that one — the thoughtless all-knower, the supreme Lord of samādhi; the liberation-reality is to be known — made manifest by the hidden distinction. [ADJUDICATED: T2's "that one" — the demonstrative pointing to the fourth-mind knower]

**2/19** - saṃsārārṇavanirmuktaṃ kalpanātītagocaram | nirvikalpaṃ sadā jñānaṃ kathitaṃ jñānakārikam ||
> Free of the ocean of saṃsāra, beyond the reach of fabrication: the ever-construct-free knowledge is told [in] the Jñānakārikā.

**2/20** - cetanācetanā caiva kalpanā bhāvanāstathā | dhāraṇādharavinyāsacandrasūryāgnimaṇḍale ||
> The conscious and the unconscious, the fabrication and the cultivation — in the disc of moon, sun, and fire, the dhāraṇā's placement of the supports —

**2/21** - vāyurākāśastathā sṛtyāḥ brahmā rudrādidevatāḥ | sarve te kalpanā proktaṃ nirvikalpastathānyathā ||
> — wind, ether, and the streams, Brahmā and Rudra and the rest of the gods: all those are proclaimed fabrication; the construct-free is otherwise.

**2/22** - kalpanājñānayuktāstu bhramanti ghaṭayantravat | paripūrṇaṃ kathaṃ teṣāṃ siddhakramavivarjitam ||
> Those joined with the fabrication-knowledge wander like the potter's wheel; how [could there be] the full for them, devoid of the siddha-order? [ADJUDICATED: "order" = the graded siddha-path, NOT the Krama-school — the report's krama-warning applied]

**2/23** - patanti kṣipraṃ saṃsāre cittajñānena rañjitaḥ | na yānti ceha saṃsāre ye sthitā uttamaṃ padam ||
> Stained by the mind-knowledge, they quickly fall into saṃsāra; they do not go hence [from] saṃsāra, those who stand in the supreme state.

**2/24** - narake gamanantasya na svarge gamanantathā | pāśadvayavinirūpastu dharmādharmanibandhane ||
> No going to hell [for him], nor a going to heaven either — the one stripped of the two bonds, in the binding of merit-and-demerit.

**2/25** - jñānakhaḍgena chitvā pāśadvayanibandhanam | andhapāśaṃ yadā cittaṃ kuto gacchanti yoginaḥ ||
> Having cut the binding of the two bonds with the sword of knowledge — when the mind is the blind bond itself, where do the yogins go?

**2/26** - dharmādharmadvayaḥ pāśo patantyutpatanti ca | tena pāśena baddhastu bhuñjate karmasañcayam ||
> The two, merit and demerit, are the bond — they fall and rise again; bound by that bond, they enjoy the karma-accumulation.

**2/27** - dharmeṇa bhuñjate svargamadharmaṃ narakādiṣu | dharmādharmau tu dvau yo'sau trailokyasya nibandhanam ||
> By merit they enjoy heaven, by demerit [the states] of hell and the rest; the two — merit and demerit — that is the binding of the three worlds.

**2/28** - dharmādharmaṃ yadā tyaktaṃ kṣipraṃ muñcanti mānavāḥ | dharmā caiva adharmā dvau dvividhaṃ parikīrtitam ||
> When merit-and-demerit is abandoned, men quickly are released; merit and demerit, the two — the twofold is proclaimed.

**2/29** - sabāhyābhyantaracetaḥ dharmādharmapratiṣṭhitam | adhyātmajātasadbhāvaṃ yogināmevasaṃsthitaḥ ||
> The mind with outer and inner [objects] stands in merit-and-demerit; the reality born of the inner, stands only in the yogins.

**2/30** - laukikāsthalakarmañcādhyātmakasyedaṃ sūkṣmam | ādhyātmike caiva dharmādharmasthitiḥ sadā ||
> The worldly state's karma is gross; the inner one's is subtle — and in the inner [path] the standing in merit-and-demerit [is] always [transcended].

**2/31** - ūrddhvacāro bhaveddharmo'dvayasyo'dharmalakṣaṇam | dharmādharmakṣaye kṣīṇe mucyate sarvair bandhanaiḥ ||
> The upward-movement is the merit; of the non-dual, demerit is the mark. When merit-and-demerit is exhausted, one is released from all the bonds. [ADJUDICATED: the bandhanaiḥ-emendation — the paṭala's pāśa-language; the print's "dhanaiḥ" flagged, print pending]

**2/32** - dharmādharmadvayaṃ jñātvā punarjñānamavāpnuyāt | dharmādharmavicāro'yaṃ kathitaṃ jñānakārikaiḥ ||
> Having known the two, merit and demerit, one attains knowledge again; this discrimination of merit and demerit is told in the Jñānakārikās.

*Here ends, in the Jñānakārikā, the second paṭala called the Discrimination of Merit and Demerit.*

## Patala 3 - the caryadhikara

**3/1** - atha caryān pravakṣyāmi yogināmupajāyate | sa cetājñāna na sarvaḥ caitanyaṃ jñānavarjitam ||
> Now I will declare the conduct — arising for the yogins: that consciousness is not the knowing, is not the all — the consciousness free of knowledge. [ADJUDICATED: T2's syntax; the cetājñāna-dvandva (consciousness-and-knowing) recorded as the live alternative]

**3/2** - cetācetasamāyuktaṃ jñānatvaṃ samprakīrtitam | bhuktitattvaṃ yadā cetaṃ sa cettammuktilakṣaṇam ||
> Joined with conscious-and-unconscious, the knowing-ness is proclaimed; when the consciousness is the enjoyment-principle, then that is the mark of liberation.

**3/3** - cetācetasamāyuktaṃ caitanyaṃ śāśvataṃ padam | yogināṃ yogacintitamāryasaṃjñānavarjitam ||
> The consciousness joined with conscious-and-unconscious is the eternal state: the yogins' yoga-thought, free of the noble (ārya) knowledge.

**3/4** - yo mārgavinirmuktaṃ yogināṃ muktilakṣaṇam | ekaliṅge śmaśāne vā nadīnāṃ saṅgameṣu ca ||
> The path-free [state], the yogins' mark of liberation: at the single-liṅga, at the burning ground, at the confluences of rivers —

**3/5** - śūnyāgāre guhāvāse vṛkṣamūle tu catvare | mahodadhitaṭe caiva tripathe vāpi sādhakaḥ ||
> — in the empty house, in the cave-dwelling, at the tree-root, in the cross-roads, at the shore of the great ocean, or at the three-way road, the sādhaka —

**3/6** - nagnāste muktakeśāstu madirānandacetasaḥ | mālānirmālitā yogī ekākī bhramate sadā ||
> — naked, with loosened hair, with minds delighting in the wine: the yogi, garland-stripped, wanders ever alone.

**3/7** - liṅgantu kathayiṣyāmi yalliṅgaṃ kaulikaṃ smṛtam | layapūjāṣṭakaṃ yattaṃ liṅgantu sa carācaram ||
> The liṅga I will declare — that liṅga which is remembered as the Kaulika: the eightfold dissolution-worship — that liṅga is the moving-and-unmoving [all].

**3/8** - laya vai yāni sarveṣāṃ tena liṅgaṃ mudā kṛtam | etat kaulikaṃ yalliṅgaṃ na śailahemaraupyajam ||
> The dissolutions of all [things] — by that the liṅga is made with joy: this Kaulika liṅga is not born of stone, gold, or silver.

**3/9** - kathitaṃ dehajaṃ liṅgaṃ ekantu na dvitīyakam | ekaliṅgaṃ samākhyātaṃ śmaśānaṃ kathayāmi te ||
> The body-born liṅga is told — one only, no second. The single-liṅga is proclaimed; the burning-ground I will tell you:

**3/10** - śobhate ca yadā dehaḥ sarveṣāṃ ekadehinām | nisvāsasvāsasaṃyuktaṃ śmaśānaṃ parikīrtitam ||
> The body shines for all the embodied — [that is] joined with breath and out-breath: it is proclaimed the burning-ground.

**3/11** - adhorddhaṃ prāṇasañcāraḥ bahate caiva nityaśaḥ | ūrddhvacāre bhaved gaṅgā yadā cādho vyavasthitaḥ ||
> The life-breath's movement flows upward continually; in the upward-movement the Gaṅgā arises, when [it] is settled below. [ADJUDICATED: the yadā-emendation — the conditional restores the breath's turn; the print's "yasnā" flagged, print pending]

**3/12** - dvābhyāṃ melāpakaṃ yatna sa na metat prakīrtitā | aśarīraṃ yadā tattvaṃ svadehe sā sthitā yadi ||
> By the two, the melāpaka (the fusion) — with effort [OPEN: "sa na metat" corrupt]; [when] the principle is the bodiless, if it stands in one's own body — [ADJUDICATED: melāpaka transliterated + glossed — the KJN's siddhi-fusion, the concordance-verified]

**3/13** - śūnyaṃ niranñjanaṃ jñātvā mucyate nātra saṃśayaḥ | guhyaṃ tu kathayiṣyāmi guhye sacarācaram ||
> Knowing the void, the Stainless, one is released — no doubt here. The secret I will declare: in the secret [stands] the moving-and-unmoving.

**3/14** - guhyaṃ śarīramityuktaṃ vāsitaṃ jñānarāśinā | guhyāvāsaṃ sthito yogī caryā tasya na ucyate ||
> The secret is called the body, perfumed by the mass of knowledge; the yogi established in the secret-dwelling — no conduct is spoken for him.

**3/15** - vāsastu kathitaṃ divyaṃ vṛkṣamūlaṃ śṛṇu tathā | vṛkṣaḥ śarīramityuktaṃ pādādikaraśākhayoḥ ||
> The dwelling is told as the divine; hear the tree-root likewise: the body is called the tree — with feet and so on [as] the roots, the hands [as] the branches.

**3/16** - ūrddhvamūlaṃ bhavedvaktraṃ mūlave vṛkṣadhārakam | vṛkṣamūlaṃ tato jñātvā bāhyavṛkṣasya varjitam ||
> The mouth is the upward-root, the root [is] the tree's holder; knowing the tree-root thus, [one is] free of the outer tree. [X: "mūlave" — the inverted-tree of the body; the Upaniṣadic ūrdhvamūla transposed]

**3/17** - vṛkṣamūlaṃ samākhyātaṃ catvāraḥ śṛṇu sāmpratam | jayā ca vijayā caiva ajitā cāparājitā ||
> The tree-root is proclaimed — hear the four now: Jayā and Vijayā, Ajitā and Aparājitā — [ADJUDICATED: the names kept — the śakti-quartet, born of the four Mothers; cf. the ŚS 2.7's Ambā/Jyeṣṭhā/Vāmā/Raudrī]

**3/18** - catuḥśaktisamopetaṃ catvāraṃ mātrakodbhavam | catvāraṃ kathitaṃ divyaṃ mahodadhitaṭaḥ śṛṇu ||
> — joined with the four powers, born of the four Mother[-letters]; the four are told as divine — hear the shore of the great ocean:

**3/19** - bindustañca parāśaktiḥ taṭasthaś cārurūpiṇī | binducārusthitā nityaṃ taṭasthaṃ bindumaṇḍalam ||
> The bindu is the supreme power, standing at the shore, of lovely form; ever established in the lovely bindu, the disc of the bindu at the shore — [ADJUDICATED: T1's single-subject — the bindu IS the supreme power; the list-reading rejected]

**3/20** - taṭasthañca sthitā yogī taṭaṃ caiva prakīrtitam | candraṃ tyaktvā yadā yāti ādhāraṃ sūryamaṇḍalam ||
> — the yogi stands at the shore; the shore is proclaimed. When, leaving the moon, it goes to the sun-disc as the support —

**3/83** - yogapāragato yogī mahāpāravivarjitaḥ | pānayogāmṛtaṃ divyaṃ yogināṃ samprakīrtitam ||
