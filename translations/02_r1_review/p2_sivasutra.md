# P2 — Śivasūtra with Bhāskara's Vārttika

*Peer-review over the pass-1 readings (files: `sivasutra_bhaskara_1_1-1_5_three_readings.md`, `..._1_6-1_19_pass1.md`, `..._adhikara2_pass1.md`, `..._adhikara3_pass1.md`). Pass-1 files untouched. Varieties → most-likely verdict → natural commentary. Anchor: Dyczkowski, Aphorisms of Śiva (on disk).*

## 1.1 — caitanyam ātmā
**P1:** "Consciousness is the Self."
**Varieties:** a) "Consciousness is the Self" (bare identity) · b) "The Self is consciousness" (inversion for English weight) · c) "Consciousness is the Self — one's own essential nature."
**Most likely:** (a) — the anchor agrees; the inversion (b) is defensible but loses the sūtra's axiomatic forward-stance; (c) imports the Vārttika's content into the root, which our three-readings discipline forbids at the literal level.
**Commentary:** Muller-Ortega's foreword notes the "is" is our grammatical intrusion — the Sanskrit is "Consciousness—Self," a pointing. Our literal reading should ideally preserve that bare apposition; the pass-1 rendering stands with this note.

## 1.3 — yonivargaḥ kalāśarīram
**P1:** "The group of yonis is the body of the kalās" (with Bhāskara's four-śakti gloss; Kṣemarāja's māyā-reading kept separate).
**Varieties:** a) "The group of sources constitutes the body of obscuring energies" (anchor) · b) our literal · c) Kṣemarāja's "the group of the Womb [=māyā] with the body of obscuring energies."
**Most likely:** (a/b agree on grammar); the real fork is the *referent* of yoni — the four powers (Bhāskara, followed) vs māyā (Kṣemarāja). Our [D]-handling (keeping both) is the right method; the *scholarly* reading favors Bhāskara here since his gloss "yonayaḥ śaktayaḥ" is explicit in the text we translate.
**Commentary:** the four powers (Ambā, Jyeṣṭhā, Raudrī, Vāmā) return at ŚS 2.7's bracket as Mātṛkā's fourfold — the strongest internal evidence that Bhāskara's reading is the commentary's own spine.

## 1.13 — icchāśaktir umā kumārī (KSTS4: icchāśaktitamā kumārī)
**P1:** "The power of will is Umā, the maiden" (following the parenthetical variant).
**Correction (C1, applied here):** the printed **icchāśaktitamā** is Bhāskara's own reading; the umā-variant is Abhinavagupta/Kṣemarāja's. For a Bhāskara-translation the primary rendering should be:
**Varieties:** a) "The supreme power of will is the maiden" (icchāśaktitamā — "the most-will-power") · b) "The power of will is Umā, the maiden" (the Abhinava-tradition reading).
**Most likely:** (a) for our Bhāskara-focus, with (b) recorded as the Vimarśinī-tradition reading (per Dyczkowski's Exposition: "Ksemaraja acknowledges the validity of Bhaskara's reading... and adopts a variant reading").
**Commentary:** the kumārī-gloss (ku-mārayati — "she kills māyā," per Kṣemarāja) belongs to the (b)-tradition; under (a) the maiden is the unspent will — the will before it has "given birth" to manifestation. Both readings see the same goddess through different lenses.

## 2.1 — cittaṃ mantraḥ
**P1:** "The mind is Mantra."
**Varieties:** a) "The mind is Mantra" · b) "Mind is Mantra" · c) "The mind is the mantra" (with the definite article).
**Most likely:** (a) — the anchor's exact rendering; "Mantra" capitalized as a principle (our capitalization policy).
**Commentary:** the Vārttika's abhedopacāra (metonymic identity) is the sūtra's grammar: mind IS mantra because both are self-experiencing. The commentary should note that this is the *method* of the whole Śāktopāya: no technique outside consciousness (cf. Dyczkowski's Exposition on 1.1: "consciousness is known by being conscious").

## 2.3 — vidyāśarīrasattā mantrarahasyam
**P1:** "The vidyā's bodily existence is the mantra's secret."
**Varieties:** a) ours · b) "The secret of Mantra is the Being of the Body of Knowledge" (anchor's sūtra-list rendering) · c) "The mantra's secret is the vidyā's body-presence."
**Most likely:** (b) grammatically reorders (the predicate-complement structure is fluid); ours and (c) agree on the sense. The anchor's "Being of the Body of Knowledge" captures śarīra-sattā as an ontological claim (presence-as-body).
**Commentary:** the bracket's four-speech doctrine (parā→paśyantī→madhyamā→vaikharī) is the verse's real content: the mantra's "body" is speech itself descending. This is where the commentary will eventually quote TĀ 15's Śabdabrahman-material (Dyczkowski vol. 2, on disk).

## 2.7 — mātṛkācakrasaṃbodhaḥ
**P1:** "The awakening of the Wheel of Mātṛkā."
**Varieties:** a) ours · b) "The awakening of the Wheel of Matrka" (anchor).
**Most likely:** (a/b identical; transliteration policy only). No substantive fork.
**Commentary:** the bracket identifies Mātṛkā with **kriyāśakti** and her fourfold with the ŚS-1.3 catuṣṭaya — the cross-adhikāra identity the glossary records. The "awakening" (saṃbodha) turns all speech into mantra — the vidyā-śravaṇa claim in Trika form (cf. our Kubjikā files).

## 3.9 — nartaka ātmā
**P1:** "The Self is the dancer."
**Varieties:** a) ours ("dancer") · b) anchor ("The Self is the actor").
**Most likely:** both defensible; keep (a) for nartaka (literal) with the anchor's "actor" noted — the bracket's imagery is the actor (naṭa), which the glossary renders "actor" consistently.
**Commentary:** the actor-metaphor's fourth attestation in Bhāskara (1.2, 2.9, 3.9 + our Siddhitrayī) — the lineage's signature answer to divine agency-without-alienation.

## 3.19 — kavargādiṣu māheśvaryādyāḥ paśumātaraḥ
**P1:** "In the ka-varga and the rest, Māheśvarī and the others are the mothers of the bound."
**Varieties:** a) ours · b) "the mothers of the bound soul" vs "the mothers who bind."
**Most likely:** (a) — paśu as "the bound" (our glossary's ruling); the genitive "of the bound" is right (they are the paśu's mothers, governing his phoneme-world).
**Commentary:** the ghorā/aghora pair recurs from 1.4 — the same mothers, two faces (binding for the paśu, liberating for the pati). The domesti-cation lens (STUB-A) applies: the alphabet-mothers are the "wild" phonology domesticated into the Trika's maṇḍala.

## 3.44 — nāsikāntarmadhyasaṃyamāt
**P1:** "From the restraint in the middle of the nose — what is there in the left, right, and suṣumnā [channels]?"
**Varieties:** a) ours · b) anchor: "(The movement of the vital breath is stilled) by concentrating on the centre within the nose. Of what use (then) are the left and right channels or Susumna?"
**Most likely:** (b) supplies the understood verb (the stilling of the breath); ours keeps the bare sūtra. Both correct; the commentary-version should print the anchor's completion in parentheses (the Dyczkowski convention).
**Commentary:** the verse cites the **Svacchandatantra** by name (the bracket) — the author's named source, in our library (M00091). The "middle suffices" doctrine is the āṇava-path's anti-technique move: the heart, not the channels.

## 3.45 — bhūyaḥ syātpratimīlanam
**P1:** "Again there is the re-closing [into the Lord]."
**Varieties:** a) ours ("re-closing") · b) "the closing again" · c) "reabsorption."
**Most likely:** (a) — pratimīlana as the re-closing (the opposite of unmeṣa, the opening); the bracket's "return to his own state" confirms.
**Commentary:** the Vārttika's close is the full circle: the world-drama (the naṭa of 3.9) ends in the re-closing — the text's own image of the return. This is the commentary's final note for the whole work.
