# I. Kulānanda

## v. 1 — kailāsaśikharāsīnaṃ... ekānte jñānamuttamam
**P1:** "asks in private for the supreme knowledge."
**Most likely:** ours — the frame is the Matsyendra-bundle's standard (the KJN's Pārvatī-questions); "ekānte" (in private) is the secrecy-trope of the transmission.
**Commentary:** the Kulānanda's opening question (the purabheda — the "city-fracture") is the same siddhi-practice register as the KJN 4 — the bundle's texts teach the same body, in different orders.

## v. 4 — pāśastobhañca vedhanaṃ dhūnanaṃ kampanaṃ khecaraṃ samarasaṃ
**P1:** the six-fold siddhi-list.
**Most likely:** ours — every term is attested in the bundle: pāśastobha (KJN 4/2), khecara (KJN 5's sky-going), samarasa (Akulavīra's terminus), dhūnana/kampana (KJN 4, the fumigation and the trembling).
**Commentary:** the verse is a checklist of the bundle's practice-lexicon — the commentary's job: map each term to its other attestations (the glossary does this).

## v. 27 — palitāstambhayeddevi māsamekena suvrate
**P1:** "he stops the gray-hair, O Devī, within a single month."
**Most likely:** ours — the gray-hair-stopping is the palitastambha of the KJN 7 (the same practice, same term).
**Commentary:** the two texts treat the same practice with the same claim (the KJN's "within six months," the Kulānanda's "within one month") — the shared siddhi-arithmetic of the bundle.

**P1:** "in the middle of the yoni (bhaga), below the yoni, the fire... the women become overwhelmed within a single moment."

**Commentary:** the yoni's two faces (STUB-D) meet here: the literal practice-object (this verse, the Kubjikā 9) vs the doctrinal source (the ŚS 1.3). The commentary must hold both without flattening.

## v. 55–58 — the saṃgama-mokṣa and the samarasa
**P1:** "...[the knowledge] that is the cause of liberation from union [X]... the equal-taste, the lord-and-servant [pair]."
**Varieties:** a) ours · b) "the union's liberation" (saṃgama as the kula's union with the śakti) · c) "the contact's liberation" (sparśa).
**Most likely:** (b) — the verse's drift (the samarasa + the lord-servant pair) is the kula's union-doctrine; the "saṃgama" is the śakti-union whose knowledge liberates.
**Commentary:** the lord-and-servant (prabhu-bhṛtya) pair inside the samarasa = the non-dual identity's social form: the Lord and the devotee as one taste — the kula's answer to the bhakti-register.

# II. Unmattabhairavahṛdaya

## The kīlaka-section (K1–K11)
**P1:** the nailing-doctrine (the Vedic/muni/sun-moon nailings).
**Most likely:** ours — the kīlaka as the mantra's fixing (the nailing that pins the deity) is the standard mantra-śāstra term (cf. the Kubjikā 4's kīlaya-kīlaya and 8's daśadik-kīlaka).
**Commentary:** the Unmattabhairava's kīlaka-section + the Kubjikā's kīlaka-practices = the batch's "nailing" complex: the power must be nailed to be held — the inverse of the Akulavīra's "no restraint" (manorodhaṃ na kartavyam). The two registers (the kula's technique vs the akula's non-technique) are the bundle's dialectic.

## The 12 names (S9–S11)
**P1:** the names' series (Unmatta, Bhairava, Bhīmarūpā, Yoginīpati, Mātṛkānātha, Nirlajja, Paramānanda, Aṣṭabhairavāḥ, Gaṇanātha, Ḍākinīhata, Ghorā, Sātvikī).
**Varieties:** a) "the eighth — the eight Bhairavas" · b) "the eighth, 'Aṣṭabhairava'" (a single name).
**Most likely:** (a) — the list's grammar ("aṣṭamaṃ 'ṣṭabhairavāḥ" — the eighth: the Eight-Bhairavas) gives a group-name; the eighth name is the octad itself.
**Commentary:** the names form a theophany-catalog: the mad one, the shameless, the supreme-bliss, the eight-fold — the Unmattabhairava as the Kālīkula's antinomian Lord (the nirlajja — the shameless — is the vīra's own mark).

## The hṛdaya-section (H1–H54)
**P1:** the heart-mantra's frame with the Vārāṇasī-colophon and the copyist's date.
**Most likely:** ours — the Vārāṇasī-geography (the Unmattabhairava of Vārāṇasī) + the copyist-date (saṃvat 2972 ≈ 1915 CE) = the text's late, living transmission.
**Commentary:** the copyist's colophon (the "servant of Unmattabhairava, Śarman," dated 1915) is the text's last voice — a modern scribe copying the mad Lord's heart-mantra. The commentary should end the Unmattabhairava with this human signature.
