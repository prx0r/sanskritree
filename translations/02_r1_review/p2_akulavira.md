# P2 — Akulavīratantra

*Peer-review over the pass-1 readings (`kaula_frame_pass1.md`, `kaula_frame_pass1_cont.md`, `akulavira_completion_pass1.md`). Anchor: the archive.org Nātha translation (on disk: `sources/akulavira_tantra_eng_archive.org.txt`).*

## Maṅgala — mīnasahajānanda... sarvamādhāragambhīram
**P1:** "...the profound support of all (sarvamādhāragambhīram)."
**Varieties:** a) ours — "the profound support of all" · b) archive — "in whom all the ādharas are deep and still" (ādhāra = the cakras, the Nātha reading) · c) "deep in all the supports."
**Most likely:** (b) is the tradition-internal reading (the Nātha ādhāra-terminology — the text's own vv. 15–18 negate the ādhāras, so the maṅgala's positive use is deliberate); ours is grammatically simpler. For a Nātha-context commentary, (b) deserves the note; the pass-1 (a) stands as the literal.
**Commentary:** the maṅgala's "born from his own body" (svakīyāṅgasamudbhavam) = the sahaja-claim in epithet-form: the teaching is the teacher's own nature, not an acquired doctrine.

## v. 14 — svabhāvamatimataṃ śāntaṃ mano yasya manomayam
**P1:** "...the tranquil state of one's own nature, thought, whose mind is made of mind" [X].
**Varieties:** a) "whose mind is made of mind, the tranquil, the thought-of-own-nature" · b) emend "svabhāvamati-mataṃ" → "svabhāvam upagataṃ" (gone to own-nature) — the simplest repair · c) keep the print.
**Most likely:** (c) with the (b)-emendation noted — the printed text (confirmed in the archive's Devanagari) is likely a scribal wobble for a compound meaning "the tranquil state of own-nature." The verse's drift (bhāvābhāva-free, rising-setting-free) supports "own-nature" as the terminus.
**Commentary:** the manomaya (made-of-mind) paradox: the state is both "of mind" and beyond mind — the unmana-complex the Akulavīra shares with the KJN's unmanībhāva (14/83).

## v. 23 — viṃśātmaka... pudgala
**P1:** "...where this twenty-formed one and the person do not exist" [X: the "twenty" unidentified].
**Varieties:** a) "the twenty-formed" = the twenty tattvas of the impure creation (kalā through earth: the 24 minus the 4 pure?) · b) "the twentieth" (a numerical scheme) · c) a garbled viṃśā-varga.
**Most likely:** (a) — the Sāṅkhya-adjacent counting is the best fit: the negation says the principle-counting itself (the tattva-arithmetic) is absent in the Akulavīra. Marked [X] still — needs the Bagchi print.
**Commentary:** the verse's pair (the twenty-formed + the person) = the tattva-scheme + the jīva — both negated — the same double-negation as the KJN 17's jīva-doctrine (there affirmed as nirañjana, here dissolved).

## v. 39 — nibaddhañca malakṣaṇam
**P1:** "and [yet] bound, of the nature of the impurity" [X].
**Varieties:** a) ours · b) emend to "nibaddhañca nirlakṣaṇam" (and the bound, the mark-less) — parallel to the verse's other nir-compounds (nirākula, nirvikalpa) · c) "bound and marked."
**Most likely:** (b) — the verse's anuprāsa (nir-... nir-...) strongly favors a nir-compound; "nirlakṣaṇam" (mark-less) fits the apophatic register perfectly. Emendation recommended for pass-3.
**Commentary:** the line "advaitam acalaṃ śāntaṃ... nibaddhaṃ [nir]lakṣaṇam" — the non-dual, unmoving, tranquil, and the bound... mark-less: the Akulavīra includes the bound state within the negation — the paśu too is dissolved.

## v. 56 — kaulamārge dvau santi
**P1:** "On the Kaula path there are two: the sahaja and the [kṛtaka]."
**Varieties:** a) ours · b) archive: "the path of the Kaula is of two types — the artificial (kritaka) and the sahaja (spontaneous)."
**Most likely:** (b's content) — the two limbs are kṛtakā (the artificial observances) and sahaja (the spontaneous, samarasa). Our pass-1 noted the lacuna; the archive's rendering confirms the pair (its verse 57 names kṛtakā explicitly).
**Commentary:** this is the lineage's self-diagnosis (STUB-F): the kula's own theory of its two registers — the domesticated shell (kṛtaka) vs the wild core (sahaja). The KJN's samaya-teaching is the kṛtaka-limb taught seriously; the Akulavīra dissolves it.

## v. 99 — sārātsārataraṃ param
**P1:** "the more-essential-than-the-essential, the supreme."
**Most likely:** ours, confirmed — the cross-text superlative (Kubjikā 1.3/1.7, KJN 25/2) in its Akulavīra attestation.
**Commentary:** three texts, one superlative — the batch's shared name for the supreme teaching. The glossary must register it.

## v. 103 — akulavīraṃ vindanti kāntamānaiḥ kumārikāḥ
**P1:** "The virgins find the Akulavīra by their beloved measures" [X].
**Varieties:** a) ours · b) "the maidens find the Akulavīra, free of the directions' garments, free of place and color" (reading kāntamāna as a corruption of the following epithets) · c) the kumārīs as the kula's virgin-worshippers (cf. Kubjikā 7's kumārī-pūjā).
**Most likely:** (c) as the *contextual* reading — the verse sits between the śāstra/kumārīśruti image (v. 102) and the apophatic epithets (vv. 104–105); the "virgins" are likely the kula's ritual-maidens, and the verse claims they attain the Akulavīra by the "beloved measures" (the kula's own worship). [X] remains — needs the print.
**Commentary:** if (c) is right, the verse is a striking democratization: the maidens (not the sages) find the Stainless — the same inversion as the KJN's yoginīs and the Kubjikā's kumārīs.

## v. 106 — na jñātaṃ yena dehasthaṃ sa muktaḥ
**P1:** "...by whom, dwelling in the body, it is known [X: the print's "na jñātaṃ" — the negative]."
**Varieties:** a) "by whom it is NOT known, he is freed" (the print — but then the apodosis is paradoxical) · b) emend to "yena jñātaṃ" (by whom known) — the natural reading · c) "without knowing which, none is freed" (restructuring).
**Most likely:** (b) — the print's na is a scribal slip (the parallel verses 105 and 107 both affirm knowledge-as-release). Emendation recommended.
**Commentary:** the verse's claim — the Akulavīra "dwelling in the body" (dehastham) known = freedom — is the same body-location as the Kubjikā's nirañjanapada and the KJN's kapālabheda: the Stainless is not elsewhere but in the body.

## v. 130 — viśākhyaḥ mayūrākṣo
**P1:** "he is Viśākha, Mayūrākṣa, the Arhat, the Buddha."
**Varieties:** a) ours · b) archive: "He is Vishakhya (Skanda), he is Mayuraksha (Indra)" (the translator's glosses).
**Most likely:** (a) with the archive's glosses noted: Viśākha = Skanda (the six-faced, cf. the KJN's ṣaṇmukha), Mayūrākṣa = "peacock-eyed" (Indra's epithet). The verse's inclusivism (the god-list including the Buddha, the Arhat) is the text's most ecumenical moment.
**Commentary:** the deity-list's inclusivism mirrors the school-list's exclusivism (vv. 142–145): the gods are all one in the equal-taste, but the schools are all vain — the Akulavīra's theology of unity and its epistemology of exclusion.

---
**P2 verdict (Akulavīra):** three emendations recommended (v. 39 nirlakṣaṇam, v. 106 yena jñātaṃ, v. 14's compound), one contextual re-reading (v. 103), one confirmation (v. 56's kṛtakā/sahaja), one anchor-gloss (v. 130). Commentary-seeds: the sahaja-body (maṅgala), the democratization (v. 103), the inclusivism/exclusivism pair (v. 130).
