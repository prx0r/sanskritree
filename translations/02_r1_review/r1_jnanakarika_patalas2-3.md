## Patala 2 - the dharmadharma-vicara

**2/3 - pañcabhirāheto deho**
**Varieties:** a) "the body is heard [to be built] of the five" (pañcabhir + āheto as the corrupt pāda) · b) emend to "pañcabhūtātmako deho" - the body's five-element essence (the standard doctrine) · c) "pañcabhī rāheto" - "by the five, of the chariot" (the body-as-chariot reading).
**Most likely:** (a) with the corruption flagged - the verse's contrast (the five-built body vs the unbounded pervader) is the same apophatic structure as the bundle's; the emendation (b) is attractive but the print's pāda keeps the [X].
**Commentary:** the body-as-five vs the pervader-unbounded is the mokṣa-path's first negation - the same "not the elements" as KJN 2/16; note the text affirms the five here and negates them at 2/16.

**2/5 - nirāśrayañ ca mauṣadam**
**Varieties:** a) "the supportless is the medicine (mauṣadam)" - the [X]-flagged oddity · b) emend to "nirāśrayaṃ cauṣadham" (auṣadha = the remedy) · c) the medicinal metaphor (cf. 2/4's jñānaratna-jewel, 2/10's nimitta).
**Most likely:** (b) - auṣadha (the remedy) is the natural partner of the jewel-image; the T1's [X] stands pending the Bagchi print.
**Commentary:** the 2/4-2/5 chain (jewel → remedy) is the text's self-framing: the knowledge is the healing - link to the bundle's "free of all diseases" language.

**2/10 - cittābhāvavinirmuktaṃ**
**Varieties:** a) "free of the mind's absence [too]" - the subtle double-negation (neither the mind nor its absence) · b) "freed from the non-mind" (cittābhāva as a state, the nirodha) · c) "free of the mind's dissolution" (the samādhi-term).
**Most likely:** (a) - the verse's ladder (anābhyāsa → sarvacintā → cittābhāva) is the progressive stripping; the "absence of mind" as a *trap* is the text's own dialectic (cf. 2/12-13's citta/cittātīta pair, 2/17's "na cittācittakaḥ").
**Commentary:** the T1's reading is the strongest - the text negates the mind's presence AND its absence, the bundle's characteristic both/neither apophasis.

**2/18 - yo so niścintasarvajñaḥ**
**Varieties:** a) "he is the thoughtless all-knower" · b) "that very one, the thoughtless all-knower" (so = saḥ, the demonstrative) · c) "the one who, being thoughtless, knows all."
**Most likely:** (a) - the mokṣa-thesis's subject; the "he" is the knowledge-state itself, the guṇātīta knower of 2/8-9.
**Commentary:** the verse crowns paṭala 2's ladder: the fourth mind (2/8) → the thoughtless all-knower (2/18) → the knowledge-alone thesis (1/11). The paṭala's doctrinal peak.

**2/25 - andhapāśaṃ yadā cittaṃ**
**Varieties:** a) "when the mind is the blind bond" · b) "when the blind [ignorance's] bond is the mind" · c) "andha-pāśam" (the bond of blindness, the avidyā).
**Most likely:** (a) with the note - the sword of knowledge cuts the two bonds, then the question: "if the mind IS the blind bond, where do the yogins go?" The mind-as-bond is the paśu-state.
**Commentary:** the sword-of-knowledge (jñānakhaḍga) is the text's signature weapon-image - the C1 should carry the martial metaphor (knowledge as the cutting, not the accumulating).

**2/31 - mucyate sarvairdhanaiḥ**
**Varieties:** a) "released from all the wealths (dhanaiḥ)" - the T1's [X] · b) emend to "sarvair bandhanaiḥ" ("from all the bonds") - the metrical/doctrinal fix · c) "dhanaiḥ" as the guṇa-treasures (the sattva/rajas/tamas of 2/6-7).
**Most likely:** (b) - the "bonds" is the doctrine's word (2/26-28's pāśa-language); the T1's [X] is right to flag "wealths" as the print's wobble.
**Commentary:** the emendation aligns the verse with the paṭala's own pāśa-terminology - R2 should check the Bagchi print's actual reading.

## Patala 3 - the caryā

**3/1 - sa cetājñāna na sarvaḥ caitanyaṃ jñānavarjitam**
**Varieties:** a) "this consciousness is not [mere] knowing; not all - the consciousness free of knowledge" · b) "this consciousness, not-knowing, not-all" · c) the cetājñāna compound as "consciousness-and-knowledge" (a dvandva).
**Most likely:** (a) - the verse opens the caryā's own apophasis: the consciousness is NOT the knowing, is NOT the all - it is the knowledge-free. Mirrors paṭala 2's "na cittācittakaḥ."
**Commentary:** the caryā's opening negation (consciousness ≠ knowing) is the same both/neither structure - tie 3/1 to 2/17's "na cintā."

**3/11 - ūrddhvacāre bhaved gaṅgā yasnā cādho vyavasthitaḥ**
**Varieties:** a) "in the upward-movement the Gaṅgā arises; [when] it is settled below [X]" · b) emend "yasnā" → "yadā" ("when it is settled below") · c) the Gaṅgā as the ascending nectar (the haṭha-yoga's nādī-language).
**Most likely:** (b) - the yadā-emendation restores the verse's conditional structure (cf. 3/10's breath, 3/12's union); the Gaṅgā = the upward stream.
**Commentary:** the Gaṅgā-image is the body-geography's first river - the caryā's interiorized sacred space (the body as the tīrtha, cf. the Kaulārcanadīpikā's "kula-Gaṅgā"). Link the two Gaṅgā-senses.

**3/12 - dvābhyāṃ melāpakaṃ**
**Varieties:** a) "the union of the two, with effort" · b) "by the two, the melāpaka" - melāpaka as a technical term (the fusion) · c) the prāṇa/apāna meeting (the body-yoga's kumbhaka).
**Most likely:** (b) with the KJN-evidence - the concordance shows the KJN's melāpaka = the siddhi-fusion ("siddhimelāpakaṃ," "melāpakaṃ labdhvā yoginīsiddhiḥ") - the term is technical in this lineage, not a generic "union."
**Commentary:** the concordance verifies the T1's cross-reference: the melāpaka is the bundle's fusion-terminology (the siddhi's attainment) - the same term the Kuladīpikā's kula/akula alternation presupposes.

**3/16 - ūrddhvamūlaṃ bhavedvaktraṃ mūlave vṛkṣadhārakam**
**Varieties:** a) "the mouth is the upward-root, the root [is] the tree's holder" · b) emend "mūlave" → "mūlato" ("from the root") · c) the inverted-tree (ūrdhvamūla) as the body - the Upaniṣadic avakṣāṇa-tree.
**Most likely:** (a) with the emendation-note - the inverted tree of the body: the mouth the root above, the root (the base) holding the tree.
**Commentary:** the inversion (the root above) is the caryā's signature - the interiorized geography turns the world-tree into the body-tree; collect the Upaniṣadic antecedent.

**3/17 - jayā ca vijayā caiva ajitā cāparājitā**
**Varieties:** a) the four powers as proper names (Jayā, Vijayā, Ajitā, Aparājitā) · b) the four as epithets ("the victorious, the triumphant, the unconquered, the unvanquished").
**Most likely:** (a) - the names are the established śakti-list; the verse's "catvāro mātrakodbhavam" (born of the four Mothers) makes them the letter-powers.
**Commentary:** the four powers are the same śakti-catuṣṭaya as the ŚS 2.7's bracket (Ambā, Jyeṣṭhā, Vāmā, Raudrī) under different names - R2 should check the cross-tradition identity.

**3/19 - bindustañca parāśaktiḥ taṭasthaś cārurūpiṇī**
**Varieties:** a) "the bindu is the supreme power, standing at the shore, of lovely form" · b) "bindu, śakti, taṭastha, cāru-rūpiṇī" (a four-item list) · c) the taṭastha as "the neutral/indifferent" (the Sāṃkhya's middle term).
**Most likely:** (a) - the bindu as the supreme power (the glossary's bindu = the seed-point of the pure path); the "shore" (taṭa) is the ocean's edge - the caryā's geography, not the Sāṃkhya term.
**Commentary:** the bindu-as-parāśakti is the same identification as the ŚS 2.7's bracket (the bindu's prollāsa); the taṭastha (shore) is the body-geography's liminal point.

**3/78 - tripañcakaṃ**
**Varieties:** a) "the three-and-five" (the three guṇas + the five elements) - the T1's reading · b) "the fifteen" (tripañcaka as a count) · c) "the three-five-fold" (a technical division).
**Most likely:** (a) - the verse's own gloss ("sattvarajastamaś caiva ekatve tu yathā sthitam" - the guṇas standing in unity) confirms the three guṇas; the five are supplied by the paṭala's element-references.
**Commentary:** the guṇas-in-unity is the same doctrine as 2/8's "guṇatrayavinirmuktaṃ" - the caryā affirms in the body what paṭala 2 negated of the mind.

**3/82 - ṣaḍbhirmukto yadā yogī muktakeśaḥ sa ucyate**
**Varieties:** a) "when the yogi is freed from the six, he is called the loosened-hair" · b) the six as the six foes (the kāma, krodha...) · c) the six as the six cakras.
**Most likely:** (a) with the note - the six foes is the standard list (the ṣaḍ-ripu), the likely referent; the loosened-hair (muktakeśa) as the freedom-sign.
**Commentary:** the muktakeśa (loosened hair) is the Kaula yogin's mark (cf. the Jñānakārikā 3/6's own "muktakeśāḥ") - the paṭala's caryā-images (naked, loosened-hair, lone) are the same Kaula signs as the Kulapradīpa's bhoga-yoga.

**3/84 - madirāmatta ucyate**
**Varieties:** a) "the one drunk with wine (madirā)" · b) the madirā as the kula-nectar (the internal wine) · c) the intoxicated as the āveśa-state.
**Most likely:** (b) - the caryā's register is internal (the body-geography); the madirā here is the kula-nectar of the ascending stream, not the literal liquor - cf. the Kaulārcanadīpikā's "madirā jñānadā."
**Commentary:** the wine-words of the Kaula (madirā, āsava, surā) carry the same ambiguity as the yoni - the internal sense is primary in the caryā; the C1 should carry the ambiguity honestly.

**3/85 - nairmālyamālino yogī**
**Varieties:** a) "the yogi wearing the stripped-garland (nairmālya-mālin)" - the T1's reading · b) "the garland-stripped one" (the na + mālya as the negation) · c) the nirmālya (the offered-garland) as the ritually-cast-off.
**Most likely:** (c) - the nirmālya (the discarded offering-garland) is the standard term; the "stripped-garland" yogi wears what the rite has cast off - the Kaula's use of the leftover.
**Commentary:** the nirmālya-image is the Kaula's consecration-through-the-discarded - link to the paṭala's śmaśāna/body-geography (the impure as the pure).

**3/86 - ekākīmatithiryastu bhramite pravadet sadā**
**Varieties:** a) "he who would ever speak [as] the guest while wandering" · b) the atithi as the welcome-stranger (the "guest" = the wanderer's own state) · c) emend "atithi" → "asahāya" (the companionless - the verse's own gloss "asaṃhāyo bhramate dhīraḥ").
**Most likely:** (a) with the note - the verse's second half glosses the ekākī (the lone one) as the companionless wanderer; the atithi (guest) is the wanderer's self-image - the yogin as the world's guest, owning nothing.
**Commentary:** the ekākī/atithi is the caryā's social form of the supportless (nirāśraya) of paṭala 2 - the commentary should collect the social-images (lone, guest, naked) as the āśraya-negation made social.

**3/87 - ekākī tu samākhyātā śāstraṃ milakārikā**
**Varieties:** a) "the lone one is proclaimed - the treatise, the union-kārikā (milakārikā?)" - the T1's [X] · b) emend to "śāstraṃ mūlakārikā" ("the treatise, the root-kārikā") · c) "milakārikā" as the melāpaka-related (the union-kārikā of 3/12's fusion).
**Most likely:** (b) - the "root-kārikā" (mūlakārikā) names the text's own genre (the Jñānakārikā as the root-verses); the [X] stands pending the print, but the melāpaka-reading (c) is doctrinally interesting.
**Commentary:** the self-naming (the text calls itself the root-kārikā) is the paṭala's close - the caryā's teaching summarized as the "lone one" and the "root-verses"; the commentary should note the text's genre-consciousness.
