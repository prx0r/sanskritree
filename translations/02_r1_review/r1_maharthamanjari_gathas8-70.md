## g.8–12 — the heart-pulse and the prakāśa-lamp

**g.10 — vimarshadashAmukhAnItadahyamAnamalAlitailavichChardaH**
**Varieties:** a) "the malāli-oil's flow, drawn through the ten-reflexions' mouth, burning" · b) the variant: "by the ten-reflexions' mouth, the malāli-oil's flow is burnt" · c) the malāli (the jasmine-garland) as the sense-objects, the "oil" as the experience.
**Most likely:** (a) with the variant noted — the prakāśa's upward blaze fed by the reflexion's oil; the burning of the sense-flow is the lamp's fuel. The Parimala's gloss should decide the subject.
**Commentary:** the lamp-image — the prakāśa burning the world's oil into light — is the A-thesis made vivid; the C1 should carry the lamp-image.

**g.12 — pR^ithvIparamashivayoH pratyAhAre**
**Varieties:** a) "in the withdrawal of the earth and the supreme-Śiva" (the prakāśa's supreme-meaning) · b) "between the earth [tattva] and the supreme-Śiva [the tattva]" (the whole tattva-range as the withdrawal's span).
**Most likely:** (b) — the withdrawal spans the earth-tattva to the supreme-Śiva; the mutual-distinction within that span is the heart's reflexion-upsurge. The report's prakāśa/vimarśa pair governs.
**Commentary:** the vimarśa-unmeṣa (the reflexion-upsurge) as the "distinction" — the A-thesis's dynamism between the poles.

## g.13–18 — the powers

**g.15 — j~nAnaM kriyeti dvayorapi prathamonmeShe**
**Varieties:** a) "in the first-upsurge of the two [knowledge and action], the god is Sadāśiva" · b) "knowledge, action — of the two, in the first-upsurge, Sadāśiva the god" · c) the ullekha (the tracing) as the second's specific.
**Most likely:** (a) — the two powers' upsurge-ladder: the first-upsurge = Sadāśiva, the second's tracing = Īśvara. The report's śakti-dossier (the differentiated powers).
**Commentary:** the tattva-ladder of the powers — the same descent-chain as the KJN's jñāna ⊃ kriyā ⊃ icchā.

**g.17 — mAyeti lokapateH paramasvatantrasya mohanI shaktiH**
**Varieties:** a) "the deluding power of the supremely free world-lord" · b) the māyā as the power's own self-delusion in the world's appearing.
**Most likely:** (a) — the māyā is the free Lord's deluding power, NOT an external illusion-principle; the report's māyā/kañcuka note.
**Commentary:** the free one's māyā — the same non-illusionist reading as the report's "finite limitation is not yet bondage."

## g.21–25 — the karaṇas

**g.21 — AkarShantIdantAM tatrAhantAM chAtrArpayanti**
**Varieties:** a) "draw the this-ness and offer the I-ness" · b) the variant: "and the I-ness" (the both-offered).
**Most likely:** (a) — the karaṇas draw the "this" into the "I" — the report's this-I transition (the A→B), in the Krama's instrument-language.
**Commentary:** this is the ownership-argument's Krama-form — the "this" grounded in the "I" (cf. the APS's so 'ham).

**g.25 — styAnasya kramavashAdikShurasasyeva**
**Varieties:** a) "by the order of the thickening, like the sugar-cane-juice's" · b) the śiva-prakāśa's own thickening (the world's condensation into the elements).
**Most likely:** (a) with the image carried — the elements' sweetness (the prakāśa's presence) persists through the thickening; the styāna as the condensation-process.
**Commentary:** the elements as sugar-lumps in the syrup — the prakāśa's immanence in the thick world.

## g.26–32 — the yāmala and the māyā

**g.27 — yadadhvanAM cha ShaTkaM tatra prakAshArthalakShaNamardham | vimarshashabdasvabhAvamardhamiti shivasya yAmalollAsaH**
**Varieties:** a) "the half is of the prakāśa-meaning's mark; the half of the reflexion-word's nature" · b) the six courses split: the three prakāśa-halves vs the three reflexion-halves.
**Most likely:** (a) — the yāmala (the pair) as the prakāśa/reflexion split of the adhvans; the report's yāmala/pair-register.
**Commentary:** the yāmala as the fundamental pair — the same as the KMT's yāmala and the TĀ's "tadubhayayāmala" (our TĀ 1.1).

**g.32 — kaH sadbhAvavisheShaH kusumAdbhavati gaganakusumasya**
**Varieties:** a) "what real distinction arises of the sky-flower from the [real] flower?" · b) the sky-flower as the mere-appearance (the report's ābhāsa).
**Most likely:** (a) — the distinction's nullity: the world's appearing is the common flashing (sphuraṇa), the real-flower vs the sky-flower differ only in the bhāva. The report's ābhāsa-dossier.
**Commentary:** the sphuraṇa (flashing) as common to all — the report's A-thesis (the manifest as such).

## g.33–41 — the pīṭha-arithmetic

**g.39 — sR^iShTau dasha kalAH sthitau dvAvishatirbhavanti shaktayaH | ekAdasha saMhAre trayodasha tAsturIyaparvaNi**
**Varieties:** a) "in creation the ten kalās; in maintenance the twenty-two; in dissolution the eleven; the thirteen in the fourth-level" · b) the counts as the Krama's own goddess-arithmetic.
**Most likely:** (a) — the kalā-counts across the four operations; the Parimala should verify each count against the pīṭha-schema.
**Commentary:** the Krama's arithmetic of the operations — the same counted-goddesses as the daśākhaṇḍa (the ten) in the Mahānayaprakāśa.

**g.40 — ShoDashAdhikA devI**
**Varieties:** a) "the sixteen-and-more goddess" · b) "the seventeenth" (ṣoḍaśādhikā as the sixteenth-plus-one).
**Most likely:** (a) with the note — the sixteen-plus (the Krama's khecarī-count); the concordance confirms ṣoḍaśādhikā (43 occurrences).
**Commentary:** the sixteen-and-more — the goddess's count beyond the sixteen, the report's khecarī-dossier's sixteen-part goddess.

## g.42–50 — the rites and the vāc

**g.46 — kulabindutarpaNaM**
**Varieties:** a) "the kula-bindu's libation" · b) "the libation at the kula's point."
**Most likely:** (a) — the kulabindu (the point of the kula) as the libation's target; the concordance confirms kulabindu (6 occurrences — rare, the text's own term).
**Commentary:** the kulabindu — the Kaula's seed-point, the same bindu as the glossary's.

**g.50 — vaikhari(kA) nAma kriyA j~nAnamayI bhavati madhyamA vAk | ichChA punaH pashyantI sUkShmA**
**Varieties:** a) "the vaikharī is the action; the madhyamā made of knowledge; the will the paśyantī, the subtle" · b) the four-speech ladder (parā/paśyantī/madhyamā/vaikharī).
**Most likely:** (b) — the four-speech's ladder, with the vaikharī = action, madhyamā = knowledge, paśyantī = will; the report's visarga/phonemic dossier.
**Commentary:** the vāc-ladder's mapping onto the powers — the same four-speech as the TĀ 15 and the Mahānayaprakāśa's commentary.

## g.51–71 — the yogi's states and the close

**g.53 — kramiko bhavati na devaH**
**Varieties:** a) "the god is not sequential (kramika)" · b) the kramika as "subject to the sequence [of states]."
**Most likely:** (b) — the god is not subject to the sequence (the krama of the states); the report's krama-dossier: here the krama is the temporal sequence, negated of the eternal.
**Commentary:** the "kramika" negation — the god beyond the sequence, the Krama's own term turned against the temporal.

**g.58 — ye kulakumbhasudhAsavapAnamahAmakhotsave**
**Varieties:** a) "the great sacrifice-festival of the kula-jar's nectar-wine-drinking" · b) the kula-kumbha (the jar of the kula) as the ritual-vessel.
**Most likely:** (a) — the kula's wine-sacrifice (the Kaula's makāra-rite); the "alternative-seed-tasters" who are bold to instruct.
**Commentary:** the Kaula's wine-rite in the Krama's text — the same makāra-language as the Kaulārcanadīpikā; the C1 should note the Kaula-link.

**g.68 — manthAnabhairavo devaH**
**Varieties:** a) "the Manthānabhairava-god churns" · b) the Manthānabhairava as the name of a text/deity.
**Most likely:** (a) with the name kept — the Manthānabhairava (the churning-Bhairava); the concordance confirms the name (17 occurrences). This is the Krama's link to the Kubjikā's Manthāna — the report's cross-tradition note.
**Commentary:** the Manthānabhairava-reference is the Krama's own bridge to the Kubjikā-tradition's Manthāna-corpus — the Dyczkowski Manthānabhairavatantram anchor (on disk) verifies it.

**g.70 — ShoDashasahasrashaktirdeva upadishati sma mAdhavaH**
**Varieties:** a) "the god with the sixteen-thousand powers, Mādhava, taught" · b) the Mādhava as Kṛṣṇa (the Gītā-frame).
**Most likely:** (b) — the Gītā-allusion (the Pāṇḍu-son's war); the "sixteen-thousand powers" as Kṛṣṇa's own.
**Commentary:** the text ends with the Gītā-frame — the same "yuddhārambhe" as the Bhagavadgītā's opening; the C1 should note the cross-text.
