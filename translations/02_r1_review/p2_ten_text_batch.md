# P2 — The 10-Text Batch (openings)

*Peer-review over the openings in `ten_text_batch1_pass1.md` + `ten_text_batch2_pass1.md`. Each text gets its 1–2 crux-verses. Most texts appear untranslated; the review leans on the batch-network.*

# 1. Unmattabhairavahṛdaya (the yantra-section)
**v. 15 — unmādabhaganāśārthaṃ... chāgaivali prayuktārai bhūtvā dhāra vāruṇim**
**Varieties:** a) "for the destruction of the madness and the [X]... with the goat-offering made, he should bear the Vāruṇī [mudrā? wine?]" · b) emend "bhaga" → "bhava" ("the madness of existence") · c) "vāruṇim" = the Varuṇa-mudrā (the water-gesture).
**Most likely:** (b/c) — the bhava-emendation fits the unmāda-destruction theme; the Vāruṇī as the water-mudrā (not the liquor) fits the yantra-ritual register.
**Commentary:** the goat-offering inside a yantra-rite = the kula's bali-practice (cf. the Kubjikā 8's animal-offerings) — the same Kaula economy of blood.

# 2. Kulānanda (v. 34)
**v. 34 — evaṃ jñātvā vīrārohe vicaret yathāsukham**
**Varieties:** a) "knowing this, O heroine (vīrārohe — 'mounted on the hero'), one should move at ease" · b) "O you who ride the hero" (a vocative to the Devī) · c) "the hero-mount [the śakti?]" (a technical term).
**Most likely:** (b) — the vocative to the Devī (the text's addressee throughout) reads naturally; "vīrāroha" as the kula's term for the śakti-union is the commentary's extension.
**Commentary:** the verse's close ("hard to obtain for gods, asuras, and men") + the vocative = the teaching's gift to the goddess: the siddha is the rarest being, and she alone receives the word.

# 3. Jñānasaṅkalinītantra (vv. 7–8)
**v. 7 — yā punaḥ śāmbhavī vidyā guptā kulavadhūriva**
**P1:** "but the Śāmbhavī-vidyā — hidden like a kula-bride."
**Most likely:** ours — the kulavadhū-image (the hidden bride) is the transmission's self-image (cf. the Kubjikā's "guarded like one's own yoni"; the KJN's "hidden like the son-of-the-mother's-lover").
**Commentary:** three texts, three images of the same secrecy: the bride, the yoni, the shameful son. The commentary should collect them — the Kula's epistemology of concealment.

# 4. Kulasāra (vv. 5–11, the soma-myth)
**Varieties:** the corrupt pādas make the myth's details uncertain; the spine is clear: the gods lose their power without the surā; the kula-mata restores it through the female principle; the varṇa-question follows (the brāhmaṇa as the guru of all).
**Most likely:** the spine; the details [X].
**Commentary:** the soma-loss myth is the Kula's etiology: the orthoprax world (the surā-less gods) is enfeebled; the kula's restoration is through what the orthopraxy excludes — the same inversion as the KJN's Kaivarta-avatar.

# 5. Azeṣakulavallarī (the liturgy)
**Most likely:** ours — the Mārtaṇḍabhairava-nyāsa liturgy is procedural (the kalā-offerings, the śaktinavaka, the planets); the review confirms the procedural renderings against the parallel nyāsa-material (the Kubjikā 12's vyākhyā).
**Commentary:** the text's kalā-discs (10/12/16) are the same kalā-arithmetic as the ŚS 2.7 and the KJN 3/6–9 — the liturgy is the ontology in practice.

# 6. Kākacaṇḍeśvarīmata (the opening)
**Most likely:** ours — the Krama's goddess-family (Kaṅkālī, Kālarātrī, Kākacaṇḍeśvarī) with the yoginī-octad frame; the "doctrine of Kākacaṇḍeśvarī" = the text's self-title.
**Commentary:** the text's goddesses are the Krama's Kālī-series (cf. Wenta's twelve-Kālīs in our torella_book ch9) — the commentary's link to the Krama scholarship on disk.

# 7. Kulacūḍāmaṇi (vv. 1–6)
**v. 1 — asaṃkhyā tripurādevī...**
**Varieties:** a) "countless is the goddess Tripurā" · b) "the countless Tripurā-devī" (a name, Asaṃkhyā-Tripurā).
**Most likely:** (a) — the asaṃkhyā (countless) as a predicate, not a name; the verse's catalogue-pattern (the goddesses listed) supports the predicate-read.
**Commentary:** the Avalon-edited text's goddess-list is the kula's pantheon in its consolidating form (the Kālīkula's absorption of the Śrīvidyā-register — Tripurā first!).

# 8. Nareśvaraparīkṣā (the parīkṣā-frame)
**Most likely:** ours — Kṣemarāja's mitaśakti-argument (every rival's god is limited) verified against the anchor-stack (the same school-list as the Akulavīra 142–143, here examined one by one).
**Commentary:** the parīkṣā-genre is the tradition's own "gate" — the philosophical peer-review our truthmap replicates. The commentary should note this genealogy (Kṣemarāja's parīkṣā → the truthmap's nyāya-gate).

# 9. Kaulāvalīnirṇaya (the maṅgala + canon-list)
**Most likely:** ours — the maṅgala's structure (guru → the text → the goddess of speech) and the canon-list (the Kulacūḍāmaṇi, the Kulasāra — both in our batch).
**Commentary:** the canon is self-referential: the Kaulāvalīnirṇaya cites the texts we translate — the batch's texts are the canon's members. The commentary should map the canon-list against the library's holdings (which survive, which are lost).

# 10. Nityākaulatantra (the fragment)
**Most likely:** ours — the Nityā-list (Hṛllekhā, Kledanī, Nandā, Kṣobhanī, Madanāturā, Nīrajanā, Rāgavatī, ...danāvatī, Kalā, Drāvanī, Vegavatī + the twelfth Madana) per Dyczkowski's own header-note (the pre-Śrīvidyā pool).
**Commentary:** the fragment is the Śrīcakra's prehistory (Dyczkowski's note): the sixteen Nityās of the Śrīvidyā descend from this pool. The commentary's task: compare against the Nityāṣoḍaśikārṇava's list (in our library) when the fragment's lacunae are collated.
