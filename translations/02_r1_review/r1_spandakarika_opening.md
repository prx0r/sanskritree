# Spandakārikā — R1 (the review of the CP1 translation) — the opening verses vs the Dyczkowski anchor

*Peer-review over `SPANDAKARIKA_TRANSLATION_V1.md` (the CP1 pipeline translation) against the **Dyczkowski anchor** (the Stanzas on Vibration, with the four commentaries — extracted). The three-way check: CP1 vs the anchor vs the Sanskrit. The report's frame: the Spandakārikā is the report's "A+" Spanda control — spanda, śakti, svātantrya all in the report's dossiers.*

## spk.1.1 — the maṅgala
**CP1:** "We praise that Shankara, the source of the manifestation of the wheel of Shakti."
**Anchor (Dyczkowski):** "We praise that Śaṅkara, the source of the power of the Wheel of Energies" (whose opening and closing of the eyes destroys and creates the universe).
**Sanskrit:** taṃ śakticakravibhavaprabhavaṃ śaṃkaraṃ stumaḥ
**Verdict:** ✓ the substance agrees — the "source" (prabhava) + the wheel (cakra); the register differs: CP1's "manifestation of the wheel of Shakti" vs the anchor's "power of the Wheel of Energies." The report's śakti-dossier: "not merely energy" — the anchor's "Energies" (capitalized, the power-hosts) is the better register; CP1's "Shakti" (transliterated) is defensible but loses the wheel-of-powers force.
**Commentary:** the report's note: SPK 1, 18–19 — "śakti: power/dynamism constitutive of manifestation and cognition." The opening's wheel-of-energies is the whole text's subject.

## spk.1.2 — the unobstructed
**CP1:** "Uncertain: Because its nature is unobstructed, there is no obstruction anywhere."
**Anchor:** "In whom all this is established, and from whom it has issued forth — because His nature is unobstructed, there is no obstruction anywhere."
**Sanskrit:** tasyānāvṛtarūpatvān na nirodho 'sti kutracit
**Verdict:** ✓ the "unobstructed nature → no obstruction" is the hard core; the CP1's "[X]-uncertainty" was over-cautious — the anchor's fuller first-half (yatra sthitam idam sarvam... yasmāc ca nirgatam) supplies the antecedent that the CP1's fragment omitted.
**Commentary:** the verse's first half (from the anchor) gives the "in-whom / from-whom" — the CP1's verse-fragment should be checked against the full text; the R2 should restore the first half.

## spk.1.3 — the perceiver's nature
**CP1:** "The perceiver never deviates from his own nature."
**Anchor:** "Even in the division of the waking and other states, it pervades undivided; the perceiver never deviates from his own nature."
**Sanskrit:** jagradādivibhede 'pi tadabhinne prasarpati | nivartate nijān naiva svabhāvād upalabdhṛtaḥ
**Verdict:** ✓ the second half agrees; the CP1 dropped the first half (the undivided-pervasion across states).
**Commentary:** the report's A-thesis (reflexive presence): the perceiver's nature persists across the waking/dream/sleep division — the same structure as the ŚS 1.7's Fourth.

## spk.1.4 — the states
**CP1:** "Clearly, they exist elsewhere in a state permeated by happiness and so on."
**Anchor:** "Clearly, the cognitions 'I am happy,' 'I am unhappy,' 'I am attached' and so on are elsewhere, in a state permeated by happiness and so on."
**Sanskrit:** ahaṃ sukhi ca duḥkhi ca raktas cety ādisaṃvidāḥ | sukhādyavasthānusyūte vartante 'nyatra tāḥ sphuṭam
**Verdict:** ✓ with a gap — the CP1's "they" was under-specified; the anchor's "the cognitions 'I am happy,' 'I am unhappy,' 'I am attached'" restores the subject (the ahaṃ-saṃvids).
**Commentary:** the verse's ahaṃ-cognitions are the ownership-language — the report's A→B transition (the "my"-experience) in the Spanda's form.

## spk.1.5 — the ultimate
**CP1:** "There is no state of foolishness at all; that exists in ultimate reality."
**Anchor:** "Neither is there any state of bewilderment; that [state] exists in ultimate reality."
**Sanskrit:** na cāsti mūḍhabhāvo 'pi tadasti paramārthataḥ
**Verdict:** ✓ the hard core agrees; "foolishness" vs "bewilderment" is a register-choice (the mūḍha-bhāva).
**Commentary:** the verse's "that [state] exists in ultimate reality" — the report's "states of experience" note; the negated bewilderment (the paśu-state) is the same apophasis as the bundle's.

## spk.1.6 — the wheel's triad
**CP1:** "Uncertain: With the inner wheel, the creation, maintenance, and dissolution."
**Anchor:** "By means of the inner wheel [i.e., the wheel of the powers within], the host of causes, though bewildered, as if not bewildered, achieves creation, maintenance, and dissolution."
**Sanskrit:** yataḥ kāraṇavargo 'yaṃ vimūḍho 'mūḍhavat svayam | sahāntareṇa cakreṇa pravṛttisthitisaṃhṛtīḥ ||
**Verdict:** ✗ the CP1's "Uncertain" was a real miss — the anchor restores the full first half (the host of causes, bewildered-as-if-not) that the CP1's fragment dropped; the verse's point: the inner wheel drives the triad. The CP1's fragment was too thin.
**Commentary:** the R2's correction: restore the first half; the "host of causes... as if not bewildered" is the paśu's unconscious instrumentality — the report's A-thesis (the hidden agency) in the Spanda's form.
