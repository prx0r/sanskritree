# 6. Kākacaṇḍeśvarīmata (M00287)

### Time-place-context
- **PERIOD:** Krama-lineage text (the Kākacaṇḍeśvarī = the Crow-goddess of the Krama); the yoginī-octad frame is the Krama's liturgical-ontological core. Date [X] — the Krama layer (10th–12th c.).
- **PLACE/PATH:** M00287 e-text.
- **GENRE/REGISTER:** dialogue-tantra (Bhr̥gu-Śaṅkara / Devī-Bhairava frames); the yoginī-octad (yoginyaṣṭaka) doctrine.
- **FRAME:** the "mata" (doctrine) of Kākacaṇḍeśvarī — one of the Krama's goddesses (cf. the twelve Kālīs of the Krama — our truth/torella_book's ch9_wenta_twelve_kalis!).

## The opening
**Frame** — bhṛgvādi devadeveśaṃ ahaṃ pṛcchāmiśaṃkaraṃ | skaṃdādyāśca mahāmāyā bhṛṃgā caiva vinā ekaiḥ || śrīvakratuṃḍāya namaḥ ||
> I, with Bhr̥gu and the rest, ask the god of gods, Śaṅkara — with Skanda and the others, and the great illusion (mahāmāyā), and Bhr̥ṅgā — homage to the crooked-faced one (Vakratuṇḍa).

**Frame 2** — kailāsaśikharāsīne umāsārdhaṃ gajatguruḥ | yoginyaṣṭakataṃtre ca guhyādguhyataraṃparaṃ ||
> [Śaṅkara,] seated on Kailāsa's peak, half-joined with Umā, the moving guru: in the Yoginī-octad tantra, [the teaching] more secret than secret, the supreme —

**Frame 3** — sarvajñāna prakāśāya sarvajñāsarvakacchrayaḥ | etaikāryaṃ kathaṃ devetvayā sarvaṃmamopari ||
> — to the illuminator of all knowledge, the refuge of all knowledge: how is this one work, O god, by you — all [this] upon me?

**The goddesses** — kaṃkālī kālarātrī ca kākacaṃḍā vacāṃvikā | kaṃkālī kālarātrī ca kākacaṃḍeśvarīmatā ||
> Kaṅkālī and Kālarātrī, and Kākacaṇḍā, the mistress of speech: Kaṅkālī, Kālarātrī — this is the doctrine of Kākacaṇḍeśvarī.

**The worship-scene** — evamardya tathākānyau yoginīgaṇanāyakaiḥ | nṛtyavādyavacobhedaisānaṃdaiḥ hṛṣṭhacetasāṃ || tatastaṃ bhairavaṃ devaṃ yogiṇīgaṇaveṣṭhitaṃ | kecit stuvaṃti saṃhṛṣṭāḥ kecit stuvaṃti guhyakāḥ || kecit nṛtyaṃti tasyāgre keciddhāsya ca kurvataṃ | etais tu jñānabhaktaistu arcitastatra bhairavaḥ ||
> Having adored thus, with the other [goddesses], by the leaders of the yoginī-hosts, with the distinctions of dance, music, speech, and joy, their minds delighted: then that god Bhairava, surrounded by the yoginī-hosts — some praise him rejoicing, some praise him hidden (guhyaka); some dance before him, some make laughter. By these, devoted through knowledge, Bhairava is worshipped there.

**Devī's question** — dṛṣṭvā tu bhairavaṃ devaṃ paṃcavaktraṃ trilocanaṃ |
> Having seen the god Bhairava, five-faced, three-eyed —

Notes: the Krama's goddess-family (Kaṅkālī, Kālarātrī, Kākacaṇḍeśvarī) — [P: cf. our truth/torella_book's ch9 (Wenta on the twelve Kālīs — the Krama's Kālī-series); the yoginī-octad — the same octad-structure as our ŚS 3.19's māheśvarī-octad; the guhyaka-worship — the hidden devotees of the Krama.]

# 7. Kulacūḍāmaṇi (M00402)

### Time-place-context
- **PERIOD:** the Nigama of the Kula — a classic Kaula text; **edited by Arthur Avalon (Madras edition)** — the printed edition's date [X: early 20th c., Madras]. The 64-tantras list places it in the consolidating Kaula canon.
- **PLACE/PATH:** Avalon's Madras edition; e-text M00402.
- **GENRE/REGISTER:** nigama (the Śiva-side teaching) — doctrinal dialogue; the goddess-catalog and the tantra-canon.
- **FRAME:** opens with the goddess-list and the 64-tantras discussion (with Avalon's apparatus citing the Vāmākeśvaratantra's parallel list).

## The opening (vv. 1–6)
**1** — asaṃkhyā tripurādevī asaṃkhyātā [asaṃkhyā caiva] ca kālikā | vāgīśvarī tathāsaṃkhyā tathā ca sukulākulā ||
> Countless is the goddess Tripurā; countless [also] is Kālikā; Vāgīśvarī likewise countless, and so the Sukulākulā.

**2** — mātāṅginī tathā pūrṇā vimalā caṇḍanāyikā | tripuraikajaṭā durgā yā cānyā kulasundarī ||
> Mātaṅginī, Pūrṇā, Vimalā, Caṇḍanāyikā, Tripuraikajaṭā, Durgā — and whatever other Kula-beauty (kulasundarī).

**3** — vaiṣṇavaṃ gāṇapatyaṃ ca mahāsauramataṃ tathā | śaivaṃ śāṃkarajātaṃ ca yatkiṃcid bhūtasaṃbhavam ||
> The Vaiṣṇava, the Gāṇapatya, the great Saurya-doctrine, the Śaiva, the Śāṅkara-born — whatever arises from the beings (bhūta) —

**4-6** — catuḥṣaṣṭiśca tantrāṇi mātṛṇāmuttamāni ca | ... (the 64-tantras list with the editor's apparatus) ...
> — and the sixty-four tantras, the supreme of the Mothers. [The editor's apparatus (Avalon) notes: the Vāmākeśvaratantra's parallel list begins "mahāmāyāśambaraṃ ca" and enumerates the 64 names; "this reading, accepted by the great ones, is retained and explained." The list: Mahāmāyā, Śambara, Yoginījāla (Jālaśambara), Tattvaśambara, Bhairavāṣṭaka — six named; then "asitāṅgo ruruścaṇḍaḥ krodha..." — the Bhairava-series continues.]

Notes: the goddess-list (the kula's pantheon — Tripurā/Kālikā as "countless") and the 64-tantras canon — the Kula's self-positioning within the tantra-universe. [P: the asaṃkhyā-motif (the goddesses' countlessness) — cf. our Kubjikā's aṣṭāviṃśati-arithmetic; Avalon's apparatus = a named-editor anchor for the text's edition-history.]

# 8. Nareśvaraparīkṣā (M00039) — Kṣemarāja

### Time-place-context
- **PERIOD:** **Kṣemarāja** (early 11th c., Kashmir — the stack's own Kṣemarāja: Spandanirṇaya, Vimarśinī, Pratyabhijñāhṛdaya). A philosophical parīkṣā (examination) of the Lord-concepts.
- **PLACE/PATH:** Srinagar edition (KSTS line); e-text M00039.
- **GENRE/REGISTER:** philosophical śāstra — the parīkṣā-genre (critical examination of the rival īśvara-concepts); scholarly prose with verse-maṅgalas.
- **FRAME:** Kṣemarāja's own examination of the schools' gods (Sāṅkhya's prakṛti, Vedānta's brahman, Pāñcarātra's Nārāyaṇa, the īśvara-kāraṇavāda, the puruṣa-viśeṣa...) — all shown to have limited power (mitaśakti) — preparatory to the Śaiva's own Lord.

## The opening
**1** — atha meyābdhiratnasya śaṅkarasyāmitadyuteḥ | parīkṣāṃ leśato vacmi puṃsparīkṣāpurassaram ||
> Now, of Śaṅkara — the jewel of the sea of all that is to be known (meya), of unmeasured brilliance — I will speak the examination (parīkṣā) in brief, preceded by the examination of persons (puṃsparīkṣā).

**2** — taṃ śaktiśaktimadrūpaṃ natvā nāreśvaraṃ param | nareśvaraparīkṣāyāmakṣarārthaḥ prakāśyate ||
> Bowing to him — of power-and-powerful-one form — the supreme Nareśvara, in the Examination of the Lord of Men the meaning of the syllables is made manifest.

**The prose (the parīkṣā's frame)** — "The purpose of this section: the Lord-examination is the subject to be taught; its purpose is the production of the knowledge of the supreme Lord — the means of the supreme good, as will be said at the section's end. Here, as the essence of the whole ocean of objects — the tattvas, the worlds — the Lord is the jewel; by the word 'Śaṅkara' that is said: he who is the doer of good is examined by all like the wishing-gem (cintāmaṇi). The connection is of the nature of means-and-end; the attainment of the end — thus, for every thoughtful reader (prekṣāvat), the commencement [of the work] is accomplished. By this it is shown that, for the gods established by the other schools — Hari, Hara, Hiraṇyagarbha and the rest — since they are not 'Śaṅkara' (not-augurers-of-good), the thoughtful do not enter upon the examination."

**The school-critique (prose)** — "He who is conceived as the prakṛti by the prakṛti-Īśvara-vādins; as brahman by the brahma-knowers; as Nārāyaṇa by the Pāñcarātras; as īśvara, the material cause of the world, [by the īśvara-kāraṇavādins] — he, from that very [conception], being unconscious like clay and the rest, has only limited power. Even by the Sāṅkhyas, with the agency of prakṛti admitted, the īśvara is admitted as non-agent. By the vikāra-Īśvara-vādins [the Vedāntins], brahman's [state] as Hiraṇyagarbha — the great glory — and as the turīya-natured īśvara: for him, from his connection with creation and dissolution, limited power is admitted. Those who describe the īśvara as a special puruṣa endowed with superior intellect and free of the kleśas — for them too, since the intellect and the rest are manifest and necessarily perish, his limited power must be accepted. Those who sing of the īśvara as joined with the mind — by them, the mind being unconscious, and plural, and as an effect perishable like a pot, even he must be accepted as of limited power; and even with the mind's eternity, like the released selves, since the conjunction is non-eternal..."

Notes: the philosophical parīkṣā — the "mitaśakti" (limited-power) argument applied to every rival's god; the prekṣāvat (the thoughtful reader) — the Nyāya-methodological term; the cintāmaṇi-image. [P: the same school-list as the Akulavīra's (Nyāya/Vaiśeṣika/Sāṅkhya/Vedānta/Pāñcarātra — here examined one by one, not dismissed en bloc); the parīkṣā-genre — cf. the truthmap's gate (the machine-verification of claims — this is the tradition's own "gate").]

# 9. Kaulāvalīnirṇaya (M00231)

### Time-place-context
- **PERIOD:** attributed in the e-text to **Jñānānanda paramahaṃsa**; a Kaula manual (the "determination of the Kaula lineage"); date [X] (the tantra-list and the maṅgala suggest the late-medieval consolidation; the Kaulāvalīnirṇaya is cited in our earlier nirañjana-greps — "keciddhaṃsapadaṃ nirañjanapadaṃ").
- **PLACE/PATH:** M00231 e-text.
- **GENRE/REGISTER:** śāstra-maṅgala + tantra-canon list — the self-presentation of the Kaula tradition.
- **FRAME:** the maṅgala names the author's lineage-source (Jñānānanda) and the text's pedigree among the kula-tantras.

## The opening (the maṅgala + canon-list)
**1** — natvā śrīgurupādapadmayugalaṃ siddhaiḥ sadā sevitaṃ śāktānāṃ paribhāvamokṣajanakaṃ kaulāvalīnirṇayam | vakṣye'haṃ kulavardhanaṃ sukṛtibhiḥ sevyaṃ sadā kaulikai-rbhāvaireva manoharaiśca caraṇābjaikaprasādālayaṃ ||
> Bowing to the blessed pair of the guru's lotus-feet, ever served by the siddhas — I will declare the Kaulāvalīnirṇaya, which gives the Śāktas' [state of] pondering and liberation, the increaser of the kula, ever to be served by the kaulikas with captivating devotional states (bhāva), the abode of the sole grace of the lotus-feet.

**2** — vāñchākalpataruṃ tanoti śivayorvākyānusārārthato | jñānānandagirirmaheśacaraṇābjaikaprasādālayaḥ ||
> The mountain of knowledge-bliss (Jñānānanda), the abode of the sole grace of the great Lord's lotus-feet, spreads the wishing-tree of desire in accord with the meaning of the pair-of-Śivas' words.

**3** — yasyāḥ prasādato lebhe śāstrārthaṃ tattvanirṇayam | tāṃ namāmi mahādevīṃ bāṇīṃ vāgvibhavapradām ||
> By whose grace he obtained the meaning of the śāstras, the determination of the principles — to her I bow, the great goddess Vāṇī (Speech), giving the wealth of speech.

**The canon-list (vv. 4–6)** — saṃvīkṣya yāmalaṃ rudrabrahmaviṣṇukasaṃjñakam | yāmalaṃ śaktisaṃjñañca bhāvacūḍāmaṇiṃ tathā || tantracūḍāmaṇiñcaiva kulacūḍāmaṇiṃ tathā | kulasāraṃ kuloḍḍīśaṃ kulāmṛtakulārṇavam ||
> Having considered the Yāmala named Rudra-Brahmā-Viṣṇu, and the Yāmala named Śakti, and the Bhāvacūḍāmaṇi, the Tantracūḍāmaṇi, the Kulacūḍāmaṇi, the Kulasāra, the Kuloḍḍīśa, the Kulāmṛta, the Kulārṇava —

Notes: the maṅgala's structure (guru → the text → the goddess of speech); the canon-list = the kula's self-identified sources (Kulacūḍāmaṇi and Kulasāra are IN our 10-text batch — the canon is self-referential). [P: the bhāva (devotional state) — the kula's bhāva-doctrine (cf. our Kubjikā's bhāvaśuddhi); the Kaulāvalīnirṇaya's nirañjana-pada passage (from our earlier greps) — the batch's cross-links.]

# 10. Nityākaulatantra (M00287-adjacent fragment — M00316)

### Time-place-context
- **PERIOD:** the fragment precedes the Śrīvidyā consolidation — **Dyczkowski's own note (in the e-text's header):** "Kubjikā Tantras that precede the emergence of the well-known śrīvidyā tradition... She developed out of the pool of Nityā Yoginīs... around which a group of sixteen Nityās are configured in her distinctive yantra, the śrīcakra. This fragment is thus of special importance for the historian."
- **PLACE/PATH:** M00316 e-text (data under Dyczkowski's direction, 2014); the manuscript is fragmentary — asterisk-lacunae throughout.
- **GENRE/REGISTER:** deity-yoga liturgy (the Nityā-goddesses' visualization); the Nityā-list.
- **FRAME:** the fragment preserves the Nityā-octad/sixteen list pre-Śrīvidyā — the goddess-arithmetic of the Kālīkula's Nityā-pool.

## The readable opening
**The āsana-frame** — prāguktamāsanaṃ nyastvā ṣaṭkoṇaṃ vahnimaṇḍalaṃ | tanmadhyavartinī * * * * * * * * * *##?
> Having established the previously taught seat, the six-cornered fire-disc, the [goddess] standing in its middle [X: lacuna] —

**The visualization-fragments** — cārurūpāṃ mamāharāṃ | lāvaṇyaṃ kānti vibhavākṣīvānana nibhekṣaṇā || ... vāhya mṛnāla sadṛśyaṃ pinonnata payodharāṃ || ... jaṭāmakuṭadhāriṇyāṃ trine * * * * * * * *? || raktāṃgarāgajananīraṃjayatīmidaṃ jagat | vane sthitā ||
> The lovely-formed, the enchanting; with beauty, radiance, majesty, with lotus-eyes and lotus-face; [her arms] like the outer lotus-stalk; with high, full breasts; wearing the matted-hair crown, with the three... [X: lacuna]; red-limbed, born of passion, conquering this world — standing in the forest.

**The Nityā-list** — hṛllekhā kledanī naṃdā kṣobhanī madanāturā || nirajanārāgavatī * * * * * * * *? | danāvatī || khe kalā drāvanī caiva tathā vegavatī varā | ekādaśaitā devyāste madanā dvādaśa smṛtaḥ ||
> Hṛllekhā, Kledanī, Nandā, Kṣobhanī, Madanāturā; Nīrajanā, Rāgavatī, [Kṣobha?]danāvatī; in the khe — Kalā, Drāvanī, and the excellent Vegavatī: these eleven are the goddesses; the Madana is remembered as the twelfth.

**The cakra-rule** — ārakeṣṭantarāṣu cakraṃ vyāpya vyavasthitaṃ | karabhoru mahācakraṃ pūjayeta sumadhyame ||
> In the intervals of the spokes [of the cakra], the cakra extends and stands — the great cakra, O fair-thighed one, should be worshipped in its center.

Notes: the Nityā-list (Hṛllekhā, Kledanī, Nandā, Kṣobhanī, Madanāturā, Nīrajanā, Rāgavatī, ...danāvatī, Kalā, Drāvanī, Vegavatī + the twelfth Madana) — the pre-Śrīvidyā Nityā-pool per Dyczkowski's note. [X: the lacunae make several pādas unreadable — the fragment is preserved as-is; pass 2: compare against the Nityāṣoḍaśikārṇava's Nityā-list (M00115-adjacent, in the library) — the sixteen Nityās of the Śrīvidyā are the fragment's descendants.]
