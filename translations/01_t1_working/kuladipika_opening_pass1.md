## Paṭala 1 — the kula-yoga and the yonimudrā

**The supreme state** — koṭisamaprabham | tadūrdhve tu śikhā sūkṣmā cidrūpā paramākalā | tathā sahitam ātmānam ekībhūtaṃ vicintayet ||
> [The state] of the light of a crore [of suns]. Above it, the subtle flame (śikhā), the consciousness-formed, the supreme kalā: [then] one should contemplate the self as united with it, become one.

**The ascent** — gacchantī * hmamārgeṇa liṅgabhedakrameṇa tu | sūryakoṭipratīkāśaṃ candrakoṭi suśītalam ||
> Going by the path of the [h]ma (?) [X: "hmamārgeṇa" corrupt], by the order of the liṅga's piercing: [the state] like a crore of suns, cool like a crore of moons —

**The nectar** — amṛtaṃ yadvisargasthaṃ paramānandalakṣaṇam | dṛtaraktasutejāḍhyadhārāpāta pravarṣiṇam ||
> — the nectar that stands in the emission (visarga), marked by the supreme bliss: raining the fall of the stream rich with the seen-blood-red tejas —

**The drinking** — pītvā kulāmṛtaṃ divyaṃ punareva viśet kulam | punarevākulaṃ gacchen mātrāyogena nānyathā ||
> — having drunk the divine kula-nectar, one enters the kula again; again one goes to the akula — by the mātrā-yoga (the measure-union), not otherwise.

**The prāṇa** — sā ca prāṇāḥ samākhyātā tantre 'smin parameśvari | udghātaḥ procyate so 'pi prāṇāntaṃ spṛśate yadā ||
> And that is declared the life-breath (prāṇa) in this tantra, O supreme Lady; that is called the udghāta (the thrust) when it touches the breath's end.

**The fruit** — evamabhyasya mānasya ahanyahani niścayāt | jarāmaraṇaduḥkhaughaiḥ rmucyate bhava bandhanāt ||
> Thus practicing, meditating day after day with certainty, one is released — from old age, death, the floods of sorrow, from the bond of becoming.

**The fourfold creation** — caturvidhā tu yā sṛṣṭir yasyāṃ yonau pravartate | punaḥ pralīyate tasyāṃ kālāgnyādi śivāntakam ||
> The creation that is fourfold, which proceeds in the yoni — it is dissolved again in it: the kāla-fire and the rest, ending in Śiva.

**The yonimudrā** — yonimudrā parā sā tu bandhaṃ tasyāḥ prakīrtitam | mudrāyā bandhamātreṇa kiṃ na sādhayate bhuvi ||
> That is the supreme yonimudrā; its binding (bandha) is proclaimed. By the mere binding of the mudrā, what does one not accomplish on earth?

**The mis-practice** — anyathā japate mantram anyathā kurute tu yaḥ | nāsau sidhyati deveśi satyaṃ satyaṃ puna punaḥ ||
> He who recites the mantra one way and does [the rite] another way — he does not succeed, O Devī — truly, truly, again and again.

**The mantra-failures** — chinnārudhāstu ye mantrā stambhitāḥ kīlitā bhṛśam | dagdhāḥ svatantrārbhātāśca malināśca tiraskṛtāḥ || mūrchitāmadasaṃyuktāḥ suṣuptā mūrchitā dṛtāḥ | mandābālāstathā vṛddhāḥ prauḍhā yauvanagarhitāḥ || ari pakṣe sthitā ye ca nirbījāstatvavarjitāḥ | aṃśakena vihīnāśca khaṇḍaśaḥ śatadhā kṛtāḥ ||
> The mantras that are cut (chinna), obstructed (ruddha), paralyzed (stambhita), nailed (kīlita) firmly, burnt (dagdha), independent-blasted (svatantrārbhāta), stained (malina), and hidden (tiraskṛta); joined with swoon-and-drunkenness (mūrchitāmada), asleep (suṣupta), swooning (mūrchita), bound (dṛta); the slow (manda), the childish (bāla), the old (vṛddha), the mature (prauḍha), the condemned-of-youth; those standing in the enemy's side, the seedless (nirbīja), the principle-bereft (tattvavarjita), the part-deprived, the broken — made into a hundred pieces —

**The remedy** — vidhinānena yuktāste sambhavantya cireṇate | siddhimokṣapradāḥ sarve deśikena niyojitāḥ || yadyaduccarate mantrī varṇarūpaṃ śubhāśubham | tattat sidhyatyanāyāsād yonimudrā prabandhanāt ||
> — joined with this rite, they become, after a while, all givers of siddhi and mokṣa when directed by the deśika (the master). Whatever syllable or form, auspicious or inauspicious, the mantrin utters — that succeeds without effort, by the binding of the yonimudrā.

**The dīkṣā's gate** — dīkṣayitvā vidhānena abhiṣicya sahasradhā | tato 'dhikārī deveśi tantre 'smin nānyathā bhavet ||
> Having initiated by the rite, having anointed a thousand times, then one becomes the adhikārī (the qualified) in this tantra, O Devī — not otherwise.

**The testing** — mudrāmaṇḍalavinyāsair japahomavratādibhiḥ | samtāpya bahukālaṃ vai bhaktiṃ vijñāya niścalām || jñātvā tyāgaṃ balaṃ tāpaṃ tattastasya prakāśayet | asmin kāle 'thavā devi devyādeśaḥ prajāyate || tatredaṃ kathayet jñānam anyathā narakaṃ vrajet | trātā kiṃtu na tasyāsti yo parīkṣya pradāpayet || tasmāt parīkṣya dātavyaṃ yadīcchej jīvituṃ ciram ||
> Having tested [the disciple] with the mudrā-maṇḍala-arrangements, with japa, homa, vows and the rest, for a long time, and having known [his] devotion as unwavering; having known [his] renunciation, strength, and ardor, then to that one he should reveal [the teaching]. At this time, O Devī, the Devī's command arises. Then one should tell the knowledge; otherwise one goes to hell. And there is no savior for him who gives [it] without testing. Therefore one should give only after testing — if one wishes to live long.

**The sin's burning** — kṛtvā pāpasahasrāṇi asaṃkhyātāni sundari | dahā * * * mantreṇa yonibandhena nānyathā | na tasya puṇyapāpāni tamaḥ sūryodaye yathā ||
> Having done thousands of sins, countless, O fair one — [he burns them] with the mantra, by the yoni-binding, not otherwise: his merit and sin [vanish] like darkness at the sun's rising.

**The nourishment** — santarpya bahudhā kālaṃ prāṇastrī draviṇādibhiḥ | tasmādabhyasanaṃ nityaṃ kartavyaṃ puṇyaka * * kṣibhiḥ ||
> Having satisfied [the guru] long with wealth, women, substance and the rest — therefore the practice is ever to be done by the meritorious.

**The abhyāsa's fruits** — a * * jjāyate siddhir abhyāsāṃ mokṣamāpnuyāt | saṃvittiṃ labhatebhyāsā yogo bhyāsaḥ pravartate || mantrāṇāṃ siddhi * * sādabhyāsā dvāyusādhanam | kālavañcanam abhyāsāt tathā mṛtyuñjayo bhavet ||
> From practice siddhi arises; by practice one attains mokṣa; by practice one obtains awareness; yoga proceeds by practice; the mantras' siddhi, the breath's sādhana [come] from practice; the deceiving of time [comes] from practice; thus one becomes the conqueror of death.

*iti prathamaḥ paṭalaḥ* — "Here ends the first paṭala."

## Paṭala 2 — the Trailokyaḍāmara and the sādhaka's equipment (the opening)

**The mantra** — athātaḥ saṃpravakṣyāmi saṃpradāyasamanvitam | trailokyaḍāmaraṃ mantraṃ tripurāvācakaṃ mahat ||
> Now I will declare, endowed with the tradition, the Trailokyaḍāmara-mantra, the great utterance of Tripurā.

**The place** — susame bhūpradeśe tu nimnonnata vivarjite | puṣpaprakarasaṃkīrṇe gandhadhūpādhivāsite ||
> In a level tract of ground, free of hollows and heights, strewn with masses of flowers, scented with incense and perfumes —

**The sādhaka** — lalāṭe tilakopeto netrakajjalarañjitaḥ | tāmbūlapūrṇagandhastu madirānandacetanaḥ || kuṅkumena viliptāṅgo lākṣālaktakaśobhitaḥ | raktavarṇaparīdhāno raktamālyopaśobhitaḥ || karpūrakṣodandhāṅgaḥ kesapaiśūnyavarjitaḥ ||
> — with the forehead's tilaka, the eyes tinted with collyrium, the mouth full of betel and fragrance, the mind rejoicing in the wine; the limbs anointed with saffron (kuṅkuma), adorned with lac and red-dye, wearing red garments, adorned with the red garland; the limbs smeared with camphor-powder, free of any scantness of hair —
