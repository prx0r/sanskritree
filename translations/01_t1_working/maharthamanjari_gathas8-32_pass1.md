## The heart-pulse and the prakāśa-lamp (8–12)

**g.8** — paryAlochanavimukhe vastusvabhAvasyAtmano hR^idaye | sha~NkAviShavegeneva saMsArabhayena muhyati lokaH ||
> The world, in the heart of the self — whose object-nature is [normally] turned from examination — is bewildered by the fear of saṃsāra, as by the poison of doubt.

**g.9** — mANikyapraveka iva nicholito nijamayUkhalekhayA | pratibhAti laukikAnAmatyantasphuTo.apyasphuTa AtmA ||
> Like a peerless ruby wrapped in its own filaments of light: the self appears to the worldly as utterly-manifest-yet-unmanifest.

**g.10** — Urdhvo jvalati prakAsho lokAlokasya ma~NgalapradIpaH | vimarshadashAmukhAnItadahyamAnamalAlitailavichChardaH ||
> Upward blazes the prakāśa — the lamp of auspiciousness of the world-and-non-world — its malāli-oil's flow drawn through the ten-reflexions' mouth, burning. [variant: "by the ten-reflexions' mouth the malāli-oil's flow is burnt"]

**g.11** — sannaiva hR^idayaprakAsho bhavanakriyAyA bhavati kartA | saiva kriyAvimarshaH svasthA kShubhitA cha vishvavistAraH ||
> The heart-prakāśa, being, becomes the doer of the becoming-action; that same [awareness], the action-reflexion — at rest, the world's expansion [when] stirred.

**g.12** — pR^ithvIparamashivayoH pratyAhAre prakAshaparamArthe | yo.anyonyavisheShaH sa eva hR^idayasya vimarshonmeShaH ||
> In the withdrawal of the earth and the supreme-Śiva, in the prakāśa's supreme-meaning: that which is the mutual distinction is itself the heart's reflexion-upsurge (vimarśa-unmeṣa).

## The powers (13–18)

**g.13** — tathA tathA dR^ishyamAnAnAM shaktisahasrANAmekasa~NghaTTaH | nijahR^idayodyamarUpo bhavati shivo nAma paramasvachChandaH ||
> Thus, of the thousands of powers as they are seen, the single-collision: becoming the form of one's own heart's effort — that is called Śiva, the supreme free (parama-svacchanda).

**g.14** — sa eva vishvamIkShituM sthAtuM kartuM chonmukho bhavan | shaktisvabhAvaH kathito hR^idayatrikoNamadhumAsalollAsaH ||
> He himself, becoming intent to see the world, to stand, to act — the power-natured, is declared the surge of the honey in the heart's triangle.

**g.15** — j~nAnaM kriyeti dvayorapi prathamonmeShe sadAshivo devaH | dvitIyAyA ullekhe dvitIyaH sa bhavatIshvaro nAma ||
> Knowledge and action — in the first-upsurge of the two, the god is Sadāśiva; in the second's tracing (ullekha), he becomes the second, named Īśvara.

**g.16** — j~nAtA sa AtmA j~neyasvabhAvashcha lokavyavahAraH | ekarasAM saMsR^iShTi yatra gatau sA khalu nistuShA vidyA ||
> He is the knower, the self; the world-transaction is of the known's nature; where the going is the one-taste's creation — that indeed is the huskless vidyā.

**g.17** — ekarase svabhAve udbhAvayantI vikalpashilpAni | mAyeti lokapateH paramasvatantrasya mohanI shaktiH ||
> In the one-taste nature, causing the crafts of the alternatives to arise — she is called māyā, the deluding power of the world-lord, the supremely free.

**g.18** — sarvakaraH sarvaj~naH pUrNo nityo.asa~Nkuchitashcha | viparIta iva mahesho yAbhistA bhavanti pa~nchashaktayaH ||
> The all-doer, the all-knower, the full, the eternal, the unshrinking — the great Lord, as it were inverted: by which [powers] those five powers come to be.

## The karaṇas and the senses (21–25)

**g.21** — kallolAyamAnAni sadA hR^idayAmbunidhau trINi karaNAni | AkarShantIdantAM tatrAhantAM (tathA ahantAM) chAtrArpayanti ||
> The three instruments (karaṇas), ever waving in the heart-ocean, draw the "this"-ness and there offer the "I"-ness. [variant: "and the I-ness"]

**g.22** — hR^idayasthitasya vibhorviShayAloko vishR^i~Nkhalo bhavati | j~nAnendriyadIpeShu nijanijagolAgranityalagneShu ||
> For the lord dwelling in the heart, the object-light becomes unchained, in the knowledge-sense-lamps ever-attached to their own spheres' tips.

**g.23** — bhavanti karaNAni pa~ncha khalu karmapradhAnAni lokanAthasya | spandate svaira yairjano jaDAdvilakShaNo bhavan ||
> The five instruments, indeed, are the action-principals of the world-lord, by which, moving freely, the person, becoming distinct from the inert, pulses (spanda).

**g.24** — vishvodyAnavirUDhAni gandhapramukhAni sugandhIni puShpANi | pa~nchApyAjighran krIDati trailokyadhUrto devaH ||
> The five fragrant flowers grown in the world's garden — smell and the rest — sniffing them all, the trickster-of-the-three-worlds god plays.

**g.25** — styAnasya kramavashAdikShurasasyeva shivaprakAshasya | guDapiNDA iva pa~nchApi bhUtAni madhuratAM na mu~nchanti ||
> By the order of the thickening (styāna), like the sugar-cane-juice's: the five elements, like lumps of raw sugar [in the syrup], do not lose their sweetness — [so] of the Śiva-prakāśa.

## The yāmala and the māyā (26–32)

**g.26** — sarvasya bhuvanavibhramayantrollAsasya tantuvallIva | vimarshasaMrambhamayI ujjR^imbhate shambhormahAshaktiH ||
> Like a creeper-thread [through pearls] of all the worlds' whirling upsurge: the reflexion-fervor-natured great-power of Śambhu unfolds (ujjṛmbhate).

**g.27** — yadadhvanAM cha ShaTkaM tatra prakAshArthalakShaNamardham | vimarshashabdasvabhAvamardhamiti shivasya yAmalollAsaH ||
> The six of the courses (adhvans): there the half is of the prakāśa-meaning's mark; the half of the reflexion-word's nature — such is Śiva's yāmala-upsurge.

**g.28** — AlekhyavisheSha iva gajavR^iShabhayordvayaH pratibhAsaH | ekasminnevArthe shivashaktivibhAgakalpanAM kurmaH ||
> Like the appearance of the elephant-and-bull in a painting: in the single object we make the Śiva-Śakti division's imagining.

**g.29** — tilamAtre.api sharIre prekShadhvaM kITasyaitAvatI shaktiH | sA svachChandashriyo vishvasharIrasya kiyatI bhavatu ||
> In a body of mere sesame's size — look! — such is the power of the worm: how great must it be of the world-body, the fortune of the free?

**g.30** — vishvonmeShadashAyAM deshikanAthasya yAvAnprasaraH | kalalAvasthayAM (sthito.api) vishvanimeShe.api tAvAnbhavati ||
> In the state of the world's opening, whatever the teacher-lord's outflow — even standing in the embryo-state, in the world's closing, he is as much.

**g.31** — tripuTImayaM khalu samastaM tatra cha j~neye j~nAtari cha samam | dR^iDhagranthirj~nAnakalA kalayati trailokyamekakalam ||
> All [this] is indeed made of the triad — there, in the known and the knower, alike: the firmly-knotted knowledge-kalā makes the three worlds single-kalā.

**g.32** — kaH sadbhAvavisheShaH kusumAdbhavati gaganakusumasya | yatsphuraNAnuprANo lokaH sphuraNaM cha sarvasAmAnyam ||
> What real distinction arises of the sky-flower from the [real] flower? — that by whose flashing the world is quickened: and the flashing is common to all.
