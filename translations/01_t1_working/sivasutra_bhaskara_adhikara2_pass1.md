# Śivasūtra, Second Adhikāra (Prakāśa 2: sahajavidyodaya) with Bhāskara's Vārttika — Pass 1

*Hop 1 continuation, batch pass 1. Same author-line as the 1.1–1.19 files: Vasugupta (9th c.) + Bhāskara (≈mid-11th c., KSTS 4, Chatterji 1916, M00083). Anchor: Dyczkowski, Aphorisms of Śiva (SUNY 1992).*

### Time-place-context assessment
- **PERIOD:** 9th-c. Kashmir root sūtras; commentary mid-11th c. (post-Utpaladeva, known to Abhinavagupta). Vocabulary sits in the Trika's middle layer — after Spandakārikā, before Abhinava's TĀ synthesis.
- **PLACE/PATH:** Kashmir; transmitted through the KSTS line (Chatterji 1916). No Bengali-script wobble here — the Kashmir transmission is phonologically stable in our text.
- **GENRE/REGISTER:** sūtra + verse-commentary (Vārttika) — aphoristic root, systematic prose-and-kārikā gloss. The register carries mantraśāstra terms (mudrā, vidyā, mantra-vīrya) inside a philosophical frame.
- **FRAME:** the second adhikāra = Śāktopāya (Power Means) — the middle path between the Divine Means (adhikāra 1) and the Individual Means (adhikāra 3). Intertextual web: Bhāskara cites the Trikasāra-Ṣaṭsahasra (2-6: "trikasāre ṣaṭsahasre śivenaiva nirūpitam") — a Kubjikā-lineage text in our corpus's orbit; the four-śakti doctrine (Ambā/Jyeṣṭhā/Vāmā/Raudrī) recurs from 1.3; the naṭa (actor) image recurs from 1.2 and our Siddhitrayī.

## 2.1 — cittaṃ mantraḥ
**The mind is Mantra.**

Vārttika (kārikā 2-3): "Mind itself is to be known as Śiva, the cognizer without adjuncts (nirupādhika), endowed with omniscience and the rest, free of the kalana of space and time; because its dharma is self-experience (svātmānubhava), it is sung as mantra."

Bracket [2-1]: consciousness descends into the world-play, takes the "attitude of tending toward" (aunmukhya) contracted objects — *that* is what "mind" (citta) names; in the ascent (as if climbing stairs, sopāna) it sheds the limitation and becomes the Śiva-nature of dense light; therefore the metonymic identity (abhedopacāra) of mind and mantra.

Notes: citta here = consciousness *in descent* (not the Sāṅkhya internal organ). "abhedopacāra" — the upacāra (secondary designation) of identity: mind is called mantra because both are self-experiencing. [P: cf. Kaulajñānanirṇaya 3/2 — yatra dṛṣṭir manas tatra — the mind as the site of the gaze.]

## 2.2 — prayatnaḥ sādhakaḥ
**Effort is the sādhaka.**

Vārttika (kārikā 2-4/5): "In the absorption of the mind's nature, supreme effort (prayatno'tiśayaḥ) — repeatedly withdrawing the external functions, dissolving the perceptions distinct from the knower, one-pointedness on the identity with the object of the proclaimed mantra's nature — that is called the sādhaka."

Notes: the sādhaka is not a person but the *effort itself* — the sūtra's grammatical point (prayatnaḥ, nom. sg. n., is the subject). The triad of the adhikāra: mantra (2.1), sādhaka (2.2), vidyā (2.3).

## 2.3 — vidyāśarīrasattā mantrarahasyam
**The vidyā's bodily existence is the mantra's secret.**

Vārttika (kārikā 2-9/10): "The sudden unmeṣa (opening) that arises in absorption into pure consciousness — that is defined as vidyā, called the unfolding of self-light (svaprakāśavikāsa). Its bodily presence (śarīrasattā) is the mantra; that is the supreme secret."

Bracket [2-3]: the full speech-ontology: the power of consciousness in bindu-form (bindusvarūpiṇī parāvāṅmayī śakti), taking the subtle life-breath (sūkṣmaprāṇa), unfolds (unmīlayati) paśyantī (without the order of signifier/signified), then madhyamā (with the order, clear/unclear), then vaikharī (with instruments, clear order) — the four levels of speech (parā > paśyantī > madhyamā > vaikharī) as the mantra's descending embodiment.

Notes: vidyā = the sudden self-opening; its "body" = the mantra (speech-levels); rahasya = secret. [P: the four-speech doctrine — cf. TĀ (Dyczkowski vol. 3, "Beyond the plane of the Sound Absolute") and the Yoginīhṛdaya tradition in our library. The bindu/parā-speech complex: cf. Kaulajñānanirṇaya 3/20 (bindumadhye).]

## 2.4 — garbhe cittavikāso viśiṣṭo'vidyāsvapnaḥ
**In the womb [of consciousness], the mind's expansion — distinguished [as such] — is the sleep of avidyā.**

Vārttika (kārikās 2-12/15): "By the force of withdrawal from objects into the essence of consciousness and bliss, the mind abides intensely as pure consciousness — the supreme expansion (paro vikāsaḥ), flooded by the streaming nectar of the power, the world-become-bliss. When that exists, avidyā, spreading the web of principles down to the earth, is 'slept away' (svapna-volopana) — destroyed by the means of satisfaction. Thus vidyā itself is correctly established."

Notes: [X] — the KSTS 4 reading "viśiṣṭo'vidyāsvapnaḥ" ("distinguished [as] the sleep of ignorance") vs the Vimarśinī's "garbhe cittavikāso'viśiṣṭavidyāsvapanaḥ" ("the mind's expansion in the womb is the dreaming of the unparticularized vidyā") — the edition records both. Dyczkowski's anchor follows the Vimarśinī-style reading: "The expansion of the mind in the womb (of consciousness) is the slumber of (all) particular forms of ignorance." I render the KSTS 4 text as printed, diverging knowingly from the anchor. [D]

## 2.5 — vidyāsamutthāne svābhāvike khecarī śivāvasthā
**In the natural arising of vidyā, Khecarī is the Śiva-state.**

Vārttika (kārikās 2-17/20): "The vidyā defined as the pure self-light (svāloka); in its arising, as declared, there is its natural (svābhāvika), innate (sahaja) power, uncontrived in the self (anākṛtrima) — that mudrā by which the yogī moves in the sky of supreme consciousness (paracidākāśa) is Khecarī; it is directly the Śiva-state. She is called Khecarī because she moves (carati) in the sky (khe), the expanse of one's own consciousness."

Notes: svābhāvika/sahaja — the innate — [P: the exact word-pair of Akulavīratantra's sahajānanda (vv. 1, 5, 14 of our Kaula file) and KJN 3/2's svabhāva-language: the same "innate-ness" complex across the Kaula and Trika lines]. khecarī as the state, not a technique.

## 2.6 — gurur upāyaḥ
**The Master is the means.**

Vārttika (kārikā 2-23/25): "Here the Master is the power of consciousness (śakti), the gracious power of the Lord (pārameśvarī anugrāhikā śaktir), the means to the attainment of the goal, by which the devotee rests in the unmana-state (aunmanasapada) called the Supreme Śiva. The Śāmbhavī power, always bestowing grace... he is called guru because he speaks the true things (vaktṛtvāt tāttvikārthānām) and shows the path of rest in the unmana abode."

Notes: guru = the power of grace itself, not the person — the sūtra grammaticalizes: the Master *is* the means (upāya). [P: śaktipāta-language connects to the Śivasūtra's frame in Dyczkowski's exposition of the three upāyas; cf. the "grace" terminology across our Kālīkula files (anugrahāya lokānām — Akulavīra v. 2).]

## 2.7 — mātṛkācakrasaṃbodhaḥ
**The awakening of the Wheel of Mātṛkā.**

Vārttika (kārikās 2-26/33): "Mātṛkā is to be known as self-luminous (svābhāsā), the Lord's supreme power of action (kriyāśaktiḥ parā). Her Wheel (cakra) is declared to be the aggregate of her powers (kalāsamūha). [Bracket:] In the Supreme Śiva, willing to create the world by his own will, there is the first pulse (prathama-spanda) — the reflective self-grasp (svarūpapratyavamarśana) — the power of action called Mātṛkā, one with him. She, having manifested the fourfold power in the form Ambā-Jyeṣṭhā-Vāmā-Raudrī, and having flashed forth the bindu (the pure path in seed-form) and the visarga (the emission), unfolds the impure path within the celebration of the yoni-natured power (yonyātmāśaktollāsa). In it, with the will-power (icchāśakti) that embraces the whole wheel of powers entering the state of the life-breath, from the unfolding of the unstruck sound (anāhata-dhvani) arises the activity of speech pregnant with words and sentences. Thus Mātṛkā alone is the manifester of this whole world of signifier and signified — the primary cause of mantras. Her complete correct knowledge is the awakening (saṃbodha); [then] the yogī, awakened (prabuddha), whatever he utters — even ordinary speech (niranvayaṃ laukikaṃ) — flows without restriction as mantra, achieving its ends."

Notes: THE specialist-term convergence of the whole batch: (1) the **four powers Ambā-Jyeṣṭhā-Vāmā-Raudrī** — the exact catuṣṭaya of sūtra 1.3's yonivarga, now identified as Mātṛkā's fourfold manifestation; (2) **mātṛkā** as kriyāśakti — matches sūtra 1.4 (jñānādhiṣṭhānaṃ mātṛkā) and our Kubjikā work (mātṛkā = the goddess's body); (3) **prathama-spanda** — the Spanda vocabulary of our CP1 stack; (4) **icchāśakti** — cf. 1.13 and KJN 2/6-7; (5) **bindu/visarga/yoni** — the Kula-cosmology terms of our Kaula and Kālīkula files. The awakening turns all speech into mantra — the vidyā-śravaṇa claim of the Kālīkula texts, here in Trika form.

## 2.8 — śarīraṃ haviḥ
**The body is the oblation.**

Vārttika (kārikās 2-35/37): "In the blazing fire of consciousness, at one with Mantra, the physical body — the bondage of karma — melts away (pragalati); being the oblation (havis), it becomes the divine body: this is the supreme body of the sacrificing conscious Lord, in the unbarred great fire, ever-risen."

Notes: the fire-ritual (iṣṭi) interiorized: the sacrificer (yajamāna), the fire (jñānānale), the oblation (śarīraṃ haviḥ). [P: the yajamāna/naṭa figure — the "actor" of 1.2's bracket and our Ajaḍapramātṛsiddhi Stage E (the Lord as actor) — same metaphor-family.]

## 2.9 — jñānam annam
**Knowledge is food.**

Vārttika (kārikā 2-38/39 + bracket): "The knowledge meant here is the supreme [knowledge] correctly taught in these two sections; it is to be known like food, producing satiety (paritṛpti) — fullness (prapūrṇatā) from repeatedly contemplating it. [Bracket:] Established in the supreme state, the awakened one, like an awakened actor (prabuddhanaṭavat), gathers the objects with the senses — as if sipping the nectar of supreme experience — and offers them to himself; he does not, like the bound soul (paśu), sink to the lower state by clinging to objects."

Notes: jñānam = the vidyā-knowledge of the adhikāra (the sahaja-vidyā); annam = what nourishes. The naṭavat bracket: [P] the actor-image — Bhāskara's own 1.2 (naṭasyeva), our Siddhitrayī translation, and here 2.9: the awakened *actor* who performs the world without being bound by it. This is the cross-text insight the user wanted: the actor-metaphor is Bhāskara's signature (3 attestations in one commentary).

## 2.10 — vidyāsaṃhāre tadutthasvapnadarśanam
**In the withdrawal of vidyā, [there is] the vision of the dream arising from it.**

Vārttika (kārikās 2-41/44): "The vidyā that perceives saṃsāra is the common (aviśiṣṭā) one; its withdrawal occurs when the self-light arises. When it is withdrawn, the host of beings arising from it — the deluder — is [seen as] dream (svapna). Its vision (darśana) is the inspection (pratyavekṣaṇa) [of them as dream]. [Bracket:] The yogī, examining the worlds of becoming and non-becoming as dream, immersed and rising in the clear mirror of consciousness, without restriction, like Śiva, moves in the station of the arising of innate vidyā (sahajavidyodayapada) with the powers of eternal omniscience and the rest."

Notes: the adhikāra's close: the world born of avidyā is seen as dream — the *vision of the dream as dream* (svapnadarśana) is the final state. [P: avidyā/svapna vocabulary — cf. 1.9's svapno vikalpāḥ (dream = constructs); the adhikāra closes the loop 2.3's vidyā → 2.10's vidyāsaṃhāra.]
