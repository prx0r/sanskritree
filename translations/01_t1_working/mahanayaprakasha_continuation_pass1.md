## The triune praise (the deśabhāṣā-hymns)

**The first hymn (the commentary's decode)** — devata akka kiśī paru rāji jaga ghasmaru bhairu bhakṣeta | nanta śatta gāsaka nerāji samavāñī āśayatakṣeta ||
> "O divine [goddess], the Akkā (the mother), the Kṛśā (the slender), the supreme, the ruler; the world-devouring (jagad-ghasmaru) Bhairava she devours; the endless (ananta) [power], the [ś]atta... the pure-ruled (nīrājī); the calming (śamavānī), the mind-tamer (āśaya-takṣetā)."
> *[The commentary's gloss: "by the āgama's way, the one supreme Devī, in her whole self, the Kṛśā, shines; the world-devouring Bhairava she devours — for 'Kāla creates beings, Kāla destroys creatures, Kāla wakes in sleepers, Kāla is hard to pass' (the verse cited); having devoured the world-eating Bhairava, the Kāla-in-Bhairava's form creates the world from the cause to the worm."]*

**The commentary's doctrine (the kāla-Bhairava)** — kālaḥ sṛjati bhūtāni kālaḥ saṃharate prajāḥ | kālaḥ supteṣu jāgarti kālo hi duratikramaḥ || (cited) → kālo bhairavaḥ, sa ca nādasvabhāvaḥ prāṇarūpaḥ; sthāvaraṃ jaṅgamaṃ caiva sarvaṃ prāṇasya bhojanam ||
> "Kāla creates beings; Kāla destroys creatures; Kāla wakes in sleepers; Kāla is hard to pass. — Kāla is Bhairava, and he is of the nāda's nature, of the prāṇa's form: 'the unmoving and the moving — all is the prāṇa's food.'"
> *[The Krama's core doctrine: the time-eater is the prāṇa itself; the Devī devours that prāṇa. The commentary: "the Devī, having devoured and dissolved that Bhairava-becoming prāṇa, the endless powers (anantāḥ śaktayaḥ) of the nine waves, risen to devour — per the āgama: 'the goddesses risen from the pores, blazing with the world's light, having vomited the world and devoured it, are Kālī's single morsel.'"]

**The second hymn (the ahalā-śakti)** — akula chutta vyāpaka bodhārana kulagata ahalī śatta gumūna | ganthi cakka ādhāra vidhārana sā nekarūpa akkai vijayūna ||
> "The Akula, released, the pervader, the bodha-arani (the source of awakening), gone into the kula; the ahalā [śakti], the... the one who rends the knots, the cakras, the ādhāras; she, the many-formed, the Akkā, may she triumph."
> *[The commentary: "the Akula touches the kula-transcended form, the pervader, the source of awakening, then gone into the kula; the ahalā-śakti — the hala (the 'h'-form) removed, the akāra-and-hakāra [the first and last phonemes] she takes — the vowel-natured kuṇḍalinī, unwritten, utterable, the rending of the knots, cakras, and ādhāras; sounding like the humming bee, she rises through the ahalā-station, the śakti's form, the opener of the aperture for the upward-going, flashing by the sound's manifestation — thus, the many-formed, may she triumph."]*

**The commentary's citation (the Āmāvāsyātriṃśikā)** — yo 'sau parāparaḥ śāntaḥ śivaḥ sarvagato mahān | aprameyo hyanantaśca vyāpī sarveśvareśvaraḥ || tasyāsti sahajā śaktiḥ sarvaśaktimayī parā | icchājñānakriyātvena saivaikā bahudhā sthitā || → tānantastu samāhṛtya sāmarasye śive sthitā ||
> "He who is the supreme-and-non-supreme, the tranquil Śiva, all-going, great; immeasurable, endless, the pervader, the lord of all lords — his is the innate power, the supreme, made of all the powers; as will, knowledge, and action, she alone stands in many ways. — Gathering those [powers] within, she stands in the equal-taste, in Śiva."

**The triune-praise's frame** — evaṃ darśanārthasya śaktitvāt sthūlā viśvatanurdevī sūkṣmā cinmātrarūpiṇī | parā nityoditā śāntā brahmasattāsvarūpiṇī ||
> "Thus, because the [object-]seeing is a power: the gross Devī is the world's body; the subtle is of the mere-consciousness's form; the supreme is the ever-risen, the tranquil, of the being-of-Brahma's own form."

## The third hymn (the paryanta — the supreme's limit)

**The third hymn** — paryantāna pari paryanti vannavibhāgasa hetu ahetu | khacarīcakka phuri yasa hanti so akka mahāthu jayi aniketu ||
> "The paryanta (the limit), around, surrounding — of the division of the waters, the cause and the non-cause; the khacarī-cakra, flashing, whose glory [it] destroys — that Akkā, the great-meaning, may the homeless (aniketa) triumph."
> *[The commentary: "the one whose self stretches its foot to the end of the operation of the wheel of the activities — the one alone, holding the great-meaning's state, with the shattered expansion of duality's power in his womb; standing in the birth-and-dissolution's field, on the fifth-sixty-fifth station, the crest of the sixty-fourth-limit's final level; the homeless (aniketa) — not established in any one — the pervader of all the dig/deśa/kāla/jāti/vyakti distinctions by his own single form."]*

**The commentary's Pratyabhijñā-citations** — yatkiṃcideva bhūtānāṃ bhavadāvaraṇaṃ prati | na kiṃcideva bhaktānāṃ bhavadāvaraṇaṃ prati || (Rājānaka's own praise) →
> "Whatever [is] the covering of the beings before you — nothing [is] the covering of the devotees before you."
> *[The commentary: the second-level-lord, and beyond him the supreme's limit (paryanta) — "the pure self, the parā-vāk's form, the resting-place of even those selves; the great-meaning (mahārtha), the supreme self" — with the Pratyabhijñā's own citation: "homage to the body of the knower, the ocean of Śiva-consciousness" + the Gītā's jñāna-verse.]*

**The commentary's culmination (the jñāna-bhūmikās)** — jāgratsvapnasuṣuptair jñānabhūmiketi jñānarūpaṃ; tadantaturyabhūmikāyāṃ jñātṛrūpa ātmā; tadatītasturyātītabhūmikāyāṃ... ||
> "The jñāna-level is [constituted] by waking, dream, deep-sleep — the knowledge-form; in the fourth-level within it, the self is of the knower's form; beyond that, in the fourth-beyond level... the piṇḍa-bound ego, dwelling in the first city-of-eight, the body-corseted, the piṇḍa-dwelling; its source the nāda-power, the wave-formed mātṛkā, the prakṛti, the sound-power's form, the expansion of the word-meaning-prapañca, the varṇa-mantra-word-name-vivarta — the middle city-of-eight's form, inner-outer yet the inner-outer-pervading knower's form, and its specific, from the nāda-bindu's yāmala-ness, wrapped in fire-and-wind, the sense-domain, pregnant with the kalā-tattva-bhuvana-adhvan, the bindu-tattva-formed..."
