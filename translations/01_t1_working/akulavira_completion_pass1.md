# Part 3: the kula-rites question and the similes (vv. 50–67)

**50** — eteṣu mocanā nāsti akulavīravivarjitāḥ, yathā tā na jānanti svādaṃ kaṭumadhurasya tu ||
> In these there is no liberation — for those devoid of the Akulavīra: as they do not know the taste of the sweet-and-sour.

**51** — tathā akulavīrantu na jānanti svabhāvagam ||
> So they do not know the Akulavīra, which goes by own-nature.

**52** — rasyaparamāndam iti guhyaṃ sugopitam | lokānāṃ ca hitārthāya siddhanāthena bhāṣitam ||
> "The supreme-bliss essence" — the secret, well-hidden: for the good of the worlds it was spoken by the Siddha-nātha.

**53** — nirvikalpaṃ padaṃ śāntaṃ yatra līnaṃ parāparam | mokṣasya tanmahāsthānaṃ mantra-rūpavivarjitam ||
> The construct-free tranquil state, where the highest-of-the-high is dissolved: that great station of liberation, free of mantra-form.

**54** — tatraiva sṛṣṭirūpeṇa punastatra layaṃ gatā | kiṃ tena bahunoktena sarvabandhavivarjitam ||
> There creation, and again there dissolution gone: what [need] of much speaking? — free of all bonds.

**55** — akulavīraṃ yadā labdhaṃ tadā kiṃ koulikeḥ krameḥ | labdhvā tu mokṣasattvam akulavīraṃ mahāphalam ||
> When the Akulavīra is gained, what [need] of the Kaula-rites? Having gained the liberation-reality — the Akulavīra, the great fruit.

**56** — kaulamārge dvau santi tathā sahaja tathā | kṛtrimā tathā jñeyā sahaja samarasa sthitā ||
> On the Kaula path there are two: the sahaja and the [kṛtaka]. The artificial (kṛtrimā) is to be known thus; the sahaja stands in the equal-taste (samarasa).

**57** — preyaskarabhāvasthā kṛtakā sābhidhīyate | tataḥ sa patyed bhūme mudrāmantraindriyair yoge ||
> The state of the desired-maker [X: "preyaskara" doubtful] is called the kṛtaka: from it one falls to the earth, in the yoga of mudrā, mantra, and senses.

**58** — āvṛtte patane cānye karṇajapen dhūpakeḥ | etat sādhyam idaṃ tattvam etad dhyānaṃ dhāraṇā ||
> In the turning and the falling, with the ear-japa, with the incense: "this is to be accomplished, this the principle, this the meditation, this the fixation" —

**59** — anekeḥ karmasaṃghāteḥ nānāmārgavibhāvaneḥ | vikalpakallolodbhāvā udghāntā bhrāntacetasaḥ ||
> — by the many karma-aggregates, by the many-path contemplations, with the waves of constructs rising: the confused-minded.

**60** — hṛdi śokena santaptā vyāsaṅgā mahābhaye | harṣaviṣādisaṃpannā śocyamānā muhurmuhuḥ ||
> Tormented by grief in the heart, stricken by great fear, seized by joy and poison, lamenting again and again —

**61** — tāvad bhramanti saṃsāre kalpakalpair bhavā... | dagdhabījeṣu saṃbhūtyā tathā naiva prajāyate ||
> — so long they wander in saṃsāra... as from burnt seeds nothing is born.

**62** — mūlacchinne yathā vṛkṣe na prarohaṃ vidyate | akulavīragataṃ bhinnaṃ nānābhāvānubandhane ||
> As in a root-cut tree no growth is found, so the divided [mind], gone to the Akulavīra, [grows not] in the bonds of manifold beings.

**63** — na badhyate yathā vimale rasaṃ vipralayaṃ gatam | tadvad akulavīre ca satve... ||
> As in the pure [water] the taste gone to dissolution is not bound, so in the Akulavīra, in the being...

**64** — timireṇa yathācchannam uditāṅkaṃ na paśyati | ajñānamanas tadvad bhrāntijālavimohitāḥ ||
> As one covered by darkness does not see the risen moon, so the ignorant-minded, deluded by the net of error.

**65** — akule vīre ca saṃprāpte sarvam eti vinśyati | dadhimadhye yathā sarpiḥ kāṣṭhe cāgni sthito yathā ||
> When the akula and the vīra are attained, everything goes to dissolution: as ghee [latent] in curd, as fire standing in wood —

**66** — puṣpe gandhi stile tailaṃ vṛkṣe chāyā samāśritā | madhye... ānandaṃ dīpe prabhā samāśritā ||
> — as fragrance in the flower, oil in the sesame, shade in the tree; bliss in the middle...; light in the lamp —

**67** — padmamadhye ca kuṇḍalyā aṅgapratyaṅgameva ca | raktākhyakulavīre ca tatsarvaṃ viniyojitam ||
> — and in the lotus-middle of the kuṇḍalī, limb and member: in the red-named kula-vīra all that is disposed.

Notes: vv. 55–58 = the **kṛtakā/sahaja doctrine** (the Kaula path's two limbs — our pass-2 C7 finding, now in full context); v. 62-64 = the simile-series (the cut root, the dissolved taste, the moon-in-darkness); v. 67 = the kuṇḍalī-padma image. [P: v. 61's "dagdhabīja" — cf. the KJN's seed-images; the similes' structure = the ghee-in-milk series of the haṭha-tradition.]

# Part 4: the state's marks (vv. 68–105)

**68-69** — bhāvābhāvādisaṃyukteḥ pratyayairindriyagocare | akulavīraṃ na jānanti kṛtakairmohitātmanaḥ || pāśajālanibaddhāś ca mahāmāyāvimohitāḥ | na jānanti padaṃ śāntam acintyaṃ nityasambhavam ||
> By the union of being and non-being, by the perceptions in the senses' domain, they do not know the Akulavīra — those whose selves are deluded by the artificial. Bound in the net of bonds, deluded by the great illusion, they do not know the tranquil state, the unthinkable, ever-arising.

**70-73** — sarvavyāpibhāvasthaṃ sthānavarṇavivarjitam | sarvabhūtisthitaṃ hyekam akṣayaṃ dhyeyavarjitam || sa ca sarvagato bhāvaḥ sthire pūrṇo nirantare | tatra mano vilīnaṃ tu acalaṃ bhāvanāmayam || manovṛtti sthitā cintyā kṣiprā tanmayatāṃ gatā | yathā tiṣṭhati tattvasthaḥ śivaniṣkalam avyaye || tadā tanmayatāṃ yāti nirmalaṃ niṣkalaṃ padam | akulavīraṃ mahadbhūtam ekavīraṃ ca sarvagam ||
> Standing in the all-pervading state, free of place and color; standing in all beings, the one, the imperishable, free of the meditated. And that state, all-going: in the firm, the full, the unbroken. There the mind, dissolved, immovable, made of contemplation. The mind's function stands, to-be-thought, swift, gone to identity-with-it: as he stands in the principle, the Śiva-partless, in the imperishable — then he goes to identity-with-it: the stainless, partless state, the Akulavīra, the great being, the sole hero, all-going.

**74-76** — durlabhaṃ surasiddhānāṃ yoginīnāṃ ca gocaram | kecid vadanti idaṃ dharma idaṃ śāstram idaṃ tapaḥ || ayaṃ loka imaṃ svargam idaṃ sādhyam idaṃ phalam | idaṃ jñānaṃ ca vijñānaṃ śuddhāśuddhim idaṃ param || jñeyaṃ ca tattvakuṭaṃ ca yatra dhyānaṃ dhāraṇā | tadā sau yoginī hyekaḥ nānyastu hi dvitīyakaḥ ||
> Hard to obtain for the divine siddhas, in the yoginīs' domain: some say "this is dharma, this the śāstra, this austerity"; "this world, this heaven, this the accomplishable, this the fruit; this is knowledge and discernment, this the pure-impure, this the supreme." The knowable and the principle's peak, where meditation and fixation [are]: then he is the one yogin — there is truly no other second.

**77-79** — anāgataṃ tu gatā caiva... na tiṣṭhati | na bhūtaṃ na bhaviṣyaṃ ca sthitilayavivarjitam || na cāhaṃ pratyayoddoṣaiḥ lipyate na kadācana | nāhaṃ kaścin na me kaścin baddho na ca bādhakaḥ || na mukti vai na ca na muktam me mokṣasya ca spṛhā | gacchanti sthānaṃ svapnaṃ jāgrat... maitune'pi vā ||
> The not-yet-come and the gone... it does not stand: not the past, not the future — free of persistence and dissolution. "Nor am I ever touched by the faults of the perceptions: I am not anyone; nothing is mine; no bound one, no binder." No liberation indeed, nor the not-liberated; no longing for liberation in me: [the states] go — station, dream, waking... even in union.

**80-84** — bhayādair... śokaiś ca vividhair bhakṣaṇaiḥ tathā | cikitsā naiva kuryāt... indriyārthair na kadācana || ācaret sarvavarṇeṣu tu na tu bhikṣyaṃ vicārayet | evaṃ sa carate yogī yathāruci hutāśanaḥ || piṇḍabandhādi nāsti avasthā mokṣavāsanām | somasūnyas tathā viddhi prāṇāyāmavivarjitam || aprameya nirābhāsaṃ dhāraṇādhyānavivarjitam | yena janmaśatānāni bhāvyā saṃpūjito guruḥ || te labhanti mahājñānaṃ akulavīraṃ tu mokṣadam | yoginīrākiniścakre yasya bhaktiḥ suniścalā ||
> By fears... griefs, and the manifold devourings: no healing should he make — never by the sense-objects. He should behave among all the classes, but not deliberate the alms: so the yogī moves, like the fire, as he likes. No body-bondage and the rest; the state of liberation's impression; empty of the moon [breath]... know: free of prāṇāyāma. The immeasurable, appearing-less, free of fixation and meditation — by whom, through hundreds of births, the guru is worshipped: they gain the great knowledge, the Akulavīra giving liberation — he whose devotion to the yoginīs, the ḍākinīs, and the cakras is quite unshaken.

**85-91** — akulavīraṃ mahadbhūtaṃ gambhīraṃ gahanāmayam | piṇḍātītaṃ yadā jñeyaṃ apiṇḍaṃ piṇḍavivarjitam || padavyākhyānirmuktaṃ vimalaṃ satauditam | tatra... tanmayātmānaṃ vindate śāntaṃ padam || citātītaṃ bhavet sa hi yogasaṃyogavivarjitam | nirvāṇaṃ vāsanāhīnaṃ tṛptātmā ca nirāmayaḥ || tena labdhaṃ na sandeho 'malaṃ malachedanāt | tasya pravartate kṣipraṃ tasyaiva sarvam sarvagam || vedasiddhāntaśāstrāṇi nānāvidhāni śāsanāni ca | tāni sarvāṇi mohāni kāyākleśair nirarthakam || vidyāhrādo... tāstu gativṛttāḥ kugatiṃ gatāḥ | anarthena ca santuṣṭā bahugranthārthacintakāḥ || akulavīraṃ na vindanti kṛtakairmohitātmanām | gativṛttānāṃ kuto jñānaṃ granthakoṭiśatairapi ||
> The Akulavīra, the great being, deep, of the dense nature: beyond the piṇḍa when it is to be known — the piṇḍa-less, free of the piṇḍa. Free of word-and-explanation, stainless, ever-risen: there he finds the self made-of-it, the tranquil state. He becomes beyond thought, free of yoga-and-union: the nirvāṇa without impressions, the satisfied self, free of illness. By it gained — no doubt — the stainless, from the cutting of the impurities: his [power] proceeds swiftly; his is everything, all-going. The Vedas, the Siddhāntas, the śāstras, the manifold teachings: all those are delusions — with the body's torments, worthless. The vidyās... they have gone to the bad state: satisfied with the worthless, thinking over the meaning of many books. They do not find the Akulavīra, those whose selves are deluded by the artificial: for the state-bound, whence knowledge, even by hundreds of crores of books?

**92-99** — karpūrakuṅkumādīnāṃ vṛthā tāmbūlameva ca | ... sarvam tasya nirarthakam | akulavīraṃ tu dehasthaṃ yadā paśyati sarvagam || dharmādharmaphalaṃ nāsti na udakaṃ tīrthasevanā | na kriyā satyamevaṃ vā karmakāṇḍe na bhāvanā || na tasya karmakarmāṇi lokācārāṇi yāni ca | caritāḥ samayācārā janair bhānti vimohitaiḥ || akulavīraṃ na jānanti kiṃ viśiṣṭaṃ kṛtaḥ sthitam | kṛtakā bandhanā loke kalpitāś ca kupiṇḍitaiḥ || samkalpavikalpaṃ ca kalākarmāṇi yāni ca | siddhayo vividhā loke pātālaṃ ca rasāyanam || pratyakṣaṃ yadā labdhaṃ na vigṛhyāt kadācana | sarve te pāśabaddhāś ca adhomārgapradāyakāḥ || na caitān muktir... samarase akulaṃ vīravivarjitāḥ | yathā madirāmānandaṃ kathitaṃ naiva jāyate || tadvad akulavīraṃ tu svasamvedyanirūpamam | na jānanti narā mūḍhāḥ sārātsārataraṃ param ||
> Of camphor, saffron and the rest, and the betel — all that is worthless for him: when he sees the Akulavīra, dwelling in the body, all-going. No fruit of dharma and adharma; no water, no service of the tīrthas; no rite — truly so — no contemplation in the karma-section. Not his the karma and the karmas, whatever the world-customs: the samaya-observances, practiced by the deluded people, shine [as bonds]. They do not know the Akulavīra — what is distinguished, made, standing: the artificial [rites] are bondages in the world, contrived by the corrupt. The conceptualization and the construct, the kalā-works: the manifold siddhis in the world, the underworld, the rasāyana — even what is directly obtained, one should never grasp: all those are bound in bonds, giving the downward path. Not from these is liberation, for those devoid of the akula-vīra in the equal-taste: as the bliss of wine, told [but untasted], never arises — so the Akulavīra, comparable only to self-experience: the fools do not know it — **the more-essential-than-the-essential, the supreme (sārātsārataraṃ param)**.

Notes: v. 99's **sārātsārataraṃ** — our Kubjikā paṭala-1 term, now attested in the Matsyendra-school text — [P: glossary entry: the "essence-of-essence" spans the Kubjikā (1.3/1.7) and the Akulavīra (99) — the late-Kaula dialect's superlative for the supreme teaching].

**100-105** — tāvad bhrāntivimukhatmā yāval... na vindati | citātīte yadā yogī sa yogī yogacintakaḥ || virāgā vāsanā yasya tṛptātmā ca nirāmayaḥ | tāvad bhramanti mohātmā nānābhāvānubandhane || yāvat samam ekatvaṃ paramānandaṃ na vindati | mūḍhānāṃ ca yathāśāstraṃ kumārīśrutitam yathā || akulavīraṃ vindanti kāntamānaiḥ kumārikāḥ | digveśāvinirmuktam sthānavarṇavivarjitam || nirākulaṃ nirvikalpaṃ nirguṇaṃ tu sunirmalam | anāthaṃ sarvanāthaṃ ca pramādonmādavivarjitam ||
> So long [the self is turned by error] until he finds: when the yogī is beyond thought, he is the yogī, the thinker of yoga. He whose impressions are dispassioned, the satisfied self, free of illness — [while others] wander, delusion-souled, in the bonds of manifold beings — until he finds the equal, the oneness, the supreme bliss: as for the fools, [it is] like the śāstra, like the virgin's hearing. The virgins find the Akulavīra by their beloved measures: freed of the directions' garments, free of place and color. The unagitated, the construct-free, the attributeless, quite stainless: the lordless, the lord of all, free of intoxication and madness.

Notes: v. 103's "the virgins find the Akulavīra" — [X: the kumārī-passage is puzzling (the kumārīs' "beloved measures"?); possible the virgin-echo of the kula's kumārī-pūjā — cf. our Kubjikā paṭala 7's kumārī-arithmetic. Pass 2.]

# Part 5: the final affirmation (vv. 106–155)

**106-112** — sarvatra samarasaṃ bhāvitam akulavīraṃ tu kevalam | na jñātaṃ yena dehasthaṃ sa muktaḥ sarvabandhanāt || na tasya kriyābandhena na vedyaṃ na ca vedanā | na yajño nopavāsaśca na ceṣṭā na kriyodayaḥ || na varṇo varṇabhedo... akulavīraṃ yadāgatam | na japo nārcanāgnīnāṃ na homo naiva sādhanam || nāgnipraveśanaṃ tasya mantrapūjācaraṇādi kam | niyamādi na tasyāsti kṣetrapīṭhe ca sevane || na kriyā nārcanā kāmair... tīrthāni vratāni ca | nirālambapadaṃ śāntaṃ tathātītaṃ nirañjanam || sarvajñaṃ paripūrṇaṃ ca svabhāvena vilakṣyate | kāryakāraṇanirmuktam acintitaṃ ca anāmayam || māyātītaṃ nirālambaṃ vyāpakaṃ sarvatomukham | svadehe saṃsthitaṃ śāntam akulavīraṃ tad ucyate ||
> The Akulavīra alone, contemplated as the equal-taste everywhere: by whom, dwelling in the body, it is known [X: the print's "na jñātaṃ" — the negative; read "yena jñātaṃ" — "by whom known"], he is freed from all bonds. No rite-bondage for him; no knowable, no feeling; no sacrifice, no fasting, no effort, no rise of action. No class, no class-distinction, when the Akulavīra is come; no japa, no worship of the fires, no oblation, no sādhana. No entering the fire for him, no mantra-worship and the rest; no restraints for him, in the field-seats and the service. No rite, no worship with desires; no tīrthas, no vows: **the tranquil station without support, and the stainless beyond all that (nirālambapadaṃ śāntaṃ tathātītaṃ nirañjanam)**. All-knowing, complete, distinguished by own-nature: free of cause and effect, unthought, free of illness. Beyond māyā, supportless, pervasive, facing everywhere: standing in one's own body, tranquil — that is called the Akulavīra.

**113-117** — samastam ekadābhūtaṃ dvaitābhāvavivarjitam | akulavīraṃ mahadbhūtam astināstivivarjitam || manobuddhicittasthā... naiva svacetana | na kālakalanā caiva na śaktiś ca na cendriyaḥ || na bhūte gṛhyate sa hi na duḥkhaṃ sukham eva ca | na raso 'dirasasaṅgo... na kṛtakaṃ naiva kārakam || na chāyā na tapaḥ... na śītoṣṇavivedanā | na dinaṃ rātrim ity uktam udayāstamanavarjitam || na mano dṛśyate tatra na uddhvamadhyaṃ ca jāyate | akṣobhyam acalaṃ śāntam īdṛśaṃ tattvanirṇayam ||
> The all, become one at once, free of the absence of duality: the Akulavīra, the great being, free of being and non-being. Mind, intellect, mind-stuff... not self-conscious: no time, no kalana, no power, no sense. Not grasped in the elements; no pain, no pleasure; no taste, no contact with distaste; no made, no maker. No shade, no heat, no cold-heat feeling: no day, no night, so said — free of rising and setting. No mind is seen there, no high-and-middle arises: the unshaken, immovable, tranquil — such is the determination of the principle.

**118-127** — yādṛśena tu bhāvena puruṣo bhāvayet sadā | tādṛśaṃ phalam āpnoti nātra kāryavicāraṇāt || evaṃ tu kulasattvam avācyaṃ paramāmṛtam | agamyaṃ gamyate kasmād bhrāntijñānavihodhitāḥ || na dūre na nikaṭe caiva pratyakṣaṃ na parokṣataḥ | na bhārito na rikto vā nipuṇo nāpi cādhikaḥ || etat pakṣavinirmukto hetudṛṣṭāntavarjitaḥ | kṛtakairmohitā mūḍhāḥ karmakāṇḍaratāḥ tu ye || na teṣāṃ muktiḥ saṃsāre narake yonisaṅkule | akulavīraṃ mahadbhūtaṃ yadā paśyati sarvagam || sabāhyābhyantaraikyaṃ sarvatraiva vyavasthitam | nistaraṅgaṃ nirābhāsaṃ padacchedavivarjitam || sarvāvayavanirmuktaṃ nirvikāraṃ tu nirmalam | adṛśyaṃ nirguṇaṃ nityaṃ nirirodhaṃ ca niṣkalam || na dhyānaṃ dhāraṇā naiva na sthānaṃ varṇameva ca | na recakaṃ pūrakaṃ caiva nārocakam... kumbhakam || na cāntam ādimadhyasthaṃ na stotraṃ vṛttireva ca | grāhyagrāhakanirmuktaṃ grāhyātītaṃ yad bhavet || etaiḥ sarvair vinirmuktaṃ hetudṛṣṭāntavarjitam | sabāhyābhyantaraikatvaṃ sarvatraiva vyavasthitam ||
> By whatever state a man always contemplates, of that kind he obtains the fruit — no deliberation of works here. Thus the kula-reality is unsayable, the supreme nectar: the unattainable — how is it attained? — [by those] awakened by erroneous knowledge [X: "kasmād" — the rhetorical question]. Not far, not near; not directly seen, not indirectly; not full, not empty; not skillful, not more. This is free of the alternatives, free of argument and example: the fools deluded by the artificial, those delighting in the karma-section — no liberation for them in saṃsāra, in the hell crowded with wombs: [but] when he sees the Akulavīra, the great being, all-going — the unity of within and without, established everywhere: waveless, appearing-less, free of the divisions of states; free of all members, changeless, stainless; unseen, attributeless, eternal, unrestrained, partless. No meditation, no fixation, no station, no syllable; no exhalation, no inhalation, no... retention. No end, beginning, middle; no hymn, no function: free of the graspable and the grasper — what is beyond the graspable. Freed from all these, free of argument and example: the unity of within and without, established everywhere.

**128-133** — samarasānandarūpeṇa ekākāraṃ carācare | ye ca jñātaṃ svadehastham akulavīraṃ mahadbhūtam || yasya vaśe sthitaḥ kaścit samarasaṃ rasasthitam | sa brahmā sa haris tveva sa rudro... īśvaras tathā || sa śivaḥ śānto devaḥ sa ca somārkaśaṅkaraḥ | sa viśākhyaḥ mayūrākṣo arhanto buddha eva ca || svayaṃ devī svayaṃ devaḥ svayaṃ śiṣyaḥ svayaṃ guruḥ | svayaṃ dhyānaṃ svayaṃ dhyātā svayaṃ sarveśvaro guruḥ || sarvajño sarvam āśritya sarvato hitalakṣaṇaḥ | sarvayoginī tatra stāḥ sarve siddhāś ca tatra vai || sarvaṃ sarvārthakaṃ caiva sarvajñānaṃ ca tatra vai | yathāsau mahārthaḥ akulavīra iti smṛtam ||
> With the equal-taste-bliss form, one-formed in the moving and unmoving — those who have known the Akulavīra dwelling in their own body, the great being: in whose power someone stands, established in the equal-taste-rasa — he is Brahmā, he is Hari, he is Rudra, he is Īśvara; he is Śiva, the tranquil god; he is moon, sun, Śaṅkara; he is Viśākha, Mayūrākṣa, the Arhat, the Buddha. Himself the goddess, himself the god; himself the pupil, himself the guru; himself the meditation, himself the meditator; himself the lord of all, the guru. All-knowing, resorting to all, of the good-of-all mark: all the yoginīs are there, all the siddhas there indeed. Everything, all-purposeful, all-knowledge — there indeed: so that great one is remembered as the Akulavīra.

**134-145** — śabdaḥ sparśo raso rūpaṃ gandho vāṅmanam eva ca | sarve bhāvāś ca tatraiva ye pralīnāḥ pralayaṃ gatāḥ || nādhāre dhyeyalakṣye ca na nādagocare pare | na iḍā... nābhi kaṇṭhe vā vaktre ghaṇṭikāranadhrayoḥ || na iḍā piṅgalā caiva suṣumṇā ca gamāgame | na nābhicakre kaṇṭhe ca na śire binduke tathā || cakṣuḥ karṇonmīlanaṃ naivaṃ nāsikāgranirīkṣaṇe | na pūrake kumbhake caiva recake ca tathā punaḥ || na bindubhedagranthau ca lalāṭe na ca candramāḥ | praveśe nirgame caiva śivo... uddhve na binduke || na karaṇaiḥ saro mudraiḥ nākāśo vāyumaṇḍale | nākāśe candrasūrye ca bhāvābhāve samāgame || anopamyaṃ nirālambaṃ pakṣapakṣavivarjitam | ajñānamala... stātmā mahāmāyāvimohitāḥ || śāstrārthena vimūḍhātmā mohitā viduṣo janāḥ | na vindanti padaṃ śāntaṃ kaivalyaṃ niṣkalaṃ gurum || sāṅkhyādyāś ca ye kecit nyāyavaiśeṣikās tathā | bauddhārhatāś ca ye kecit somasiddhāntadakṣiṇāḥ || mīmāṃsā pañcarātrāś ca vāmadakṣiṇakaulikāḥ | itihāsapurāṇāni bhūtatattvaṃ ca gāruḍam || ete caiva samāḥ sarve kecid vāpi kriyānvitāḥ | vikalpasiddhidāḥ sarve tadvijñānaṃ ca paṇḍitāḥ || vikalpabahulāḥ sarve mithyāvādinirarthakāḥ | na te mucyanti saṃsāre akulavīravivarjitāḥ ||
> Sound, touch, taste, form, smell, speech and mind: all beings are there, dissolved, gone to dissolution. Not in the support, in the meditation's object; not in the domain of the supreme nāda; not iḍā... in the navel, the throat, the mouth, the uvula, the palatal apertures. Not iḍā, piṅgalā, suṣumṇā, in coming and going; not in the navel-cakra, the throat, the head, the bindu. No opening of the eye and ear; no gazing at the nose-tip; not in inhalation, retention, exhalation again. Not in the bindu-piercing and the knots, not in the forehead, not the moon; not in entering and exiting... not in the bindu above. Not with the instruments, with the pools [X] and the mudrās; no space in the wind-disc; not in space, in sun and moon, in the meeting of being and non-being. The incomparable, supportless, free of the alternatives' sides: the selves stained by the ignorance-impurity, deluded by the great illusion. With the śāstras' meaning, the deluded-souled, the deceived-knowing people do not find the tranquil state, the isolation (kaivalya), the partless guru. The Sāṅkhyas and the rest, the Nyāya-Vaiśeṣikas, the Buddhists and the Arhats, the Somasiddhānta and the Dakṣiṇa[-schools]; Mīmāṃsā, the Pañcarātras, the Vāma, the Dakṣiṇa-Kaulikas; the Itihāsas and Purāṇas, the element-principle, the Gāruḍa — these, all equal, or joined with rites: all give the construct-siddhi — and the pandits' knowledge of it. All full of constructs, false-speaking, worthless: they do not attain release in saṃsāra, those devoid of the Akulavīra.

**146-155** — yāni kāni ca sthānāni girīnagarāsāgaram | sarvatra saṃsthitaṃ nityaṃ sthāvare jaṅgameṣu ca || pañcabhūtātmakaṃ sarve yat kiñcit sacarācaram | śivād devaparyantaṃ sarvaṃ tatraiva saṃsthitam || īdṛśaṃ yoginaṃ dṛṣṭvā upasarpanti ye narāḥ | gandhaiḥ puṣpaiś ca dhūpaiś ca pānapānādibhakṣaṇaiḥ || tarpayanti ca ye bhaktyā vidhibhiś caivāntarātmanā | te'pi bandhaiḥ pramucyanti muktimārgī na kāñcana || brahmendraviṣṇurudrāś ca arhantā buddha eva ca | viśākhyo mayūrākṣā ye ca ṛṣayas tapodhanāḥ || devādibho narendrāś ca ye cānye mokṣakāṅkṣiṇaḥ | te sarve mokṣamicchanti akulavīraṃ tu mokṣadam || athānyaṃ sampravakṣyāmi bhināvasthāṃ svabhāgataḥ | pūrvaṃ yat... sarvān avamārge... kaulike || [153 lacuna] nātra saṃśayaḥ || na jarā teṣāṃ na mṛtyuś ca na śoko duḥkham eva ca | sarvavyādhiharaś caiva na punarbhavasaṃbhavaḥ || akulavīraṃ sthitaṃ divyaṃ siddhanāthaprasādataḥ | sarvataḥ sarvadā śuddhaḥ sarvataḥ sarvadā prabhuḥ ||
> Whatever stations there are — mountains, cities, the ocean: it stands everywhere, eternal, in the stationary and the moving. The five-element-natured, whatever is moving and unmoving: from Śiva down to the gods — everything stands there indeed. Seeing such a yogī, the men who approach him with perfumes, flowers, incense, drinks, foods and the rest; and those who satisfy him with devotion and the inner self's rites — they too are released from the bonds; [but] no path to liberation [elsewhere]. Brahmā, Indra, Viṣṇu, Rudra, the Arhats and the Buddha; Viśākha, Mayūrākṣa, and the sages rich in austerity; the gods and the kings, and the others longing for liberation: they all desire liberation — the Akulavīra gives liberation. Now I will tell the other, the divided state, from its own share: what before... all on the downward path, in the Kaulika [rites] [153: lacuna]. "No doubt here." No old age for them, no death, no grief, no pain; the remover of all diseases; no rebirth arises. The Akulavīra, standing, divine, by the grace of the Siddha-nātha: pure everywhere, always; the Lord everywhere, always.

> Thus the Akulavīra, descended through Macchendrapāda, at the Kāmarūpa-station, by the grace of the Yoginī — complete.
