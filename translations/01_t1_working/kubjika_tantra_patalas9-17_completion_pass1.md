# Paṭala 9 — the marks of mantra-success and the puraścaraṇa

## The siddhi-omens (9/1–9/18)
**9/1** — bhagavan prāṇanātheśa sarvatantraviśārada | lakṣaṇaṃ mantrasiddheśca kathyatāṃ bhutabhāvana ||
> O Lord, lord of life, skilled in all the tantras — tell the marks of the mantra's success, O purifier of beings.

**9/2-9/7** — śṛṇu bhadre pravakṣyāmi śruyatāṃ sāvadhānataḥ | hṛdayagranthibhedaśca romnaśca harṣaṇantathā || tannāmnā gadagadaṃ vākyaṃ unmatta iva lakṣate | nityaharṣo nityaśraddhā sarvadā vedabhūṣitam || vāsanāvaddhadeve ca svapne tāñca prapaśyati | ajñānenānusandhānāt paścāt jñānaṃ prapaśyati || tajajñānaṃ sthiratāmeti dṛḍhañca jāyate punaḥ | devatāpratisandhānāt karuṇoktiḥ prajāyate || sarvadā devatāṃ paśyan devatā bhāvasiddhaye | divyaṃ jñānaṃ śarīre ca sarvadā devatarpaṇam || nirāvilaṃ samānañca yaḥ paśyati sa vuddhimān | loṣṭre ca kāñcane caiva samajñānañca paśyati ||
> Hear, O blessed one — I will tell; hear with attention: the breaking of the heart-knot (hṛdayagranthibheda), and the thrilling of the body-hairs (romaharṣaṇa). The speech becomes choked (gadagada) by that name; he appears like one mad (unmatta). Constant joy, constant faith, ever adorned with the Vedas. The impression-laden one sees the goddess in dreams; from the un-knowing pursuit, afterward he sees the knowledge. That knowledge becomes firm and grows strong again. From the re-union with the goddess, the speech of compassion arises. Ever seeing the goddess, for the attainment of the deity-state: the divine knowledge in the body, ever the satisfaction of the deity. He who sees the unclouded and the equal — he is the wise one: in a clod and in gold he sees the equal-knowledge.

**9/8-9/16** — sa eva sādhakaśreṣṭho jīvanmukto na saṃśayaḥ | sākārabhāvane devi pūtravat snehamācaret || ātmīyatvaṃ samādāya hastapādādisaṃyutam | mama dehe ca nānyosti devatā caiva titaṣṭhati || nānyaddevaṃ kadācittu sarvadā murddhni tiṣṭhati | triśuladhāriṇī devī sarvadā parirakṣati || evañca dhyānayogena jñānayogena vā punaḥ | sarvadānandahṛdaye mahājñānaṃ dine dine || evamabhyasyamānaśca ahanyahani sādhakaḥ | sākāreṇa maheśāni nirākārañca bhāvayet || sākāreṇa vinā devi nirākāraṃna paśyati | sākāre mūlakaṃ sarvaṃ sākārañca prapaśyati || abhyāsena sadā devi nirākāraṃ prapadyate | nirākāraṃ samāsādya nirañjanapadaṃ labhet || nirākāreṇa deveśi kṛtakṛtyaśca sādhakaḥ | evaṃ sarvātmanā devi kāmarūpo na saṃśayaḥ ||
> He alone is the best of sādhakas, liberated-while-living — no doubt. In the contemplation of the formed (sākāra), O Devī, he should act with love like a son [to the goddess]. Taking up the own-ness, joined with hands, feet and the rest: "In my body there is no other — the deity indeed dwells." Never any other god; always she stands in the head; the trident-bearing Devī ever protects. And thus, by the yoga of meditation or the yoga of knowledge, in the heart of ever-bliss, the great knowledge [grows] day by day. Practicing thus, day after day, the sādhaka, O great Īśānī, should contemplate the formless (nirākāra) through the formed. Without the formed, O Devī, he does not see the formless: in the formed is the root of all; through the formed he sees [it]. By the practice, always, O Devī, he attains the formless: **attaining the formless, he obtains the station of the Stainless (nirañjanapada)**. By the formless, O Lady of the gods, the sādhaka is fulfilled — thus with the whole self, O Devī, he is of desire-form (kāmarūpa), no doubt.

Notes: 9/15 = the text's own definitional verse (the internal self-consistency we found in the first searches — "nirākāraṃ samāsādya nirañjanapadaṃ labhet"); the sākāra→nirākāra ladder; the "unmatta iva" (like one mad — the Unmattabhairava's name-connection!).

## The puraścaraṇa (9/19–9/47)
**9/19-9/20** — hṛdayeśa mahādeva parameśa purātana | kenopāyena deveśa mantrasiddhirbhaved dhruvam | puraścaryāvidhānañca kathyatāṃ prāṇavallabha ||
> O lord of the heart, great god, ancient supreme Lord — by what means, O Lord of gods, does the mantra's success surely arise? And the procedure of the preliminary worship (puraścaryā) — tell it, O beloved of life.

**9/21-9/25** — sādhu sādhu tvayā pṛṣṭam śruyatāṃ varavarṇini | anāyāsena mastrasya siddhirbhavati sundari || tatsarvaṃ kathayiṣyāmi satyaṃ satyaṃ suniścitam | ramābījaṃ viśvabījaṃ vadhūbījaṃ maheśvari || puṭitaikajaṭāmantraṃ prajapet sādhakottamaḥ | aṣṭasahasraṃ maheśāni japettu sādhakottamaḥ || gadyapadyamayīvāṇī bhavettasya na saṃśayaḥ | kūrcabījaṃ śaktibījaṃ kṣakāraṃ tadanantaram || puṭitaṃ dakṣiṇāmantraṃ ṣaṭsahasraṃ krameṇa tu | sarvarājyesvaro bhūtvā vicareddhi mahītale ||
> Well asked, well asked — hear, O fair-colored one: without effort the mantra's success arises, O lovely one. All that I will tell — truth, truth, quite certain: with the ramā-bīja, the viśva-bīja, the vadhū-bīja, wrapping the Eka-jaṭā-mantra, the best of sādhakas should chant; eight thousand, O great Īśānī, the best of sādhakas should chant — his speech becomes made of prose-and-verse, no doubt. With the kūrca-bīja, the śakti-bīja, the kṣa after them, wrapping the Dākṣiṇā-mantra, six thousand in order: becoming the lord of all kingdoms, he moves upon the earth.

**9/26-9/31** — kulastrīyonimadhye tu dhumāmantraṃ samālikhet | śaktyā tu paṭitaṃ kṛtvā navalakṣam japet sudhīḥ || yuddhe vijayamāpnoti sa eva sādhakottamaḥ | kulantrīyonimadhye tu dhūmāmantraṃ likhet punaḥ || aṣṭasahasraṃ japeddhīmān vicareddhi mahītale | yuddhe vijayamamāpnoti śatrūn nāśayate kṣaṇāt || kulastrībhālamūle ca likhenmantraṃ sulakṣaṇam | taṃ dṛṣṭvā sādhakaśreṣṭhaḥ pañcalakṣaṃ japet punaḥ || sa bhaveddevatātulyo narendraśca na saṃśayaḥ | mātṛkāpuṭitaṃ kṛtvā dhanadāmantramuttamam || dvisahasraṃ japet prājñaḥ sarvarājyeśvarobhavet | vicaranti yathā devāstathaiva sādhakottamaḥ ||
> In the middle of the kula-woman's yoni he should write the Dhūmā-mantra; wrapped by the śakti [bīja], the wise one should chant nine lakhs: he gains victory in battle — he alone, the best of sādhakas. In the middle of the kula-woman's yoni let him write the Dhūmā-mantra again; having chanted eight thousand, the wise one moves upon the earth: he gains victory in battle; he destroys the enemies in an instant. At the root of the kula-woman's forehead he should write the well-marked mantra; seeing that, the best of sādhakas chants five lakhs again: he becomes equal to the deity, and a king among men — no doubt. Having made the mātṛkā-wrapping, the excellent Dhanadā-mantra — two thousand the wise one chants: he becomes the lord of all kingdoms; as the gods move, so the best of sādhakas.

**9/32-9/39** — mahāpadmasya madhye tu aṣṭapatraṃ likhettataḥ | aṣṭapatrasya madhye tu vagalāmantrakaṃ punaḥ || hṛdaye sthāpayitvā tu japetta sādhakottamaḥ | satāṃ kṣobhaṃ nayatyevamarīnmarddayate kṣaṇāt || aṣṭasahasraṃ dvisahasraṃ navasahasraṃ japet sudhīḥ | trailokyaṃ kṣobhayatyeva satyaṃ satyaṃ na saṃśayaḥ || kāmabījaṃ ramābījaṃ gurubījaṃ maheśvari | dhanadāpuṭitaṃ kṛtvā dik sahasraṃ japet sudhīḥ || vātastambhaṃ jalastambhaṃ vahniśaityaṃ nayed dhruvam | surasundarī mahāmantraṃ kalau pūrṇaphalapradam || pūṭitaṃ śaktivījantu trimāsāt parato dhruvam | strīrūpā sundarī devi nikacai brajati dhruvam || anāyāsena deveśi kalau pūrṇaphalapradā | tasmāt sarvaprayatnena kartavyañca maheśvari || ratisundarī mahādevī ratibījasamanvitā | aṣṭasahasraṃ japeddevi tataḥsiddhiṃ samālabhet ||
> In the middle of the great lotus he should write the eight-petaled; in the middle of the eight-petaled, the Vagalā-mantra again. Placing it in the heart, the best of sādhakas chants: he brings agitation to the good; he crushes the enemies in an instant. Eight thousand, two thousand, nine thousand — the wise one chants: he agitates the three worlds — truly, truly, no doubt. With the kāma-bīja, the ramā-bīja, the guru-bīja, O great Īśvarī, wrapping the Dhanadā, the wise one chants ten thousand: he brings the stambha of the wind, the stambha of the water, the cooling of the fire — surely. The Surasundarī great mantra gives full fruit in the Kali age; wrapped in the śakti-bīja, surely after three months: the goddess Sundarī of woman-form moves... surely [X: "nikacai" corrupt]. Without effort, O Lady of the gods, in the Kali age she gives full fruit — therefore with all effort it should be done, O great Īśvarī. The great goddess Rati-sundarī, joined with the rati-bīja — eight thousand the Devī-chanting one chants; then he obtains the siddhi.

**9/40-9/47** — prāṇahīno yathā dehaḥ sarvakarmasu na kṣamaḥ | puraścaraṇahīnastu tathāmantraḥ prakīrtitaḥ || pañcāṅgopāsanañcaiva puraścaraṇakarmaṇi | japahomau tarpaṇañcābhiṣeko brahmaṇabhojanaṃ || pañcāṅgāni maheśāni sādhakānāṃ sukhāvaham | mahābhiyekāt puruṣaḥ sarva karmasu ca kṣamaḥ || mahābhiṣekaṃ vinā devi na kuryānmantracintanam | tasmāt sarvaprayatnena abhiṣekī bhavennaraḥ || śarat kāle mahādevi kartavyaṃ mantracintanam | navamīntu samārabhya yāvacca navamī bhavet || tāvajjapaḥ prakartavyaḥ puraścaraṇamiṣyate | caturthyāntu samārabhya yāvacca navamī bhavet || tāvajjapaḥ prakartavyaḥ sakhaṇḍaṃ parikīrtitam | saptamyāñca samārabhya yāvacca navamī bhavet || tāvajjapādikaṃ kuryāt sarvakāmārthasiddhaye | śubhakāle samārabhya mantrasādhanamācaret || candrasūryagrahe caiva kartavyaṃ mantracintanam ||
> As a body without breath is unfit for all works, so the mantra without the preliminary worship (puraścaraṇa) is proclaimed. And the five-limbed worship in the preliminary work: japa, homa, tarpana, the consecration (abhiṣeka), and the feeding of the brāhmaṇas — the five limbs, O great Īśānī, bringing ease to the sādhakas. From the great consecration (mahābhiṣeka) a man is fit for all works. Without the great consecration, O Devī, one should not perform the mantra-contemplation — therefore with all effort the man should be consecrated. In the autumn season, O great Devī, the mantra-contemplation is to be done: beginning from the ninth, until the ninth [of the fortnight], so long the japa is to be made — that is the prescribed preliminary. Beginning from the fourth until the ninth — that japa is proclaimed "with the broken [vow]" (sakhaṇḍa). Beginning from the seventh until the ninth, the japa and the rest should be done for the attainment of all desires. Beginning at the auspicious time, the mantra-sādhana should be performed; at the eclipse of sun and moon, the mantra-contemplation is to be done.

Notes: the siddhi-omens (the classic lakṣaṇa-list); the sākāra/nirākāra ladder ending in **nirañjanapada** (9/15 — the opening's goal, delivered); the puraścaraṇa's five limbs; the tithi-arithmetic. # Paṭala 10 — the bādhaka-prakaraṇa (the four obstacles to conception)

## The four obstacles (10.1–10.4)
**The frame** — athedānīṃ pravakṣyāmi vandhyāyāścottamaṃ vidhim | raktamātrī ca ṣaṣṭhī ca ḍāṅkuro jalakumārakaḥ || caturvidho bādhakaḥ syāt strīṇāṃ munivibhāṣitaḥ | teṣāṃ svabhāvaṃ vakṣyāmi yathāśāstraṃ vidhānataḥ | eteṣāṃ pūjanaṃ (saphalaṃ) kāryaṃ janaiḥ santānakāṅkṣibhiḥ | niḥsāraṇaṃ (niśārccanaṃ) sthāpanañca validānaṃ viśeṣataḥ || karttavyaṃ guruvākyena jñātvā śāstrasya lakṣaṇam | caturvidho bādhakaḥ syād jāyate ṛtukālataḥ ||
> Now I will tell the supreme rite for the barren woman: Raktamātrī, Ṣaṣṭhī, Ḍāṅkura, and the Water-Child (jalakumāraka) — the fourfold obstacle (bādhaka) for women, declared by the sages. Their natures I will tell, by the rule of the śāstra. Their worship is to be done, fruitful, by the people desiring offspring; the expulsion (niḥsāraṇa), the establishing (sthāpana), and the bali-offering especially — to be done by the guru's word, knowing the śāstra's marks. The fourfold obstacle arises from the time of the season (ṛtu).

**Raktamātrī** (10.2) — vyathā kaṭyāṃ tathā nābheradhaḥ pārśve stane tathā | raktamātrī pradoṣeṇa jāyate phalahīnatā || māsamekaṃ dvayaṃ vāpi ṛtūhīnā bhavedyadi | raktamātrīpradoṣeṇa jāyate phalahīnatā ||
> Pain in the hip, below the navel, in the side, in the breast: from the fault of the Blood-Mother (raktamātrī) the fruitlessness arises. If she is without the season for a month or two, from the Raktamātrī-fault the fruitlessness arises. — Thus the Raktamātrī-obstacle.

**Ṣaṣṭhī** (10.3) — netre haste bhavejjvālā yonau caiva viśeṣataḥ | lālāsaṃyukta raktañca (vivarṇaḥ) ṣaṣṭhīnāṃ vādhakena ca || māsaikena bhaved ṣasyā ṛtusnānaṃ dvayaṃ tathā | malinā raktayoniḥ syāt ṣaṣṭhīnāṃ dūṣaṇaṃ bhavet ||
> Burning in the eye, in the hand, and especially in the yoni; the blood joined with saliva, discolored, by the obstacle of the Ṣaṣṭhīs. Within a month there is the... of the pregnant one; the season-bath twice; the blood-yoni becomes stained — this is the Ṣaṣṭhī-corruption.

**Ḍāṅkura** (10.4) — udvego gurutā dehe raktasrāvo bhavenmuhūḥ | nābheradho bhavecchūlaṃ bhāṅkuraḥ sutahārakaḥ || māsatraye caturthe vā ṛtuhīnā bhavedyadi | kṛśāṅgī karapāde syājjvālā ḍāṅkuradūṣaṇam ||
> Anxiety, heaviness in the body, the blood-flow arising at times; below the navel the colic — the Ḍāṅkura, the destroyer of the child. If she is without the season in the third or fourth month, thin-limbed, with burning in hand and foot — the Ḍāṅkura-corruption.

**Jalakumāraka** (10.5) — saśūlā ca sagarbhā ca śuṣkadehālparaktikā | jalakumāradoṣeṇa jāyate phalahīnatā || sadā kruddhā bhavet sthūlā vahūkāle ṛtustathā | gurustanī svalparaktā jalakumāradūṣaṇam ||
> With colic, with [false] pregnancy, with dried body, little blood — from the Water-Child's fault the fruitlessness arises. Ever angry, stout, with the season at the wrong time; heavy-breasted, of little blood — the Water-Child's corruption.

## The worship and the mantras (10.6–10.10)
The pūjā-yantras: the triangle, the nine-cornered, the six-cornered, the mandala-form — to be written and invoked with the mantras. The dhyānas: Raktamātrī = the fair-bodied (gaurāṅgī), moon-crowned, three-eyed, two-armed, the world-holder dwelling in the lying-in chamber (sūtikāgṛha); Ṣaṣṭhī = the white, two-armed, dwelling in the lying-in house, drinking the woman's rajas, the tranquil Tribhuvaneśvarī; Ḍāṅkura and Jalakumāraka = the white two-armed children likewise.

The mantras:
- Raktamātrī: oṃ hrīṃ śrīṃ hūṃ phaṭ svāhā — "oṃ hrīṃ śrīṃ hūṃ phaṭ svāhā" (the tāra-māyā-lakṣmī with ḍrūṃ phaṭ svāhā-end)
- Ṣaṣṭhī: oṃ klīṃ klīṃ phaṭ svāhā — "the tāra with the double kāma, phaṭ svāhā-end" — "giving the siddhi of sons"
- Ḍāṅkura: oṃ hrīṃ vajrayoginī oṃ hrīṃ svāhā — "the hrīṃ with the vimalā-bīja... the Raktayoginī-mantra"
- Jalakumāraka: oṃ śūlāya vajrahastāya svāhā — "the tāra, to the colic-one, the vajra-handed"

## The medicines (10.11–10.13)
**The frame** — snānakāle samutpanne deyamauṣadhamuttamam | raktakārpāsamūlañca nāgadānaṃ tathaiva ca || viḍvalayāśca mūlañca maricena samanvitam | deyañca tridinaṃ yāvadaśuciḥ syādyadāṅganā || snānopari ca dātavyaṃ vārtākīkīṭamuttamam | ekaviṃśaddinaṃ yāvad dugdhena miśritaṃ pivet ||
> At the time of the bath arising, the supreme medicine is to be given: the root of the red cotton-plant, and the nāgadāna; the root of the viḍvalayā, joined with pepper — to be given for three days, while the woman is impure. After the bath it is to be given: the supreme eggplant-insect [X], mixed with milk, she should drink for twenty-one days.

**The recipe** — methitolakadvitīyaṃ (tolakamekañca) khaṇḍañca tolakadvaya | ghṛtaṃ tokalamekañca dugdhena militaṃ pivet | dugdhaṃ ṣoḍaśatolañca pramāṇaṃ parikīrtitam | mṛtavatsā mṛtagarbhā kākavandhyā tathaiva ca || putrahīnā ca vandhyā ca pareṇaivāparādhitā | saṃharet sarvadoṣāṃśca methibhakṣaṇamuttamam || nāḍīśuddhāyāṃ striyāmapara ṛtukāle idamauṣadhaṃ deyam ||
> The methi (fenugreek) two tolas, the sugar one tola, the ghee one tola, mixed with milk — she should drink; the milk sixteen tolas is the declared measure. The stillbirth-woman, the dead-fetus-woman, the crow-barren (kākavandhyā), the sonless, the barren, the one offended by another: the eating of the methi [mixture] removes all the faults — the supreme. To the woman whose channels are pure, at the next season-time, this medicine is to be given.

**The second recipe** — aśvagandhā vaṭavyālo lakṣaṇasya ca mūlakam | (tadabhāve vāṭyālakaṃ śvetañca varavarṇini) | bhakṣayet sapurātrāṇi dugdhe piṣṭvā punaḥ punaḥ | avaśyaṃ santatistasyāḥ puṃhīnā garbhadhāriṇī ||
> The aśvagandhā, the vaṭavyāla, the root of the lakṣaṇa: (in its absence, the white vāṭyālaka, O fair-colored one) — she should eat it with the purāṇas [X: "sapurātrāṇi" — possibly "sapuraścaraṇāṇi" — with the rites?], ground in milk, again and again: surely her offspring, free of the male-deficient, bears the womb.

Notes: the medical-tantra paṭala — the gynecology of the Kālīkula (the four conception-obstacles, their mantras and medicines). [P: the sūtikāgṛha (lying-in chamber) goddesses — the maternity-deities of the Kaula household; the vajrayoginī (Ḍāṅkura's mantra) — cf. the yoginī-cult vocabulary; the methi-recipe — the living Bengali medical practice.]

# Paṭala 11 — the ritual procedure (the Raktamātrī-pūjā)

# Paṭala 14 — the mental worship (mānasapūjā)

**The bhāva-offerings** — hṛtpadmamāsanaṃ dadyāt sahasrāracyutāmṛtaṃ pādyaṃ caraṇayordadyāt manaścārghyaṃ nivedayet | tenāmṛtenācamanaṃ snānīiyaṃ tena ca smṛtam | ākāśatantraṃ(?) vastraṃ syāt gandhaḥ syādgandhatattvakam | cittaṃ prakalpayet puṣpaṃ dhupaṃ prāṇān prakalpayet | tejastattvañca dīpārthaṃ naivedyaṃ syāt sudhāmbudhiḥ | anāhatadhvanirghaṇṭā śabdatattvañca gītakam | nṛtyamindriyacāñcalyaṃ vāyutattvañca cāmaram | sahasrāraṃ bhavet chatraṃ haṃsaḥ syāt pādukādvayam ||
> The heart-lotus as the seat he should give; the amṛta dripping from the thousand [petals] as the foot-water at the feet; the mind itself as the arghya he should offer. The unstruck sound (anāhata-dhvani) is the bell; the sound-principle the song. The dance is the senses' restlessness; the wind-principle the fly-whisk. The thousand-petaled is the parasol; the haṃsa the pair of sandals.

**The ten virtue-flowers** — amāyādyairbhāvapuṣpairarccayedtāvagocarām | amāyā manahaṅkāra-marāgamadamantataḥ | amohakamadantañca advevākṣobhakau tataḥ | amātsaryasalobhañca daśapuṣpaṃ vidurvudhāḥ || ahiṃsā paramaṃ puṣpaṃ puṣpamintriyanigraham | jñānapuṣpaṃ dayāpuṣpaṃ kṣamāpuṣpaṃ tathaiva ca || iti pañcadaśairbhāvapuṣpaiḥ saṃpūjayecchivām ||

Non-violence (ahiṃsā) is the supreme flower; the flower of the senses' restraint; the knowledge-flower, the compassion-flower, the forgiveness-flower likewise — with the fifteen state-flowers he should worship Śivā.

# Paṭala 15 — the male-infertility remedy (puṃvandhyā-praśamana)

**The recipe** — tejomūlaṃ śatamūlī bhūmikuṣmāṇḍameva ca | aśvagandhā godhūmaśca śālmaleḥ kuṭnalantathā | gokṣuraṃ vānarībījaṃ valāyābījameva ca | koṭilā kṣīrakākolī samabhāgaṃ tribhiḥ palaiḥ | piṣṭvā māhiṣasarpirbhirmodakaṃ śarkarāyutam | mākṣikeṇa samāyuktaṃ gandhadravyasamanvitam | modakaṃ sammadaṃ nāma puṃvandyātvavināśanam | anupānaṃ ghṛtaṃ dugdhaṃ pakṣiṇo māṃsabhakṣaṇam | valadaṃ sarvadoṣaghnaṃ bīryadoṣavināśanam | kṣīṇaśukro'pyaśukraśca mandadṛṣṭistathaiva ca | valahīnaḥ kṣīṇadeho vākyapāruṣyasaṅkulaḥ | vahūdāraśca vṛddhaśca kanyāsantatireva ca || bhakṣayedauṣadhaṃ māsairvṛddho'pi taruṇo bhavet ||

Ground with buffalo-ghee into a modaka (pill), joined with sugar, mixed with honey, furnished with the fragrant substances: the modaka named Sammada ("the intoxication"), the destroyer of the male-barrenness. The after-drink is ghee and milk, the eating of bird's flesh. The valada (the strength-giver) destroys all faults, destroys the seed-faults. # Paṭala 16 — the bath-rite (snānavidhi) and the herb-lists

**The rite** — sārddhahastapramāṇāṃ vedikāṃ kṛtvā tadupari kūrmapṛṣṭhaṃ sthāpayitvā kūrmapṛṣṭhopari sindūreṇa kūrmaṃ nirmāya tatra bījatrayaṃ tasyopari likhitvā | oṃ hrīṃ hrīṃ | tasyopari striyaṃ pūrvābhimukhīṃ saṃsthāpya caturddikṣu anena kāṇḍacatuṣṭayaṃ samāropya raktasūtreṇa saptadhā veṣṭayitvā hūṃ iti mantreṇa śvetaśarṣaparakṣāṃ vidhāya pañcagavyādibhiḥ snāpayet ||
> Making the altar of one-and-a-half hastas, placing the tortoise-back (kūrmapṛṣṭha) upon it; on the tortoise-back drawing the tortoise with sindūra, writing the three bījas above it — oṃ hrīṃ hrīṃ; placing the woman upon it facing east; raising the four branches (kāṇḍa) on the four sides; winding [it] seven times with the red thread; making the white-mustard protection with the hūṃ-mantra; bathing her with the pañcagavya and the rest —

**The bath-sequences** — tatra prathamaṃ hrīṃ hrīṃ iti (mantreṇa) gomūtreṇa | oṃ hrīṃ gomayena || oṃ dugdhena | hūṃ dadhnā | hūṃ ghṛtena | hrīṃ kuśodarena | oṃ śarkarayā | hrīṃ sarvauṣadhi mahauṣadhibhyāṃ | klīṃ jalena | gaṅgāmṛttikayā | oṃ śaṅyajalena | krāṃ ghṛtena iti pañcaratnena | hūṃ dhānyodakena | hrīṃ dūrvājalena | sarvadravyamekīkṛtya sahasradhārayā snāpayet | oṃ hrīṃ hrīṃ hūṃ astrāya phaṭ iti sahasradhārayā stvāpayet ||
> There, first with the hrīṃ hrīṃ-mantra, with cow's urine; oṃ hrīṃ, with cow-dung; oṃ, with milk; hūṃ, with curd; hūṃ, with ghee; hrīṃ, with kuśa-water; oṃ, with sugar; hrīṃ, with the all-herbs and the great-herbs; klīṃ, with water; with the Gaṅgā-clay; oṃ, with conch-water; krāṃ, with ghee — with the five gems; hūṃ, with grain-water; hrīṃ, with dūrvā-water: combining all the substances, he bathes her with the thousand-streams (sahasradhārā). With "oṃ hrīṃ hrīṃ hūṃ astrāya phaṭ," he bathes her with the thousand-streams.

**The herb-lists** — pṛśnipaṇī śyāmalatā bhṛṅgarājaḥ śatāvarī | guḍūcī sahadevī ca mahauṣadhigaṇaḥ smṛtaḥ || surāmāṃsī vacā kuṣṭhaṃ śaileyaṃ rajanīdvayam | (haridrā dāruharidrā) | śuṇḍī campakamūstañca sarvauṣadhigaṇaḥ smṛtaḥ ||
> Pṛśnipaṇī, Śyāmalatā, Bhṛṅgarāja, Śatāvarī, Guḍūcī, Sahadevī — the group of the great herbs. Surāmāṃsī, Vacā, Kuṣṭha, Śaileya, the two Rajanīs (turmeric and dark-turmeric), Śuṇḍī, Campaka, Mūsta — the group of the all-herbs.

**The stillbirth-bath (baṅgalā)** — atha mṛtavatsāsnānam (baṅgalā) —
> Now the stillbirth-bath (in Bengali).

# Paṭala 17 — the stillbirth-remedy and the kavacas

**The vernacular incantation** — ākāṅkiramūla vāṭiyā ṛtusnāna kariyā khā-ive, khā-ile garbha janme | ye ākāṅkiramūla uttare yā-iyā thāke, tāhā vāṭiyā yonite ṛtukāle pralepa dile avaśya garbha haya ||
> (Bengali) The ākāṅkī-root: ground and eaten after the season-bath, eating it, the womb-conception arises. The ākāṅkī-root that grows in the north: grinding it and applying it as a salve in the yoni at the season-time, conception surely occurs.

**The Narasiṃha-kavaca** — oṃ namo narasiṃhāya | hiraṇyakasiporvakṣovidārāya tribhuvanañcopakārāyahākinī kulolmathanāya amukyā doṣān hara hara sara sara ehi rudrāṃ jñāpayati svāhā | hata siddhi narasiṃhera rava | oṃ oṃ hāṃ hāṃ hrīṃ hrīṃ svāhā ||
> Oṃ, homage to Narasiṃha: to the splitter of Hiraṇyakaśipu's chest, doing good to the three worlds, the churner of the Hākinī-kula: "O one, take away, take away, the faults of So-and-so; flow, flow — this he proclaims to Rudra, svāhā." "Victory, the siddhi of Narasiṃha's roar!" Oṃ oṃ hāṃ hāṃ hrīṃ hrīṃ svāhā.

*Written on a birch-bark and worn, the stillbirth-woman goes far away. Chanting "oṃ namo narasiṃhāya svāhā" seven times, reciting it with the [X: "kāgaja" — the paper?], kindling the fire: all the dānavas roar afar... [the vernacular incantations: "kāmadeva hāḍī ādā navakavisthiramanabījam | oṃ hāṃ hrīṃ amukīra garbha rakṣa rakṣa svāhā" — "O kāma-deva... protect, protect the womb of So-and-so, svāhā" — and the tying of the śikhā; the mustard-scattering formula: "āra ānalet śarṣapa paḍiyā śayyāra cā-ira digamavye puḍive pā/cakhāna tave mṛtavatsā dura hara" — (Bengali) "having scattered the mustard in the fire, in the bed's four directions, [the stillbirth] flees far"]; the bath-incantation ("viṣe viṣa hare āṭhā se-i kīṭapataṅga mare" — "poison takes the poison; that insect and moth dies").*

**The Mṛtavatsā-kavaca** — hrīṃ aiṃ śrīṃ vaiṃ vaiṃ vaiṃ vaiṃ rakṣa rakṣa sarvabhūtopadravebyaḥ svāhā || hrīṃ pātu kālī sarvāṅgaṃ saṃso bhaṭarājāya svāhā || hrīṃ pātu vṛddharūpasamātṛkā sarvatoharatu ||

**The frame-verse** — idantu paramaṃ guhyaṃ sādhakābhīṣṭadāyakam | sarvarakṣākarañcaiva bhūtapretairviśeṣataḥ || anapatyayā ca yā nārī mṛtavat sā ca yā bhavet | vahūputrī jīvavatsā bhavatyeva na saṃśayaḥ || bhūtapretapiśācāśca ye cānye'niṣṭakārakāḥ | durādeva palāyante kavacasya ca dhāraṇāt || bhūtānāñca prakṛtirmṛtavat sāharā bhavet | tava dhāryaṃ prayatnena mṛtavatsā pranāśakam | avaśyaṃ dhārayet tasyā mṛtavatsādūraṃ gatā ||
> This is the supreme secret, giving the sādhaka's desire; the maker of all protection, especially from the ghosts and the dead.
