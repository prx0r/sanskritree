## Why Bhāskara and not Kṣemarāja first
- Dyczkowski's *Aphorisms of Śiva* is the **first and only English translation of Bhāskara's commentary** (his intro: "no English translation of Bhaskara's commentary on the Aphorisms has been published") — so this hop is anchored AND nearly-untranslated at once.
- Kṣemarāja's Vimarśinī (early 11th c., KSTS 1) is the famous rival commentary — **Jaideva Singh's translation** ("Śiva Sūtras: The Yoga of Supreme Identity") is the acquisition target for Hop 1 stage 2; the Lakshmanjoo *Supreme Awakening* scan (OCR-pending) is a second reference.
- The two commentaries diverge on the same sūtras (Dyczkowski: "Bhaskara and Ksemaraja: Two Visions, One Truth") — our three-readings method is built to display exactly this.

# Part II. Word-level analysis with usage parallels

## Sūtra 1.1 — caitanyam ātmā

**Sandhi:** caitanyam + ātmā. **Grammar:** two nominative singulars in apposition — identity predication: "Consciousness [is] the Self."

**Bhāskara's Vārttika (KSTS 4, kārikā 1-15):**
> caitanyam ātmano rūpaṃ siddhaṃ jñānakriyātmakam |
> tasyānāvṛtarūpatvāc chivatvaṃ kena vāryate ||
> "Consciousness is the Self's own nature, established (siddha) as knowledge-and-action; because its nature is unobscured, by whom is its Śiva-ness warded off?"

**Word lab:**
- **caitanya** — bhāva-noun from *cetana* ("the state of being conscious"). In the Śaiva stack it is not a quality (Nyāya's guṇa) nor a nondual substrate (Vedānta) but the *active* reality: light + reflective awareness (prakāśa–vimarśa), identified by the tradition with absolute freedom (svātantrya). Dyczkowski's exposition: "consciousness is pure Being, the absolute itself, beyond all specification... consciousness is itself perfect freedom understood as the union of all knowledge and action."
- **ātmā** — deliberately repurposed: not the Nyāya substance-self nor Vedānta's ātman=brahman, but *svarūpa* (one's own essential nature). Bhāskara's own prose (per anchor): "The nature of the Self is consciousness which is proved (siddha) to be both knowledge and action."
- **jñānakriyātmaka** — "of the nature of knowledge and action": the two constitutive powers (icchā/jñāna/kriyā triad reduced to its two productive members).
- **anāvṛta** — unobscured: the *negative* definition sets up sūtra 1.2 (bandha = obscuration).

**Usage parallels:**
- TĀ 3.172 (Dyczkowski vol. 2, our stack): the Stainless (nirañjana) as the Supreme Abode — same unobscuration doctrine.
- Ajaḍapramātṛsiddhi 9–15 (our translation, `truth/truthtranslation`): the "this" rests in "I" — reflexivity as the mark of consciousness.
- Vārttika kārikā 1-4 (KSTS 4): the lineage verse "śrīkallaṭāya so'pyevaṃ catuḥkhaṇḍāni tānyatha" — the transmission Vasugupta → Kallaṭa.

## Sūtra 1.2 — jñānaṃ bandhaḥ

**Grammar:** nominative + nominative: "Knowledge [is] bondage." The shock-value predication: the *root text's* first aphorism after 1.1 asserts that what we call knowing is binding.

**Bhāskara's Vārttika (kārikās 1-16 to 1-18):**
> ahaṃ mamedam iti yaj jñānaṃ bhedaprathātmakam |
> śabdānuviddhato jātaṃ māyīyamalamūlakam || 1-16 ||
> tadvandhanaṃ samākhyātam avidyāvṛtilakṣaṇam || 1-17 ||
> sūtraṃ paśormohikānāṃ kalānāṃ pratipādakam || 1-18 ||
> "That knowledge — 'I' and 'this is mine' — which consists in the apperception of difference, born of penetration by speech, rooted in the impurity of māyā... that is proclaimed bondage, whose mark is the covering of ignorance... [this] sūtra teaches the deluding energies of the bound soul."

**Word lab:**
- **jñāna** — NOT the pure consciousness of 1.1 (that is caitanya): this is *discursive, object-directed cognition* — knowledge structured as possession and self-identity.
- **ahaṃ mamedam** — "I" + "this is mine": the egocentric-possessive doublet; the grammatical form of bondage.
- **bhedaprathātmaka** — "consisting in the apperception (prathā) of difference": prathā = presentation/apperception (technical: the manifesting of an object as distinct). Dyczkowski: "perception (pratha) of relative distinctions."
- **śabdānuviddha** — "penetrated/pervaded by speech": the claim that language *infuses* cognition — bondage is linguistic. Bhāskara's signature theme (his "mysticism of Light" pairs with a speech-theology).
- **māyīyamalamūlaka** — "rooted in the impurity of māyā": the impurity that engenders duality.
- **avṛti** — the covering: 1.2 is the direct negation of 1.1's *anāvṛta*.

**Usage parallels:**
- Dyczkowski's exposition notes Bhāskara vs Kṣemarāja: Bhāskara attributes this knowledge to māyīyamala; Kṣemarāja to āṇavamala (the deeper impurity of self-contraction) — a genuine interpretive divergence our method records.
- The "actor" motif in Bhāskara's prose (naṭa iva — Śiva as actor assuming the role of the bound soul) — same image as our Ajaḍapramātṛsiddhi vv. 22–24 translation (`truth/truthtranslation`, Stage E).
- TĀ 9–10 (māyā and the five kañcukas) — Dyczkowski vols. 4–5, our reference stack.

## Sūtra 1.3 — yonivargaḥ kalāśarīram

**Sandhi:** yoni-vargaḥ; kalā-śarīram. **Grammar:** two nominatives: "The group of yonis [is] the body of the kalās."

**Bhāskara's Vārttika (kārikās 1-19 to 1-21):**
> yonayaḥ śaktayo jñeyāś catasraḥ sarvakāraṇam |
> ambā jyeṣṭhābhidhā raudrī vāmā ca śivamūrtayaḥ || 1-19 ||
> tāsāṃ vargaḥ samūhaḥ syāt tac charīraṃ kalāsu ca |
> akārādikṣaparyantā kalās tāḥ śabdakāraṇam || 1-20 ||
> mātaraḥ śaktayo devyo raśmayaś ca kalāḥ smṛtāḥ |
> etāḥ śabdānuviddhena pratyayodbhāsikāḥ paśoḥ || 1-21 ||
> "Know the yonis to be the four powers, the cause of everything: Ambā, named Jyeṣṭhā, Raudrī, and Vāmā — forms of Śiva. Their group is the aggregate; it is the body within the kalās. The kalās, from A to Kṣ, are the cause of speech. These kalās are called 'mothers,' 'powers,' 'goddesses,' and 'rays.' They flash forth the notions of the bound soul through penetration by speech."

**Word lab:**
- **yoni** — Bhāskara glosses explicitly: *yonayaḥ śaktayaḥ* — the yonis are the powers. NOT the sexual sense here; the womb/source metaphor holds (they are *sarvakāraṇam*, the cause of everything). Dyczkowski renders "sources." Note: in our Kubjikā work the yoni is the goddess's womb-triangle; here the same metaphor is grammaticalized as the four energies.
- **catasraḥ** — the four: Ambā, Jyeṣṭhā, Raudrī, Vāmā — *śivamūrtayaḥ* ("forms of Śiva" — they are Śiva himself in energy-form). Dyczkowski's exposition expands: Vāmā (bliss/vyomavāmeśvarī — the transcendental awareness beyond time), Jyeṣṭhā (persistence/sthiti), Raudrī (withdrawal/saṃhāra), Ambikā (steadying/steady state).
- **kalā** — here the *phonemic energies* (akārādi-kṣaparyantā: the letters A…Kṣ). Two senses in play: "obscuring energy" (from *kalayati*, to generate/limit — Kṣemarāja's etymology, quoted in the anchor) and "articulate unit." Dyczkowski renders "obscuring energies"; the kalās are the 50 phonemes as binding energies.
- **śarīram** — "body": the letters are the *embodiment* of the four powers — speech is the body of the energies.
- **mātaraḥ ... raśmayaḥ** — the four synonym-cluster: mothers/powers/goddesses/rays.
- **pratyayodbhāsikāḥ** — "they illuminate/flash forth the notions" — pratyaya = mental representation/cognition; the kalās generate cognition by pervading it with speech (śabdānuviddha again — echoing 1.2).

**Usage parallels:**
- Kṣemarāja reads 1.3 *with* 1.2 (per the anchor's Exposition): the Womb (yoni = māyā as jagadyoni, the Womb of the Universe) + the body of obscuring energies (kañcukas + karmamala) — a very different translation, preserved in our Synthesis.
- The four śaktis (Ambā/Vāmā/Jyeṣṭhā/Raudrī) are the same four found in Vāmākeśvarīmata (in the Muktabodha library, M0xxxx) — the Kula heritage of this doctrine.
- Mātṛkā doctrine connects to our Kubjikātantra work: the letters as the goddess's body.

## Sūtra 1.4 — jñānādhiṣṭhānaṃ mātṛkā

**Sandhi:** jñāna-adhiṣṭhānam. **Grammar:** "The ground of knowledge [is] Mātṛkā."

**Bhāskara's Vārttika (prose + kārikā):** "There are two forms of knowledge, superior and inferior. Mātṛkā is the one power which is the ground or support (of both)... In the case of superior knowledge (She is) the power called Aghora... Inferior (knowledge is the domain of) Ghorāśakti who directs the consciousness of the fettered soul out of himself."

**Word lab:**
- **adhiṣṭhāna** — ground/support/seat: that on which knowledge stands. (Same term as the later "adhiṣṭhāna" of divine presence.)
- **mātṛkā** — the Matrix: the alphabet as the Mother. Lit. "little mother" — Abhinava (quoted in the anchor's Exposition): "Mātṛkā is the power one with Bhairava in His form as the Mass of Sounds (śabdarāśi)... it is called Mātṛkā because (this energy) contains in a potential state (the manifest universe like an expectant) mother."
- **dvi-vidha jñāna** — the two-fold knowledge: bheda-pratīti (perception of division, inferior) vs abheda-udbhāvana (manifestation of unity, superior).
- **Aghora/Ghorā** — the two aspects: Aghora ("not-fierce") = liberating; Ghorā = binding.

**Usage parallels:**
- **The Śrītantrasadbhāva quote inside the anchor** (Dyczkowski quoting Kṣemarāja): "O dear one, all Mantras consist of letters and energy is the soul of these (letters), while energy is Mātṛkā and one should know Her to be Śiva's nature." — This is a quotation from the *Tantrasadbhāva*, the crown text on our acquisition list: the leapfrog map already points to it.
- TĀ 3–4: the 50 phonemes as the body of consciousness (Dyczkowski vols. 2).
- Mālinīvijayottara (in the Muktabodha library): the mālīnī alphabet as the supreme matrix — same doctrine, different school-layer.

## Sūtra 1.5 — udyamo bhairavaḥ

**Grammar:** "Upsurge [is] Bhairava."

**Bhāskara's Vārttika (prose):** "Śiva is the conscious nature (cidātman) which is full in all respects. 'Exertion' (udyoga), 'expansion' (unmeṣa) and 'upsurge' (udyama) are the terms denoting His state of being when He wishes to generate phenomena (kārya) without abandoning His own essential nature (svasvarūpa). This same (state) is said to be the universe, Śiva, and Bhairava. Bhairava also abides there in the brilliant radiance of (His) own vibration (parispanda)."

**Word lab:**
- **udyama** — upsurge/exertion/rising-effort: the first stirring of the absolute toward manifestation. Dyczkowski's rendering: "Upsurge."
- **bhairava** — the name etymologized in the Vārttika: *bharana* (filling), *ravaṇa* (resounding), *vamana* (emitting) — "He sustains all things by filling them, plays the game of destruction, and emits them out of Himself" + "He perceives His own undivided light (nijābhāsa)."
- **unmeṣa** — "expansion" — the opening of the eye: the same term as in Spandakārikā's doctrine of the pulse.
- **parispanda** — "vibration" — the self-movement of consciousness; connects directly to the spanda doctrine of our translated Spandakārikā.
- **śāmbhavopāya** — the frame: those fit for the Divine Means "are mystically absorbed in the highest Bhairava" without meditation or mantra.

**Usage parallels:**
- Spandakārikā 1.1 (our CP1 translation): "taṃ śakticakravibhavaprabhavaṃ śaṃkaraṃ stumaḥ" — the same first-stirring metaphysics.
- TĀ 3.1ff: unmeṣa/nimeṣa as the expansion-contraction of consciousness (Dyczkowski vol. 2).
- Our Kubjikātantra work: the same "first movement" frame with the vidyā as its content.

# Part III. The three translations with choice trails

## Sūtra 1.1 — caitanyam ātmā

**T1 — Literal (syntax-bound):** "Consciousness is the Self."
*Trail:* two nominatives, identity predication, no verb (nominal sentence). "Consciousness" for caitanya (bhāva-noun), "Self" for ātmā. No glosses.

**T2 — Expressive:** "The Self is consciousness — nothing else."
*Trail:* inverts for English emphasis (English nominal-identity reads better with the heavy term first); "nothing else" licensed by the Vārttika's *anāvṛtarūpatva* (unobscured → nothing additional).

**T3 — Doctrinal (Bhāskara's Vārttika carried through):** "Consciousness is the Self — one's own essential nature, established as knowledge and action, and being unobscured in nature, its Śiva-hood is not to be warded off by anyone."
*Trail:* adds the Vārttika's content as the sūtra's sense: ātmā = svarūpa; jñānakriyātmaka; anāvṛta. "Śiva-hood" for śivatva (kārikā 1-15: śivatvaṃ kena vāryate).

## Sūtra 1.2 — jñānaṃ bandhaḥ

**T1 — Literal:** "Knowledge is bondage."
*Trail:* the shock-predication kept bare; jñāna left unglossed (its ambiguity is the point: see T3).

**T2 — Expressive:** "To know, as we know — that is bondage."
*Trail:* renders jñāna as the *process* the Vārttika describes (ahaṃ mamedam iti yaj jñānam), since English "knowledge" misleads (we value knowledge). The relative clause "as we know" carries bhedaprathātmaka (relative distinctions).

**T3 — Doctrinal:** "The knowledge that is 'I' and 'mine,' consisting in the apperception of difference, born of the pervasion of speech and rooted in the impurity of māyā — that knowledge is bondage, whose mark is the covering of ignorance."
*Trail:* full Vārttika content (kārikās 1-16/17). "Apperception" for prathā (technical: presentation of the distinct). "Pervasion of speech" for śabdānuviddha — the key Bhāskara claim (language infuses cognition). Note divergence in synthesis: Kṣemarāja attributes it to āṇavamala, not māyīyamala.

## Sūtra 1.3 — yonivargaḥ kalāśarīram

**T1 — Literal:** "The group of yonis is the body of the kalās."
*Trail:* yoni kept untranslated (its gloss is contested — see word lab); "kalās" transliterated as technical.

**T2 — Expressive (Bhāskara's reading):** "The four sources — Ambā, Jyeṣṭhā, Raudrī, Vāmā, all forms of Śiva — their aggregate is the body of the fifty phonemes, A to Kṣ, which are the cause of speech."
*Trail:* Bhāskara's gloss carried through (yonayaḥ śaktayaś catasraḥ); "sources" for yoni (Dyczkowski's rendering); the kalā-body = the phonemes as speech-cause (akārādikṣaparyantā, śabdakāraṇam).

**T3 — Doctrinal (Kṣemarāja's competing reading, kept separate):** "The group of the Womb (of diversity, i.e., māyā's kañcuka-categories) together with the body of obscuring energies (which give rise to limited action) is binding."
*Trail:* per the anchor's Exposition, Kṣemarāja reads 1.3 with 1.2 and identifies yoni with māyā (jagadyoni, the Womb of the Universe). This is a *different translation of the same Sanskrit* — the three-readings method surfaces it rather than merging it.

## Sūtra 1.4 — jñānādhiṣṭhānaṃ mātṛkā

**T1 — Literal:** "The support of knowledge is Mātṛkā."
*Trail:* adhiṣṭhāna = support; Mātṛkā transliterated.

**T2 — Expressive:** "What knowledge stands on is the Mother — the matrix of sounds."
*Trail:* "stands on" for adhiṣṭhāna (ground); "Mother — the matrix of sounds" glosses mātṛkā via Abhinava's śabdarāśi gloss, without importing the full doctrine.

**T3 — Doctrinal:** "The ground of the twofold knowledge — superior and inferior — is Mātṛkā, the power one with Bhairava as the Mass of Sounds, who as Aghora manifests the inner and the outer as one, and as Ghorā turns the fettered soul outward."
*Trail:* the Vārttika's full content: dvi-vidha jñāna, Aghora/Ghorā. "Mass of Sounds" for śabdarāśi (Abhinava's definition, quoted in the anchor). Connects to the Śrītantrasadbhāva quote (mantras = letters; energy = their soul = Mātṛkā = Śiva's nature) — our acquisition target.

## Sūtra 1.5 — udyamo bhairavaḥ

**T1 — Literal:** "Upsurge is Bhairava."
*Trail:* udyama = upsurge (Dyczkowski's rendering adopted after checking the Vārttika's synonym-triple udyoga/unmeṣa/udyama).

**T2 — Expressive:** "The first rising of consciousness — that is Bhairava."
*Trail:* renders udyama by its function (the stirring toward manifestation) rather than its lexical sense; "that is" keeps the identity-predication of the nominal sentence.

**T3 — Doctrinal:** "Upsurge — exertion, expansion, the rising of the will to generate phenomena without abandoning its own nature — is Bhairava, who fills (bharana), resounds (ravaṇa), and emits (vamana), abiding in the radiance of his own vibration (parispanda)."
*Trail:* full Vārttika content: the udyoga/unmeṣa/udyama triple, svasvarūpa-anasvāda (without abandoning own nature), the bharana/ravaṇa/vamana etymology of Bhairava, parispanda. "Vibration" for parispanda links to the Spanda doctrine (our CP1 translation).

# Part IV. Synthesis

## The invariant spine (all three readings agree)
The first adhikāra opens with a five-step descent-and-return: (1) consciousness is one's own nature; (2) discursive knowledge is bondage; (3) the four powers, embodied as the phonemes A–Kṣ, generate that binding knowledge through speech; (4) the matrix of sounds (Mātṛkā) is the ground of both the binding and the liberating knowledge; (5) the upsurge of consciousness itself is Bhairava — the return that the whole discipline serves. Bhāskara's reading is coherent and internally complete: light (1.1) → obscuration (1.2) → its machinery (1.3–1.4) → its dissolution (1.5).

## Genuine divergences
| Crux | Bhāskara (this translation) | Kṣemarāja (per anchor's Exposition) | Can be settled by |
|------|------------------------------|--------------------------------------|-------------------|
| 1.2 jñāna | rooted in māyīyamala (impurity of māyā) | rooted in āṇavamala (impurity of self-contraction) | reading both Vārttika and Vimarśinī in full (both on disk) |
| 1.3 yoni | the four powers (Ambā, Jyeṣṭhā, Raudrī, Vāmā), śivamūrtayaḥ | the Womb = māyā's kañcuka-categories | comparing their intra-textual cross-references |
| 1.3 kalā | the phonemes A–Kṣ (speech-cause) | the obscuring energies = karmamala + kañcukas | their different readings of "body" (śarīram) |
| 1.5 udyama | the will's rising to generate; Bhairava by bharaṇa/ravaṇa/vamana | (see Vimarśinī when acquired) | Hop 1 stage 2 (Jaideva Singh) |

## What this hop adds to the corpus glossary
śabdānuviddha (speech-pervaded cognition), prathā (apperception), mātṛkā (matrix), kalā (phonemic energy), udyama/unmeṣa (upsurge/expansion), adhiṣṭhāna (ground), āṇavamala/māyīyamala (the two impurities) — most of these carry directly into the T3 (Trika philosophy) hop: the same terms recur in the Vimarśinī, Vivṛtivimarśinī, and Tantrāloka work.
