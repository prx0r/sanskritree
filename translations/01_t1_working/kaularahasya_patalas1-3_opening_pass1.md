## Paṭala 1 — the vāma/dakṣiṇa discrimination (the opening)

*The e-text opens mid-sentence (folios 1–8 lost); the surviving opening is the vāma-mārga doctrine.*

**[The makāra-rites]** — * * *? hito dakṣiṇācārasaṃjñakaḥ | madyaṃ māṃsaṃ tathā matsyaṃ mudrā maithunameva ca | makārapaṃcakairdevi yonau kaulaḥ prapūjayet ||
> ...the one called the dakṣiṇācāra (the right-hand observance): wine (madya), meat (māṃsa), fish (matsya), the gesture (mudrā), and union (maithuna) — with the five makāras, O Devī, the Kaula worships in the yoni.

**The hands** — vāmahastāt pānapūjā kramād vāmī prakīrtitaḥ | dakṣihastāt pānavāgād vāmahastapratarpaṇāt || dakṣahastāpūjanāddhi kaula ityabhidhīyate | kevalaṃ dakṣahastena pūjaṇād dakṣiṇābhidhaḥ ||
> With the left hand the wine-worship [is done] — hence he is proclaimed the vāmī (the left-hand one) in turn; with the right hand, from the drinking and the rest, from the left hand's libations — from worshipping with the right hand he is called the Kaula; from worshipping with the right hand alone he is called the dakṣiṇa (the right-hand one).

**The two paths** — upāsanād vidhā proktā sakāmākāmavarjitā | mokṣabījaṃ bhumiḥ kāmā sakāmā bhavatījakaṃ ||
> The worship is declared twofold — free of the desiring and the desireless: the earth is the mokṣa-seed; desire, with desire, is the jaka (?) [X: "bhavatījakaṃ" corrupt].

**The guru's way** — paṃthā tu dakṣiṇaḥ śreṣṭho vāmaḥ śreṣṭhātaro mataḥ | yastu vāmaṃ vijā * *? sa eva paramo guruḥ ||
> The right path is the excellent; the left is the more excellent, it is held: he who knows the left — that one is the supreme guru.

**The śāstra-arithmetic** — ahaṃ jānāmi pūrṇaṃ pādāna sanakādayaḥ | arddha ca pipaśiṣṭhādyāḥ sa * * * * *? saṃmitaṃ || *? nanti *? thāḥ ṣaṣṭhāṃśaṃ koghaṃ śatitare janāḥ | paṭanti koṭayaścātra jāyante kṛmi yoniṣu ||
> I know the full [truth]; the pādas [know] Sanaka and the rest; the half [know] Pi[p]paśiṣṭha and the rest [X: the verse's arithmetic of levels of knowledge is corrupt]. ...the sixty-fourth part, the one-hundred-and-sixth... the crores read here; [the rest] are born in the wombs of worms —

**The end** — taṃ mārgaye vijānanti te muktā nātrasaṃśayaḥ | śrīkṛṣṇā vāmamārgaṃ tu phalgunāyopadiṣṭavān ||
> — they who know that path are released — no doubt here. Śrīkṛṣṇa taught the vāma-mārga to Phalgunā (Arjuna).

**The eligibility** — mayā cintāyatā dṛṣṭāmunityaśvanirūpitaḥ | cāṇḍālo vā turuṣko vā brahmahā vātha maskarī || śrotriyo vā vāmamārgī bhuktimuktyoḥ sa bhājanaṃ ||
> This has been seen by me, reflecting, determined ever: whether a caṇḍāla, a Turuṣka, a brahmahā (brahmin-killer), or a maskarin (heretic-beggar), or a śrotriya (a Vedic scholar) — the vāmamārgī (the left-path man) is the vessel of enjoyment and liberation both.

**The goddess-arithmetic** — vāmadikṣiṇamāgo hi kālītārā vidhau smṛtaḥ | chinnāvidhau vāmamārgo vagalāvāmadakṣagā | suṃdarī vāmadivyā ca dakṣamārge ca saṃsthitā || vālāvidyā dakṣavidyā mātaṃgī vāmadakṣiṇā | kevalā bhuvanā lakṣmā dhūmādakṣiṇamārgagā || sumukhīvāmasaṃtuṣṭhā vaṭukovāmadakṣiṇaḥ ||
> The vāma-dakṣiṇa path is remembered for Kālī and Tārā's rite; for the Chinnā-rite the vāma-mārga; Vāgalā goes by left-and-right; Sundarī is the vāma-divyā and stands in the dakṣa-mārga; Vālā-vidyā is the dakṣa-vidyā; Mātaṃgī is vāma-dakṣiṇā; Kevalā and Bhuvanā and Lakṣmī and Dhūmā go by the dakṣiṇa-mārga; Sumukhī is content with the vāma; Vaṭuka is vāma-dakṣiṇa.

**The āmnāya-geography** — pūrvāmnāye dakṣi vāmau dakṣiṇe divyavāmake | kauladakṣau paścime tu kaulavāmau tathottare || ūrddhadivyau maheśāni pātāle vāma eva ca | yakṣiṇyādyāḥ sarvavidyāḥ kāminīyoganāyikā || vāmamārgeṇa siddhyanti nānyathā siddhidā kvacit | vāmadakṣiṇayogena ubhayācāratatparā ||
> In the east-āmnāya: the dakṣa and the vāma; in the south, the divya-vāma; the Kaula-dakṣa in the west; the Kaula-vāma in the north; the upper-[āmnāya] is the divya, O great Lady; in the pātāla, the vāma itself. The Yakṣiṇīs and the rest — all the vidyās, the Kāminīs, the Yoganāyikās — succeed by the vāma-mārga; nowhere else is siddhi given. By the vāma-dakṣiṇa union [one becomes] devoted to both observances.

**The schools transcended** — śaive śākte gāṇapatya saure svāyaṃbhuve tathā | cāndre cīne pāṃcarātre vaiṣṇave vaidikepi ca || vāma cokte bhaviṣyanti kale dvaijātayaḥ | evaṃ darśanamārge pi na hi vamaṃ karoti cet || tasya siddhirna deveśi mama vairī prakīrtitaḥ | sarvasmādedhiko vāmadharmaḥ proktaḥ kulāgame ||
> In the Śaiva, the Śākta, the Gāṇapatya, the Saurya, the Svāyambhuva, likewise in the Cāndra, the Cīna, the Pāñcarātra, the Vaiṣṇava, and even the Vaidika — the vāma is said [to appear] in the Kali-age, among the twice-born. Thus, in the darśana-paths, if one does not do the vāma, his siddhi [fails] — O Devī, he is proclaimed my enemy. More than everything, the vāma-dharma is proclaimed in the kulāgama.

**Kālī over all** — tatrāpi kālikā tārā vidhau priyataraḥ smṛtaḥ | kāpālike maheśāni vāmamārgotra kāraṇaṃ || vāmamārgaprakāreṇa sarva siddhyanti nānyathā | vāmamārga vinā devi na hi siddhyanti devatāḥ ||
> And there, in Kālikā and Tārā's rite, [the vāma] is remembered as the dearer; in the Kāpālika, O great Lady, the vāma-mārga is the means here. By the vāma-mārga's method all succeed, not otherwise; without the vāma-mārga, O Devī, the deities themselves do not succeed.

**The āmnāya-necessities** — uttarāmnāyasaṃproktā devyāstuṣyanti paṃcamaiḥ | ūnaḥ padārtha ekaścet sādhakaṃ te śapanti ca || paścimāmnāyadevānāṃ surāpānaṃ kṛtākṛtaṃ | macatuṣṭayamicchanti taṃ vinā praśapanti ca || pūrvvāmnāyoktadevānāṃ tatsmāṃsasaśca kṛtākṛtaṃ | āvaśyakaṃ matrayaṃ ca taṃ vinā praśapanti te || ūrddhāmnāyoktadevīnāṃ ta matsyaśca kṛtākṛtāḥ | āvaśyakaṃ madvayaṃ ca taṃ vinā praśapanti te || dakṣiṇāmnāyadevānāṃ niṣirddhaṃ macatuṣṭāyaṃ | menakaina vinā te pi praśapanti hi sādhakaṃ ||
> For the goddesses declared in the uttarāmnāya (the north-lineage), the five makāras please them; if one item is missing, they curse the sādhaka. For the paścimāmnāya's deities, the wine-drinking is required-and-not (the makāra is both); they want the four makāras — without that they curse. For the pūrvāmnāya's deities, the meat is required-and-not; the three makāras are the necessity — without that they curse. For the ūrdhvāmnāya's goddesses, the fish is required-and-not; the two makāras are the necessity — without that they curse. For the dakṣiṇāmnāya's deities, the four makāras are forbidden; even without [X: "menakaina" corrupt] they curse the sādhaka.

**The path-conflict** — kaulikaṃ kurvataḥ karma vaidikaṃ nābhidhāyate | vaidikaṃ kurvataḥ karma kaulikaṃ nābhidhīyate || virurddhādubhayaṃ naṣṭaṃ tasmādeva padhā caret | vāmamāgī yadā dakṣaṃ praviśet tatsuraiḥ sa tu || vighnyate pīḍyate cāpi na siddhimadhigacchati | dakṣamārgī yadā vāmaṃ praviśet tatsurāstadā || lokadvayādvyāpayanti taṃ grasanti ca vāminaṃ | tasmāt svakulamārgaṃ tu jñātvā kuryādupāsanāṃ ||
> For one doing the Kaulika rite, the Vaidika rite is not prescribed; for one doing the Vaidika rite, the Kaulika is not prescribed. Mixed, both are destroyed — therefore one should practice one path. When the vāma-mārgī enters the dakṣa [rite], then by the gods he is obstructed and afflicted, and does not attain siddhi. When the dakṣa-mārgī enters the vāma, then... they extend beyond the two worlds and devour the vāmin. Therefore, knowing one's own kula-mārga, one should perform the worship [accordingly].

## Paṭala 2 — the secrecy and the dīkṣā's necessity

**The śāstra-discipline** — yadṛcchayā śrutaṃ tantraṃ cchadmanāpi valena vā | pareritaṃ vāgāthā vā tarjaped yadyanarthakṛt || na śāstramālokya vadet nācarenna jat kvacit | na paśyennopadiśyacca na kuryānnaiva sādhayet ||
> The tantra heard by chance, or by fraud or by force, or another's utterance or song — if it works harm, one should [not trust it]: one should not speak having looked at a śāstra [casually], nor practice [it] anywhere ever; one should not see [such], nor teach, nor perform, nor accomplish.

**The book-mantra's sin** — pustake likhitān mantrānālokya prajapanti ye | brahmahatvā samaṃ teṣāṃ pātakaṃ parikīrtitaṃ || tasmād dikṣāṃ prayatnena sadā kuryācca tāntrikīṃ ||
> Those who, having looked at mantras written in a book, recite them — for them the sin is proclaimed equal to brahmahatyā (killing a brahmin). Therefore one should always perform the tāntrika dīkṣā with effort.

*iti śrīkaularahasya dvitiye paṭalaḥ* — "Here ends the Kaularahasya's second paṭala."

## Paṭala 3 — the dīkṣā (initiation)

**The ten saṃskāras** — atha dīkṣā tatrādau daśasaṃskāraḥ | jananaṃ jivanaṃ paśyā tāḍanaṃ bodhanaṃ tathā | tathābhiseko vimalīkaraṇārpyāyane punaḥ || tarpaṇaṃ dīpanaṃ tupti daśa yaite mantrasaṃskriyā | ataḥ prathamato dīkṣāṃ pravakṣyāmi viśeṣataḥ ||
> Now the dīkṣā — first the ten saṃskāras there: janana (birth), jīvana (life), [vi]paśyā, tāḍana (striking), bodhana (awakening), and the abhiṣeka (anointing), vimalīkaraṇa (purification), arpāyaṇa, again; tarpaṇa (libation), dīpana (kindling), tupti (satisfaction) — these ten are the mantra-saṃskāras. Hence, first, I will declare the dīkṣā in particular.

**The etymology** — dīyate jñānaṃ kṣīyante pāpanāśayaḥ | tena dīkṣā iti proktā prāpta cet sadgururmukhāt ||
> Knowledge is given (dīyate); the masses of sin are destroyed (kṣīyante) — hence it is called dīkṣā, when obtained from the mouth of a good guru.

**The undīkṣita's doom** — adīkṣitānāṃ martyānāṃ doṣaṃ śṛṇuntu sādhakāḥ | annaṃ viṣṭāsamaṃ sūtramasantathā || adīkṣitakṛtaṃ śrārddhaṃ cādīkṣitasya vā | gṛhītvā pitarasteṣāṃ narake ca sudāruṇe || pataṃtyeva na saṃdeho yāvadiṃdrāścaturdaśa |
> Hear the fault of the undīkṣita mortals, O sādhakas: their food [is] equal to feces (viṣṭā), their thread... and so on. The śrāddha done by an undīkṣita, or for an undīkṣita — having taken it, the fathers of those [undīkṣitas] fall into the most terrible hell — no doubt — for fourteen Indras' [lifetimes].

**The rite's futility** — sahasrairupacāraiśca bhaktiyukto ya japed yadi | tathārpyadī tasyārcaṃ devā gṛhnanti naiva ca || karṇākhitvaṃ vṛthā yasmāt tasmādadīkṣitaḥ paśuḥ | tasmādadīkṣitaṃ dṛṣṭvā kiṃcit kārya na sādhayet ||
> Even if one, devoted, recites with thousands of upacāras, and offers — the gods do not accept his worship at all. Because it is vain [the mantra] heard by the ear, therefore the undīkṣita is a paśu (the bound beast). Therefore, having seen an undīkṣita, one should not accomplish any work [with him].

**The dīkṣā's power** — dīkṣāgrahaṇakāle tu pāparāśiḥ palāyate | nirvāṇapadasaṃyukto vahi gacchati tatparaḥ || tadā devapitṛṇāṃ tu karyāho bhavati svayaṃ | anyathātkṛtaṃ karma hutaṃ bhasmani havyava ||
> At the moment of taking the dīkṣā, the heap of sins flees; joined with the nirvāṇa-state, it goes beyond, intent on that. Then, of the gods and fathers, the worthy [offerings] become himself; otherwise the work done is like an oblation (havya) poured into ashes.

**The universal necessity** — sarvvāśrame tu dīkṣāyā nityatvaṃ parivakṣate | anīsvarasya mantrasya nāsti trātā jano bhuvi || tathā dīkṣāvihinasya neha svāmi paratra ca | na tapobhirjipaihomairvvidhidhairvvāpi sādhanaiḥ || na kriyāpi kadācit tu dīkṣāhīnyasya siddhyati | dvijānāmanupetānāṃ svakarmmādhyayanādiṣu || yathādhikāro nāstīha syāccopanayanādiṣu | tathā cādīkṣitānāṃ ca mantradevācca nādiṣu | nāmadhikkārossyataḥ kuryādātmānaṃ mantrasaṃskṛtaṃ ||
> In every āśrama the dīkṣā's necessity is declared: for a mantra without a Lord there is no savior in the world. Likewise for the one devoid of dīkṣā — no master here or hereafter: neither by austerities, nor by japa, homas, rites, nor sādhanas; nor does any rite ever succeed for the one lacking dīkṣā. As the twice-born without the upanayana have no right here to their karma, study, and so on — like the uninitiated for the upanayana-rites — so the undīkṣita has no right in the mantra-deities' recitations. Therefore, one should make oneself mantra-sanctified.

**The dīkṣā as root** — dīkṣāmūlaṃ jagatsarvaṃ dīkṣāmūlaṃ parantapa | dīkṣāmāśritya nivased yatra kutrāśrame japena || adīkṣātaśca maraṇai raurame narake vrajet | ato dīkṣeti sā proktā deśikaistantravedibhiḥ ||
> The dīkṣā is the root of the whole world; the dīkṣā is the root, O tormentor of foes: relying on the dīkṣā, one dwells in whatever āśrama, by japa. And from [dying] without dīkṣā, one goes to the Raurava-hell; hence it is called dīkṣā by the deśikas who know the tantra.

**The fruits** — yāvan na prāpyate dīkṣā puruṣārtha phalapradā | sarvasiddhikarīkṣā sarvasaṃpat pradāyanī || pratibhāsvaravarṇāni kāntipuṣṭi dhanāni ca | mohanaṃ staṃbhanā kṛṣṭimāraṇoccāṭanāni ca || vaśīryasukavitābuddhiṃśvadhāraṇāyutā | tāraṇaṃ ca samudrasya vahneśaitvaṃ raṇe jayaṃ || vipine cābhayaṃ caiva śokaduḥkhāpahaṃ tathā | dharmārtha kāmamokṣāṇi dadātyeṣā na saṃśayaḥ ||
> Until the dīkṣā is obtained, [nothing]: [the dīkṣā] gives the fruits of the puruṣārthas; it makes all siddhis; it grants all prosperity: radiant complexions, beauty, health, wealth; deluding, paralyzing, attracting, killing, expelling; the power of fascination, the poetic-intellect with the dhāraṇā-companion; crossing the ocean, lordship of fire, victory in battle; and safety in the wilderness, and the removal of grief and sorrow; the dharma, artha, kāma, mokṣa it gives — no doubt.

**The one guru-word** — anekakoṭi mantrebhyastathā vidyā sva eva ca | ekaṃ guru mukhāllabdhaṃ sarvasiddhi pravartakaṃ || gurupadeśato labdhaṃ japanyāsārcanādikaṃ | paścāt tat sādhayet sarvaṃ sa tadā bhāvabhāvitaḥ ||
> Than crores of mantras and vidyās — one [mantra] obtained from the guru's mouth sets all siddhis in motion. Whatever is obtained from the guru's instruction — japa, nyāsa, worship and the rest — afterward one should accomplish it all, filled with that being.

**The guru's rank** — vicāre gururityukta upadeśe guru guruḥ | parāparatvaṃ pūjāyāṃ prayoge parameṣṭhisau || na guroḥ sadṛśaṃ vastu na devaḥ śaṃkaropamaḥ | na ca kaurāt paroyāgo na vidyā kālikā samā || gakārāt jñānasaṃpattīraktārastat prakāśakaḥ | ukārācchivadātmyaṃ dadyāditi guru smṛtaḥ ||
> In deliberation the guru is called the guru; in instruction the guru is the guru; in worship the supremacy; in application the Parameṣṭhin. There is nothing equal to the guru; no god like Śaṅkara; no rite beyond the Kaula; no vidyā equal to Kālī. From the syllable ga — the knowledge-wealth; ra — its illuminator; from the syllable u — he gives Śiva-identity: thus the guru is remembered.

**The paśu** — pāśavarddhaḥ paśuḥ proktaḥ pāśamukto maheśvaraḥ | tasmāt pāśaharopyatre sa gurunānya ucyate || nirvīyyo hi piturmantraḥ śaive śākte na duṣyati | dakṣamārge tu nirvīryaḥ piturmmātā mahasya ca ||
> The one bound by the pāśas is proclaimed the paśu (the bound); the one freed of the pāśas is Maheśvara. Therefore the remover of the pāśa here — he alone is called the guru. The father's mantra, though powerless, does not fail in the Śaiva [and] Śākta [paths]; in the dakṣa-mārga, the father's and the mother's [lineage] is powerless.
