- **Author:** Annaṃbhaṭṭa (Ānam Bhaṭṭa), a South-Indian (Andhra) brāhmaṇa, 17th century
- **Text:** Tarkasaṃgraha ("the Compendium of Logic") — the standard Navya-Nyāya primer for beginners; with its auto-commentary (the Dīpikā) it is the entry to the school's technical vocabulary

### Time-place-context
- **PERIOD:** 17th-century Navya-Nyāya — the formalized epistemology whose language our Trika-corpus's opponents (the "Nyāya and Vaiśeṣika logicians" of the Akulavīra's school-list, the mitaśakti-gods of Kṣemarāja's parīkṣā) used. The primer postdates the classical layer by seven centuries — the pramāṇa-language reached its technical peak.
- **PLACE/PATH:** South India (the Andhra region); the text's enormous success as the school's entry-portal (commentaries across India, the English translations from 1872 on).
- **GENRE/REGISTER:** śāstra-primer — the analytic register at its most technical: padārtha (category), the substance/quality/action trichotomy, the samavāya-relations, the pramāṇa-definitions, the anumāna-machinery (vyāpti, pakṣadharmatā, the five members, the hetvābhāsas).
- **FRAME:** the maṅgala's "bālānāṃ sukhabodhāya" (for the easy understanding of the children) — the text's own stated purpose: the Navya primer, written for beginners. The whole pipeline's Hop 10 opens with a text that says what it is.

## The working translation

**AnTs_1 (the maṅgala)** — nidhāya hṛdi viśveśaṃ vidhāya guruvandanam | bālānāṃ sukhabodhāya kriyate tarkasaṃgrahaḥ
> Having placed the Lord of the Universe in the heart, having paid homage to the guru — for the easy understanding of the children, the Tarkasaṃgraha is composed.

**AnTs_2** — dravyaguṇakarmasāmānyaviśeṣasamavāyābhāvāḥ sapta padārthāḥ
> Substance, quality, action, the universal, the particular, inherence, and absence — these are the seven categories.

**AnTs_3** — tatra dravyāṇi pṛthivyaptejovāyvākāśakāladigātmamanāṃsi navaiva
> Of these, the substances are nine only: earth, water, fire, wind, space, time, direction, the self, and the mind.

**AnTs_4** — rūparasagandhasparśasaṃkhyāparimāṇapṛthaktvasaṃyogavibhāgaparatvāparatvagurutvadravatvasnehaśabdabuddhisukhaduḥkhecchādveṣaprayatnadharmādharmasaṃskārāś caturviṃśati guṇāḥ
> Color, taste, smell, touch, number, measure, distinctness, conjunction, disjunction, farness, nearness, weight, fluidity, viscidity, sound, cognition, pleasure, pain, desire, aversion, effort, merit, demerit, and the traces — these are the twenty-four qualities.

**AnTs_5** — utkṣepaṇāvakṣepaṇākuñcanaprasāraṇagamanāni pañca karmāṇi
> The actions are five: throwing up, throwing down, contraction, expansion, and motion.

**AnTs_6** — param aparaṃ ceti dvividhaṃ sāmānyam
> The universal is two-fold: the higher and the lower.

**AnTs_7** — nityadravyavṛttayo viśeṣās tv anantā eva
> The particulars (viśeṣa) — residing in the eternal substances — are infinite indeed.

**AnTs_8** — samavāyas tv eka eva
> Inherence, however, is one only.

**AnTs_9** — abhāvaś caturvidhaḥ | prāgabhāvaḥ pradhvaṃsābhāvo 'tyantābhāvo 'nyonyābhāvaś ceti
> Absence is four-fold: prior absence, post-destruction absence, absolute absence, and mutual absence.

**AnTs_10** — gandhavatī pṛthivī / sā dvividhā nityānityā ca / nityā paramāṇurūpā / anityā kāryarūpā / punas trividhā śarīrendriyaviṣayabhedāt / śarīram asmadādīnām / indriyaṃ gandhagrāhakaṃ ghrāṇaṃ nāsāgravarti / viṣayo mṛtpāṣāṇādiḥ
> Earth is odor-bearing. It is two-fold: eternal and non-eternal. The eternal is of the form of atoms; the non-eternal is of the form of effects. Again it is three-fold by the distinction of body, organ, and object: the body is ours and the like; the organ is the nose, the odor-grasper, located at the nose-tip; the object is clay, stone, and the like.

**AnTs_11** — śitasparśavatya āpaḥ / ... / śarīraṃ varuṇaloke / indriyaṃ rasagrāhakaṃ rasanaṃ jihvāgravarti / viṣayaḥ saritsamudrādiḥ
> Water is cold-touch. ... The body is in Varuṇa's world; the organ is the tongue, the taste-grasper, located at the tongue-tip; the object is rivers, oceans, and the like.

**AnTs_12** — uṣṇasparśavat tejaḥ / ... / śarīram ādityaloke / indriyaṃ rūpagrāhakaṃ cakṣuḥ kṛṣṇatārāgravarti / viṣayaś caturvidhaḥ / bhaumadivyaudaryākarajabhedāt / bhaumaṃ vahnyādikam / abindhanaṃ divyaṃ vidyudādi / bhuktasya pariṇāmahetur audaryam. ākarajaṃ suvarṇādi
> Fire is hot-touch. ... The body is in the sun's world; the organ is the eye, the color-grasper, located at the dark pupil's tip; the object is four-fold: earth-born (fire and the like), divine (lightning and the like — without fuel), gastric (the cause of the digestion of what is eaten), and mine-born (gold and the like).

**AnTs_13** — rūparahitasparśavān vāyuḥ / ... / śarīraṃ vāyuloke / indriyaṃ sparśagrāhakaṃ tvaksarvaśarīravarti / viṣayo vṛkṣādikampanahetuḥ // śarīrāntaḥsaṃcārī vāyuḥ prāṇaḥ / sa caiko 'py upādhibhedāt prāṇāpānādisaṃjñā labhate
> Wind is touch without color. ... The body is in the wind's world; the organ is the skin, the touch-grasper, pervading the whole body; the object is the cause of the trembling of trees and the like. The wind moving within the body is the breath (prāṇa); though one, by the difference of the limiting conditions it receives the names prāṇa, apāna, and the rest.

**AnTs_14** — śabdaguṇam ākāśam / tac caikaṃ vibhu nityaṃ ca
> Space is sound-qualified. It is one, pervasive, and eternal.

**AnTs_15** — atītādivyavahārahetuḥ kālaḥ / sa caiko vibhur nityaś ca
> Time is the cause of the usage of "past" and the rest. It is one, pervasive, and eternal.

**AnTs_16** — prācyādivyavahārahetur dik / sā caikā vibhvī nityā ca
> Direction is the cause of the usage of "east" and the rest. It is one, pervasive, and eternal.

**AnTs_17** — jñānādhikaraṇam ātmā / sa dvividhaḥ paramātmā jīvātmā ca / tatreśvaraḥ sarvajñaḥ paramātmaika eva / jīvātmā pratiśarīraṃ bhinno vibhur nityaś ca
> The self is the substrate of cognition. It is two-fold: the supreme self and the living self. Of these, the Lord, the omniscient, is the one supreme self only; the living self is distinct per body, pervasive, and eternal.

**AnTs_18** — sukhādyupalabdhisādhanam indriyaṃ manaḥ / tac ca pratyātmaniyatatvād anantaṃ paramāṇurūpaṃ nityaṃ ca
> The mind is the instrument of the apprehension of pleasure and the rest. Because it is restricted per individual self, it is infinite, of atomic form, and eternal.

**AnTs_36** — yathārthānubhavaś caturvidhaḥ pratyakṣānumityupamitiśabdabhedāt / tatkaraṇam api caturvidhaṃ pratyakṣānumānopamānaśabdabhedāt
> True awareness is four-fold, by the distinction of perception, inference, comparison, and verbal testimony; its instruments likewise are four: perception, inference, comparison, and verbal testimony.

**AnTs_42** — tatra pratyakṣajñānakaraṇaṃ pratyakṣam / indriyārthasannikarṣajanyaṃ jñānaṃ pratyakṣam / tad dvividhaṃ nirvikalpakaṃ savikalpakaṃ ceti / tatra nirvikalpakaṃ jñānaṃ nirvikalpakaṃ yathedaṃ kiṃcit / saparakārakaṃ jñānaṃ savikalpakaṃ yathā ḍittho'yam brahmaṇo'yaṃ śyāmo'yam iti
> Of these, perception (pratyakṣa) is the instrument of perceptual cognition; the cognition produced by the contact of the sense with its object is perception. It is two-fold: the non-constructive and the constructive. Of these, the non-constructive cognition is the non-constructive, as "this is something"; the cognition with the constructive factor is the constructive, as "this is a ḍittha," "this is a brāhmaṇa," "this is dark."

**[X: "ḍittho'yam" — the standard example-word (ḍittha, the wood-apple) — the textbook's example; the Dīpikā glosses it. The 1872-English anchor's rendering to be checked when OCR'd.]**

**AnTs_43** — pratyakṣajñānahetur indriyārthasannikarṣaḥ ṣaḍvidhaḥ / saṃyogaḥ saṃyuktasamavāyaḥ saṃyuktasamavetasamavāyaḥ samavāyaḥ samavetasamavāyo viśeṣaṇaviśeṣyabhāvaś ceti / ...
> The cause of perceptual cognition — the sense-object contact — is six-fold: conjunction; conjunction-inherence; conjunction-inherence-inherence; inherence; inherence-inherence; and the qualifier-qualified relation. [The full sūtra with the six examples: by the eye, the pot-perception arises from conjunction; the pot's color-perception from the inherence of the color in the pot conjoined with the eye; the universal color-ness from the inherence-inherence... the absence-perception from the qualifier-qualified relation (the no-pot on the ground).] Thus the cognition born of the six contacts is perception; its instrument is the sense; therefore the sense is established as the perceptual pramāṇa.

**AnTs_44** — anumitikaraṇam anumānam / parāmarśajanyaṃ jñānam anumitiḥ / vyāptiviśiṣṭapakṣadharmatājñānaṃ parāmarśaḥ / yathā vahnivyāpyadhūmavān ayaṃ parvata iti jñānaṃ parāmarśaḥ / taj janyaṃ parvato vahnimān iti jñānam anumitiḥ / yatra yatra dhūmas tatrāgnir iti sāhacaryaniyamo vyāptiḥ / vyāpyasya parvatādivṛttitvaṃ pakṣadharmatā
> Inference (anumāna) is the instrument of inferential cognition. The cognition produced by the consideration (parāmarśa) is the inferential cognition (anumiti). The cognition of the property-of-the-subject qualified by the pervasion is the consideration: as the cognition "this mountain is smoky, [the smoke] being pervaded by fire" is the consideration; the cognition born of it — "the mountain has fire" — is the inferential cognition. The constant co-presence "wherever there is smoke there is fire" is the pervasion (vyāpti); the occurrence of the pervaded [smoke] on the mountain and the rest is the subject-property (pakṣadharmatā).

**AnTs_45** — anumānaṃ dvividhaṃ svārthaṃ parārthaṃ ca / ...
> Inference is two-fold: for oneself and for another. For oneself: having grasped the pervasion by repeated observation — "where there is smoke there is fire" — in the kitchen and the like, going near the mountain, doubting whether it has fire, seeing smoke on it, recalling the pervasion, the cognition arises: "this mountain is smoky, [the smoke being] pervaded by fire." This is called the consideration of the mark. Then the cognition arises: "the mountain has fire" — this is the inferential cognition. This is inference for oneself. When, having inferred the fire from the smoke for himself, one utters the five-membered sentence for the other's comprehension — "the mountain has fire, because it is smoky; whatever is smoky has fire, as the kitchen; so is this; therefore so" — by this, from the stated mark, the other too comprehends the fire. This is inference for another.

**AnTs_53** — savyabhicāro'naikāntikaḥ / ... (the hetvābhāsa-series begins)
> The erring [mark] is the non-conclusive (anaikāntika). It is three-fold: the common, the uncommon, and the non-summarizing. [The examples: the mountain has fire because it is an object of cognition — the common, since the object-of-cognition-ness occurs in the lake, which lacks fire; the uncommon: sound is eternal because it is sound-ness — the mark occurring only in sound; the non-summarizing: everything is non-eternal because it is an object of cognition — since everything is the subject, there is no example.]

**AnTs_56** — asiddhas trividhaḥ / ... (the unestablished-mark series)
> The unestablished (asiddha) is three-fold: the substrate-unestablished, the form-unestablished, and the pervaded-ness-unestablished. [The examples: the sky-lotus is fragrant because it is a lotus, like the pond-lotus — the substrate (the sky-lotus) does not exist; sound is a quality because it is visible — the visible-ness is not in sound, since sound is audible; the mountain has smoke because it has fire — here the wet-fuel-contact is the co-condition (upādhi): wherever smoke, there the wet-fuel-contact (the pervader of the goal); wherever fire, there the absence of the wet-fuel-contact (non-pervader of the means).]
