# Śivasūtra 1.6–1.19 with Bhāskara's Vārttika — Pass 1 Working Translation

*Hop 1, batch pass 1. Full three-readings treatment scheduled for later passes; here: working translation with cruxes flagged inline [X].*
*Text: KSTS 4 (Chatterji 1916) via Muktabodha M00083 · Author: Vasugupta (9th c.) · Commentary: Bhāskara (≈mid-11th c.) · Anchor: Dyczkowski, Aphorisms of Śiva (SUNY 1992). See `sivasutra_bhaskara_1_1-1_5_three_readings.md` for metadata and the method.*

## 1.6 — śakticakrasaṃdhāne viśvasaṃhāraḥ
**In the union (saṃdhāna) of the wheel of powers is the withdrawal (saṃhāra) of the universe.**

Vārttika (kārikās 1-27/28, 1-31/32): "That [upsurge] is called the world and the Śārvī principle and Bhairava; and in its own vibration's splendor (svaparispandasphurattā), there it is Bhairava — called so from filling (bharaṇa), delighting (ramaṇa) and emitting (vamana), and from the manifesting of its unbroken own-light. The powers are the whole world, and they are of the nature of knowledge and action... their union is the taking-back (svīkaraṇa) into oneself, by withdrawal (nimeṣa); withdrawal is the state of being dissolved (pravilīnatva) — and that is its Śiva-ness."

Notes: saṃdhāna (union/contact) — the powers' self-collection; nimeṣa (closing) pairs with 1.5's unmeṣa (opening) — the pulse-pair of our Spanda vocabulary. [X: KSTS4 prints "tāśca jñānakriyātmikāḥ" — "the powers are knowledge-and-action in nature"; the cakra (wheel) of the powers = the totality of energies as one wheel.]

## 1.7 — jāgratsvapnasuṣuptabhede turyābhogasaṃvit
**In the distinction of waking, dream and deep sleep, [there is] the consciousness of the Fourth's experience (turyābhoga).**

Vārttika (1-35/36): "The reflection (vimarśa) that is the single mass of one's own nature is called the Fourth-state (turya-bhūḥ); because of pervading all, with the wavering [of the three states] dissolved, the consciousness called the Fourth's experience is the very nature of the experiencer (upalabdhṛsvarūpatā)."

Notes: the sūtra's grammar: *bhede* (locative) — the Fourth is *within* the distinction of the three, not after them. [X: Kṣemarāja's mūla reads *turyābhogasaṃbhavaḥ* ("the arising of the Fourth's experience") — the KSTS 4 text itself records both readings in a parenthesis. Our edition prints turyābhogasaṃvit.]

## 1.8 — jñānaṃ jāgrat
**Knowledge is waking.**

Vārttika (1-38/40): "That which is of the form of the intellect, of the Saugatas [Buddhist], with conceptual construction (sākalpa), verbal, tactile, born of form, born of taste, born of smell, and whatever other sense-born knowledge — here, in the form of the grasped, the grasper and the grasping, the knowledge-power of the conscious self flashes (sphurati) — that is the waking state (jāgradvṛtti); for the bound soul (paśu) it is said to be the loss of his own nature (svarūpahāni); for the Lords (pati) it gives the flash of own nature."

Notes: the five sense-modalities listed; jñāna here = determinate sense cognition. The same "pati/paśu" pair as our Siddhitrayī work.

## 1.9 — svapno vikalpāḥ
**Dream is mental constructs (vikalpa).**

Vārttika (1-41): "For the all-pervading Lord whose nature is seeing (dṛṣṭisvabhāva), the ever-new arising within of constructs, excluding external objects (bāhyārthanirāsa) — that is called dream: the ever-fresh appearance of constructs — cities, mountains, parks — which perform their own functions (svārthakriyākāriṇaḥ) without dependence on outer objects, and which cover the bound soul's own nature."

Notes: vikalpa = the inner construction; the dream definition is *structural* (object-independent cognition) — it covers imagination generally, not sleep alone. Same terms as in our Siddhitrayī (vikalpa vs the Buddhist's kalpanā) and the Śivadṛṣṭi tradition.

## 1.10 — aviveko māyāsauṣuptam
**Non-discrimination is māyā's deep sleep.**

Vārttika (1-43/44): "When the power, whose nature is knowing-and-known, does not arise — that is non-discrimination (aviveka) of the conscious self, through lack of reflection (avimarśa). It is called māyā because it nourishes the web of coverings (āvṛtijāla). The memory of objects (arthasmṛti) resting only in the conscious self — that is deep sleep (suṣuptatā)."

Notes: aviveka (non-discrimination) vs viveka (1.18's vitarka); māyā as the *nourisher of the covering-web* — a Bhāskara-specific formulation; suṣupta as the state where object-memory rests within consciousness (not nothing).

## 1.11 — tritayabhoktā vīreśaḥ
**The enjoyer of the triad is the Hero-Lord (Vīreśa).**

Vārttika (1-48/51): "Sattva, rajas, tamas — the triad called the guṇas; they arise from one's own nature in accord with the states; they cover the bound ones, but for the all-pervading Lord they do not cover own-nature because [he is] of the form of pervading all (sarvānusyūta). The conscious body is the enjoyer (bhoktā) of the outflows (niḥṣyanda) of the guṇas' vibration (guṇaspanda), because of taking them in (svīkaraṇa) and because of the Lordliness of its expanse — hence the Hero-Lord, whose single mode is self-light (svaprakāśaikavṛtti)."

Notes: "guṇaspandaniḥṣyandā" — the *outflow of the vibration of the guṇas*: the Vārttika is using the spanda vocabulary (our CP1 stack). [X: "ācchādakaṃ paśūnāṃ tatsvasthitestu vibhoḥ punaḥ" — the guṇas cover the bound, but the Lord's own state is uncovered.]

## 1.12 — vismayo yogabhūmikāḥ
**Wonder is the ground of yoga.**

Vārttika (1-54/56): "The conscious body, the Lord, is the cause of the whole creation and so on; gazing at this world as founded on it, he who stands wonder-struck (vismayāviṣṭa), endowed with insight (pratibhānavān) — from his connection with the mass of states beginning with the bindu (bindvādibhāvaugha) there arises wonder; those are the grounds of yoga (yogabhūmayas) — the stages of absorption (samādhivyaktayaḥ)."

Notes: vismaya = the *astonishment at reality as one's own manifestation* — an epistemic state, not emotion; yogabhūmikā = the ladder of absorption stages. This anticipates the "pratibhā" (flash of insight) vocabulary of 1.16-1.19.

## 1.13 — icchāśaktir umā kumārī
**The power of will is Umā, the maiden.**

Vārttika (1-58/59): "The power of will (icchā) alone is the surpassing power of the supreme Self; by its union with the state of seeing-and-doing in all things (sarvadṛkkartṛtāyoga) it is the destroyer of aversion-and-attachment (kutsāvatsṛti? — "the flow of loathing and craving"); hence, from absorption into the unobscured conscious nature (nirāvaraṇacidrūpasamāveśa), it becomes the cause of the unbroken Lord in all things: as he wills, so it manifests."

Notes: icchā = the first of the triadic powers (icchā/jñāna/kriyā); Umā as *icchāśakti* — the goddess as will-power (the Kula identification of the goddess with will). [X: the edition prints "icchāśaktitamā" with the variant 'icchā śaktir umā kumārī' in parenthesis — the latter, clearer reading is followed. "kumārī" (maiden) — the unspent, undifferentiated will.]

## 1.14 — dṛśyaṃ śarīram
**The visible is the body.**

Vārttika (1-61/63): "Because the conscious nature is pervasive (vyāpaka), it does not have a single body; not only the external object — whatever visible [object] is born of construct (vikalpaja) — that is its body, because of pervasiveness and because all beings arise from it; by identity with its own class's conscious nature (svavargacitsvarūpeṇa tādātmya), it is the bearer of all bodies, like a reflection in a mirror apart from the body [of the mirror]... he for whom the visible is pervasive — he is the abode of the fortune of liberation (mokṣaśrīniketana)."

Notes: the mirror-reflection doctrine — the visible world as Śiva's *body* = its reflection in the mirror of consciousness; "vikalpaja" — the visible is *born of construct* — consistent with 1.9's vikalpa-theory.

## 1.15 — hṛdaye cittasaṃghaṭṭād dṛśyasvāpadarśanam
**In the heart, from the collision of the mind, there is the vision of the visible-as-sleep (dṛśyasvāpa).**

Vārttika (1-65/71): "In the heart — the support (ādhāra), the ground (adhiṣṭhāna) of the networks of all senses and channels — the mind of one detached from external objects, by practice and the rest, comes to rest; the visible objects dissolve; the deep-sleep space (sauṣuptaṃ vyoma), the heart, is called 'middle' because of its centrality; there the mind dissolves like fire without fuel; in that mindless, fireless, darkness-free [state], in the Fourth space (turīyākāśa), there is the immediate apprehension of one's own nature and the cessation of the ignorance rooted in the misery of birth and death... For the unawakened, that is the experience of sleep; for the awakened, the divine mudrā's absorption (divyamudrāsamāveśa) — the vision (darśana) of the visible-as-sleep."

Notes: saṃghaṭṭa (collision/friction) of the mind in the heart — the friction that stills the mind (like the churning-stick); dṛśyasvāpa = the visible "sleeping" — objects dissolved into their luminous source. The divyamudrā term connects to the kārikā-material in the Vṛtti tradition.

## 1.16 — śuddhatattvasaṃdhānād vā
**Or from union with the pure principle.**

Vārttika (1-74/75): "The pure principle, without limiting adjunct (nirupadhi), of the nature of light (jyotīrūpa), is called Śiva. Its union — the reflection of identity with it (tadātmatvavimarśa) — brings the breaking of the veil of darkness, by the state of being completely full (paripūrṇaikarūpatā); by the abandoning of the outer sense of "I" (bāhyāhaṃbhāvasaṃtyāga), one's unbroken own-state (akhaṇḍitanijasthiti)."

Notes: [X: KSTS 4 splits what the Vimarśinī reads as one sūtra — "śuddhatattvasaṃdhānād vā paśuśaktiḥ" — into two: śuddhatattvasaṃdhānād vā (1-16) and svapadaśaktiḥ (1-17). The edition's own apparatus records this. Follow the edition, note the alternative.] "Or" (vā) — the alternative path to 1.15.

## 1.17 — svapadaśaktiḥ
**The power of one's own state.**

Vārttika (1-76/77): "One's own state is to be known as the 'true state' (satpada), proclaimed by the name Śiva; its power (vīrya) is of the form of seeing-and-acting (dṛkkriyārūpa) — that is proclaimed the power (śakti). Its entry into its own agent-part (kartraṃśasaṃveśa), its being-dissolved (līnatā) — that is its state (sthiti); that itself is the flash of insight (pratibhāloka), the single radiance of light (prakāśaikamanthara)."

Notes: the second division of the "two sūtras" from 1.16's apparatus note; śakti as the *power of one's own state* = the energy that is the self's own — the "paśuśakti" of the Vimarśinī reading.

## 1.18 — vitarka ātmajñānam
**Reflective inquiry is self-knowledge.**

Vārttika (1-78/80): "Discrimination (viveka), the examination (pratyavekṣaṇa) of the distinction between adjuncts and adjuncted (upādhyupādhisambheda) — that is reflective inquiry (vitarka), of the single mass of the reflection of one's own nature, in establishing the recognition (pratyabhijñāprasādhana) of the Self that is of the nature of Śiva. That is to be known as the supreme means (paraṃ sādhanaṃ); when that exists, there is self-knowledge (ātmavedana); from it, or from the flash (pratibhonmeṣa), arises the supreme pure self."

Notes: vitarka = reflective discrimination — the *recognitive* inquiry (pratyabhijñā vocabulary!); "upādhyupādhi" — distinguishing the condition from the conditioned — our Siddhitrayī/recognition stack.

## 1.19 — lokānandaḥ samādhisukham
**The bliss of the world is the joy of samādhi.**

Vārttika (1-83/84): "The supreme light of consciousness (citprakāśaḥ paraḥ), pure, arisen as if within and without — eternal, all-formed, all, source of all beings, the all-pervading Lord — that is to be known as the world (loka); that itself is called bliss (ānanda)."

Notes: the world *is* the light — hence "the bliss of the world"; for the yogī whose own nature flashes (sphuritasvātmasphūrti), the joy of the attainment of own nature (svarūpalabdhisukha) and the shining of the one light in all beings arise at once — the Vārttika's final [bracket] says this is "from the service of the means taught [above]."
