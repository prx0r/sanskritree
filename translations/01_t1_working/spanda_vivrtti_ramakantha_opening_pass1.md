## The Vivṛti's opening (maṅgala-section)

*The KSTS 6 text opens with Rāmakaṇṭha's own verses introducing the work (before the root kārikā 1.1 "taṃ śakticakravibhavaprabhavaṃ..." of our CP1 edition). Note the numbering: in the Vivṛti's recension these opening verses carry their own count.*

**Vivṛti v. 1** — daśādikkālādyair akalitacidālokavapuṣaḥ sadā tādṛksvātmānubhavitṛtayā visphurati yaḥ |
nijo dharmaḥ śambhoranupamacamatkārasarasaḥ paraṃ śāktaṃ tattvaṃ jagati jayati spanda iti tat ||
> That which is ever flashing (visphurati) with the same self-experiencing subjectivity, its body the unobstructed light of consciousness (akalita-cid-āloka), unseizable by the ten directions, time, and the rest — the own nature (nija dharma) of Śambhu, rich with incomparable wonder (camatkāra) — that supreme Power-principle (śākta-tattva) triumphs in the world: it is called Spanda.

*Notes: the opening thesis — spanda is Śiva's own nature as self-experiencing light; "camatkāra" (wonder-taste) — the aesthetic term in the heart of the metaphysical definition (cf. our Śivasūtra 1.12's vismaya!). Rāmakaṇṭha's prose [bracket]: "The functions of the directions etc. (digādivṛttayaḥ), unable to unfold distinctly in the power, remain absorbed in it, reduced to mere traces (tanmātrāvaśeṣāḥ) — the power itself, by its inclination toward the powerful one (śaktimad-unmukhatayā), embraces only the flashing of the experiencer-form. Thus by the first half-verse the powerful one (śaktimān) is grasped as pure consciousness; by the rest, to show that the word 'spanda' is meaningful, the power's unfolding (śāktavijṛmbhaṇā), synonymous with brahmāhrāda (the delight of Brahman) and the rest, is reflected on. By the word 'triumphs' (jayati) the supreme excellence of the power-principle is understood; and for those who plunge into the enjoyment and savoring of its absorption (samāveśarasa-paribhoga-gāḍhatā), the attainment of enjoyment and liberation and their desirability to all is suggested (dhvanyate)."*

**Vivṛti v. 2** — tajjñair adarśitadiśaḥ svayam āviśantaḥ kecin nisargagahanaṃ viṣamaṃ bhramantu |
tenaiva yatpunaramī hatavartmanānyān ākārayanti gamanāya mahān bhramo'yam ||
> Let those wander, entering by themselves into the rugged wilds of the deep [ocean] to which no direction is shown by the knowers — but those who, by that very [false] path, induce others to journey [with them]: this is a great delusion.

*Notes: the guide-critique: entering without a teacher is their own wandering (bhramantu — optative, permission); *inducing others* on the wrong path is mahān bhramaḥ (great error). Rāmakaṇṭha's prose: "Without the true lineage taught by the wise (vijñajanopadiṣṭasat-sampradāya), those whose consciousness is veiled by the Left power (vāmākhyāśaktisaṃvṛta) plunge into the deep ocean of the Śaiva āgamas by their own lights — they sink midway without reaching the far shore; and those who follow their bad path are led into the well of a blind man's eye-opening. Hence [the author says] 'this is my path to cross over' for such people."* — [X: "taccakṣurunmeṣamandhakūpanipatanam" — "the falling into the blind well of an eye-opening" — the proverb of the blind leading the blind.]

**Vivṛti v. 3** — nānāvādānilaughān anavasarakṛtānyonyasaṃghaṭṭaghorodghātān āpādayadbhiḥ |
śravaṇavidalanoddāmaśabdaprabandhān |
śāstrābdhiḥ kṣobhyate yair vivaraṇarabhasād udyadāvartabhīmabhrāntibhraṣṭāgamārthaplava-vidhuramati-stārito'rthī janas taiḥ ||
> The ocean of scriptures is churned by those who, with the gusts of many doctrines, breed terrible explosions of mutual collision at every moment, with rampages of speech that split the ear — and the seeker (arthī), his mind unsteady, tossed about by the rising whirlpools, the terrible confusions, the shipwreck of the āgamas' meaning — by them he is carried across.

*Notes: the churning image (the Ocean of Scriptures churned like the Milk Ocean — with the seeker as the one cast across); the mixed metaphor resolves: despite the turmoil, the true seeker is *carried over* — a hidden grace-note. [X: the text's last words "stārito'rthī janas taiḥ" — "by them the seeker is ferried" — ironically the destructive disputants effect the crossing.]*

**Vivṛti v. 4** — kaścid bhinnarucir vidann api paraṃ tattvaṃ viparyasyati |
dveṣadhvāntaviruddhabuddhir itaro jātyaiva cānyo jaḍaḥ |
itthaṃ durlabha eva sarvahṛdayagrāhī girāṃ saṃgrahaḥ |
kiṃ tv ete'dya vivekinaḥ spandakArikA...
> One, of broken taste, even knowing the supreme principle, inverts it (viparyasyati); another, his intellect obstructed by the darkness of hatred, [opposes]; and yet another is simply dull by nature. Thus the collection of utterances that grasps the heart of all (sarvahṛdayagrāhī) is truly hard to obtain — but these today are the discerning ones (vivekinaḥ)...

*Notes: the three failure-types (perverse-knower, hostile, dull) → the rarity of the heart-grasping teaching; "sarvahṛdayagrāhī girāṃ saṃgrahaḥ" — the Spandakārikā collection itself. [X: the verse continues into the root text's frame — collation against KSTS 6 print needed for the exact split.]*

## Bridge to the root kārikās

The Vivṛti's maṅgala-complex establishes: (1) spanda = Śiva's own nature as unobstructed self-experiencing light; (2) the necessity of the true lineage (sat-sampradāya — our Kaula/Kubjikā vocabulary of the day); (3) the text's function: to carry the seeker across the churned ocean; (4) the rarity of the heart-grasping teaching. The root kārikā 1.1 of our CP1 edition ("taṃ śakticakravibhavaprabhavaṃ śaṃkaraṃ stumaḥ") follows in the Vivṛti's own sequence.
