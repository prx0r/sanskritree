## The Dāṇḍaka — the great praise of Mālinī (KubjT_2.1–2.2)

**2/1** — jaya tvaṃ mālinī devī nirmale malanāśinī | jñānaśaktiḥ prabhur devī buddhis tvaṃ tejavardhanī ||
> Triumph, O goddess Mālinī, the stainless, the destroyer of stain: the knowledge-power, the lord, the goddess; you are the intellect, the increaser of brilliance —

**2/2** — jananī sarvabhūtānāṃ saṃsāre 'smin vyavasthitā | mātā vīrāvalī devī kāruṇyaṃ kuru vatsale ||
> — the mother of all beings, established in this saṃsāra; the mother, O goddess Vīrāvalī — show compassion, O dear one.

**[The Dāṇḍaka (the prose-hymn)]** — the report's mālinī-dossier in full: the deity-alphabet-power overlap made explicit.

> "(1) Triumphant [art thou], the fire-formed born of the supreme-principle's nirvāṇa, issued forth, of manifest form: the supreme knowledge-power — thou; the will and the action, the straight-line again, like a sleeping great-serpent; (2) of the kuṇḍala-form, the lord, the nāda-power art thou sung, the radiant; (3) the light-formed, the beautiful, the auspicious (śivā), the eldest-named, the Vāmā, the Raudrī, the Ambikā of the mind-names; (4) the bindu-formed, the avadhūtā, of the half-moon's form art thou; the triangle [formed of] the a-u-ma-kāras joined with the i-kāra and the e-kāra into the one — the principle-formed, standing in the bhaga-form; (5) born of the first-principle, the yoni-formed, the awakener of Śrīkaṇṭha, the Rudra-mother; (6) the infinite power likewise, the supremely subtle, honoured by the three-formed and the lord-of-the-devas, bearing the burdens; (7) of the tithis' nature, the Sthāṇu-born, the Hara-named, honoured by Jhaṇṭīśa and Bhauktīśa, the Sadyojāta-natured, honoured by Anugraheśa; in the fierce union, the consort of Mahāsena; (8) with the sixteenth-limit's nectar, the flood of the bindu's outpouring drenching the whole body, giving the supreme-bliss of the perfect nirvāṇa-joy — O Bhairavī, attached to the play in Bhairava's garden; (9) the supreme Mālinī, honoured in the Rudra-garland, the Rudraśakti, the sky-goer, the siddha-yogeśvarī, the siddha-mother, the lord — the mass of sound (śabdarāśi) — in the yoni-ocean; (10) thou art the speech-purified, the lord of speech; the Mātṛkā-accomplished, the will and the action, the auspicious, the siddha-Lakṣmī, the majesty, the good-being, the motion, the eternal, the fame, the Nārāyaṇī, the red-fierce, the fierce-eyed, the terrible-formed; (11) delighting in the great-śuṣma-sacrifice — thou, the triumphant, the unconquered, the deluder of Rudra; thou who art mounted on the lap of the nine-selfed god; (12) by the mantra-path's followers, the mantrins attached to the vīra-drink, the true devotees, art thou worshipped, O Devī, with the five-nectars, with the divine-drink festivals, by those men arisen in one-birth, two-birth, three-, four-, five-, six-, seven-births; (13) with the good and the weak, art thou satisfied, delighting in wine and meat; by the blazing of the mantra-vidyā-vow, the skull-and-skeleton kāpālikas, mounted on the divine-conduct, with homage of the oṃ-kāra, svāhā, svadhā, vauṣaḍ, vaṣaṭ, phaṭ, hūṃ-sounds; (14) by these utterers of the mantra-syllables, by the sādhakas standing with the left hand [counting] the rosary, by the sons, the mothers, the maṇḍala-initiated, the yogis — with the yoginī-hosts' melāpaka, the Rudra-sport's play — art thou worshipped, O Devī, giving the yogins' yoga-siddhi; (15) those whom thou lookest on with the lotus-petal eyes full of love — for him, dwelling in the divine and the atmosphere, the siddhi of the seven-pātālas' khecarīs stands unobstructed. (16) Whoever, devoted, recites this Dāṇḍaka once, twice, thrice; whoever, pure, ever remembers it — that man thou protectest even in weapons, fire, thieves, the ocean, on the mountain-peak, O Devī, by the affection of a son; (17) the great-Lakṣmīs, attached to gold, thieves, others' wives, the brahmin-killers, the cow-killers, the greatly-defiled — remembering thy face like the full-moon, with the flashing ruby earrings rubbing the cheeks, they are released; (18) even those bound with firm bonds, the nāga-nooses, the arm-bound foot-bolts — even they, by the singing of thy name, O Devī, are released; (19) from the terrible great-diseases, remembering thy two lotus-feet — O Mahākālī, radiant with the fire-of-time's brilliance, served by Skanda, Govinda, Brahmā, Indra, Candra, Arka, and the flower-weaponed [Kāma], with the garland of the hair-locks [and] the lotus-filaments — (20) O all-vīra Ambikā, O Bhairavī — I have come to Bhairava as thy refuge: forgive my offence, forgive my offence, O auspicious one."

## The Kubjikā's emergence (2/3–2/15)

**2/3** — evaṃ stutā mahādevī bhairaveṇa mahātmanā | tato liṅgaṃ vinirbhidya nirgatā parameśvarī ||
> Thus praised by the great-souled Bhairava, the great goddess then, piercing through the liṅga, issued forth — the supreme Lady.

**2/4** — nīlāñjanasamaprakhyā kubjarūpā vṛkodarā | īṣatkarālavadanā barbarordhvaśiroruhā ||
> Like the black collyrium in complexion, of the hunchback (kubjā) form, wolf-bellied; her face slightly terrible, with the barbarous hair raised up —

**2/5** — surūpā ca virūpā ca anekākārarūpiṇī | vāmaprasāritakarā vāmadevī-m-uvāca ha ||
> — the beautiful and the deformed, of manifold forms; with the left hand outstretched, the Vāmadevī spoke:

**2/6** — ājñānandasamāviṣṭā stutyānandākulīkṛtā | na vedmi ko 'tra māṃ stauti kāhaṃ kasya varapradā ||
> "Entered with the command's bliss, stirred by the praise's bliss — I know not who praises me here: who am I, whose boon-giver?"

**2/7** — uvācaivaṃ mahāsattvā dṛṣṭipāto madīyakaḥ | āśīviṣeva duṣprekṣyaḥ sa kathaṃ dhāritas tvayā ||
> Thus spoke the great being: "My glance's fall [is] like the venomous serpent — hard to look on; how has he been sustained by you?"

**2/8** — prārthayasva tadā kiñcid yat te manasi rocate ||
> "Ask then something that pleases your mind."

**2/9** — prasādāya mahādevi dadājñānugrahaṃ mama | tapasā tava cogreṇa mama hāniḥ kujāmbike ||
> "For [my] favor, O great goddess, give me the command's grace: by your fierce austerity, O Kujāmbikā, my ruin [was done]."

**2/10** — sañjātā tena me devi pūrvam uktam idaṃ mayā | evaṃ śrutvā mahādevī salajjā gadgadekṣanā ||
> "That has arisen; therefore, O Devī, this was said by me before." Hearing this, the great goddess, ashamed, with faltering glance —

**2/11** — kiṃ te siddhaṃ mahādeva yena lajjāpitā vayam ||
> — "What has been accomplished by you, O great god, that we are made ashamed?"

**2/12** — pūrvam uktaṃ mayā tubhyam ājñāsamayagocare | mattulyānugṛhītvā tu paścād bhava gaṇāmbikā ||
> "It was said before by me to you, in the scope of the command's rule: having graced [one] equal to me, then become the Ambikā of the gaṇas."

**2/13** — kasyedaṃ siddhasantānaṃ pāramparyakramāgatam | matsakāśāt punas tubhyaṃ tvatsakāśāt punar mama ||
> "Whose is this siddha-lineage, come by the succession-order? From me to you again, from you to me again."

**2/14** — evaṃ tad bhairavaṃ vākyaṃ śrutvā devī parāṅmukhī | sañjātā kubjikārūpā lajjāto rabhasoditā ||
> Hearing this speech of Bhairava, the goddess, turned away, become the Kubjikā-form, from shame spoke in haste:

**2/15** — kiṃ tu lajjāyase devi pūrvam ājñā mayā tava | idānīṃ dada me śīghraṃ mā śaṅkā mā vilambaya ||
> "But why are you ashamed, O Devī? The command was [given] before by me to you. Now give [it] to me quickly — no doubt, no delay."

## The ājñā-dialogue (2/16–2/22) — the lineage's law

**2/16** — aprabuddhapramattena yadā tad rabhasoditam | tat kiṃ nigrahabuddhyā vā yuktaṃ tvedaṃ kujeśvara ||
> "What was spoken in haste by the unawakened, the heedless — is that fitting for you, O Kujeśvara, with the mind of reproach?"

**2/17** — sarvānugrahake devi kiṃ na budhyasi cātmani | na mayā rahitaṃ kiñcin na tvayā rahitaṃ kvacit ||
> "O Devī, giver of all graces, do you not understand in yourself? Nothing [is] separated from me; nothing anywhere [is] separated from you."

**2/18** — anyonyaguṇayogena kāryakāraṇayogataḥ | tvaṃ gurur mama deveśi ahaṃ te na vicāraṇāt ||
> "By the union of each other's qualities, by the effect-and-cause union: you are my guru, O goddess, and I [am] yours — not from deliberation."

**2/19** — rudrabhairavavīrāṇām eṣā cājñā na kasyacit | yadi śiṣyaṃ na manyetha mitratvena tadā dada ||
> "This command [is] not [for] any of the Rudras, the Bhairavas, the vīras. If you do not hold [me] as a disciple, then give [it] in friendship."

**2/20** — evaṃ brūte tadā devyā sarvam etad bhaviṣyati | paścimedaṃ kṛtaṃ deva pūrvabhāgavivarjitam ||
> Then the goddess says: "All this will be. This Western [transmission] is made, O god, bereft of the Eastern part."

**2/21** — candradvīpaṃ manoramyaṃ deva tyaktuṃ na me manaḥ | paścimaṃ sarvamārgāṇāṃ tvaṃ tāvad anuśīlaya ||
> "The delightful Candradvīpa, O god, my mind [is] not [willing] to abandon. The Western [transmission] of all the paths — you, for now, practice."

**2/22** — paścimāmnāyamārgo 'yaṃ siddhānām akhilaṃ dada | gacchāmy ahaṃ punas tatra bhārate kulaparvatam ||
> "This Paścimāmnāya-path — give the siddhas' whole [transmission]. I will go again there — to the kulaparvata in Bhārata."

## The sacred geography (2/23–2/39) — the lineage's map

**2/23** — anādiyugaparyantaṃ kīrtayāmāsa tadvidām | śrīparvataṃ kumārākhyaṃ chāyāchatravibhūṣitam ||
> "From the beginningless age to its end, the knowers have praised the Śrī-parvata named Kumāra, adorned with the shade-canopy —

**2/24** — evam uktvā gatā tūrṇaṃ śrīmatkaumāraparvatam | tatra chāyātmikā devī avyaktā vyaktarūpiṇī ||
> — having spoken thus, she went swiftly to the blessed Kaumāra-parvata. There the goddess, of the shadow-nature, unmanifest, of manifest form —

**2/25** — kṣapitvā kālaparyāyaṃ yāvad ālokayed diśām | uttarāṃ tāvat tat sarvaṃ liṅgapūrṇaṃ mahāvanam ||
> — having spent the time's course, when she looked to the northern direction, then all that [was] a great forest filled with liṅgas —

**2/26** — aśītiyojanāyāmaṃ samantāt parimaṇḍalam | caturdvārasamopetaṃ tīrthakoṭibhir āvṛtam ||
> — eighty yojanas in length, circular all around, furnished with the four gates, surrounded by crores of sacred-fords —

**2/27** — anekasiddhasaṃchannaṃ manoramyam anopamam | tamoguṇagaṇākīrṇam anekāścaryasaṃkulam ||
> — hidden with many siddhas, delightful, incomparable; filled with the hosts of the tamas-quality, crowded with many wonders —

**2/28** — devyādṛṣṭinipātena akasmāc chrīr upasthitā | tena śrīśailam uddiṣṭaṃ devyānāmapratiṣṭhitam ||
> — by the goddess's glance's fall, suddenly the Śrī (fortune) stood there. Therefore it is called the Śrīśaila, established by the goddess's name.

**2/29** — aṅguṣṭhena kṛtā rekhā svasthānasya ca tasya vai | tatra jātā nadī divyā sāsīmā ubhayor api ||
> A line was drawn with the thumb, of that [goddess's] own place: there arose a divine river, the boundary of both —

**2/30** — tacchāyāṃ niścalāṃ kṛtvā ājñāṃ dattvā tu śāmbhavīm | atra yo viśate kaścit sa me tulyo bhaviṣyati ||
> — having made her shadow motionless, having given the Śāmbhavī-command: "Whoever enters here will become equal to me."

**2/31** — hartā kartā svatantro 'sau bhraṣṭajñānaprakāśakaḥ | ājñāto guṇam aiśvaryaṃ trailokye sacarācare ||
> "He is the taker, the doer, the independent; the illuminator of the fallen-knowledge; [by] the commanded quality, the lordship in the three worlds, moving and unmoving."

**2/32** — evam ākṣepayitvā tu gatā trikūṭaparvatam | tatra kālaṃ kṣapitvā tu kiṣkindhākhyam anugrahet ||
> Having thus cast [the command], she went to the Trikūṭa-parvata. There, having spent the time, she graced [the place] named Kiṣkindhā —

**2/33** — tasya cājñāvibhūtiṃ tu dattvānugṛhya rākṣasān | yena tiṣṭhāmy ahaṃ tīre samudrasya tv aśaṅkitā ||
> — having given that [place] the command's majesty and graced the rākṣasas — "by which I stand, fearless, on the ocean's shore" —

**2/34** — tatra kanyākumārī tvaṃ gatvā kālasya paryayam | samudram anugṛhītvā daradaṇḍīṃ gatā punaḥ ||
> — there, having gone as the Kanyā-kumārī, the time's course; having graced the ocean, she went again to the Daradaṇḍī —

**2/35** — tatra chāyādharī devī avyaktaguṇacetasā | lokānugrahahetvarthaṃ tatrājñāṃ mocayet punaḥ ||
> — there the goddess, the shadow-holder, with the mind of the unmanifest qualities, for the sake of the world's grace, would release the command again.

**2/36** — pūrvasthāne tu yā vācā sā tv atraiva bhaviṣyati | evam uktvā gatā dūraṃ paścimaṃ himagahvaram ||
> "What speech [was] at the earlier place, that will be here itself." Having spoken thus, she went far to the Western Himalayan cave —

**2/37** — yatra olambikā nāma tiṣṭhate vanapallikā | raktāmbaradharā raktā raktasthā ratilālasā ||
> — where [she who is] named Olambikā dwells, the forest-shrine: the red-robed, the red, standing in the red, longing for delight —

**2/38** — tatrasthā gahvarāntasthā guhāgahanavāsinī | yāvat santiṣṭhate kālaṃ tāvad yogimayaṃ khilam ||
> — standing there, in the cavern's depth, the dweller in the dense cave: for as long as she abides the time, the whole is made of the yogis —

**2/39** — tais tu santoṣitā devī nayopāyair anekadhā | tataḥ prasannagambhīrā uvācedaṃ kujeśvarī ||
> — by those [yogis] the goddess is satisfied with the manifold fitting means. Then the pleased, the profound Kujeśvarī spoke this:

**2/40** — anekopāyaracanā vivekaguṇaśālinī ||
> "The construction of manifold means, endowed with the quality of discernment —"
