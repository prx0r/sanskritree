# Akulavīratantra, vv. 19–155 — P3/T2 (the alternative-reading translation)

*Pass 3 (= T2). vv. 1–18 done (`akulavira_t2.md` → R2 → T3-FINAL). Strategy: re-derive from the IAST, take the live alternative, mark the fork. T1 = `kaula_frame_pass1_cont.md` (19–49) + `akulavira_completion_pass1.md` (50–155).*

## v. 19 — praveśanirgame naiva nāvāhanavisarjanam
**T1:** "No entry, no exit; no invocation, no dismissal."
**T2:** "Neither entering nor leaving; neither summoning nor sending away" — *fork: the naiva as "no... no" (the list) vs "neither... nor" (the pair-structure — the negations as paired opposites). The verse's anuprāsa (praveśa/nirgama, āvāhana/visarjana — the paired rituals) supports T2's pairing: each ritual pair refused as a pair.*

## v. 23 — viṃśātmako hyeṣa pudgala nāsti yatra vai
**T1:** "...where this twenty-formed one and the person do not exist" [X: the twenty].
**T2:** "...where this twentieth [tattva-count] and the person are not" — *fork: viṃśātmaka as "the twenty-formed" (the group) vs "the twentieth" (the ordinal — a specific member of a count). If the verse negates the tattva-arithmetic, T2's ordinal (the count itself) is the sharper. Both [X]-adjacent; the print needed.*

## v. 27 — yādṛśena tu bhāvena puruṣo bhāvayet sadā
**T1:** "By whatever state a man always contemplates, of that kind he obtains the fruit."
**T2:** "As a man thinks, so he becomes — what he contemplates, he obtains" — *fork: the proverb-like force (the verse IS a maxim — cf. the Śvetāśvatara's "yādṛśo yādṛśo bhavati" tradition). T2's aphoristic register renders the maxim as a maxim; T1's is the literal.*

## v. 39 — nibaddhañca malakṣaṇam
**T1:** "and [yet] bound, of the nature of the impurity" [X].
**T2:** "and the bound — the mark-less" (nibaddhaṃ ca nirlakṣaṇam — the emendation) — *fork: the R1-emendation vs the print. T2 takes the emendation (the verse's nir-anuprāsa: nirākula, nirvikalpa, nirguṇa, sunirmala... nir[lakṣaṇa]); the print's malakṣaṇam breaks the series. The emendation is recommended for T3.*

## v. 55 — tadā kiṃ koulikeḥ krameḥ
**T1:** "what [need] of the Kaula-rites?"
**T2:** "then what of the Kaulika-orders?" — *fork: krama = "rites/procedures" (the practices) vs "orders/sequences" (the graded initiatory ladders). The verse's context (the Akulavīra gained — the rites obsolete) supports both; the "orders" reading adds the initiatory sense (the graded paths).*

## v. 56 — kaulamārge dvau santi tathā sahaja tathā
**T1:** "On the Kaula path there are two: the sahaja and the [kṛtaka]."
**T2:** "Two there are on the Kaula road — the innate, and the made" — *fork: sahaja/kṛtakā as "the innate/the artificial" (the pair-names) vs "the innate, and the made" (the plain rendering — with kṛtaka supplied). T2's plainness keeps the pair's force without the technical ring.*

## v. 65 — akule vīre ca saṃprāpte sarvam eti vinśyati
**T1:** "When the akula and the vīra are attained, everything goes to dissolution."
**T2:** "When the akula-hero is reached, everything dissolves" — *fork: akule vīre as the pair (the akula AND the vīra — two attainments) vs the compound-sense (the akula-vīra — one). The verse's next line (the ghee-in-curd simile) supports the single-attainment: the Akulavīra IS the akula-hero. T2's is the better reading — T1's pair was a mis-split.*
**Commentary-seed:** this is a genuine P3-find: the verse's "akule vīre" is the name-split (akula-vīra), not a pair — the whole text's title-word in two halves.

## v. 67 — raktākhyakulavīre ca tatsarvaṃ viniyojitam
**T1:** "in the red-named kula-vīra all that is disposed."
**T2:** "and in the Red-named — the Kula-Hero — all that is placed" — *fork: raktākhya-kulavīra as an epithet-description vs a quasi-name (the Red-named — a name of the Akulavīra). T2's name-reading makes the verse a self-naming; T1's is the descriptive. Both live; the name-reading is the more interesting (cf. the archive's "red-named").*

## v. 78 — nāhaṃ kaścin na me kaścin baddho na ca bādhakaḥ
**T1:** "I am not anyone; nothing is mine; no bound one, no binder."
**T2:** "I am no one; nothing is mine; no one is bound; no one binds" — *fork: baddho/bādhakaḥ as the noun-pair ("the bound one, the binder") vs the verbal ("no one is bound, no one binds"). T2's verbal reading is the more radically nondual — the binding-relation itself negated, not just its participants.*

## v. 99 — sārātsārataraṃ param
**T1:** "the more-essential-than-the-essential, the supreme."
**T2:** "the essence of the essence — the highest of the high" — *fork: the ablative-comparative's force ("more-essential-THAN-the-essential" — T1, the precise) vs the superlative-parallel ("essence of the essence" — T2, the fluent). The Kubjikā's parallel (sārātsārataraṃ — 1.3/1.7) is the same construction; the superlative-reading is the tradition's sense. Both render the same superlative.*

## v. 103 — kāntamānaiḥ kumārikāḥ
**T1:** "The virgins find the Akulavīra by their beloved measures" [X].
**T2:** "The maidens find the Akulavīra — by their dear standards" — *fork: kāntamāna as "beloved measures" (the kula's own worship) vs "their lovely measure" (the maidens' own beauty — the aesthetic reading) vs the corrupt-repair. The verse's position (after the śāstra/kumārīśruti image) supports the ritual-reading (T1); T2's "dear standards" keeps the ambiguity.*

## v. 106 — na jñātaṃ yena dehasthaṃ sa muktaḥ
**T1:** "...by whom, dwelling in the body, it is known [X: the print's na]."
**T2:** "Whoever has not known the body-dwelling — he is freed" — *fork: the print's na jñātaṃ as a genuine negative ("by whom it is NOT known, he is freed" — the paradox-reading: freedom without knowledge? No — impossible in context) vs the emendation (yena jñātaṃ — by whom known). T2's paradox-version is worth recording ONLY as the print's literal — the emendation stands.*

## v. 110 — nirālambapadaṃ śāntaṃ tathātītaṃ nirañjanam
**T1:** "the tranquil station without support, and the stainless beyond all that."
**T2:** "the supportless, tranquil state — and the Stainless beyond it" — *fork: nirālamba-pada = "the station without support" (T1) vs "the supportless state" (T2 — pada as state, the KJN's "śāntam padam" register); nirañjana as "the Stainless" (the glossary-capital) vs "the unstained." The glossary's capital-rule governs: the Stainless.*

## v. 130 — sa viśākhyaḥ mayūrākṣo arhanto buddha eva ca
**T1:** "he is Viśākha, Mayūrākṣa, the Arhat, the Buddha."
**T2:** "he is Skanda-the-six-faced, Peacock-eye, the Arhat, and the Buddha himself" — *fork: viśākhya = Viśākha (the name) vs "the six-faced" (the epithet — the archive's "Vishakhya (Skanda)" gloss); mayūrākṣa = "peacock-eyed" (the epithet — Indra's). T2 renders the epithets' meanings; T1 keeps the names. The inclusivism is the point either way.*

## v. 152 — bhināvasthāṃ svabhāgataḥ
**T1:** "the divided state, from its own share."
**T2:** "the state of division — by its own portion" — *fork: bhināvasthā = "the divided state" (the fragmentation) vs "the state of difference" (the dualistic state); svabhāgataḥ = "from its own share" vs "in its own portion" (the locative-ablative wobble). The verse introduces the final section's positive teaching; the "state of difference" (the dual state overcome) fits the close.*
