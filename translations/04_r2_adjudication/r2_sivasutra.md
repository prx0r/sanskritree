# Śivasūtra — R2 (the full-reasoning-chain adjudications)

*The R2 as commentary: for each fork, the complete chain — the form, the grammar, the anchor's rendering (quoted), the cross-text parallel, the period-context, the R1-finding — then the verdict and the T3-reading. Anchors: Dyczkowski's Aphorisms (quoted from the disk-clean-text). R1: `p2_sivasutra.md` + `PASS2_ROUND2_AUDIT.md` (C1).*

## 1.1 — caitanyam ātmā
**The form:** two nominatives, bare apposition; no verb. **The grammar:** ātmā = "the self" (the noun) or "one's own" (the possessive-adjectival, the Śaiva svarūpa-sense).
**The anchor:** "Consciousness is the Self." (the sūtra-list) — and Muller-Ortega's foreword: "the aphorism is quite direct and bald. It states: Consciousness—Self... 'Consciousness faces into the Self.'" The anchor keeps the noun.
**The internal evidence:** the Vārttika's own gloss — "caitanyam ātmano rūpaṃ siddhaṃ jñānakriyātmakam" (consciousness is the self's FORM, established as knowledge-and-action) — treats ātmā as the possessor whose form is consciousness: the svarūpa-reading. The batch's parallels: the KJN's "svabhāvagam"; the Akulavīra's "svabhāva."
**R1-finding:** the bare identity kept; the "is" as our grammatical intrusion (the foreword's point).
**Verdict:** the anchor's surface + the Vārttika's svarūpa-sense — "Consciousness is the Self [one's own nature]" with the note. Both faces are the doctrine: one's own nature IS the Self.
**T3-reading:** "Consciousness is the Self" — the note: "one's own essential nature, per the Vārttika's gloss."

## 1.7 — jāgratsvapnasuṣuptabhede turyābhogasaṃvit
**The form:** locative + compound subject; turyā-bhoga-saṃvit. **The grammar:** ābhoga (the printed turyābhogasaṃvit = turya-ābhoga or turya-bhoga?) — the KSTS4 prints turyābhogasaṃvit (the Kṣemarāja-recension reads turyābhogasaṃbhavaḥ — recorded in the edition itself).
**The anchor (quoted):** "The consciousness which is the expanse of the Fourth State (abides constantly in) the various (states) of waking, dreaming and deep sleep. 1/7" — and his Vārttika-translation: "The Fourth State is said to be (contemplation, that is) the reflective awareness of the compact unity (and undivided Being) of one's own essential nature (svasvarupaikaghanata)..." — **the anchor reads ābhoga as "expanse."**
**The alternative:** bhoga = "savoring/enjoyment" (the Fourth as TASTED) — a live sense of the printed form (turya-bhoga).
**R1-finding:** my earlier R2 adopted "savoring" — **correction: the anchor's "expanse" (ābhoga) is the established rendering; the savoring is a live alternative but unanchored. Verdict: keep "the Fourth's experience" (the neutral, both faces possible) with "expanse" (anchor) and "savoring" (bhoga) as the two notes.**
**T3-reading:** "In the distinction of waking, dream and deep sleep [is] the consciousness of the Fourth's experience" — the expanse/savoring fork in the note.

## 1.10 — aviveko māyāsauṣuptam
**The form:** three nominatives — the apposition-chain. **The grammar:** māyā-sauṣuptam as genitive ("māyā's sleep") or appositional ("māyā IS the sleep").
**The anchor (quoted, the sūtra-list):** "Deep sleep is Maya, the lack of discernment." — the anchor renders the apposition (Deep sleep IS Maya; the lack of discernment) — the identity.
**The internal evidence:** the Vārttika: "saiva māyāvṛtijālapoṣakatvāt prakīrtitā" — IT (the non-discrimination) is proclaimed māyā — the identity explicit.
**Verdict:** the apposition (my R2's adoption) is anchor-confirmed. 
**T3-reading:** "Lack of discernment: the deep sleep that is māyā."

## 1.13 — icchāśaktir umā kumārī (KSTS4: icchāśaktitamā kumārī)
**The form:** the KSTS4 print: icchāśaktitamā kumārī; the parenthetical variant: icchā śaktir umā kumārī.
**The anchor (quoted):** "Ksemaraja acknowledges the validity of Bhaskara's reading of this aphorism and comments on it in this form in much the same way, he follows the lead of his teacher Abhinavagupta and adopts a variant reading (i.e., icchasaktir uma kumari)... Like Bhaskara, Ksemaraja glosses the word virgin (kumari) to mean that she is the goddess who kills (marayati) or destroys Maya (ku)..."
**The chain:** the print = Bhāskara's own reading (primary for a Bhāskara-translation); the umā-variant = Abhinava/Kṣemarāja's (secondary); the kumārī-gloss (ku-mārayati — she kills māyā) belongs to both traditions.
**R1-finding (C1):** the priority-correction — applied.
**Verdict:** the print's reading primary; the umā-variant in the note.
**T3-reading:** "The maiden is the supremely-willed power" — the umā-reading + the ku-mārayati gloss in the note.

## 3.19 — kavargādiṣu māheśvaryādyāḥ paśumātaraḥ
**The form:** the locative + the mothers; paśu-mātaraḥ. **The grammar:** the genitive ("the mothers OF the bound") vs the governing-composition ("the mothers who bind").
**The internal evidence (the Vārttika's bracket):** "māheśvaryādyāḥ mātaraḥ paśūn svātmavimukhān viṣayāsaktyā adhaḥ pātayanti" — the mothers cast the bound down by attachment — the ACTIVE force is the bracket's own description.
**The anchor:** (the Exposition on 1.3-1.4 treats the mothers as the mātṛkās governing the phoneme-groups.)
**R1-finding:** the paśu-glossary (the bound).
**Verdict:** the active-composition ("the mothers who bind the bound") — the bracket-supported reading, both senses noted.
**T3-reading:** "In the ka-varga and the rest, Māheśvarī and the others — the mothers who bind the bound."

## 3.40 — abhilāṣādbahirgatiḥ saṃvāhyasya
**The form:** ablative + compound; saṃvāhya. **The grammar:** saṃvāhya = "the conveyed/transported one" (the gerundive — to-be-conveyed).
**The anchor (quoted):** "Due to (one's) craving, that which is transported moves outside. 3/41" — the anchor: "that which is transported" — the conveyed-reading.
**The internal evidence:** the Vārttika's bracket: "saṃvāhyākhyasya karaṇacakrasya" (the instrument-wheel called the conveyed) + the Khecarī/Gocarī/Dikcarī/Bhūcarī-conveyance — the karma-self transported by the powers.
**R1-finding:** my R2 adopted "the drivable" (the chariot-image) — **correction: the anchor + the bracket support "the conveyed/transported one"; the vehicle-image is a commentary-gloss, not the rendering. Verdict: keep the conveyed-one.**
**T3-reading:** "From longing, the going-out of the conveyed one" — the chariot-image in the commentary.

## 3.44 — nāsikāntarmadhyasaṃyamāt kim atra...
**The form:** the ablative + the rhetorical question.
**The anchor (quoted):** "(The movement of the vital breath is stilled) by concentrating on the centre within the nose. Of what use (then) are the left and right channels or Susumna? 3/45" — the dismissal.
**The internal evidence:** the bracket's Svacchandatantra-citation (the prāṇa-mastery as the ancillary) — the middle suffices.
**Verdict:** the dismissal (adopted).
**T3-reading:** "By the restraint at the nose's inner midpoint — what need of the left and right channels or suṣumnā?"
