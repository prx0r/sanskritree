# Kubjikātantra — C1 (plain-English interpretations)

## 1.1–1.5 — the frame
The goddess asks the Lord for the essence of the essence — and the frame sets the rules before the answer. The teaching is "not to be revealed, never at any time"; it is guarded like a kula-bride, like one's own yoni. The secrecy is not about excluding people; it is about the nature of the thing itself — what cannot be given can only be received by the right readiness. And the answer to "what is the essence of the Vedas?" turns out to be a list of goddesses.

## 1.12–1.14 — the three unknowables
The sādhana's conditions are named: the meaning of the mantra, the aliveness of the mantra, the yoni-gesture. Without these three — "no perfection, even in hundreds of crores of ages." And the operative condition is one line: "by the clarity of the state alone." Not technique, not ritual correctness — the state's purity. The Akulavīra's sahaja, in this text's own words.

## 1.15–1.20 — the ten vidyās
The essence of the Vedas is not a doctrine — it is ten goddesses. Kālī, Tārā, the fierce forms, the ten Mahāvidyās — and the claim that follows is the text's most radical: *by merely hearing her, the sādhaka is liberated while still alive.* The goddess IS the teaching; to hear her is to receive her.

## 6.67 — the yoni-mudrā delivered
The whole middle of the text answers 1.12's question — and the answer is a practice: the cakra-by-cakra visualization of the goddess, from the mūlādhāra's triangle up through the thousand-petaled lotus, ending in the serpent-power's ascent. "Thus, O Devī, the yoni-mudrā has been told to you." The Womb-Seal: the seal of the source — the practice that re-enters the womb of the goddess, which is also the source of everything. The ring closes.

## 7.6 — the three states
Three qualities of consciousness, not three classes of society. The divine see the goddess everywhere and worship with non-difference. The heroic perform the fierce rites — the night-worship, the offerings, the power's company. The bound cannot see the goddess in the kula's forms at all, and their mantras are "mere letter-forms," dead in their hands. What you can see determines what you are.

## 8 — the fierce practices
The enemy-destroying rites: the mantras at the crossroads, the skulls at the banyan root, the corpse-seat, the offerings. This is a Kālīkula manual, and this is what it teaches. But the goddess who destroys enemies is the same goddess who liberates. The fierceness is not an exception to the teaching; it is its shadow-side, for the state that needs it.

## 9.15 — the formless through the formed
The master-key, and the answer to the opening's question — "by what means does one attain the Stainless-station?" — "Having reached the formless, he gains the Uncoloured-station." You cannot jump to the formless; you go through the formed — the visualization of the goddess in her forms — until the forms teach you what is behind them. The formed is the root of all; the formless is its fruit. This one verse explains why the text carries so much detailed visualization.

## 14 — the inner worship
After the pots, the mandalas, the offerings — the mental worship, where the offerings are your own qualities. The mind as the flower, the life-breath as the incense, the unstruck sound as the bell. The fifteen virtue-flowers: non-violence, the senses' restraint, knowledge, compassion, forgiveness, the freedom from illusion, greed, hatred. The same fifteen flowers appear in the KJN — two texts, one list: the inner worship is the shared summit.

## 17 — the vernacular
The text ends in Bengali. The Sanskrit frame dissolves into the living charms: the mustard scattered in the fire, the root ground and applied, the incantation for the stillbirth-woman. The teaching that began with the secret bride ends in the village, in the mother's tongue, at the bedside. The descent complete.
