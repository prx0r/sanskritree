# Akulavīratantra — C1 (plain-English interpretations)

## The maṅgala
The homage is a compressed statement of the whole teaching. Mīnanātha is the innate bliss born from his own body: the teaching did not come from outside, it IS his own nature. He is the support of all — the ground everything stands on. The teacher, the teaching, and the goal are the same reality.

## v. 1
"More secret than secret, the secret of secrets" — not hidden because exclusive, but because it cannot be said. This is the lineage of the siddhas' own reality, which is only real when it is one's own. A secret you can be told is not this secret.

## vv. 5–6
Those who run after every teaching never find the innate bliss. They are looking outward at teachings, and the innate is what they already are. The "net of scriptures": the more they catch, the more they are caught. The partless guru is not another teacher to find; it is the state itself.

## vv. 7–10
The whole map of Indian philosophy is swept aside in a double move: their knowledge is indirect (they talk about reality) and constructed (their attainments are built of thoughts). What is constructed and indirect releases no one. The sharpest line: their attainment is itself pierced by the bonds of sin — the cure is part of the disease.

## v. 12
When the Akulavīra is seen, the mind goes still. Not disciplined, not trained, not emptied — still. You do not do anything to the mind; you see what is real, and the stillness follows on its own.

## v. 23
Even the arithmetic of reality is negated — the twenty-formed scheme, the counting itself — and the person who would be counted. Where the Akulavīra is, there is no scheme and no schemer. The activity of schematizing has ceased.

## v. 39
In the great list of negations — non-dual, unmoving, tranquil, unagitated, construct-free — one line startles: "and the bound, the mark-less." Even the bound state is included in the negation; the paśu too is dissolved. Nothing is excluded from the Akulavīra; that is what "the support of all" means.

## v. 56
The Kaula path has two limbs: the made — the observances, the rites, the graded practices — and the innate, the equal-taste. The whole text is a polemic for the second against the first: not against the lineage, but against the lineage's scaffolding. The observances bind; the innate releases.

## v. 65
"When the akula-hero is reached, everything dissolves" — the whole claim in miniature, the title-word itself split across the line. The hero beyond the kula is the state in which all dharmas, all supports, the kula's furniture dissolve. As ghee in curd: the essence was always there, latent.

## v. 78
"I am no one; nothing is mine; no one is bound; no one binds." Not a renunciant's modesty — a claim about reality. If the self is the stainless, there is no one to be bound and no binder. The apparatus of bondage dissolves because its participants dissolve.

## v. 99
The fools do not know "the more-essential-than-the-essential" — the same superlative across this corpus, in the Kubjikā, in the KJN. Three texts, one word: the tradition's shared name for what it is pointing at. "Comparable only to self-experience" — you cannot be told what it is; you can only taste it.

## v. 130
Whoever stands in the equal-taste IS the whole pantheon — Brahmā, Hari, Rudra, Śiva, the moon, the sun, Skanda, Indra, even the Arhat and the Buddha. One reality, all the names. And yet the schools were dismissed: the gods are all one, but the doctrines are all vain. The unity is in the state, not in the systems.

## v. 152
The close introduces "the state of difference" — the dualistic world — and the final arc: the Akulavīra is not merely the negation of the world; it is what the world's difference dissolves into. The last lines promise the outcome: no age, no death, no grief — the remover of all diseases, the stainless, everywhere, always.
