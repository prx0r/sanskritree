# To Translate Next

Texts still lacking English translation, with edited Sanskrit already available (best starting point — you're not fighting manuscripts, just translating).

## Kaula/Bhairava Tantras (Dyczkowski editions, Muktabodha)

12 Kaula/Bhairava Tantras edited by Dyczkowski from Nepalese manuscripts, sitting as e-texts in Muktabodha's digital library. These include Tantras of the Kalikula that developed into the Krama system, the Śrītantrasadbhāva (an important, extensive Trika text Kashmiri Śaivites adopted as their own), and two Kubjikā-tradition Tantras edited for the first time — a tradition Sanderson originally handed Dyczkowski. This is the single best set to work from: critically edited Sanskrit, zero or minimal English translation, directly in our lineage.

Source: Muktalib7

## Tier 1 — foundational, largely untranslated, huge impact if done well

### Jayadrathayāmala
Cited alongside the Kaulajñānanirṇaya, Kubjikāmata, and Kularatnoddyota as core early yoginī-cult scripture. Massive (tens of thousands of verses across four sections), only small fragments have ever been translated by specialists like Judit Törzsök. Pick one chapter, not the whole thing.

Source: Khecaridevi

## Short Nyāya texts, less-translated than the famous ones

### Nyāyasāra (Bhāsarvajña)
A 10th-century philosopher associated with Kashmir, primarily known as a Nyāya author — his Nyāyasāra is a concise, independent Nyāya treatise, genuinely less picked-over than Gautama's core Nyāyasūtra (which every intro course already covers).

Source: Wikipedia
